## Intro

Alfred charges £195 a year and then sells you energy, mobile, broadband and insurance at the price it pays for them. No margin on the units, no loyalty penalty, no annual switching ritual. It is the Costco model pointed at household bills, and the company is open about the catch: if your household doesn't use much, the membership can cost more than it saves. We checked everything Alfred has published as of 4 October 2026.

## What Alfred is

One provider for six things most households currently buy from six different companies: electricity, gas, mobile, broadband, home insurance, car insurance and breakdown cover.

The membership is **£195 a year**, plus **£125 a year** for each additional home. That fee is the whole of Alfred's margin. Everything else is sold on at cost, which is why the pitch is "members pay what we pay".

You don't have to move everything at once. Alfred's own answer is direct: *"You can start with one service and Alfred can remind you as your contract ends on the others."* The membership is £195 whether you take one service or all six, so the arithmetic improves with every service you move across.

Services are billed individually under a single direct debit, with one view of them in the app. It isn't one combined bill, which is what most people picture when they hear "one provider".

## Who actually supplies it

Alfred is not building power stations or laying fibre. It buys wholesale and resells:

- **Energy** — British Gas
- **Mobile** — EE, as an MVNO
- **Broadband** — BT Openreach and Vodafone infrastructure
- **Insurance** — brokered, with Aviva, AXA and Allianz named on the panel

That matters in a good way: the coverage, speeds and claims handling are the same ones you would get buying direct from those brands. Alfred is a buying club in front of them, not a new network.

On regulation, Alfred's own description: *"Alfred is a regulated provider of energy, mobile and broadband… However, for insurance we're both."* The insurance arm, Alfred Insurance Brokers Limited, operates as an appointed representative of PHL Insurance Brokers Ltd (FRN 301732). If you claim on a policy, the insurer handles it, not Alfred.

## What £195 has to beat

This is the only question that matters, and Alfred answers it more honestly than most companies would:

> "Alfred sells energy at the price it costs to supply, with no margin added. Instead, membership costs £195 a year. So the more energy your household uses, the more you save compared with a supplier who adds a margin to every unit. **Use less, and the membership might cost more than it saves you.**"

So this is a volume play. A four-bedroom house with two cars, two mobiles and a teenager's shower habit is the target customer. A single person in a one-bed flat who already switches every year is not.

Alfred's published estimates: **up to £1,000 a year** for busy households, and the calculator on their site returns **£949** for a three-bed detached with British Gas energy, two EE mobiles, BT broadband and two cars insured. The press launch put **more than £2,000** on the largest households. These are Alfred's figures, for specific profiles, and Alfred labels them as illustrative for a service still in development — not a quote.

Before you commit, they say they'll show you a projected price based on your actual usage, and if the savings disappoint they refund the membership.

## Alfred and Utility Warehouse

If this sounds familiar, it should. **Utility Warehouse** — part of FTSE-listed Telecom Plus — has bundled energy, broadband, mobile and insurance onto one bill for years, has **over a million customers**, and charges **no membership fee at all**. Its discounts grow as you add services.

The difference is where the money comes from. Utility Warehouse makes its margin on the services and hands some of it back as a multi-service discount. Alfred takes its margin up front as a flat fee and claims to add nothing to the services. Whether that's better depends entirely on how much you use: a flat fee is brilliant for a heavy user and poor for a light one, while a percentage discount scales with your bill either way.

Until Alfred publishes unit rates, nobody can settle it with a calculator — including us. What we can say is that Utility Warehouse is a known quantity with a service record you can look up, and Alfred is not yet.

## Where it doesn't work

**Light users can lose.** Alfred says so themselves. £195 is roughly £16 a month before you have saved a penny, and a low-usage flat may never recover it.

**You cannot see a single price.** There is no p/kWh, no standing charge, no broadband speed or monthly cost, no mobile allowance and no insurance excess published anywhere. You are being asked to join a waitlist for a deal you cannot inspect. That is unusual, and it is a legitimate reason to wait.

**The membership stops being refundable if you take insurance.** Leave early and Alfred refunds the unused months — *"if you leave after 8 months, we'll pay you back £65 (4/12 × £195)"*. But take out a 12-month insurance policy and that refund goes away for the term.

**There's an exit fee on fixed energy.** £50 if you leave the Wholesale Fixed tariff early. No exit fees on Wholesale Variable, mobile or broadband.

**No heating oil.** Homes off the mains gas grid aren't served.

**And it hasn't launched.** Alfred Wholesale Limited was incorporated on 5 March 2025, the waitlist opened on 25 August 2026, and the app is in private beta. There are no customers to ask, no complaints record, and no service history. The company has raised £5m at a £27.5m valuation from Liberty Global and others, and is aiming for 50,000 members within three years — which is real money and a real plan, but it is a plan.

## Questions people are asking

**Can I join if I can't switch everything yet?**
Yes. Alfred: *"You can start with one service."* The £195 doesn't change, so your savings improve as you move more across.

**Is it one bill?**
No. One direct debit, but each service is billed separately so you can see what each costs. The app gives you a single view.

**Who handles a claim?**
The insurer. Alfred brokers the policy; Aviva, AXA and Allianz are named on the panel.

**How is this different from Utility Warehouse?**
Utility Warehouse charges no membership fee and discounts the services. Alfred charges £195 and claims to add no margin at all. Heavy users should prefer Alfred's model, light users Utility Warehouse's.

**Can I leave?**
Yes, with no exit fee unless you're on Wholesale Fixed energy (£50). Unused membership is refunded pro-rata, unless you've taken a 12-month insurance policy.

## Verdict

Alfred is the most interesting thing to happen to household bills in a while, and the honesty of its own FAQ does it credit — a company that tells you in writing that its product may cost you money is not trying to fool anybody.

But there is nothing to buy yet and no price to check. If you have a big house, several cars, a family's worth of mobiles and no appetite for switching every year, the waitlist is worth joining and costs nothing. If you're a light user, or you already switch annually and enjoy it, wait for the rates and judge it then.
