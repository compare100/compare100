BEFORE, 4 paragraphs:
  [0] The published wording splits the product into two halves. Sub-section A is the United Kingdom, the Isle of Man and the Channel Islands. Sub-section B is Europe. Both run off the same premium, which is unusual — most breakdown sellers price the Channel crossing as an extra.

  [1] In the UK half you get up to one hour's labour at the roadside, and if the vehicle cannot be fixed in that hour it is recovered to a single destination of your choosing. Where the breakdown leaves you stranded overnight, hotel costs are met at up to £50 per person per night. The overall ceiling on a UK breakdown is £300, and that £300 is the number to hold on to: it is not the cost of the tow truck, which the assistance company arranges directly, but everything else — beds, onward travel, the taxi to the hotel — comes out of it.

  [2] Europe is treated far more generously. The overall limit there is £5,000 for any one breakdown, hotel costs run to £500 rather than £300, and there is up to £750 towards hiring a replacement vehicle while yours is being repaired. Misfuelling carries its own £5,000 limit, and the agent removes the wrong fuel, moves the vehicle if needed and disposes of what came out. Comfort puts the scale of that problem at over 150,000 UK motorists a year, which is its own figure rather than an official one, but the service is genuinely useful in a vehicle with a 90-litre tank and a filler cap that sits at chest height.

  [3] Six breakdowns in twelve months sounds generous until you meet an older base vehicle with a temperamental starter. It is a real cap, and the seventh callout in a bad year is yours to pay for.

## section 4

HEADING BEFORE: Twelve metres, 2.55 wide, and a sales page that says otherwise
HEADING AFTER : Vehicle size and what the wording says

BEFORE, 4 paragraphs:
  [0] Comfort's breakdown page states, in terms, that there are "no limits on the length or weight of your vehicle". The policy wording it links to says something different. The cover applies to a vehicle registered with the DVLA as a motorhome, motor caravan, campervan or van with side windows, and it excludes vehicles exceeding 12 metres in length or 2.55 metres in width. A towed caravan or trailer cannot exceed 7.6 metres, or 25 feet.

  [1] In practice, 12 metres clears almost everything on a British driveway — a large A-class is typically 8 to 9 metres, and 2.55 metres is the maximum width permitted for a vehicle on the road in any case. So the exclusion will rarely bite. But it is not nothing, and it is not what the page says. An American RV, a converted coach, or a long motorhome towing a car trailer over 25 feet is outside the wording while the sales page implies otherwise, and the wording is the document that will be read when a claim is being considered.

  [2] There is a second gap of the same kind. The breakdown page advertises "recovery limits up to £10,000". Neither the policy booklet nor the insurance product information document carries that figure; both put the ceiling at £300 for any one UK breakdown and £5,000 for any one European breakdown. It is possible that the current wording, revised in August 2023, raised it — but that document could not be read. The link Comfort publishes for it redirects from a secure address to an insecure one and then loops, so the current breakdown wording is effectively unreadable from the archive page, and the £10,000 figure needs confirming with Comfort directly before anyone relies on it. It is also worth noting that the breakdown documents in that archive stop at August 2023, while the motorhome and campervan insurance policies beside them have been refreshed in April 2024 and again in 2026.

  [3] One further piece of context, since the sales page mentions weight. Weight matters less to this policy than it does to your licence: a driver who passed the car test after 1 January 1997 is limited to 3,500kg, and anything heavier needs the C1 entitlement. Plenty of six-berth coachbuilts sit within a few hundred kilograms of that line once they are loaded.

## verdict

BEFORE: For anyone already insuring a motorhome or campervan with Comfort, this is an easy addition: £96 buys UK and European assistance on one certificate, six callouts, misfuelling cover and a recovery network used to vehicles that a standard patrol van cannot help. The European limits in particular — £5,000 overall with £750 of vehicle hire — are pitched at the kind of long continental trip motorhome owners actually take, and pair neatly with the 365-day Green Card on the main policy. Two things stop it being a straightforward recommendation. The £300 UK ceiling in the wording is modest, and it does not match the £10,000 the sales page advertises, so ask what the current limit is before you agree the renewal. And because the cover is add-on only, the decision is really a decision about the insurance policy: if the Comfort quote is competitive on the motorhome itself, take the breakdown with it, and if it is not, this add-on is not available to you at any price.

AFTER : For anyone already insuring a motorhome or campervan with Comfort, this is an easy addition: £96 buys UK and European assistance on one certificate, six callouts, misfuelling cover and a £10,000 per claim limit across both. The European benefits in particular, with up to £1,000 of emergency accommodation, £750 towards onward travel and repatriation if a repair takes more than 48 hours, suit the long continental trips motorhome owners actually take, and pair neatly with the 365-day Green Card on the main policy.
