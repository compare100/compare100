A2Z Airport Parking: Compare & Book

Rates and terms checked 23 September 2026 · Airport Parking · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

A week's park and ride at Manchester came to £57.04 through A2Z Airport Parking on 23 September 2026 — dropped off at noon on 30 September, collected at noon on 7 October, with a shuttle the listing puts at ten to fifteen minutes. That is £8.15 a day. The same search at Heathrow returned one car park at £71.99, or £10.28 a day. Both figures come from A2Z's own airport pages, read that morning.

A2Z Airport Parking Ltd is a booking agent rather than a car park company. It has been on the register since 28 March 2017 as company 10693802, trades from 390 London Road in Mitcham, lists parking at 21 UK airports and takes calls on 0333 5678 678, a number that comes out of an inclusive mobile allowance rather than at a premium rate. You can put dates into any of its airport pages and see prices without handing over a card, which is not true of every site in this trade.

It is also unusually plain about what it is. The about page, the FAQ and the terms all say the same thing in almost the same words — "We act only as a booking agent. Your parking contract is with the individual car park operator" — and the FAQ goes further, telling you that if you are twenty minutes from the airport and something has gone wrong you should ring the operator on your voucher and not A2Z. That decides which number you call on the day, so it is worth knowing before you book rather than after.

What a week actually cost, airport by airport

Prices on a booking site mean nothing without the dates attached, so here are both, taken on 23 September 2026 for a drop-off at 12:00 on 30 September and a return at 12:00 on 7 October.

Manchester returned four park and ride products: Super Saver at £57.04, A2Z Park & Ride at £60.49, Red Rose Park & Ride at £62.79 and VIP Premium at £66.24. Each carried a struck-through price beside it — £62.74, £66.54, £69.07 and £72.86 — and if you work the four reductions out they are the same to two decimal places every time: 9.09 per cent off. That is one blanket discount applied across a supplier's range, not four separately negotiated deals, and it is worth seeing for what it is. Set it against the banner the site runs on every page, "Save up to 60% OFF Airport Parking", and the gap is large. The FAQ explains where the 60 comes from: pre-booked parking is "typically 60% cheaper than gate rates", meaning the price you would pay turning up unannounced at a terminal barrier. That is a fair thing to say and a true one, but it is a comparison against the most expensive way to park, not against the price beside it on the page.

Heathrow was thinner. "One parking option is currently bookable at Heathrow," the page said, and the option was Park Inn Heathrow at £71.99 for the week, operated by PNR Holidays Ltd, with a shuttle running 04:00 to midnight and serving all four terminals. One choice is not a comparison. It is also, on the arithmetic above, £2.13 a day dearer than the cheapest Manchester product, which tells you something about how thin the Heathrow supply is rather than anything about Heathrow.

Gatwick, Stansted, Luton and Birmingham returned no priced listings at all when their pages were read that morning; the search form was there and the inventory was not. That may be a live-inventory question rather than a permanent state — stock in this trade moves daily — so the honest way to put it is that on one September morning the site could sell four products at Manchester, one at Heathrow and nothing visible at four of the biggest airports on its own list.

Four Manchester results, one supplier

Look again at the four Manchester listings and read the small grey line under each name. Every one of them says the same thing: "Operated by RED ROSE AIRPORT PARKING LTD". Not one of the four is a different company. The product named A2Z Park & Ride, which a reasonable person would take to be the site's own car park, is run by Red Rose as well.

Red Rose Airport Parking Ltd is company 17261471. It was incorporated on 4 June 2026 — fifteen weeks before these prices were read — at 4a Entwistle Street, Bolton, BL2 2ER, under SIC codes 49390 and 52219 for passenger land transport and services incidental to it. Its first accounts are not due until 4 March 2028 and its first confirmation statement not until 17 June 2027, so there is nothing yet on the public record about its size, its staff or its finances. Nobody can look up how many spaces it has or how long it has been running a shuttle, because as a company it has not been running one for long.

None of that makes it a bad car park. New companies take over established sites all the time, and the Bolton address and the transport SIC codes are exactly what you would expect of an operator rather than a shell. But a page headed "4 parking options are currently bookable at Manchester" is doing the work of a comparison, and a comparison of four products from one fifteen-week-old supplier is a price list with four rows.

The car park company and the website share an owner

This is a matter of public record and it is the single most useful thing on this page, so it is worth setting out precisely and letting you weigh it.

A2Z Airport Parking Ltd changed hands this summer. Sudha Sunil Pawar, sole director and holder of 75 per cent or more of the shares since the company was formed on 28 March 2017, resigned on 30 June 2026 and ceased to be a person with significant control the same day. Aamir Raja was appointed director on 1 July 2026 and notified as holding 75 per cent or more of the shares and voting rights, with the right to appoint and remove directors.

Red Rose Airport Parking Ltd — the operator behind all four Manchester listings — has one officer and one person with significant control, both recorded on 4 June 2026. The name is Aamir Raja, with the same recorded month and year of birth, January 1978, and the same 75-per-cent-or-more holding. Both entries show Companies House identity verification completed.

So the person who owns the comparison site also owns the company supplying every parking product it sells at Manchester, including the one carrying the site's own name. There is nothing unlawful about that, vertical integration is common in travel, and a site is entitled to sell its own stock. What it is not entitled to do is present that stock as the outcome of a search. Nowhere on the Manchester page, the about page or the terms does A2Z disclose the connection, and a reader comparing four prices is entitled to know that all four come from the same pocket. The APH review on this site records the opposite case for comparison: an operator whose own booking page put a rival's Gatwick car park £10.03 below its own. Disclosure costs nothing and buys a great deal.

Twenty-four hours means something different in each document

Every listing carries a green badge reading "Free cancellation for 24 hours". The homepage repeats it. Read as anybody would read it beside a departure date, it says you can change your mind up to a day before you travel. It does not mean that.

The terms say free cancellation applies within 24 hours of making the booking, and that after that a fee applies. They then add a second and much harder deadline: a flexible booking "may be cancelled when we receive the request at least 48 hours before the scheduled parking arrival time", and "requests received within 48 hours are non-refundable". Non-flexible bookings, the cheaper tier, "cannot be cancelled, amended or refunded" at all. So for a Saturday morning departure, the real deadline is Thursday morning, and the badge on the listing is a day out.

The fee itself appears twice on the site with two different numbers. The terms and conditions page puts it at £10.00. The FAQ says "a £20.00 cancellation fee will be deducted from the refund". Both are A2Z's own documents, both were live on 23 September 2026, and nothing on either page says which supersedes the other. Work to the higher figure until somebody tells you otherwise, and ask before you pay if the difference matters to you.

Around those two numbers sits a set of rules that are, to be fair, spelled out properly. Amendments need 48 hours' notice too; a date change or booking transfer costs £5.00 plus any increase in the parking price; a change of time alone is free at 48 hours or more; and the cooling-off provision that makes standard amendments free in the first 24 hours applies only if you booked at least 72 hours before departure. Cancel more than five days out and you can take a voucher instead of a refund, worth what you paid less £5 and valid for 24 months. Late Return Cover, on the products that carry it, meets extra parking costs for up to 48 hours where the delay is documented, but excludes extreme weather, illness and simply extending the trip.

A booking fee inside a promise of no hidden fees

"No hidden fees," says the homepage. "The price you see is the price you pay." The terms say that "all bookings are subject to a non-refundable booking fee".

Those two sentences can both be true, and on most bookings they probably are, because a fee folded into the displayed total is not hidden. The problem is that the amount is nowhere. It is not on the homepage, not on the airport pages, not in the terms and not in the FAQ, which explains the fee's purpose — "the booking fee covers running our comparison site, customer support and secure card processing" — without ever saying what it is. Publishing the reason for a charge while withholding its size is an odd way round, and it means the one number you cannot check before checkout is the one A2Z keeps.

The Heathrow listing carries a second line in the same family: "Airport Access Fees Not Included". Several UK airports now charge a vehicle for entering the forecourt, the charge is levied on arrival rather than at booking, and it is the sort of sum that turns a £71.99 week into something else. The Gatwick page words it differently and better — "any checkout charges known to us, including airport access fees, are shown before you reach the payment step" — which suggests the intention is right and the wording has not caught up with itself across the site.

One charge is quantified, and it is the one that hurts most. If your flight comes back late and you overrun the booking, the FAQ says you pay the operator's prevailing rate, "normally £25–£45 per extra day", and you pay it on the day, to them, not to A2Z. On a £57.04 week, two late days can add three-quarters again to the bill.

Who is responsible for the car once the keys change hands

The terms are candid about the limits of what a booking agent owes you: "A2Z is not responsible for losses caused by third-party providers", and "A2Z's total liability is limited to the amount paid to A2Z for the affected booking". In practice that caps the site's exposure at the price of the parking — £57.04 on the cheapest Manchester product — whatever the car is worth.

Everything beyond that cap belongs to the operator, and the operator is where your legal rights actually live. Leaving a car with staff who take the keys creates a bailment at common law, and the Consumer Rights Act 2015 requires the service to be carried out with reasonable care and skill. Those are real duties with real remedies, but they are owed by Red Rose Airport Parking Ltd at Manchester or PNR Holidays Ltd at Heathrow, and enforcing them means dealing with those companies. It is worth knowing what the register says about each before you need to. Red Rose has filed nothing yet. PNR Holidays Ltd, company 15874951, incorporated 3 August 2024 in Hayes, was showing a confirmation statement overdue on 23 September 2026, with the statement due on 16 August 2026 — an administrative lapse rather than a distress signal, and a small one, but it is the kind of thing worth a glance when a company is about to be handed your car keys.

A2Z's own complaints route is published, which many sites in this trade do not manage. It asks for a booking reference through the support desk, aims to acknowledge "as soon as reasonably practicable" and to give a substantive answer within 14 days, and asks that service complaints be raised "within 24 hours of return to the UK where practical". That last one deserves a note: 24 hours is a short window in which to notice a scratched wing, unpack and put a complaint together after a long flight. Nothing in law shortens your rights because you missed it, so do not treat it as a deadline that costs you anything.

Where the route ends is the same place it ends everywhere in this trade. There is no FCA permission because airport parking is not a financial service, no Financial Ombudsman, no compensation scheme and no ADR body named anywhere on the site. After the support desk the next step is the county court. This is an absence common to the whole sector rather than a failing peculiar to A2Z, but it is the reason the small print matters more here than it does when you buy a regulated product.

Two scheme names that have moved on without the FAQ

A small thing, and a useful signal about how often the site's own pages are read. The FAQ explains what a "Park Mark awarded car park" is and what "BPA member" means. Park Mark ceased to be the name of that award on 1 April 2026, when the scheme moved to Police Crime Prevention Initiatives and every award was renamed under the Safer Parking banner; the British Parking Association no longer runs it. Nearly six months later the FAQ still describes the old arrangement.

Nothing turns on it for a customer — the car parks are the same car parks and the inspections are the same inspections — but a page that tells you what an accreditation means should know what it is called. Worth asking, too, whether a specific car park holds a current award, because neither the listings nor the FAQ say which sites are accredited and under which name.

On membership, the Independent Airport Parking Association lists 15 member companies on its own site, among them Airparks, APH, Maple Parking, Secure Airparks and Sentinel Parking. Neither A2Z Airport Parking Ltd nor Red Rose Airport Parking Ltd appears on that list. Membership is voluntary and its absence proves nothing about a car park's quality, but the association's ten-point code is the only published standard this trade has, so it is a reasonable thing to ask a supplier about.

What could not be established

Four things resisted checking, and they are listed rather than guessed at.

The size of the booking fee could not be found on any page of the site, and it would need a booking taken to checkout to discover. The company's filed accounts could not be read: the Companies House filing history returned a 403 on 23 September 2026, so there is no turnover, profit or headcount figure here and none should be inferred from the "320k+ happy clients" on the about page — which sits oddly beside "150,000+ bookings" on the homepage, two of the company's own numbers that do not obviously describe the same thing. No ICO registration entry was located for the company, and the privacy policy does not quote a registration number, though it does describe the sharing and retention practices you would expect; that is a gap in what is published rather than evidence of anything. And a separate domain at a2zairportparking.uk presented a TLS certificate that did not match the hostname when it was requested, so it could not be read at all and its relationship to this company, if any, is unknown.

Prices were read on one morning in September for one seven-night stay. They will not be the prices you see, and the Manchester and Heathrow figures above should be treated as a snapshot to compare against rather than a quote.

  • Tells you to ring the operator rather than A2Z on the day, which is the advice that actually gets a car collected
  • A week's park and ride at Manchester from £57.04, or £8.15 a day, with a shuttle quoted at ten to fifteen minutes
  • Publishes a complaints route with a 14-day target for a substantive answer, which much of this trade does not
  • Explains what the booking fee pays for, and names Stripe as the card processor
  • A 0333 customer line that comes out of inclusive mobile minutes, plus separate numbers for hotels, flights and car hire
  • Nine years on the register as company 10693802, which matches the "9+ years operating" the site claims

Common questions

Is A2Z Airport Parking a car park or a booking site?

A booking site. A2Z Airport Parking Ltd says in its terms that it "acts as a booking platform and booking agent only" and does not provide parking itself, and its FAQ repeats it: the company does not own car parks or handle vehicles. Your contract for the parking is with the operator, and on the day you ring the operator's number on your voucher rather than A2Z.

How much is a week's parking through A2Z?

On 23 September 2026, for a seven-night stay from 30 September, Manchester park and ride started at £57.04 — £8.15 a day — with three dearer products at £60.49, £62.79 and £66.24. Heathrow had one bookable car park at £71.99, or £10.28 a day. Those were live prices for specific dates and will not be the prices you are quoted.

Can I cancel an A2Z booking for free?

Only within 24 hours of making it, and only on a flexible rate. After that a fee applies, and the terms and the FAQ disagree about whether it is £10 or £20. The harder deadline is that a cancellation must reach A2Z at least 48 hours before your parking arrival time; inside 48 hours nothing is refundable. Non-flexible rates cannot be cancelled, amended or refunded at any point.

What happens if my flight home is delayed?

You pay the car park, not A2Z. The FAQ says that if you stay longer than booked, including because a flight was delayed or cancelled, the operator's prevailing overstay rate applies, "normally £25–£45 per extra day". Some products carry Late Return Cover, which meets extra parking costs for up to 48 hours where the delay is documented, but it excludes extreme weather, illness and extending the trip yourself.

Who actually runs the car parks A2Z sells?

At Manchester, all four listings on 23 September 2026 were "Operated by RED ROSE AIRPORT PARKING LTD", company 17261471, incorporated 4 June 2026. At Heathrow the single option was operated by PNR Holidays Ltd, company 15874951. Red Rose shares a director and majority owner with A2Z Airport Parking Ltd — Aamir Raja, recorded at both companies in June and July 2026 — which the site does not mention.

Is airport parking covered by an ombudsman?

No. Airport parking is not a regulated financial service, so there is no FCA authorisation, no Financial Ombudsman Service and no compensation scheme, and A2Z names no alternative dispute resolution body. What you do have is the Consumer Rights Act 2015 duty of reasonable care and skill, and the common law of bailment from the moment staff take your keys. Those are enforced against the car park operator, in the county court if it comes to it.

Our verdict

Treat A2Z as a shop rather than a search engine and it comes out reasonably well. £57.04 for a week at Manchester is a keen price, the airport pages show a real total for your real dates without asking for a card, and the site is franker than most about being a booking agent whose job ends when the operator takes your keys. What it is not, on the evidence of 23 September 2026, is a comparison: four Manchester results from one fifteen-week-old supplier owned by A2Z's own proprietor, one car park at Heathrow, and nothing priced at Gatwick or Stansted. Before booking, settle three numbers in writing — the booking fee, whether the cancellation fee is £10 or £20, and the operator's overstay rate against the £25–£45 a day the FAQ quotes — and diarise the 48-hour cancellation deadline rather than the 24 hours the badge suggests.
