Admiral Car Insurance Review

Rates and terms checked 16 August 2026 · Car Insurance Deals · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Admiral is best understood through two things: MultiCar, which is the reason most people end up with them, and Essential, which is the tier to avoid.

Admiral's comprehensive policies carry a 5-star Defaqto rating — but that rating explicitly excludes Essential cover. Essential drops windscreen cover and European cover entirely. It sits in comparison tables next to the proper policies at a lower price, and a lot of drivers buy it without registering what has been removed.

MultiCar is the genuine differentiator. Add a second or third vehicle to one policy and the discount is usually larger than anything you will find shopping each car separately. It is the strongest reason to choose Admiral and it only applies to households with more than one car.

Essential is not a cheaper version of the same policy

It is a materially different product, and the two omissions matter.

No windscreen cover. A replacement windscreen on a modern car with camera-based driver assistance frequently runs past £500 once recalibration is included. On every other Admiral comprehensive tier that is covered. On Essential you pay it.

No European cover. Drive to France on an Essential policy and you are running on the legal minimum required in that country, not comprehensive cover. Damage to your own vehicle is not covered.

The Defaqto exclusion is the clearest signal available. Admiral's comprehensive range is 5-star rated and Essential specifically is not included in that rating — an independent assessor declining to put it in the same category as its siblings.

Essential makes sense for an older, low-value car that never leaves the UK, where you have decided a cracked windscreen is a cost you would absorb anyway. For anything else the saving is smaller than the exposure.

MultiCar: the actual reason to choose Admiral

MultiCar lets a household insure several vehicles under one policy, each with its own no-claims bonus, at a combined price usually below the sum of separate policies.

It suits a specific household shape: two working adults with a car each, or parents insuring a young driver alongside their own vehicles. The second case is where it bites hardest, because young-driver premiums are punishing in isolation and the MultiCar structure softens that considerably.

MultiCover extends the same idea to home insurance, bundling car and home for a further discount.

One thing to check rather than assume: the vehicles generally need to be at the same address. Adding a car kept at a student address or a second home is where MultiCar quotes tend to fall over, and it is worth confirming before you build a renewal plan around it.

If you are a single-car household with no home insurance to bundle, the main argument for Admiral disappears and you should be comparing purely on price and cover level.

The admin fees deserve attention

Around £15 to amend a policy, and £60 to £160 to cancel, depending on circumstances.

The cancellation range is the one to note. If you sell a car mid-term, write it off, or simply find a better price at month seven, you are paying a fee that can approach the cost of two months' premium. That materially changes the maths on switching mid-policy.

Amendment fees add up too if your circumstances move — a change of address, a change of job title, adding a named driver. None are large individually, but a policy that gets amended three times has quietly cost £45 more than the quoted premium.

Compare that against insurers offering free amendments if you expect your details to change during the year.

Where the cover is genuinely good

Set Essential aside and the rest of the range is strong.

Platinum bundles European cover, windscreen, courtesy car and roadside breakdown into one policy, which removes the add-on juggling that makes comparison sites so misleading. Gold is the same shape with breakdown optional.

The voluntary excess range is unusually granular — £50 increments from £0 to £500, then £700, £750 and £1,000. Most insurers offer four or five options. That granularity lets you tune the premium precisely rather than jumping between wide bands.

Child car seat cover as standard across all comprehensive tiers is a small but genuine inclusion; seats must be replaced after any impact and they are not cheap.

Courtesy cars come through Admiral-approved garages, which is standard practice but does mean you use their repair network rather than your own choice of garage.

Where it falls short

  • Cancellation fees of £60 to £160 make mid-term switching expensive

  • Single-car households without home insurance lose the main advantage

Our verdict

Strong if you have more than one vehicle, or a car and home policy to bundle — MultiCar and MultiCover are genuinely competitive and hard to beat by shopping separately. Take Platinum or Gold rather than Essential: the missing windscreen and European cover are worth more than the saving, and Defaqto's refusal to include Essential in the 5-star rating tells you what an independent assessor makes of it. If you are a single-car household with no bundle to build, compare on price like any other insurer, and factor the £60–£160 cancellation fee into any plan to switch mid-term.

Common questions

What is the difference between Admiral and Admiral Essential?

Essential removes windscreen cover and European cover, and is excluded from Admiral's 5-star Defaqto rating. The standard Admiral, Gold and Platinum tiers include windscreen cover and carry the rating.

How does MultiCar work?

You insure several vehicles under one policy, each keeping its own no-claims bonus, at a combined price usually lower than separate policies. Vehicles generally need to be at the same address — confirm this before relying on it.

What are Admiral's admin fees?

Roughly £15 to amend a policy and £60 to £160 to cancel, depending on circumstances. The cancellation fee is worth factoring in if you might switch mid-term or sell the car.

Is a courtesy car included?

Yes with comprehensive cover, provided the repair goes through an Admiral-approved garage. Using your own choice of garage may forfeit it.

Does Admiral cover driving in Europe?

Platinum includes European cover. Essential excludes it entirely, leaving you on the legal minimum abroad rather than comprehensive cover.
