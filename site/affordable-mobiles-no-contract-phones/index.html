Affordable Mobiles: No Contract Phones

Rates and terms checked 24 September 2026 · Just Phones · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Affordable Mobiles listed 13 SIM-free handsets on 24 September 2026, running from a Samsung Galaxy A57 5G at £479 to an iPhone 18 Pro Max at £1,299. Every one is sold outright and unlocked — no credit check, no minimum term, no airtime attached — and the prices above were read that morning from the shop's own SIM-free page.

The shop is part of AO. AO World plc turned over £1,266.6m in the year to 31 March 2026, up 11.4%, with adjusted profit before tax of £50.5m and 13.3 million customers on its books, and it took on 720,000 new ones during the year. That matters on a purchase you make outright, because the money goes to a listed British retailer with published accounts rather than to a name with nothing behind it.

Read the footer and the terms together and you get the useful detail early: the website belongs to Affordable Mobiles Limited, company 15476278, but the order itself is fulfilled by AO Retail Ltd, company 03914998, trading as Mobile Phones Direct, and clause 1.2 of the terms makes AO Retail the party to your contract. So the company you ring about a late parcel or a dead handset is AO Retail, and its registered office in Bolton is where a formal letter goes.

A brand that outlived the company that built it

Affordable Mobiles has been a familiar name in phone retail for a long time, and the business that built it no longer exists. A1 Comms Limited, company 04455131, ran the site from Derbyshire and had traded since 1997. On 2 February 2024 the High Court appointed David Kemp (IP number 24510) and Richard Hunt (IP number 21772) of SFP Restructuring Limited as joint administrators, under case CR-2024-000622. AO bought the intellectual property and the websites — affordablemobiles.co.uk, buymobiles.net and mobileshop.com — out of that administration for £2.2m in cash, saving 73 jobs; A1's gross assets of £41.6m, mostly contract assets and stock, stayed behind. The administration ran until 29 October 2025, when the old company passed into creditors' voluntary liquidation with Rachael Wilkinson and Peter David Dickens appointed the same day.

The company now behind the website was incorporated a week after the administrators walked in. Affordable Mobiles Limited (15476278) was registered on 9 February 2024 under SIC codes 47421 and 47910 — retail sale of mobile telephones, and retail by mail order or internet — with its first accounts made up to 31 March 2025 and the next due by 31 December 2026.

None of that is a warning. A brand rescued by a larger buyer is a better outcome for customers than a brand that disappears, and the buyer here is substantial. It is simply worth knowing that a guarantee, a cashback promise or a dispute from before February 2024 belongs to a company in liquidation, and that everything since belongs to AO.

Three companies, one Bolton postcode

Put the three registrations side by side and the structure is plain. Affordable Mobiles Limited (15476278), AO Retail Limited (03914998) and musicMagpie Limited (12977343) all give their registered office as 5a The Parklands, Lostock, Bolton, BL6 4SD.

AO Retail is the oldest of the three and has worn two other names: it was incorporated on 28 January 2000 as Appliances Online Limited, became DRL Limited on 2 May 2000, and took its present name on 30 April 2014. musicMagpie arrived by takeover — AO acquired it through a scheme of arrangement under Part 26 of the Companies Act, and the register records the company dropping its plc status on 20 January 2025, which dates the completion better than any press release.

That last point has a practical edge. The trade-in valuation offered on the Affordable Mobiles site is musicMagpie's, so the firm quoting you for your old phone and the firm selling you the new one are in the same group. The terms are good ones — a free instant valuation, a pre-paid label, payment by bank transfer on the day the phone is delivered, and a price promise that you either get the first figure offered or the handset comes back free — and musicMagpie will not take a phone snapped in half, bent, crushed, or missing components. But a valuation from a connected company is still worth checking against one that is not, and it takes five minutes.

What you give up, and what you get back, by paying once

The whole argument for a SIM-free phone is that the two purchases come apart. You buy the handset, then you buy airtime, and neither decision traps the other. Three consequences are worth spelling out because the bundled alternative hides all of them.

There is no credit agreement. A bundled phone contract is a rolling commitment with a network, and the site runs credit checks and security ID checks on those orders; a SIM-free order is a single purchase of goods, so nothing is searched and nothing is recorded. There is no minimum term and therefore no early termination charge to calculate. And the phone is unlocked from the start — the shop's own SIM-free page says that its phones "are unlocked, which means they can be used with any compatible SIM card without restrictions" and that "you can change networks at any time", which since Ofcom's ban on selling locked handsets took effect on 17 December 2021 is what every new phone sold in the UK should offer anyway.

Against that, paying once means paying it all at once. £479 is a single debit rather than £20 a month, and nobody is spreading it for you unless you arrange that separately. It also means the airtime is your problem: the page shows logos for more than a dozen networks that its SIM-free phones work with, and choosing between them is a job the bundle does for you.

The airtime the shop itself sells is worth counting, because the menu is longer than the choice. Six brands appear: O2, Three, Vodafone, Talkmobile, SMARTY and VOXI. SMARTY's own terms describe it as "a trading name of Hutchison 3G UK Limited" (03885486). VOXI's help pages say "Vodafone Limited, trading as VOXI". Talkmobile Limited (04154716) records Vodafone Limited as holding 75% or more of its shares and votes, notified on 6 April 2016. Since Vodafone and Three combined into VodafoneThree, five of those six brands sit inside one merged operator and only O2 is outside it — so six tiles represent two networks, which is a fair thing to know before you treat the list as a comparison.

Modified stock, and why a phone here can cost less than the maker charges

This is the most useful thing on the site and it is tucked into a help page rather than printed beside the prices. Some handsets sold here are modified stock, described in the shop's own words as "mobile phone stock that's sourced globally" and "inspected and officially modified by authorised partners to ensure it includes UK-compatible software and settings". The reason given for the price is equally plain: the shop sources "from a wider range of official suppliers to find the best possible price in the market", which makes modified stock "cheaper than UK stock".

Four consequences follow, and the page states three of them itself. Boxes "may arrive resealed as part of our routine quality testing process", so a factory seal is not guaranteed. Some models have no eSIM, which on a phone bought to move freely between networks is the one specification that decides whether you can switch in five minutes or must wait for a plastic SIM in the post. Modified devices are ineligible for AppleCare+ Theft & Loss in the UK. And the cover is capped: "your device comes with a 12-month warranty, provided through a combination of the manufacturer's warranty and our own cover".

That last figure is where the arithmetic bites, and the shop's own faults page supplies the comparison. It sets out UK manufacturer warranties as Apple 12 months on handset and battery, Samsung 24 months on the handset and 6 months on the battery, and Google 24 months on both. A modified Samsung or Google handset therefore carries twelve months where the same model bought as UK stock carries twenty-four. On an Apple handset nothing is lost on duration; on the other two, a year is.

The honest summary is that this is a legitimate grey-import practice, disclosed in writing, that trades warranty length and eSIM certainty for price. Plenty of buyers will take that trade knowingly and be pleased with it. The criticism is not the practice — it is that the disclosure lives on a help page while the decision is made on a product page.

Who fixes it, and when the fixing stops being theirs

The fault route is short and it changes hands at day 30. Inside the first 30 days on a new phone, Affordable Mobiles says it "will exchange it free of charge if we can identify a manufacturer's fault". After that the customer is sent to the maker: Apple on 0800 107 6285, Samsung on 0330 726 7864, Google by online support only. Refurbished devices work the other way round — the manufacturer's warranty does not apply to them and the shop handles the exchange itself.

Two rights sit above all of that and neither can be signed away. Under section 22 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 a buyer has a 30-day short-term right to reject goods that are not of satisfactory quality, and the Act says in terms that an agreement under which that right would be lost earlier "is not binding on the consumer" — so the shop may extend the window and may not shorten it. Separately, the 14-day cancellation right on a distance sale is for changing your mind and has nothing to do with faults; the shop's returns page quotes exactly that, "you have 14 days to return it". Readers conflate the two constantly, and a shop that prints only the 14 days on its returns page does nothing to stop them.

After the retailer, there is nowhere obvious to go. Handset retail has no ombudsman and no compensation scheme. Ofcom's approved complaints schemes exist, but they adjudicate the network contract, not the phone — which on a SIM-free purchase you do not have. A dispute that the shop will not settle ends in the county court.

The prices against what the makers charge for the same phone

A shop called Affordable Mobiles invites the test, so here it is, all read on 24 September 2026.

The iPhone 16 at 128GB is £799 here. Apple's own UK store shows the identical phone at £799.00. Not a pound of difference, and Apple will spread it at 0% APR over 24 months at £33.29 a month, which this listing does not offer.

The Galaxy A57 5G at 256GB — the cheapest phone on the page, and the one that sets the entry price for the whole range — is £479. Samsung's own UK product page for the same 256GB model showed "Buy from £404.00 with code A5754" the same morning. That is £75 between a shop whose name is a price claim and the manufacturer's own website, in the manufacturer's favour.

The range itself is the second finding, and it is easily counted. Thirteen tiles appeared on the SIM-free page; twelve carried a price and the Pixel 9a tile showed none on either read. Of the twelve priced, five are iPhones, six are Pixels and one is a Samsung. The filter offers four manufacturers — Apple, Samsung, Google and Motorola — and no Motorola handset appeared. For comparison, the Carphone Warehouse page on this site recorded 165 SIM-free devices on 4 September 2026. Thirteen tiles with a floor of £479 is a narrow shelf for a shop selling outright phones, and it is the single biggest limitation here: if the phone you want is not one of those twelve, there is no version of this page that helps you.

Two charges that arrive after the sale

Neither of these belongs to the SIM-free purchase, and both are waiting on the same website, so a buyer should know them.

The first is cashback, which on this site is a network-deal mechanism rather than a discount. The help page says the shop will "automatically pay it to your bank account by BACS, 90 days from the day your order was shipped", with no claim to submit — which is materially better than a cheque you have to bank. The condition is in clause 18.1 of the terms: the money is "paid directly into your bank account within 90 days" and "your network contract must be active for 90 days" or it "will become void". A cashback offer that requires you to keep a contract running for three months is the exact opposite of what the no-contract shelf is for, and the two sit side by side in the same shop.

The second is Complete Savings, a paid subscription promoted around the checkout and described by the shop as "a subscription-based savings programme, offering members exclusive cashback rewards" — a minimum of 10% back at over 1,000 UK online retailers and up to 20% off gift cards, for a monthly membership fee. It is not run by Affordable Mobiles: the programme's own site names Webloyalty Sàrl, a Swiss company with registered number CHE-344.043.246. Two details are worth carrying to the checkout. The charge appears on a bank statement as WLY*Completesave.co.uk, which is not a name anyone would connect to a phone order. And cancellation is by an online service, by email to cancel@completesavings.co.uk, by phone on 0800 389 6960 (Monday to Friday 8am to 8pm, Saturday 9am to 4pm), or by post to Complete Savings, PO Box 5305, Lancing, BN11 9WD. Anything that turns a one-off purchase into a monthly debit deserves to be read before it is agreed to, not after.

Where a returned phone can cost you money

The change-of-mind return is generous in length and exacting in condition. Clause 13.5 gives 14 days after you or a nominated person receives the goods. Clauses 14.6.1 to 14.6.5 then require the phone to be "in a resalable condition", the "original box and packaging which must remain undamaged", the "original charger, and all other in box accessories", and "pin locks or passcodes removed". The returns page repeats it in plainer words — "the phone is in a pristine condition" — and adds the sentence that costs money: "if any accessories are used, damaged, or missing, you may be charged."

Timings are published, which many retailers avoid. A refund is quoted at 14 days from the day the cancellation is confirmed, and where a network has already billed you, up to 28 days for those charges to come back. The shop pays return postage on a faulty item and the customer pays it on a change of mind, which is the ordinary position in law and worth no complaint.

Two things about that list are worth weighing rather than resenting. A screen protector peeled off and thrown away, or a charger cable used for one night, sits inside "used, damaged, or missing" as written, and the amount of any charge is nowhere published. And "pristine condition" is a higher bar than the statutory one, which allows you to handle goods as you would in a shop. If you intend to try a phone properly before committing, keep the box intact and every cable in its bag.

What could not be established

Four things resisted checking and are set down rather than guessed at.

The monthly fee for Complete Savings is not published on the shop's help page or on the programme's own homepage or terms, which say only that "the period and amount of this fee will be notified during the enrolment process". Anyone signing up should read the figure off the enrolment screen before agreeing, and the trial length is unpublished on the same pages for the same reason.

The proportion of stock that is modified could not be worked out. The help page explains the practice but nothing on the SIM-free listings read on 24 September said which of the twelve priced handsets were modified and which were ordinary UK stock — and since that decides whether a Samsung carries 12 months of cover or 24, it is the question a buyer most needs answered before paying.

No independent rating of this retailer exists. There is no Defaqto equivalent for a phone shop, Ofcom rates networks rather than the shops that sell them, and Which? does not score handset retailers. That is an absence across the whole trade rather than a mark against this shop.

Finally, the Motorola filter returned no priced handset on either read, and nothing on the page explains whether that is a temporary stock gap or a filter left in place after the range changed. Prices and counts here are a snapshot from one morning; treat them as a benchmark to check against rather than a quote.

Common questions

Who actually sells the phone when I order from Affordable Mobiles?

AO Retail Ltd, company 03914998, trading as Mobile Phones Direct. The website belongs to Affordable Mobiles Limited (15476278), but clause 1.2 of the terms makes AO Retail the party to your contract, and the site's own footer says orders "will be fulfilled by AO Retail Ltd". Both companies are registered at 5a The Parklands, Lostock, Bolton, BL6 4SD.

What is modified stock and should I avoid it?

Modified stock is handset stock sourced globally and altered by authorised partners so it carries UK software and settings, which is why it is cheaper than UK stock. The trade-offs are stated by the shop: boxes may arrive resealed, some models have no eSIM, the device cannot be covered by AppleCare+ Theft & Loss in the UK, and the warranty is 12 months made up of the manufacturer's cover plus the shop's own. On a Samsung or Google phone that is half the 24 months the shop's own table gives for UK stock, so ask which you are buying before you pay.

How cheap is the cheapest phone?

£479, for a Samsung Galaxy A57 5G with 256GB, read on 24 September 2026. That was the lowest of 13 SIM-free tiles, of which 12 carried a price, running up to £1,299 for an iPhone 18 Pro Max. Samsung's own UK page for the same 256GB model showed "Buy from £404.00 with code A5754" the same morning, so the manufacturer was cheaper on that handset.

Can I send a SIM-free phone back if I change my mind?

Yes, within 14 days of delivery, but the conditions are strict. The phone must be in resalable, pristine condition, the original box and packaging undamaged, the charger and every in-box accessory returned, and all locks and passcodes removed. The returns page warns that "if any accessories are used, damaged, or missing, you may be charged", and the amount of that charge is not published. A refund is quoted at 14 days from confirmation of the cancellation.

What happens if the phone develops a fault?

Inside 30 days the shop exchanges a new phone free of charge where it can identify a manufacturer's fault. After that you deal with the maker — Apple on 0800 107 6285, Samsung on 0330 726 7864, Google through online support. Refurbished devices are the exception and go back to the shop. Your section 22 right under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 to reject faulty goods within 30 days runs alongside all of that and cannot be shortened by any term of sale.

Is there an ombudsman if the shop will not help?

Not for the handset. Ofcom approves complaints schemes for telecoms providers, and they deal with the network contract rather than a phone bought outright, which on a SIM-free order you do not have. Handset retail has no ombudsman, no regulator and no compensation scheme, so an unresolved dispute ends in the county court under the Consumer Rights Act 2015.

What is the Complete Savings charge on my statement?

Complete Savings is a paid subscription cashback programme promoted around the checkout, run by Webloyalty Sàrl, a Swiss company registered as CHE-344.043.246. It appears on bank statements as WLY*Completesave.co.uk. The monthly fee is not published on the shop's help page or on the programme's own terms, which say only that the amount is notified during enrolment. You can cancel online, by email to cancel@completesavings.co.uk, by phone on 0800 389 6960, or by post to Complete Savings, PO Box 5305, Lancing, BN11 9WD.

Our verdict

As a place to buy a phone outright, Affordable Mobiles is straightforward and better documented than most: the terms say who the seller is, the faults page prints each maker's warranty length, the refund timings are published, and the modified-stock practice that explains the pricing is described in the shop's own words rather than left to be discovered. AO World's accounts sit behind it, which is more than the brand had on 2 February 2024 when its previous owner went into administration. The two things to settle before paying are the range and the stock. Thirteen tiles with a £479 floor is a narrow shelf, and on the two handsets that could be checked against the makers themselves the shop matched Apple exactly and came in £75 above Samsung — so check the manufacturer's own store before assuming the name is a price promise. Then ask, in writing, whether the handset you are buying is modified stock, because that single answer decides whether you get 12 months of warranty or 24, and whether the phone has an eSIM to switch networks with.
