Amazon Computers: What You Get, What Renewed Means and Who Pays the VAT

Rates and terms checked 25 September 2026 · Computers · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Amazon takes 7% of the sale price when a seller lists a computer, against 15% on most other categories, and every refurbished machine sold through Amazon Renewed carries a guarantee of one year from the day it arrives. Both figures were checked on 25 September 2026 against Amazon's own seller pricing schedule and its published Renewed quality policy, not against any comparison site.

Those two numbers explain most of what a buyer sees. The low referral fee is why the computer listings run so deep — a reseller keeps 93p in the pound here and 85p in most other departments — and the one-year guarantee is why Amazon is now one of the larger refurbished-computer shops in the country without owning a single refurbishment workshop. The scale behind it is real: Amazon's second quarter of 2026, the three months to 30 June, produced net sales of $200.6 billion, of which $46.8 billion came from third-party seller services alone.

One thing is worth knowing before you start comparing listings. A computer on Amazon can reach you three different ways — sold by Amazon itself, sold by an independent seller, or sold as Renewed by an approved refurbisher — and the guarantee, the VAT invoice and the condition of the battery are different in each case. None of that is hidden, but none of it is on the listing in one place either.

The word Renewed has a written specification behind it

Most shops that sell refurbished computers describe the grading in their own words. Amazon publishes a quality policy that applies across the UK, France, Germany, Spain and Italy, and it is unusually specific about the physical object rather than the paperwork.

A Renewed product must be "inspected, repaired (if applicable), cleaned and tested to function and look like new". The cosmetic test is a measured one: no signs of cosmetic damage such as scratches or dents should be visible when the product is held 30 centimetres away. Any markings already on the outside — the maker's logo, for instance — have to be intact, and no new markings may be added. Batteries must have a capacity that exceeds 80% of the new equivalent. The machine has to come with every accessory a new one would include, and those accessories must themselves be original parts or refurbished to like-new condition.

Two further requirements matter more on a computer than on a kettle. Personally identifiable information must be destroyed forensically, to NIST standards — that is the previous owner's data, and it is the thing a private buyer of a second-hand laptop has no way of auditing. And the box has to do its job: original packaging or a new cardboard box, clean and free of damage, markings and unrelated labels, with the product and accessories compartmentalised so they are not in direct contact, and the whole thing has to pass drop tests based on widely adopted standards.

The guarantee sitting on top of that is one year after the customer receives the product. That is Amazon's own promise, separate from anything the original manufacturer may or may not still honour on a machine that has already had one owner.

Not every seller is allowed near the programme

The entry requirements are the part of Renewed that a shopper never sees, and they do more work than the branding does.

To be approved, a seller has to supply invoices showing a total value of £20,000 in qualifying refurbished purchases in the previous 90 days. For Apple smartphones the bar is £1 million in Apple refurbished products, which gives a sense of how the thresholds are calibrated to the fraud risk in each brand. The seller's refurbishment process has to include a diagnostic test, replacement of any defective parts, a cleaning and inspection process and repackaging where applicable, and they must submit a minimum of ten images of a sample unit so that the product and its packaging can be reviewed before anything is listed. Running performance is policed too: an order defect rate of 0.8% or less across a trailing 90 days.

Personal computers and tablets are both named categories in the programme, alongside phones, cameras, headphones, kitchen appliances, power tools, televisions and games consoles. A Professional selling account is the baseline for any of it.

Set that against a private sale or a general auction listing and the difference is not the warranty, it is the audit. Someone has checked the invoices, seen photographs of a sample machine and is watching the defect rate. That is a genuine consumer protection and it is not advertised anywhere on the product page.

Seven per cent, and why the listings run so deep

Amazon publishes what it charges sellers, and the schedule is the clearest explanation of why the computer aisle looks the way it does.

The referral fee on Computers is 7% of the sale price with a minimum of 25p. Consumer Electronics is also 7%. Electronic Accessories is 15% up to £100 and 8% on anything above that, and the default rate on categories with no specific listing is a flat 15%. A seller moving a £600 laptop therefore hands over £42; the same £600 spread across accessories would cost roughly twice that. A Professional selling plan costs £25 a month excluding VAT, or an Individual plan charges 75p per item sold.

Amazon's own accounts show the scale that margin operates at. In the second quarter of 2026 — the three months to 30 June — total net sales were $200.6 billion, up 20%. North America contributed $116.2 billion, International $42.2 billion and AWS $42.2 billion. Split by activity rather than geography, online stores produced $70.4 billion and third-party seller services $46.8 billion, with advertising at $19.8 billion and subscription services at $13.7 billion. Segment operating income ran to $27.5 billion in the quarter. Net income of $62.6 billion, or $5.75 a diluted share, is not a like-for-like trading figure: $53.4 billion of non-operating pre-tax other income sits inside it, chiefly from Amazon's investments in Anthropic.

What none of that tells you is anything about Britain, or about computers. Amazon reports International as one line. There is no published UK computer sales figure, no UK unit count and no published count of how many machines are listed, so any such number a reader meets elsewhere has been estimated by somebody.

Buying through a company account, and the invoice underneath it

Computers are the category where the buyer is most likely to be a business, and Amazon Business exists for exactly that. It reports 11 million customers worldwide and $60 billion in annualised global sales, counts 97 of the Fortune 100 among its customers, and says B2B customers order 75% more units per order and make 30% fewer returns than consumer buyers. Business sellers offer VAT-exclusive prices and downloadable VAT invoices, and the quantity-discount and business-only pricing tools are offered to sellers at no additional charge.

The useful feature is a defensive one. Amazon Business lets an account display a "restricted" or "blocked" flag in search results and on product pages for items without an automatically downloadable VAT invoice. That flag matters most on exactly the machines this page has been discussing.

A refurbished computer is often sold under the VAT margin scheme, and HMRC's rules on it are unambiguous. The scheme covers goods on which the seller could not claim back any VAT when buying them. On a margin-scheme sale the seller "must not show VAT separately" on the invoice, and the invoice has to carry the words "Margin Scheme — second-hand goods". HMRC is equally clear the other way: if a purchase invoice shows a separate VAT amount, the item is not eligible to be sold under the scheme at all. The consequence for a VAT-registered buyer is simple and easy to miss — there is no VAT to reclaim on that laptop, so a £600 margin-scheme machine really costs £600, while a £600 machine bought with a full VAT invoice costs £500 net. That is a 20% difference in the true price of two listings that look identical in a search result.

Second-hand electricals come with their own rulebook

Selling used electrical goods in Great Britain is governed by the General Product Safety Regulations 2005 and the Plugs and Sockets etc. (Safety) Regulations 1994, and the trading standards guidance on it is more demanding than most sellers realise.

Before a used electrical item is offered for sale, a competent person should examine it for access to live parts, the adequacy of the insulation, and creepage and clearance. Test reports have to be kept and produced to enforcement officers on request. A dated portable appliance testing label alongside a UKCA or CE mark is treated as best practice rather than as proof — the guidance is explicit that such testing will not identify every hazard on its own. Any appliance must be supplied with an approved plug complying with BS 1363, carrying BSI or ASTA approval marks, with sleeved live and neutral pins and the correct fuse. Three-core mains leads must use the standard colours — green and yellow for earth, blue for neutral, brown for live — and leads have to be checked for fraying, breaks, kinks and exposed wires. A seller who supplies unsafe goods that go on to cause injury or damage may be liable to pay compensation, and enforcement sits with local trading standards.

Amazon's own remedy moved in the same direction in May 2024, when the A-to-z Guarantee was extended in the UK to cover customers where a defective product bought in its store causes property damage or personal injury — a step Amazon describes as going beyond its legal obligation. Under the broader A-to-z policy a seller has five business days to respond once a claim is opened.

The machine is regulated; the listing is the weak point

Computers sold in Great Britain still fall under the retained ecodesign regime for computers and computer servers, Commission Regulation (EU) No 617/2013. Its scope is drawn widely: desktop computers, integrated desktops, notebooks — including tablets, slate computers and mobile thin clients — desktop thin clients, workstations, mobile workstations, small-scale servers and computer servers. Blade systems, server appliances, multi-node servers, servers with more than four processor sockets, games consoles and docking stations are outside it. The substantive requirements applied from 1 July 2014, with a second tranche from 1 January 2016.

The register of things that have actually gone wrong tells a narrower story, and it is about the box in the bag rather than the machine. Searching the government's product safety alerts, reports and recalls database for "laptop" on 25 September 2026 returns 21 reports, and almost every one of them is a charger, an AC adapter or a power supply rather than a computer. Among them: a Lenovo USB-C laptop power bank recalled from end users on 30 November 2023; Toshiba AC adapters for laptops recalled from end users on 11 February 2025; a counterfeit USB-C laptop charger on 18 November 2025 where the outcome recorded is removal of the listing by the online marketplace; and a GEU 45W laptop charger rated high risk on 15 September 2026, stopped at the border. Several other entries record the same marketplace-removal outcome, and a handful end in destruction of the product.

Read that in both directions. Twenty-one reports across the whole register is a small number, and the fact that marketplaces are removing listings means the reporting chain works. It also means the hazard was on sale first, and that the replacement charger — the cheapest, most anonymous thing in the category — is where the risk concentrates rather than in the laptop itself.

What an independent survey will and will not tell you

For reliability rather than retailing, Which? surveys laptop owners directly. Its most recent work on which brands develop the fewest faults was an online survey carried out in February and March 2026, covering 15,755 people who own tech products, of whom 6,971 were laptop owners. A brand receiving a one-star reliability rating is automatically excluded from Which? recommendations.

The headline finding it makes public is a warning rather than a ranking: more than a third of Toshiba laptops developed some sort of fault within seven years of purchase. The brand-by-brand table itself sits behind a Which? membership, so a non-subscriber can see the method and one example and not the answer. That is worth saying plainly rather than working around, and it is a limit on this page as much as on any other.

The practical reading is that brand reliability and retailer choice are separate questions. Buying from Amazon does not change how a given laptop is built; what it changes is the paperwork behind the purchase and the route to a remedy if something fails.

Where it wins

  • A published one-year Amazon Renewed Guarantee backed by Amazon itself, separate from any manufacturer cover left on a used machine
  • The Renewed quality policy sets measurable standards — 80% battery capacity, a 30-centimetre cosmetic test, NIST-grade data destruction and drop-tested packaging
  • Approved refurbishers must show £20,000 of qualifying purchase invoices over 90 days and hold an order defect rate of 0.8% or less
  • A 7% referral fee on computers, against 15% in the default category, keeps a very deep field of sellers in the aisle
  • Amazon Business flags listings that cannot produce an automatic VAT invoice, and its quantity-discount tools cost sellers nothing extra
  • The A-to-z Guarantee was widened in May 2024 to cover property damage and personal injury from a defective product, which Amazon itself calls more than the law requires

Where it falls short

  • A Renewed computer can arrive with a battery at 80% of a new one's capacity and still meet Amazon's own written standard — that figure is the floor, not the target, and it is the component most likely to decide when the machine is retired
  • The quality policy sets a single uniform standard and defines no condition grades, so the word Renewed describes the same test on a £200 machine and a £900 one, with no published way to tell a lightly used return from a heavily repaired unit
  • The substance of the guarantee lives in a quality-policy document rather than on the product page, and Amazon's consumer help pages are closed to automated readers, so the consumer-facing wording could not be checked line by line against the seller-facing policy. Treat the customer-side detail as needing confirmation from Amazon direct
  • A search of the government safety register for "laptop" returns 21 reports, and the outcome recorded again and again is removal of the listing by the online marketplace — which means the item had already been on sale
  • A margin-scheme refurbished machine can never produce a reclaimable VAT invoice, so a VAT-registered buyer comparing two identical-looking listings can be 20% out on the real cost unless they check every offer individually
  • Amazon publishes no UK figures for computers at all — no sales, no units, no listing count — so every scale number available is global or group-wide, and the International segment is reported as a single line covering dozens of countries

Common questions

How long is the Amazon Renewed guarantee?

One year, running from the day the customer receives the product. Amazon's Renewed quality policy applies that same period in the UK, France, Germany, Spain and Italy. It is Amazon's own guarantee and sits separately from whatever remains of the original manufacturer's warranty on a machine that has already had an owner.

Will a Renewed laptop have a worn-out battery?

It should not be worn out, but it need not be new. The policy requires a capacity exceeding 80% of the new equivalent, which is the minimum the machine has to meet rather than what it is expected to deliver. On a laptop whose usefulness is measured in hours away from a socket, that is the single figure worth asking the seller about before ordering.

Can I reclaim the VAT on a refurbished computer bought on Amazon?

Often not. Second-hand goods are frequently sold under HMRC's VAT margin scheme, and on a margin-scheme sale the seller must not show VAT separately and the invoice carries the words "Margin Scheme — second-hand goods". There is no VAT for the buyer to reclaim. An Amazon Business account can flag listings that cannot produce an automatically downloadable VAT invoice, which is the quickest way to spot the difference before ordering.

Who is allowed to sell as Amazon Renewed?

Sellers approved for the programme, which requires invoices showing £20,000 of qualifying refurbished purchases in the previous 90 days, a refurbishment process covering diagnostic testing, parts replacement, cleaning and repackaging, at least ten photographs of a sample unit for review, and an order defect rate of 0.8% or less over a trailing 90 days. Apple smartphones carry a much higher bar of £1 million in refurbished Apple stock.

What does Amazon charge a seller to list a computer?

A referral fee of 7% of the sale price with a minimum of 25p, the same rate as consumer electronics. Most categories without a specific rate pay 15%, and electronic accessories pay 15% up to £100 and 8% above it. On top of the referral fee a seller pays either £25 a month excluding VAT on the Professional plan or 75p per item sold on the Individual plan.

Are laptop chargers bought on a marketplace safe?

They are the part of the category where problems cluster. A search of the government's product safety alerts, reports and recalls register for "laptop" returns 21 reports, and nearly all of them concern chargers, AC adapters and power supplies rather than computers — including a counterfeit USB-C charger whose listing an online marketplace removed in November 2025 and a 45W charger rejected at the border in September 2026. Buying the power supply from the machine's manufacturer avoids the whole question.

Our verdict

Amazon is the most heavily documented place to buy a used computer in Britain, which is not the same as the safest or the cheapest. The Renewed programme has a written specification, an audited entry bar and a one-year guarantee, and those are worth real money against a private sale. The two things to hold onto are the battery floor and the invoice: 80% of original capacity satisfies the policy, and a margin-scheme machine costs a VAT-registered buyer a fifth more than an identical listing with a full VAT invoice. Check both before comparing prices, and buy the charger from the manufacturer rather than from the cheapest listing.
