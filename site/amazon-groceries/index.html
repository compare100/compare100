Amazon Groceries Review: Delivery Fees, Date Codes and Who You Are Buying From

Rates and terms checked 26 September 2026 · Groceries · Compare100 editorial team

Prime members pay nothing to have fresh groceries delivered the same day on an Amazon order over £20, and everyone else pays £5.99 whatever the basket is worth. Those are Amazon's own figures, published on 4 June 2026 and checked on 26 September 2026. Behind them sits a grocery range Amazon puts at more than two million lines, delivered in one-hour windows, and a category the company says now accounts for roughly one in every three units sold on Amazon.co.uk.

What you are actually shopping is five shops in one search box. Amazon Fresh is Amazon's own range, and alongside it sit Morrisons, Iceland, Co-op and Gopuff, each selling as itself. That matters more than it sounds, because the contract for a Morrisons basket is with WM Morrison Supermarkets PLC of Hilmore House, Bradford, company number 358949, and an Iceland basket is with Iceland Foods Ltd of Deeside, company number 01107406. Amazon publishes all four entities, with company numbers, VAT numbers and telephone numbers, which is more than most marketplaces bother to do — and it decides which number you ring when a delivery goes wrong.

The shops themselves have gone. On 23 September 2025 Amazon set out a proposal to close all 14 of its Amazon Fresh stores in the UK, with five of them converting to Whole Foods Market by the end of 2026. Anyone searching for an Amazon grocery shop to walk into, or for click-and-collect from one, is looking for something that no longer exists; this is a delivery business now.

Five per cent on a tin of beans, and no floor under it

Amazon's published referral fee for Grocery and Gourmet is 5% on any item priced up to £10 and 15% above it, and the minimum referral fee is recorded as not applicable. Read that second half carefully, because almost nothing else on Amazon's fee schedule works that way. Pet Supplies is a flat 15% with a 25p minimum. Beauty, Health and Personal Care is 8% up to £10 and 15% above, again with a 25p minimum. Home Products is 8% at or below £20 and 15% above, with the same 25p floor.

On an 89p tin, 5% is 4p. If the 25p minimum applied, Amazon would be taking 28% of the sale, and nobody would list it. Removing the floor is the single decision that makes a cheap grocery line worth putting on the site at all, and it explains why the range runs to millions of items rather than the few thousand a delivery-only grocer normally carries. It also explains the shape of what you find: the depth is in ambient and household goods, where a seller keeps 95p in the pound, rather than in the fresh food the category is named after.

Selling costs sit on top. A Professional selling plan is £25 a month excluding VAT, or 75p an item on the Individual plan. For a seller shifting 400 jars of sauce a month, the plan is 6p a jar; for someone shifting twelve, it is more than £2 each, which is the arithmetic behind a marketplace grocery listing priced oddly high.

The date on the packet is the one thing the law lets them leave off

Buy food in a shop and the label is in your hand before you pay. Buy it online and the law splits the label in two. Article 9(1) of assimilated Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 lists twelve mandatory particulars, from (a) the name of the food and (b) the list of ingredients through to (l) the nutrition declaration. Article 14, the distance selling article, requires all of them to be available before the purchase is concluded, free of charge — with one exception, point (f), the date of minimum durability or the 'use by' date. That one need only be available at the moment of delivery.

So an online grocer is under no obligation to tell you how much life is left on the chicken before you pay for it, and a legal obligation to tell you when it arrives. The enforcement sits in the Food Information Regulations 2014 and the Food Safety Act 1990, policed by local authority Trading Standards rather than by any financial regulator.

The distinction between the two dates is worth knowing before the box is open. A use-by date goes on highly perishable food, and it is an offence to sell or offer for sale food after it — after midnight on the stated day, that item cannot lawfully be sold. Best before is a quality marker: the food may still be sold and eaten after it, and the retailer carries the responsibility for its condition if it is. Fresh fruit and vegetables that have not been peeled or cut carry no date mark at all, along with vinegar, cooking salt, solid sugar confectionery and chewing gum.

There is one thing to do on the doorstep, and it takes ten seconds. Read the dates on the chilled items while the driver is still there, because that is the moment the law gives you the information, and the mandatory particulars have to be legible when they reach you: the minimum x-height for the text is 1.2mm, dropping to 0.9mm only where the largest surface of the pack is under 80cm².

Two Amazon pages, two free-delivery thresholds, four days apart

Amazon's own pages do not agree on what free grocery delivery costs, and both were current within the same week. The newsroom announcement of 4 June 2026, which launched fresh groceries inside an ordinary Same-Day order in parts of central and east London, gives free delivery for Prime members on orders over £20 and £5.99 for non-members regardless of basket size. Amazon's Prime membership page, last modified on 31 May 2026, gives Prime members free two-hour deliveries on Amazon Fresh orders above £60.

Both are true, because they are different services: one is a dedicated Amazon Fresh slot, the other is fresh food dropped into the same basket as everything else. The practical point for a shopper is that the number depends on which route you happen to be in, and the help pages add that temporary promotions on delivery fees are not shown, so the fee is not something you can establish in advance. Assume you will see it at checkout and check it there.

The free delivery is also priced in elsewhere. Prime is £8.99 a month or £95 a year, with an 18-to-24 student rate of £4.49 a month or £47.49 a year. At £5.99 a delivery, sixteen grocery orders a year come to £95.84 — so if groceries are the only reason to hold Prime, the membership starts paying for itself at roughly one delivery every three weeks, and below that it does not.

8°C, and what happens when the bag arrives warm

Chilled food in England is governed by Schedule 4 of the Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013, SI 2013/2996. Paragraph 2 is blunt: a person who keeps food that needs temperature control at a temperature above 8°C commits an offence. There are tolerances — a period of less than four hours for service or display, and limited periods for unavoidable transfer — and general exemptions for food that is safe at ambient temperature throughout its shelf life, for canned and dehydrated goods before opening, and for cooked food held at or above 63°C.

Amazon says perishable items are stored, picked and packed in temperature-controlled conditions with checks at several points, and that temperature-sensitive products travel in insulated, recyclable packaging under a freshness guarantee. The delivery mechanics carry the rest of the answer. Unattended delivery, where the driver leaves the order without you, is available on Amazon Fresh only, for orders under £500, and is not available at all for chilled, frozen or age-restricted items — which is the right rule, and it means a chilled grocery order is an appointment rather than a drop-off.

If something arrives spilled or damaged, you have seven days from delivery to report it through Your Orders, and Amazon states it will not ask for the item back. Amazon Fresh purchases are refundable within 30 days, though the returns policy is explicit that most Fresh food is not returnable — perishables and goods that deteriorate or expire rapidly are excluded outright, as are items unsuitable for return on hygiene grounds once unsealed. On a grocery order, the remedy is a refund and nothing else.

Since 6 April 2026 the price per 100g has had to be there too

The rule that lets you compare a 400g jar with a 680g jar changed this spring, and it is the most useful change to grocery pricing law in twenty years. The Price Marking (Amendment) Order 2024, SI 2024/1055, was made on 21 October 2024. The Price Marking (Amendment) Order 2025, SI 2025/592, made on 14 May 2025, pushed its commencement from 1 October 2025 to 6 April 2026. Two changes matter to anyone filling an online basket: a new Article 7A requires a trader offering a product at more than one price — a loyalty price and a shelf price, say — to show each selling price and each unit price together with the conditions for getting them; and the amendment to Article 9 requires a price reduction to show the reduced selling price and the reduced unit price.

That was written because the old rules were not working. The Competition and Markets Authority reviewed unit pricing across the sector in July 2023, covering 11 nationwide supermarkets and 7 variety store retailers, visiting 22 supermarket and 24 variety stores and analysing more than 4,000 photographs of shelf labels. The supermarkets came out of it reasonably well, with one or two isolated errors each; the variety stores did not, and the CMA's worst example was a 250ml handwash priced at £1.19 and unit priced at £476.00 per 100ml. The review also recorded that the same product could be unit priced per litre in one shop, per 100ml in the next and per wash in a third, and noted that by December 2022 an estimated 94% of UK shoppers belonged to at least one loyalty scheme — which is why a promotional price with no unit price attached had become a hole worth closing.

Use it. On a multi-seller listing the pack sizes rarely match, and the unit price is the only number that survives the comparison.

What Amazon's own suppliers report, and the investigation that is still open

Amazon is a designated retailer under the groceries code, and it did not volunteer. The Competition and Markets Authority designated Amazon.com, Incorporated on 9 February 2022 under Article 4(1)(b) of the Groceries (Supply Chain Practices) Market Investigation Order 2009, which catches any retailer whose UK grocery retail turnover exceeds £1 billion. The obligations began on 1 March 2022, and the practical effect is that Amazon's dealings with its direct grocery suppliers are measured every year by the Groceries Code Adjudicator against the same yardstick as Tesco's and Waitrose's.

It measures badly. In the GCA's 2026 annual survey, conducted by YouGov and published on 16 April 2026, the proportion of direct suppliers saying a retailer consistently or mostly complies with the code put Waitrose top on 97.8% and Amazon bottom of all 14 designated retailers on 68.6% — a gap of 29 percentage points, with Co-op the next lowest on 89.1% and the rest of the field between 90% and 97%. Amazon accounted for 11.2% of the retailer mentions when suppliers were asked which retailer their issue concerned, and 52% of direct suppliers reported an issue of some kind, up from 51%. It is an improvement on 2024, when 3,000-plus responses gathered between 15 January and 25 February put Amazon on 47% against Co-op's 98% and the then Adjudicator, Mark White, said that the survey showed clearly that many suppliers did not believe Amazon was complying with the code.

On 20 June 2025 the GCA went further and opened a formal investigation into Amazon under the Groceries Code Adjudicator Act 2013, on reasonable grounds to suspect breaches of paragraph 5 of the code, No delay in Payments, which requires a retailer to pay a supplier within a reasonable time of the invoice. The investigation covers 1 March 2022 to 20 June 2025 with particular attention to the period since 1 January 2024, and examines Amazon's goods receipt and payment processes, how it handles supplier concerns, and its use of settlement deductions in commercial negotiations. Mark White's stated reason was that the alleged delays could expose Amazon's suppliers to excessive risk and unexpected costs, affecting their ability to invest and innovate. The call for evidence closed on 8 August 2025 and the investigation was still open as at the GCA's annual report of 16 July 2026.

Be fair about what this is and is not. The code protects suppliers, not shoppers, and nothing in it says a delivery will arrive cold or on time. It is still the only independent, annual, published measurement of how a grocery retailer behaves, and on that measurement Amazon has been last every year it has been measured.

Five shops, five contracts, and nobody to appeal to

Mixing Amazon Fresh items with ordinary Amazon items splits your shopping into separate baskets with separate checkouts and separate order confirmations, and the same is true across the partner merchants. That is a mechanical nuisance rather than a scandal, but it has a consequence worth knowing before something goes wrong: each order sits with a different company. Co-op orders are with Co-operative Group Food Limited, 1 Angel Square, Manchester, customer services 0800 068 6727. Morrisons is WM Morrison Supermarkets PLC on 0345 611 6111. Iceland is Iceland Foods Ltd on 0800 328 0800. Gopuff is GoBrands UK Holdings Ltd, company number 12793914, contactable only by email.

There is no ombudsman above any of them. A grocery order is not a regulated financial product, so there is no Financial Ombudsman Service to escalate to and no compulsory alternative dispute resolution scheme covering food retail. If a refund is refused, the routes are the merchant's own complaints process, then the local authority — Trading Standards for labelling and pricing, environmental health for temperature and hygiene — and then the small claims track. That is worth knowing in advance precisely because Amazon's own service is usually quick: the escalation path behind it is short.

Coverage is the other limit. Amazon's grocery partners are postcode-by-postcode rather than national, and the target Amazon has set itself is that more than 80% of UK Prime members will have access to at least one grocery partner by the end of 2026 — a target, not a present state. The Iceland tie-up reached Belfast, Cardiff, Edinburgh and Nottingham on 11 February 2026, having started in selected Greater Manchester postcodes; Gopuff and Co-op are narrower still. Check your own postcode before assuming any of the figures on this page apply to you.

Where it wins

  • Free same-day grocery delivery for Prime members on orders over £20, and one-hour windows on Amazon Fresh and Co-op for members and non-members alike
  • More than two million grocery and household lines, far deeper than a single supermarket's online range
  • The 5% referral fee with no minimum makes cheap ambient and household lines viable, so the long tail is genuinely long
  • All four partner merchants are published with their legal entity, company number, VAT number and a direct phone number
  • Chilled, frozen and age-restricted items cannot be left unattended, which is the right rule and is stated plainly
  • Damaged or spilled fresh items are refunded without a return, reported through Your Orders within seven days
  • Morrisons, Iceland and Co-op ranges sit behind one search box and one payment method

Where it falls short

  • Amazon publishes no minimum life on delivery. Neither the grocery help pages nor the freshness guarantee state how many days are left on a chilled item when it arrives, which is the one figure several rival grocers do publish — we could not find it and it needs confirming with customer services before a first order.
  • Two different free-delivery thresholds appear on Amazon's own pages within four days of each other, £20 on 4 June 2026 and £60 on the Prime page modified 31 May 2026, and the help pages add that temporary delivery-fee promotions are not shown. The delivery cost cannot be established before checkout.
  • Amazon has come bottom of the Groceries Code Adjudicator's supplier compliance table every year it has been measured — 68.6% against Waitrose's 97.8% in 2026 — and has been under formal investigation for late payment to suppliers since 20 June 2025, unresolved as at 16 July 2026.
  • Most food cannot be returned at all. Perishables and rapidly deteriorating goods are excluded from the returns guarantee outright, so a refund is the only remedy and the window for reporting damage is seven days.
  • All 14 Amazon Fresh shops were put forward for closure on 23 September 2025, five of them becoming Whole Foods Market by the end of 2026, so there is no Amazon grocery store to collect from or return to.
  • Coverage is postcode-by-postcode and incomplete. Amazon's own target is that more than 80% of UK Prime members will reach at least one grocery partner by the end of 2026, which means a significant minority still will not.
  • Mixing fresh items with the rest of an Amazon order creates separate baskets, separate checkouts and separate confirmations, and each merchant is a different company with its own customer service line.

Common questions

Is Amazon grocery delivery free?

For Prime members it is free on same-day grocery orders over £20, on the figures Amazon published on 4 June 2026. Non-members pay £5.99 whatever the basket is worth. Amazon's Prime membership page, modified on 31 May 2026, separately offers Prime members free two-hour Amazon Fresh deliveries on orders above £60. The two apply to different services, and Amazon's help pages note that temporary promotions on delivery fees are not displayed, so check the figure at checkout.

Can I see the use-by date before I order?

Not necessarily, and there is no legal requirement that you can. Article 14 of assimilated Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 requires the mandatory food particulars to be available free of charge before the purchase is concluded, but it expressly excludes point (f) of Article 9(1), the date of minimum durability or the use-by date. That one has to be available at the moment of delivery, which is why checking the dates while the driver is still at the door is worth doing.

Who am I actually buying from on Amazon groceries?

It depends which shopfront you use. Amazon Fresh is Amazon's own. Morrisons orders are with WM Morrison Supermarkets PLC of Bradford, company number 358949; Iceland with Iceland Foods Ltd of Deeside, company number 01107406; Co-op with Co-operative Group Food Limited of 1 Angel Square, Manchester; and Gopuff with GoBrands UK Holdings Ltd, company number 12793914. Amazon publishes each entity with its VAT number and a customer service contact, and complaints go to the merchant rather than to Amazon.

What happens if frozen food arrives defrosted?

Report it through Your Orders within seven days of delivery and Amazon states it will refund without asking for the item back. Amazon Fresh purchases are refundable within 30 days, although most Fresh food is not returnable. Separately, food needing temperature control must not be kept above 8°C under paragraph 2 of Schedule 4 to the Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013, and persistent failures are a matter for the local authority's environmental health team.

Is there an Amazon Fresh shop I can visit?

No. On 23 September 2025 Amazon proposed closing all 14 of its Amazon Fresh stores in the UK, with five converting to Whole Foods Market by the end of 2026. Amazon's grocery business in Britain is delivery only, in eligible postcodes, with the company targeting access to at least one grocery partner for more than 80% of UK Prime members by the end of 2026.

Does Amazon show a price per 100g?

Unit pricing is a legal requirement for most groceries under the Price Marking Order, and since 6 April 2026 the rules have been tighter: the Price Marking (Amendment) Order 2024, as commenced by SI 2025/592, requires a product offered at more than one price to display each selling price and each unit price, and a price reduction to display the reduced unit price as well as the reduced price. Where pack sizes differ between sellers, the unit price is the only figure that compares them.

Our verdict

Amazon's grocery business is now a delivery service with five shopfronts, and it is at its strongest on the ambient and household end of a basket, where a 5% referral fee with no minimum has pulled in a range no single supermarket matches. For a Prime member in a covered postcode, free same-day delivery over £20 alongside the rest of an Amazon order is a genuinely good offer, and the temperature and unattended-delivery rules are sensible and clearly stated. The gaps are all in what is not published: no minimum life on a chilled item, two different free-delivery thresholds four days apart, and a fee you only see at checkout. Read the use-by dates at the door, because Article 14 of the food information rules means that is the first moment anybody has to show them to you, and compare on unit price rather than pack price now that reduced and loyalty prices have had to carry one since 6 April 2026. Behind the shopfront, the supplier picture is poor and worth knowing: bottom of the Adjudicator's table on 68.6% and under investigation for late payment since June 2025.
