Argos Televisions Review 2026: The 12-Month Guarantee, the Bush Recall and the Missing Energy Label

Rates and terms checked 30 September 2026 · Televisions · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Argos promises to repair or replace a faulty television free of charge for 12 months, and its own Bush brand was the second in Britain to ship Freely, in May 2024, a month behind Hisense and a year ahead of four bigger names. Both figures come from Argos's own help centre and Everyone TV's press releases, read on 30 September 2026.

Collection is the part Argos still does better than anyone: nothing to pay at over 1,100 collection points, against a lorry delivery for anything the shop classes as large. The Bush sets built for it carry Freely, the broadcaster-owned platform that now lists more than 70 live channels over Wi-Fi, so a £150-class 32-inch set from Argos reaches the same live channels as a set costing four times as much.

One piece of corporate news belongs here rather than three sections down: on 31 July 2026 Sainsbury's agreed to sell Argos, and completion is expected in February 2027. Nothing about buying a television changes today, and the detail is set out further down the page.

What the 12-month guarantee promises, and what it leaves out

Argos states the position in one short passage on its returns and repairs page: a fault inside 30 days of receipt means you are “entitled to a refund”, and “if something goes wrong after the first 30 days but within 12 months, we'll offer to repair or replace the item at no cost to you”. If repair or replacement fails, takes too long or “causes significant inconvenience”, Argos says it will refund. Damaged or faulty goods do not need the original packaging.

That is clear, and it is also the whole of it. There is no free two-year or five-year television guarantee at Argos, and a search of its help centre for the word “guarantee” on 30 September 2026 returned no article by that name at all — the promise lives inside the returns article rather than in a guarantee document with terms you can read before buying.

Longer cover is a separate purchase. Argos Care “policies are provided by Domestic & General Insurance PLC, who are an insurer not an intermediary”, and Argos says it covers “many of the items not covered by your standard manufacturer's guarantee, including both parts and labour (although some exclusions apply)”. Where a set already carries an extended parts guarantee, Argos Care pays for labour only. You have 45 days from purchase to add it in store with your receipt, which is a genuinely useful window. What the help centre never publishes is the price, the term, or which exclusions apply — the phrase is simply “some exclusions apply”, and the policy documents sit behind the product page, which is discussed below.

Bush is Argos's own brand, and the law notices

Most of the sets on the site are other companies' brands, where Argos is the shop and nothing more. Bush is different: it is Argos's own label, sold nowhere else, and the hardware is built in Turkey by Vestel. The safety notice discussed in the next section names Vestel UK Limited — Companies House 04771550, incorporated 20 May 2003, registered at Vestel House, 1 Waterside Drive, Langley, Berkshire SL3 6EZ — as the manufacturer of the part that failed.

Two separate rulebooks treat an own-brand retailer differently from a shop. Under the Electrical Equipment (Safety) Regulations 2016, a “manufacturer” is “any person who manufactures electrical equipment, or has electrical equipment designed or manufactured; and markets that electrical equipment under that person's name or trade mark”, while a “distributor” is “any person in the supply chain, other than the manufacturer or the importer, who makes electrical equipment available on the market”. Regulation 31(1) then closes the gap: a distributor who “places electrical equipment on the market under A's own name or trademark” takes on the manufacturer's duties in regulations 4 to 13 — design, conformity with the safety objectives, technical documentation and marking.

The energy rules draw the same line in different words. Government guidance defines the supplier as “the manufacturer, authorised representative or importer” that places a product on the market, and the dealer as the retailer that displays the label. Enforcement splits accordingly: the Office for Product Safety and Standards polices suppliers, and local authorities police dealers.

So on a Samsung or an LG, Argos is a dealer with a display duty. On a Bush, the brand is Argos's own and the manufacturer's duties are in play. Exactly how they are allocated between Argos and Vestel is set by contracts nobody outside the two companies can read, and the OPSS notice records Vestel UK as the manufacturer, so this page does not claim to settle it — it needs confirming from Argos directly. What is not in doubt is that a Bush television is not a third party's product that Argos happens to stock.

Sixteen catalogue numbers, one fuse

Product safety report 2606-0164, published by OPSS on 15 June 2026, is headed “Vestel UK Plugs in Bush TiVo Televisions sold by Argos”. The fault is small and serious: “the plug's fuse does not meet the dimensional requirements of BS 1362”, and if a fault develops “the plug may overheat and catch fire”. The risk is recorded as fire, the country of origin as Turkey, the notifier as local authority Trading Standards, and the measure as a modification programme rather than a full recall.

Sixteen Argos catalogue numbers are listed: 7623916, 7615436, 7623930, 5024232, 7623057, 7140585, 6799418, 7049662, 5816505, 4743868, 5420001, 5024373, 6826271, 4723668, 7041251 and 7086388. They were sold by Argos between December 2024 and March 2026 — a 16-month window, which is wide. The remedy is a free replacement power cable or fuse, and the owner has to go and get it: register on the Argos Product Check site, then dispose of the old cable through WEEE recycling facilities.

Two legal instruments are cited as breached, and neither has appeared on this site before: the Electrical Equipment (Safety) Regulations 2016 and the Plugs and Sockets etc. (Safety) Regulations 1994. The 1994 rules are the reason a British plug is the shape it is. Regulation 8 requires a standard plug to be “of a type approved by a notified body” satisfied that it conforms to BS 1363, that the maker can reliably produce conforming plugs, and — where a sample fails — that “such plugs shall be treated for the purposes of this regulation as not conforming to the standard”, with no retest. An approval lasts “seven years or such shorter period as may be specified”. A fuse a fraction of a millimetre out of tolerance is exactly the sort of thing that regime exists to catch, and in this case it was caught by a council officer rather than by the supply chain.

For scale: a keyword search of the OPSS register for “Bush” on 30 September 2026 returns exactly one notice, this one. That is a good record for a brand Argos has sold since 2008, and it is the honest way to read an empty column on a public register.

The disposal instruction is worth one more line. Argos tells affected owners to take the old cable to WEEE recycling facilities, and a search of its own help centre for “recycling” on 30 September 2026 returned no electrical or television take-back article of any kind — only a furniture clearance service run by a third party. Ask at the counter; do not assume the shop will take it.

The energy label that never arrives

Since 1 March 2021 televisions sold in Great Britain have carried the rescaled A-to-G energy label, and for distance selling the rules require the class and the range of classes to be shown to the buyer before they commit. Argos does not manage it in the page it serves. Three URLs were opened and read directly on 30 September 2026: the television category at /browse/technology/televisions-and-accessories/televisions/c:30106/ and the product page for catalogue number 7623916, the Bush 32-inch 32FT25CB Smart FHD HDR LED Freely TV. The category page returned navigation, breadcrumbs and subcategory links and no product, no price and no energy class. The product page returned the set's name, its model number and its 32-inch screen size — and no price, no energy efficiency class, no link to a label and no guarantee wording.

None of that means Argos is hiding anything. The prices and the label arrive by script once a browser has the page, which is a design choice thousands of retailers make. It has two consequences a reader should know about. The first is practical: an Argos television price cannot be quoted by anything that reads the page rather than running it, this page included, so every figure here is a published document rather than a shelf price, and the price you see is the one on your own screen. The second is regulatory: the duty to put the class beside the product in distance selling is enforced by local authorities against dealers, and a document that arrives without it is an odd way to meet it.

The supplier half has a gap too. A GB energy label's QR code “must link to the required model information on a publicly accessible website” — the supplier's own, since Great Britain does not use the European product database. Argos runs a manuals and support portal at argos-support.co.uk, searchable by product name, catalogue number or model number, and it carries “instruction manuals, user guides, videos and telephone helplines” for televisions among other categories. It does not publish energy labels or model information sheets. Suppliers must keep the technical documentation behind those figures for 15 years after manufacturing stops; for a Bush set, that paperwork is not on the public side of the wall.

Seven years of spare parts, and a spares site run from Yeovil

The ecodesign regulation for electronic displays is the half of the rulebook nobody markets. Annex II, point D.5(a) of Regulation (EU) 2019/2021 requires a manufacturer to keep spare parts available to professional repairers for at least seven years after the last unit of a model goes on sale: “internal power supply, connectors to connect external equipment (cable, antenna, USB, DVD and Blue-Ray), capacitors, batteries and accumulators, DVD/Blue-Ray module if applicable and HD/SSD module if applicable”. Two parts — “external power supply and remote control” — must be available to ordinary owners as well, which is the reason the recall remedy above is a cable rather than a workshop visit. Orders must be delivered “within 15 working days after having received the order”, and from two years after a model launches, repairers must be given the disassembly map, the test equipment list, wiring diagrams and the fault codes.

The same annex sets the rest of the physics: an Energy Efficiency Index no higher than 0.75 for HD sets and 0.9 for 4K since 1 March 2023, off mode capped at 0.3W, standby at 0.5W and networked standby at 2.0W, polymer marking on plastic parts, and a flat prohibition — “the use of halogenated flame retardants is not allowed in the enclosure and stand of electronic displays”.

Against a seven-year parts duty, a 12-month guarantee looks short, and Argos's own signposting to parts is thin. Its help centre says only that “we can provide spare parts for some items, just head over to our Argos Spares site to find out more”, with no list of which items, no statement of how long parts stay available, and no price. The site it sends you to is more interesting than the sentence suggests: argosspares.co.uk is operated by Screwfix Spares Limited — Companies House 03593012, incorporated 6 July 1998, registered at Trade House, Mead Avenue, Houndstone Business Park, Yeovil BA22 8RT — “in association with 4ourhouse”. Screwfix belongs to Kingfisher, not to Sainsbury's, and the catalogue leans heavily towards white goods and floorcare, with televisions represented mainly by remote controls. A reader after a Bush panel or power board should not expect to find one there.

Freely needs 10Mbps before it needs an aerial

The reason to look at a Bush set rather than an unbranded one is Freely, and Argos got there early. Freely is run by Everyone TV Limited, company 05422613, the joint venture owned by the BBC, ITV, Channel 4 and Channel 5. It launched on Hisense sets in April 2024 and on Bush sets in May 2024; Everyone TV's release of 24 June 2024 confirmed Toshiba, Sharp, Panasonic and Metz as the next four partners, with their models “due to launch later this year”. Bush was therefore the second brand in the country to carry it, which for a catalogue own-label is a real achievement.

The platform has grown since. Everyone TV's release of 14 October 2025 puts Freely at “over 70 live channels over Wi-Fi”, records more than 500,000 weekly users reached earlier that year, and names 10 further streaming channels arriving in 2026 from Hearst Networks, Bloomberg TV+ and AMC Networks International UK, including BLAZE, Bloomberg TV+, Talking Pictures TV, TRUE CRIME UK and Local TV. Freely's own site claims “97% of the UK's most watched shows” and lists Hisense, Bush, Toshiba, Panasonic, Sharp, TCL, Amazon Fire TV and METZ as supported.

The catch is the connection. Freely delivers live channels over the internet, and the service asks for a minimum broadband speed of 10Mbps; an aerial is optional and unlocks more live channels rather than fewer. A buyer replacing an old set in a room with a good aerial socket and slow or no Wi-Fi has the arrangement backwards, and on a cheap Bush set the streaming platform is much of what they are paying for. Check the speed in the room the set is going in, not the speed on the router.

New owners from February 2027

Argos is changing hands. Sainsbury's announced on 31 July 2026 that it had agreed to sell the business to Swift Partners — Swift Whistle Midco Limited, backed by Richard Pennycook, Trevor Strain, Matt Truman and True Capital — for at least £120 million in cash: a minimum £70 million at completion and £50 million deferred over the three years afterwards, including proceeds from the sale of the Daventry distribution centre. Completion is expected in February 2027, with full separation by February 2029. Simon Roberts, Sainsbury's chief executive, said the agreement “allows us to focus all our resources and investment on the significant opportunities ahead”; Richard Pennycook said Argos has “an excellent platform for growth”.

The sale takes in the standalone shops, the Argos counters inside Sainsbury's stores, the sales channels, the logistics network and the sourcing offices in Shanghai and Hong Kong — and, relevant to anyone buying cover with a television, Argos Care and Argos Pet Insurance. Sainsbury's keeps the Argos defined benefit pension scheme, and the in-store counters continue under a long-term commercial agreement with rent paid to Sainsbury's.

This is the second attempt. On 13 September 2025 Sainsbury's confirmed discussions over a sale of Argos Retail Group Limited to JD.com — a company it described as 44th on the Fortune Global 500, with 2024 revenue of $158.8 billion and around 600 million annual active customers — and added that “no agreement has been reached and there is no certainty at this stage that any transaction will proceed”. It did not proceed. That release is also the source of the scale figures worth having: Argos is the UK's second largest general merchandise retailer, with “over 1,100 collection points” and the third most visited retail website in the country.

For a television buyer the practical reading is narrow. A 12-month repair-or-replace promise is a promise by Argos Limited, which continues to exist and continues to trade; an Argos Care policy is an insurance contract with Domestic & General, which is unaffected by who owns the shop. But a five-year service plan bought this autumn will spend most of its life under an owner who has not yet taken possession, and that is worth knowing before ticking the box.

Where the 12 months sits against the other shops

Argos's answer to the question this category is compared on — how much guarantee comes free with the set — is 12 months of repair or replace. John Lewis includes five years on every television it lists. Amazon gives one year on the Fire TV sets it builds itself and nothing at all on another brand's panel. Argos sits between the two, and closer to Amazon.

An independent customer score for Argos could not be verified from a free primary source this run, and it is not asserted here. For context on the benchmark rather than the shop: the UK Customer Satisfaction Index published on 7 July 2026 put the all-sector average at 78.3 out of 100, up 1.0 point year on year across the 13 sectors it tracks, with Nationwide top on 87.3, John Lewis second on 87.1 and first direct third on 86.0. Argos is not named in the public summary.

What Argos genuinely wins on is the last mile. Collection is free at over 1,100 points, most of them inside a supermarket that is open late, and a 32-inch set is a one-person carry. Home delivery is the opposite: anything the shop classes as a large item “come[s] on a lorry and need[s] 2 people to handle”, and Argos's own tell is the order number — “if it starts with a 1, it's a small item. If it starts with a 2, it's a large item”. For a big screen going up a narrow staircase, that distinction matters more than the guarantee does.

  • Free collection at over 1,100 Argos and Sainsbury's points, with supermarket opening hours behind them
  • Bush was the second brand in Britain to ship Freely, in May 2024, ahead of Toshiba, Sharp, Panasonic and Metz

Common questions

How long is the guarantee on an Argos television?

Twelve months. A fault inside 30 days of receipt entitles you to a refund; after 30 days and inside 12 months Argos says it will “repair or replace the item at no cost to you”, and refund instead if that fails, takes too long or causes significant inconvenience. There is no free multi-year television guarantee, and no separate guarantee document published in the help centre.

Is my Bush television part of the 2026 plug recall?

Check the catalogue number against OPSS report 2606-0164, published 15 June 2026. Sixteen Bush TiVo numbers are listed — 7623916, 7615436, 7623930, 5024232, 7623057, 7140585, 6799418, 7049662, 5816505, 4743868, 5420001, 5024373, 6826271, 4723668, 7041251 and 7086388 — sold by Argos between December 2024 and March 2026. The plug's fuse does not meet the dimensional requirements of BS 1362 and may overheat. Register on the Argos Product Check site for a free replacement cable or fuse, then dispose of the old cable at a WEEE recycling point.

Do Argos televisions need an aerial or broadband?

The Freely sets, which include Bush, need broadband of at least 10Mbps, because live channels come over Wi-Fi. An aerial is optional and adds further live channels rather than being the main route. Freely lists over 70 live channels and is run by Everyone TV Limited, the BBC, ITV, Channel 4 and Channel 5 joint venture.

Is Argos Care worth buying with a television?

It depends on numbers Argos does not publish. The policy is provided by Domestic & General Insurance PLC as insurer, covers parts and labour on faults outside the manufacturer's guarantee, pays for labour only where an extended parts guarantee already exists, and can be added in store within 45 days of purchase. The price, the term and the exclusions are not published in the help centre, which says only that some exclusions apply, so ask for both before paying — and note that Argos Care transfers with the business when the sale to Swift Partners completes, expected February 2027.

Can I get spare parts for an Argos television?

Only up to a point. The ecodesign rules require seven years of spare parts to professional repairers after the last unit of a model is sold, including the internal power supply, connectors and capacitors, with the external power supply and remote control available to owners too, delivered within 15 working days of an order. Argos itself says only that it can supply parts “for some items” and points at argosspares.co.uk, which is operated by Screwfix Spares Limited and stocks mainly remote controls on the television side.

Our verdict

Argos is the right shop for a cheap television you want to pick up today and a poor one for paperwork. The 12 months of free repair or replace is a fifth of what John Lewis includes, the extra cover is sold without a published price or exclusion list, and the served page gives you neither a price nor the energy class the distance-selling rules expect beside it. Against that, the Bush own-brand sets were second in the country onto Freely and now reach more than 70 live channels over broadband, collection is free at over 1,100 points, and the one open safety notice — 16 catalogue numbers with a BS 1362 fuse fault, remedied with a free cable — is the register working rather than a pattern. Buy on the screen and the collection slot, check your broadband is above 10Mbps before you rely on Freely, and if you want cover past the first year, ask for the Argos Care price and exclusions in writing before you pay.
