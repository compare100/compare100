Asda Groceries: The £40 Threshold, the Delivery Pass and the Date You Cannot See

Rates and terms checked 30 September 2026 · Groceries · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Asda came out cheapest of eight supermarkets on a basket of 220 branded groceries in August 2026, at £771.27 against £782.39 at Tesco with a Clubcard and £869.16 at Sainsbury's without a Nectar card. Those are Which?'s figures, from daily price tracking across the month, checked on 30 September 2026. Asda's own accounts put the gap at 4–7% against the other traditional supermarkets.

The delivery side is built around one number. An Asda delivery order needs £40 of shopping, a Click & Collect order needs £25, and an order below the line attracts a £4 minimum basket charge rather than being refused. You can keep adding to a booked order until 11pm the night before, an Express slot brings a full-range shop within four hours and sometimes inside one, and there are more than 450 Click & Collect points across the UK. A Delivery Pass from £3.95 a month covers the slot fee on up to one delivery a day.

One term is unusually generous and worth knowing before you order. If Asda substitutes something it cannot pick, its own policy is that “if it costs less, we charge you less. If it costs more, we won't charge you the extra” — so a swap can only move the bill downwards. Weights on loose fruit, vegetables and meat are settled when the item is actually weighed, so those lines can move either way by a few pence.

Adding up what a delivery costs before you have a basket

Asda publishes the thresholds clearly and the slot prices barely at all. The thresholds are £40 for delivery and £25 for Click & Collect, and the penalty for coming in under either is a £4 minimum basket charge — a structure worth preferring to a hard refusal, because a £28 shop still gets delivered.

Two slot prices are published outright. Express delivery costs £8.50 to £8.99 and carries no minimum spend, which is the cheapest way to have a single forgotten item brought to the door of any service on this site; Asda's wording is “there is no minimum spend, we deliver as little as you need”. Express Click & Collect is £3.50 for the full range within four hours, with a 12 noon cut-off for collection after 4pm the same day. Ordinary booked slots are priced by day, time and store, and Asda does not put that table anywhere a shopper can read before signing in.

The Delivery Pass is the way round it. Passes start at £3.95 a month, a 12-month midweek pass is a single payment of £39.50 — about £3.29 a month — and Asda advertises savings of “up to £194 a year when you pay annually”. The conditions are tighter than the headline: the £40 minimum still applies, a pass covers up to one delivery a day, the midweek version works only on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, and vouchers, coupons, eVouchers and gift cards cannot be used to buy one. Unused passes are refundable in full inside 14 days, and where a pass does not save what it promised Asda says it will “give you the difference in an eVoucher”, which is a real commitment rather than a slogan — though the arithmetic it is measured against is not published either.

For anyone shopping weekly, the sum is simple enough to do at the door. Fifty-two Express deliveries at £8.50 would be £442; the same year on a midweek pass is £39.50 plus the discipline of shopping on three named days.

The price claim, and the two baskets it depends on

Asda's central pitch is price, and for once there is independent daily measurement of it. Which? tracks 220 branded grocery lines every day for a month and averages the cost per item. In August 2026 that basket came to £771.27 at Asda, ahead of Tesco with a Clubcard at £782.39, Sainsbury's with Nectar at £787.57, Morrisons with a More card at £797.32, Ocado at £816.28 and Waitrose at £839.71. Strip the loyalty cards out and the gap widens sharply: Tesco at £860.29 is 11.54% dearer than Asda and Sainsbury's at £869.16 is 12.69% dearer. Asda's Q2 statement of 28 August 2026 says it has topped every Which? branded comparison since April and has retained The Grocer 33 annual pricing award.

The second basket is the one to read before switching. Which? also prices a mixed basket of 92 branded and own-label items across eight retailers, and in August 2026 Asda came third at £185.21, behind Aldi at £159.36 and Lidl at £161.10. That is £25.85 more than Aldi on one shop, about 16%. Both results are true and they answer different questions: Asda wins on the brands you asked for by name, and the discounters win once own-label is allowed in. Neither Aldi nor Lidl delivers a weekly grocery shop to the door, which is the reason the comparison is not quite like for like.

Behind the pricing sits a business under pressure. Asda's FY2025 update of 27 March 2026 reported sales excluding fuel of £21.0bn, down 3.3% from £21.7bn, like-for-like sales down 3.1%, and adjusted EBITDA after rent of £764m against £1.14bn the year before — a fall of 33.1%. Net debt came down by £500m to £3.1bn, with £1.3bn of cash and £2.1bn of liquidity. Store availability reached 95%, described as an eight-year high, which matters to an online shopper more than it sounds: availability is what decides how many substitutions land in the order. By Q2 2026 revenue including fuel was £6.5bn, up 0.4%, like-for-like still down 2.3%, and in the seven weeks to 18 August 2026 like-for-like sales excluding fuel turned positive at 0.2% — the first growth in more than two years, on Allan Leighton's account.

Substitutions, weights and the part that is not written down

The substitution rule is the best term on the service and it is stated in one line: “If something you have ordered is not available when we pick your order, we may choose a substitute. If it costs less, we charge you less. If it costs more, we won't charge you the extra.” A shopper who orders a £2 own-label and receives a £3.50 brand pays £2. Set against 95% availability, that turns the commonest annoyance of online grocery shopping into an occasional small windfall.

Loose goods work differently and the difference is worth a sentence. Fruit, vegetables and meat sold by weight are charged on what is actually weighed at picking, so those lines move in either direction against the basket total shown at checkout. Nothing about that is unusual, and it is the reason an Asda receipt rarely matches the order to the penny.

What is not published is how to refuse a substitute. Asda's own substitutions article says only that a substitute may be chosen and how it is priced; the mechanics — handing it back at the door, the refund route, how long the money takes — sit in help articles on support.asda.com, which refused every automated read attempted for this review. So did asda.com/terms/grocery, which is closed by robots rules, and groceries.asda.com, where product pages live. The delivery, Delivery Pass, Express and Click & Collect pages on asda.com all read normally, which is how the charges above were checked; the contract, the help centre and the product detail could not be. Anyone relying on a specific term should read it in the app before ordering rather than take it from a page like this one.

The use-by date is the one fact you cannot check before paying

In a shop you turn the pack over. Online you cannot, and the law is comfortable with that: the date of minimum durability is the particular an online grocer may leave until the moment of delivery rather than disclose before the sale is concluded. Which makes the accuracy of the printed date the whole of the protection, and that is measurable.

The governing rule is blunt. Article 14 of assimilated Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 says “food shall not be placed on the market if it is unsafe”, and food is unsafe if it is injurious to health or unfit for human consumption. Two of its paragraphs matter to a mislabelled pack in particular: paragraph 3 requires regard to “information provided to the consumer, including information on the label”, so the date is part of the safety assessment rather than a detail beside it; and paragraph 6 presumes that if one item in a batch is unsafe, the whole batch is, unless a detailed assessment shows otherwise. Separately, regulation 10 of the Food Information Regulations 2014 makes a failure to comply with the food information rules an offence, and regulation 12 pulls the Food Safety Act 1990's improvement notice machinery across to enforce it. The fine level on conviction could not be confirmed for this review — the relevant penalties regulation would not load — so it is left unstated rather than guessed.

Against that, Asda's record on the Food Standards Agency's register is specific and dated. Three Asda-brand products have been recalled since April 2024 for carrying the wrong date. The clearest is notice FSA-PRIN-52-2025 of 27 November 2025: Asda 10 Mini Duck Spring Rolls with Hoisin Dip, 220g, “labelled with an incorrect use by date of 29 December 2025 which makes them unsafe to consume after the correct use by date of 29 November 2025”. A month of life printed on a pack that had none, on a product a delivery customer had no way of inspecting before paying for it. Before that, FSA-PRIN-20-2025 of 29 April 2025 recalled ASDA Hot & Spicy Chicken Breast Slices and FSA-PRIN-16-2024 of 15 April 2024 recalled Asda Succulent Cooked & Peeled King Prawns, both for the same reason. Asda took all three back without a receipt.

How much life arrives with the shopping has been measured once, independently, and Asda came mid-table. Which? sent 12 shoppers to order 25 identical perishable items from six supermarkets between 27 March and 6 April 2023 — more than 1,700 groceries — and counted the hours left to midnight on the use-by date. Asda averaged 10.0 days, fourth behind Ocado on 11.2, Tesco on 10.2 and Morrisons on 10.1, and ahead of Waitrose on 9.9 and Sainsbury's on 9.7. One Asda order in that exercise contained stilton three days past its date. The research also settled a question a shopper might otherwise assume in Asda's favour: Asda lists a “typical life” against some products, and a typical life is not a guarantee of one. No supermarket in the exercise, Asda included, promised a minimum number of days on delivery. Which? has not repeated the study, so the 2023 ranking is the most recent independent measurement available and should be read as three years old.

Reading the recall register yourself, and what it will and will not tell you

The Food Standards Agency publishes every allergy alert, product recall notice and food alert for action as open data at data.food.gov.uk/food-alerts under the Open Government Licence, searchable by free text, alert type, allergen and date. A search on Asda returns dozens of notices going back to 2018, which is a larger number than it first appears to be and a smaller problem.

The reason is that most of them are not Asda's. Three Asda-brand recalls were posted in 2026 and only one began at Asda. FSA-PRIN-22-2026 of 30 April 2026 recalled Exceptional by ASDA Beer Battered Cod Fishcakes over possible plastic or metal pieces. FSA-PRIN-38-2026 of 7 August 2026 was Greencore's: three chilled pasta products over possible Listeria monocytogenes, of which two were Asda — the 300g Cheese & Tomato Pasta Salad with a use-by of 10 August 2026 and the 500g Nacho Cheese Pasta Salad with a use-by of 9 August — and the third was Morrisons'. FSA-PRIN-29-2026 of 23 June 2026 was Prepworld's, and it swept up 13 prepared fruit lines across six retailers over possible salmonella, with Asda's 220g Pink Lady Apple, Mango, Strawberry & Blueberry pot one of them alongside Morrisons, M&S, Sainsbury's, Tesco and Waitrose. A shared factory produces a shared recall, and reading a retailer's name count as a hygiene score gets that backwards.

The Listeria notice is worth one line of law because the number behind it is fixed. Under assimilated Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005, ready-to-eat food able to support the growth of Listeria monocytogenes must stay below 100 cfu/g through its shelf life on a plan of five samples with none permitted over the limit, and must be undetectable in 25g before it leaves the producer's control; for foods intended for infants or for special medical purposes the criterion is absence in 25g on ten samples. A chilled pasta salad is squarely in the growth-supporting category, which is why a use-by date on one is a safety instruction and not a suggestion.

One practical wrinkle for anyone ringing about a recall: the notices carry two different Asda numbers. The spring roll recall gives Customer Relations on 0800 952 0101 and the Greencore recall gives 0330 053 0111, and nothing published explains which line handles what.

Where a complaint goes when the customer service line says no

Groceries sit outside the ombudsman schemes that cover insurance, banking and energy, so the honest answer is that there is no free adjudicator for a shopper Asda has turned down. The nearest thing to a regulator in this market watches the other side of the till.

Asda Stores Ltd is one of the 14 retailers designated under the Groceries Supply Code of Practice and supervised by the Groceries Code Adjudicator. The statutory review of the Adjudicator published in April 2026, covering 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2025 after a consultation that ran from 13 May to 5 August 2025, records a mean compliance score across all designated retailers of 93% in 2025, 91% in 2024 and 92% in 2023, with every designated retailer above 90% in 2025 except Amazon on 66%. Asda is therefore in the upper band throughout, which is a reasonable proxy for how it treats the suppliers whose products end up in the delivery van. The Adjudicator completed three arbitrations and launched no new investigations in those three years, can fine a retailer up to 1% of all turnover, and ran on a levy of £2.2m in 2025/26. The Code protects suppliers, not households — a shopper cannot complain to it.

For the shopper the route is the ordinary one: Asda's own customer service, then the Consumer Rights Act through Trading Standards or the small claims court. Asda is not among the retailers listed by RetailADR, the Chartered Trading Standards Institute-approved dispute body, where roughly fifty merchants including Aldi, ASOS, IKEA and Richer Sounds do subscribe. Signing up is voluntary and most supermarkets have not, so this is a gap Asda shares with almost all of its competitors rather than one of its own making — but it is worth knowing that a stalled grocery dispute has nowhere cheap to go.

Common questions

What is the minimum spend for an Asda delivery?

£40 for a home delivery and £25 for Click & Collect. Asda does not refuse a smaller order; it applies a £4 minimum basket charge instead. Express delivery is the exception and has no minimum spend at all, at £8.50 to £8.99.

Is the Asda Delivery Pass worth buying?

It depends how often you order and on which days. Passes start at £3.95 a month and a 12-month midweek pass is £39.50, about £3.29 a month, covering Tuesday to Thursday only. Against Express delivery at £8.50 a time it pays for itself in about five shops a year. The £40 minimum still applies, a pass covers up to one delivery a day, and Asda will refund an unused pass in full within 14 days or pay the shortfall in an eVoucher if the advertised saving does not appear.

What happens if Asda substitutes an item I ordered?

You are charged the lower of the two prices. Asda's policy is that a cheaper substitute costs you less and a dearer substitute costs you nothing extra. Items sold by weight — fruit, vegetables, meat — are priced on the weight actually picked, so those lines can move a few pence either way. How to refuse a substitute at the door is set out in help articles that could not be read for this review, so check that in the app.

How much shelf life do Asda deliveries arrive with?

Asda does not publish a minimum. The “typical life” shown against some products is an estimate rather than a guarantee. The only independent measurement is Which?'s mystery shop of more than 1,700 perishable items between 27 March and 6 April 2023, in which Asda averaged 10.0 days of remaining life, fourth of six supermarkets behind Ocado at 11.2 days. Those figures are three years old.

Where can I check whether an Asda product has been recalled?

The Food Standards Agency publishes every recall and allergy alert as open data at data.food.gov.uk/food-alerts, searchable by free text and by date. Bear in mind that many notices naming Asda originate with a supplier and cover several supermarkets at once: the 23 June 2026 salmonella recall by Prepworld, for instance, covered 13 prepared fruit lines across six retailers.

Can I complain about Asda to an ombudsman?

No. Grocery retail has no ombudsman scheme, and Asda is not listed by RetailADR, the Chartered Trading Standards Institute-approved dispute body that Aldi and around fifty other merchants use. A complaint Asda refuses goes to Trading Standards or the small claims court under the Consumer Rights Act. The Groceries Code Adjudicator, which does supervise Asda, handles supplier complaints rather than customer ones.

Our verdict

On price, Asda is doing what it says: cheapest of the eight supermarkets Which? tracks on a 220-line branded basket in August 2026, and cheapest of the traditional supermarkets by its own 4–7% measure, with availability at an eight-year high and a substitution rule that can only ever move the bill down. For a household that buys brands by name and wants them delivered, that combination is hard to beat, and the £4 minimum basket charge is a kinder design than a flat £40 refusal. Two things temper it. Asda will not tell you what an ordinary slot costs until you are signed in and holding a basket, and the one fact a delivery customer cannot check — the date on the pack — is the fact three of its own recalls since April 2024 got wrong, once by a month. Read the “typical life” on a chilled line as an estimate rather than a promise, check the dates at the door while the driver is still there, and the economics work: a midweek pass at £39.50 a year against £8.50 a time pays for itself inside five shops.
