ASOS Men's Fashion Review: Delivery, Returns and the 70% Return Rate

Rates and terms checked 30 September 2026 · Men's Fashion · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Sending a parcel back to ASOS costs an account holder nothing. You get 28 days from delivery, a Royal Mail Post Office or InPost drop-off, or a free Royal Mail collection that can be booked up to 5 days in advance, and the refund is aimed at within 14 days of the item reaching the warehouse. Standard delivery is £4.50 and free over £40; ASOS Premier is £9.95 for 12 months. Every figure on this page was checked on 30 September 2026 against ASOS's own customer care pages, its annual report and the Competition and Markets Authority's case file.

One change is worth knowing before you fill a basket. Since January 2026 free returns are conditional on a personal return rate: if you have returned more than 70% of the value of your orders over the past 12 months and placed 3 or more orders, ASOS may deduct and retain £3.95 from your refund for each returned parcel, unless you keep more than £40 of that order. The rate is calculated on value rather than item count, it updates every time you order, changes to your status carry a 30-day delay, and it is shown to you in the app and at checkout before you commit. Guest checkout is charged the £3.95 on every return regardless.

What you are shopping is one of the largest menswear ranges in the country, and the company behind it is mid-turnaround rather than mid-collapse. ASOS had 16.5m active customers across more than 150 markets at its half year to 1 March 2026, an average basket of £44.43, about 10 own-brand labels running at roughly 40% of gross merchandise value, and around 150 brands inside its flexible fulfilment model. Menswear is being narrowed on purpose: the interim statement describes it as "more targeted as we rebuild momentum, emphasising functional fashion delivered with a clear styling edge rather than broad, generalist positioning".

The delivery ladder, and where Premier pays for itself

ASOS prices UK delivery in seven ways and publishes all of them. Standard Delivery is £4.50 and takes 3 to 4 business days, free once the order passes £40. Next Day Delivery is £5.95 and arrives between 7am and 10pm on the next working day. Nominated Day, which lets you pick a date up to 10 days ahead, is also £5.95. Click & Collect mirrors the same two prices through Royal Mail, at £4.50 standard or £5.95 next day. At the top, ASOS Instant costs £9.95 for an evening slot between 6pm and 10pm in London, and ASOS Instant AM with DPD costs £9.95 for delivery before 12 noon the next day.

Against that, ASOS Premier is £9.95 for a 12-month subscription, which is the price of a single Instant delivery. It gives free Standard Delivery with no minimum spend at all, and free Next Day and Nominated Day Delivery on orders over £15 after any discount is applied. Two Next Day orders in a year cover the cost. The exclusions are geographic and specific: Next Day and Nominated Day are unavailable in the Channel Islands and in the postcode areas AB31 to AB56, BF, FK, HS, IM, IV, KA27 to KA28, KW, PA20 to PA80, PH15 to PH50, TR21 to TR25 and ZE, where Standard Delivery still runs. The refund position is narrow and stated plainly in ASOS's own wording: you can get your money back only if you have made no eligible purchase within one calendar month, and otherwise "we won't be able to offer you a refund".

Students get 10% off, verified through SheerID with UniDAYS, Student Beans, TOTUM and Apprentice extra codes accepted as alternatives. It applies to students over 16 in full-time or part-time education, including apprenticeships, across 17 markets including the UK and Ireland, and it requires an account rather than a guest checkout.

The 70% line, and the arithmetic sitting behind it

This is the figure that now decides what ASOS costs a menswear shopper, because clothes are the one category where trying two sizes and sending one back is normal behaviour rather than indecision. ASOS's fair use policy sets two thresholds, and both are expressed in value, not items.

At a return rate above 70% across 3 or more orders in the past 12 months, ASOS "may deduct and retain £3.95 from your refund for each returned parcel (unless you keep more than £40 of your order)". At a return rate above 80% across 5 or more orders, the same £3.95 deduction applies and ASOS additionally adds "an additional £3.95 handling fee to your orders, in addition to our usual delivery costs". So the worst case on a single order is £3.95 charged going out and £3.95 withheld coming back, on top of whatever the delivery cost, and none of it is a postage charge as such.

Two details make this fairer than it first sounds. The rate is assessed over a rolling 12 months and recalculated every time you place an order, with a deliberate 30-day delay before your status changes, so one bad month does not flip you instantly. And ASOS tells you where you stand rather than surprising you: notification of a status change, a reminder at checkout before you order, and a further reminder when you set up the return. Since early 2026 the app also shows your personal return rate as a running figure, calculated from the value of what you have sent back against the value of what you have ordered over 12 months. A retailer publishing the number it is about to charge you on is unusual, and it is the single most useful thing on the ASOS account page for anyone who buys trousers in two waist sizes.

The threshold also reaches beyond the fee. A return rate of 70% or more across 3 or more orders blocks you from buying or renewing ASOS Premier at all. And from January 2026 the same 70% line gates the loyalty scheme: ASOS.WORLD runs four tiers, Stylist free to join, Curator at £100 of spend, Icon at £350 and A-Lister at £750, with the 12-month spend assessment excluding returned or cancelled orders, and progress to the upper tiers requiring a return rate below 70%. Returning something after a tier upgrade deducts the refunded amount from your progress, and a downgrade lands within about 60 minutes of the refund being processed.

Why ASOS is counting returns: the figures in its own accounts

The returns policy is not a pricing whim, and the published numbers make that clear. In the 52 weeks to 31 August 2025, ASOS reported revenue of £2,477.8m, down 15% year on year, with UK revenue of £1,211.8m, down 9%. Active customers fell 14% to 17.0m and shipped orders fell 16% to 57.3m, while the average basket rose 3% to £42.89. The profitability side moved the other way: adjusted gross margin improved 370 basis points to 47.1%, adjusted EBITDA came in at £131.6m on a margin up 250 basis points to 5.3%, the adjusted loss before tax narrowed to £98.2m from £126.0m, and net debt improved by £112.4m to £184.7m.

The interim results published on 23 April 2026, covering the 26 weeks to 1 March 2026, continued that shape. Revenue was £1,116.0m, down 14%, with UK revenue down 12%. Adjusted gross margin reached 48.5%, up 330 basis points. Adjusted EBITDA rose 51%, or £21.5m, to £64.0m, though the reported loss before tax was £137.9m against an adjusted loss of £52.4m, and net debt excluding lease liabilities stood at £294.9m. Active customers were 16.5m, orders 26.3m, and the average basket £44.43. Guidance for the full year, set out in the 25 March 2026 pre-close statement, is adjusted EBITDA of £150m to £180m, a gross margin of 48% to 50%, and broadly neutral free cash flow. The full-year figures for the period that has just ended are not published yet.

Inside those numbers sits the sentence that explains the policy: the underlying returns rate improved by about 160 basis points year on year in the half, which ASOS attributes to sizing and fit work plus the transparency initiative launched in early 2026. Sales are falling and margin is rising, and a large part of the margin is coming from fewer parcels going backwards. The supporting machinery has shrunk to match: around 2,800 employees, three distribution centres at Barnsley, Berlin and a returns facility in Dallas, a warehouse footprint cut by more than half since 2021, and stock down to about £400m, roughly 20% lower year on year and about 60% below the 2022 peak. The Test & React model, which puts small batches into production and repeats only what sells, passed 20% of own-brand sales in the year to August 2025, grew a further 6 percentage points in the half, and is targeted at 30% in the medium term. Buying less of the wrong thing and selling more of it at full price is the whole strategy, and the returns fee is the consumer-facing edge of it.

The green claims ASOS had to rewrite, and the clock running on them

ASOS is one of only three fashion businesses to have given the Competition and Markets Authority formal undertakings over environmental claims, and the documents are public, which makes this the most closely specified part of its sales copy. The investigation opened on 29 July 2022 and closed on 27 March 2024 when the CMA accepted undertakings from ASOS, Boohoo and George at Asda, three businesses accounting for about £4.4bn of annual UK fashion sales between them. The range at the centre of the ASOS case was the Responsible edit. Sarah Cardell, the CMA's chief executive, said that "following our action, the millions of people who shop with these well-known businesses can now have confidence in the green claims they see".

The undertaking itself was given under Part 8 of the Enterprise Act 2002, on the section 219 route, in relation to the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008, and its operative paragraphs are more precise than the press release. Paragraph 19 requires that where a product is described as containing recycled or organic fibres, ASOS must clearly and prominently set out either the specific percentage evidenced through its supplier due diligence, or the minimum percentage the product must contain. Paragraph 18 bars a recycled or organic claim where the product contains fibres that are not, unless the non-qualifying proportion is negligible. Paragraph 7 requires the criteria for any environmental range to be shown clearly and prominently in an easily identifiable and accessible location. Paragraph 6 bars filter names that would themselves amount to a misleading environmental claim, and paragraph 10 requires fabric inclusion to rest on objective characteristics rather than a subjective or ambiguous label such as "eco-fabrics". On reporting, paragraph 35 requires compliance reports within 20 working days of the end of each reporting period, and paragraph 40 requires spot check reports within 10 working days of the end of each spot check period, those periods running every 3 months.

The duration clause is where a shopper should pay attention. The undertaking runs to a reporting end date defined as the later of two years from the date of the undertaking, or two years from the launch of an ASOS New Range, meaning a successor to the Responsible edit launched within two years. The CMA's case page refers to the Responsible edit in the past tense, and no environmental product range is currently visible on the ASOS site. On the first limb, the two-year clock from 27 March 2024 would have run out on 27 March 2026. Whether the CMA still treats the reporting obligation as live, and whether ASOS has launched a qualifying new range, could not be established from any published source and needs confirming with the CMA rather than assuming either way. The same day, the CMA also wrote an open letter to the whole fashion sector setting out six principles for environmental claims, and pointed at the penalties then coming through the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Bill, which reach up to 10% of a business's worldwide turnover.

Worth stating for balance: ASOS's own reporting says 50% of the materials in its own-brand ranges were sustainable materials in the year to August 2025, against a target of 45%. That is a company-level figure and not a product claim, which is exactly the distinction the undertaking was written to force.

Who you are buying from, and who handles it when the parcel is wrong

Not every item on the site is sold by ASOS, and the difference matters most at the point you want to send something back. Items shipped by ASOS Brand Partners are returned under that brand's own policy, applied at the brand's discretion, so the 28-day window and the free Royal Mail route are not site-wide guarantees. Some partner lines are handled separately again, with New Balance having its own named returns article. Payment is affected too: American Express cannot be used on an order sold and shipped by a Brand Partner, though Visa, Mastercard, Google Pay, Apple Pay, PayPal, Clearpay, Klarna and the ASOS Credit Card are all accepted elsewhere, and ASOS notes that some payment methods are subject to a soft credit check. Orders can also be split across warehouses, which is why a single order can arrive as several parcels with different return routes attached.

The legal identity behind the checkout is ASOS.com Limited, company number 03584121, at Greater London House, Hampstead Road, London NW1 7FB, with ASOS Payments UK Limited, company number 13337408, handling payments. ASOS's own terms give a 14-day cancellation right from delivery or availability for collection under the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013, so the 28-day returns window is double the statutory minimum. On pricing mistakes the terms are explicit rather than weaselly: if an error is discovered, ASOS says it will tell you as soon as possible and give you the option of reconfirming at the correct price or cancelling.

If a complaint is not resolved, ASOS is a listed member of RetailADR, the retail alternative dispute resolution scheme run by Consumer Dispute Resolution Ltd, company number 09189773. That is a genuine point of difference: most large UK retailers, supermarkets included, have not subscribed to any ADR scheme, and retail has no ombudsman, so an unresolved dispute elsewhere ends at Trading Standards or the small claims court. Note also the limit of the fair use fee that Which? has pointed out: under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 you cannot be charged for returning goods that are faulty, of poor quality or not as described, whatever your return rate, so a fee applied to a faulty item is not lawful and should be challenged rather than absorbed.

On product safety, the Office for Product Safety and Standards register of alerts, reports and recalls returns 0 results for the keyword "asos", checked on 30 September 2026. That is an absence rather than an award, but it is an absence worth having in a category where the same register carries 248 entries for clothing and 135 for textiles.

The supply chain ASOS publishes, and the parts it does not

ASOS's modern slavery statement for September 2024 to August 2025 was approved by the board on 28 October 2025 and signed by chief executive José Antonio Ramos, and it is more numerate than most. It records 441 active factories in Tiers 1 and 2 across 21 countries, covering approximately 236,750 workers through 164 suppliers, with a workforce split of 49.87% male and 50.13% female. It maps five tiers, from finished manufacturing down to raw materials, and states visibility through Tier 2, which means subcontracted manufacturing sites are in scope and fabric mills, component makers and raw material suppliers largely are not. On grievances it reports 33 whistleblowing cases received, of which 26 have been resolved, with investigations continuing on the rest.

The gaps are stated in the document rather than hidden. It names unannounced and semi-unannounced audits and its audit partners without publishing the number of audits carried out or a breakdown of findings, so there is no way to judge how often something is found. Training distribution is given as 53 suppliers in China without a count of workers reached. And due diligence on goods not for resale is described as an area where work continues. A factory count, a country count and a worker count are real disclosure; an audit count with results attached would be better, and it is the figure a reader would actually use.

Where it wins

  • Returns cost account holders nothing in the ordinary case: 28 days from delivery, Royal Mail Post Office or 24/7 InPost drop-off, or a free Royal Mail collection bookable up to 5 days ahead
  • 28-day returns window is double the 14-day statutory cancellation right
  • ASOS Premier is £9.95 for 12 months and covers free Standard Delivery with no minimum spend, so two Next Day orders at £5.95 pay for it
  • Your personal return rate is shown in the app and at checkout before you order, so the £3.95 deduction is never a surprise
  • Standard delivery free over £40, with a published seven-option delivery ladder from £4.50 to £9.95
  • ASOS is one of roughly fifty retailers signed up to RetailADR, which most large UK retailers are not
  • Zero entries on the OPSS product safety alerts and recalls register for ASOS, checked 30 September 2026
  • 10% student discount, verified through SheerID with UniDAYS, Student Beans and TOTUM accepted
  • Free DPD ReLove charity collection of unwanted clothing alongside a delivery, with Scope, Marie Curie, British Heart Foundation, British Red Cross and The Children's Society as the named charities
  • Supply chain disclosure is specific: 441 factories, 21 countries, 164 suppliers and roughly 236,750 workers named in figures

Where it falls short

  • The £40 keep-threshold cuts the wrong way for small baskets: above a 70% return rate, sending back a single £35 shirt costs £3.95, while keeping £41 of a £300 order costs nothing
  • Above an 80% return rate across 5 or more orders the charge doubles up, with £3.95 withheld from the refund on each returned parcel and a further £3.95 handling fee added to the order on top of normal delivery
  • One number triggers three separate penalties: passing 70% across 3 or more orders brings the return deduction, blocks buying or renewing Premier at £9.95, and from January 2026 blocks progress to the Icon and A-Lister tiers of ASOS.WORLD
  • Guest checkout is charged £3.95 on every return however little is sent back, so the cheapest way to try two sizes is to hand over your details
  • Brand Partner items are returned under the partner's own policy at the partner's discretion, so the 28-day window and the free return route are not site-wide, and American Express is refused on those orders
  • A refund can take up to 10 calendar days for the parcel to be processed and then up to 10 working days to clear, which is 4 weeks in the worst case on money you have already paid
  • No product price could be read from asos.com on any fetch, so no like-for-like cheapest-item comparison with other menswear retailers is possible from published sources
  • There is no independent customer satisfaction score for a menswear retailer to set against any of this: Which? publishes rated best-and-worst-shops surveys for baby shops, technology and furniture, and none for clothing
  • The Responsible edit has gone and no successor environmental range is visible on the site, leaving it unclear whether the CMA reporting obligation, whose first limb expired on 27 March 2026, still binds anything
  • The modern slavery statement gives factory and worker counts but no number of audits carried out and no breakdown of findings, so the depth of the checking cannot be judged

Common questions

Is it free to return something to ASOS in the UK?

For an account holder in the ordinary case, yes. You have 28 days from delivery, you can drop the parcel at a Royal Mail Post Office or an InPost locker or shop, or book a free Royal Mail collection up to 5 days in advance Monday to Saturday. Two situations are charged: a guest checkout order always has £3.95 deducted from the refund, and an account whose return rate has passed 70% of order value across 3 or more orders in the past 12 months has £3.95 deducted per returned parcel unless more than £40 of that order is kept.

How does ASOS work out my return rate, and can I see it?

It compares the value of what you have returned with the value of what you have ordered over the past 12 months, so it is a value measure rather than a count of items. It is recalculated every time you place an order, and any change to your status is held for 30 days before it takes effect. Since early 2026 the figure is shown to you in the app, and you also get reminders at checkout and again when you create a return.

Is ASOS Premier worth £9.95?

Arithmetically, if you place two or more orders a year that you would otherwise pay to deliver. Premier costs £9.95 for 12 months and gives free Standard Delivery with no minimum spend, plus free Next Day and Nominated Day Delivery on orders over £15 after discounts, against £4.50 and £5.95 respectively without it. Next Day and Nominated Day are excluded in the Channel Islands and a long list of Scottish and island postcode areas. It does not cover returns, and if your return rate is 70% or higher across 3 or more orders you cannot buy or renew it at all.

Can ASOS charge me £3.95 for returning something faulty?

No. Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 you cannot be charged for returning goods that are faulty, of poor quality or not as described, and that applies regardless of your return rate, as Which? set out when the policy changed in January 2026. If a deduction is taken on a faulty item, raise it with customer care and, if it is not put right, take it to RetailADR, the alternative dispute resolution scheme ASOS is a member of.

Am I buying from ASOS or from another brand?

Both, depending on the item. Own-brand labels are about 40% of gross merchandise value across roughly 10 brands, with partner brands making up the rest and around 150 brands inside the flexible fulfilment model. Items sold and shipped by an ASOS Brand Partner are returned under that brand's policy at its discretion rather than under the 28-day ASOS policy, and American Express is not accepted on those orders. An order can also ship from more than one warehouse, so check which route each parcel takes before sending anything back.

Our verdict

For a menswear shopper who buys, keeps most of it and sends the odd thing back, ASOS is close to the cheapest large retailer to deal with: nothing to return within 28 days, free standard delivery over £40, and £9.95 a year for Premier removing the delivery question entirely. The catch is aimed squarely at the other kind of customer. Once your 12-month return rate passes 70% across 3 or more orders, £3.95 goes per returned parcel unless you keep more than £40 of the order, Premier is closed to you, and the upper loyalty tiers are closed too; above 80% across 5 orders a second £3.95 is added on the way out. Whether that matters to you is a question you can actually answer before you order, because the rate is on the account page, and that is more than most retailers charging for returns will tell you. Check it, then decide whether to order two sizes or one.
