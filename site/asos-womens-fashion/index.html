ASOS Women's Fashion: Vintage Boutiques, Resale and the Jewellery Small Print

Rates and terms checked 1 October 2026 · Women's Fashion · Compare100 editorial team

ASOS put 51 adaptive pieces on sale on 24 September 2026, priced £32 to £65 and cut in XS to 3XL, co-created with Disabled customers and the accessibility consultancy Tilting the Lens. Magnetic fastenings, hidden zips, tube access points, contoured waistbands proportioned for seated wearers, shortened jacket lengths. Figures taken from ASOS's own announcement and checked on 1 October 2026.

That is the newest thing on the womenswear side of the site, and it is worth knowing before anything else, because it is the sort of range a buyer only gets from a retailer with 2,310 jewellery styles and several thousand clothing lines to spread a risk across. The second thing worth knowing is where the second-hand clothes are. ASOS sells genuine vintage — 1,012 vintage tops and 537 vintage dresses as at 1 October 2026, from £21.60 to £189.00 — but not one piece of it is ASOS's own stock. Every item belongs to an independent boutique trading on ASOS's pages, which means your contract is with the boutique and so is the returns policy.

And ASOS will not buy your clothes back. The route out is a separate company, Thrift+, which pays between 10% and 40% of what your clothes fetch, and that is covered in full below.

The vintage rail is real, and it belongs to somebody else

ASOS ran a separate site for independent sellers for years. On 23 January 2025 it announced that the vintage and boutique sellers on it would move onto asos.com itself, and the migration was set to finish by April 2025. The sellers landed inside what ASOS calls Partner Fulfils, part of its flexible fulfilment arrangement, where the boutique holds its own stock and posts the parcel itself. Shazmeen Malik, then Brands Director, framed it as curation rather than consolidation. The old address has simply stopped being a shop: a request to marketplace.asos.com on 1 October 2026 answers with a 302 redirect to the main site and nothing else.

What survived the move is better than the phrase suggests. Women's Vintage on asos.com is broken out into New In, Accessories & Jewellery, Tops, Dresses, Skirts, Jeans, Trousers & Shorts, Coats & Jackets, Jumpers & Cardigans and Sale. The tops category alone returns 1,012 styles and dresses 537. The boutiques are named on every listing — 28 Vintage, Second Wave Vintage, Messina Hembry, Christie's Cupboard, Blank Seams, Remass, Atlas Vintage, Old But Gold Vintage, Jarlia by Jolina — and the stock ranges from £21.60 for an unbranded woven mini dress up to £189.00 for an 80s Yves Saint Laurent giraffe-print belt-waist dress. A 90s Balenciaga velvet dress and a 90s Yohji Yamamoto wool dress were both listed at £175.44 on the day this page was written.

One practical note that matters more than it sounds. Prices on these vintage pages are plainly readable, item by item, which is not true of every ASOS landing page. If you want to know what second-hand actually costs here before you commit to anything, the category pages will tell you.

Sending clothes the other way, and what the 20% band really means

ASOS has had a resale route since 6 June 2022, when it launched its second circular design collection — over 40 pieces using Texloop RCOT and TENCEL Refibra, with the denim built to the Ellen MacArthur Foundation's Jeans Redesign requirements, including 100% pre-consumer recycled materials and a design life of 30 or more home washes — and a trial partnership with Thrift+ alongside it. The trial opened with 30,000 free bags. Vanessa Spence, then Commercial Design and Visual Director, put it plainly: design is one piece of the circular puzzle, and products have to pass through circular systems too.

The scheme still runs as ASOS X Thrift+. You order a free bag, fill it with pre-owned clothing, shoes and accessories across high street, premium and designer brands, and send it back at no cost. Thrift+ photographs, lists and sells the items, and you earn credit when they sell. The rates are published: 10% of the sale price on anything that sells for £4.99 or less, 20% from £5 to £49.99, and 40% above £50. Sellers can opt into real cash instead of credit, paid monthly once the balance reaches £5.00, though credits already earned cannot be converted retrospectively. Anything unsuitable for resale is passed to charity shops or recycled, and it is not sent back.

Do the arithmetic before you fill the bag. A dress that resells at £20 sits in the 20% band and returns £4 to the person who wore it. A £4 top returns 40p. That is not a criticism of the rate so much as a description of what the labour of sorting, shooting, storing and posting somebody else's wardrobe costs; it is simply worth knowing in advance rather than after. There is a visible market for it at the ASOS end: 450 pre-owned ASOS-brand women's items were listed on Thrift+ on 1 October 2026.

Why a £6 pair of silver hoops carries no hallmark

The jewellery pages are where the legal wording gets interesting, and it is worth understanding because it explains a pattern that otherwise looks evasive. Under the Hallmarking Act 1973 it is an offence to describe an article, in the course of a business, as made wholly or partly of gold, silver, platinum or palladium unless it carries a hallmark. The Act bites on the description, not on the metal. So a retailer that writes gold tone, silver tone, 18k plated, 14k gold plated or waterproof stainless steel is not dodging anything — it is staying outside a statute that would otherwise require every piece to go through an assay office.

Across 2,310 women's jewellery styles at £5.00 to £85.00, that plated and tone wording is what ASOS's own-brand and partner listings mostly use: ASOS DESIGN, DesignB London, Pieces, Accessorize, Topshop, My Accessories, Bershka, Pull&Bear, Reclaimed Vintage, Lost Souls, D. LOUISE and Kingsley Ryan. Where a listing does claim precious metal it says so flatly — a search for sterling silver returns 240 styles from £4.00 to £255.00, including Kingsley Ryan 10mm twisted hoops in sterling silver at £6.00, a Lovisa open ring at £19.50, a Thomas Sabo chain necklace in 925 sterling silver at £50.00 and a Lauren Ralph Lauren teardrop pendant at £115.00.

Now the weights, because they decide which of those has to be marked. An article needs no UK hallmark below 1g for gold, 7.78g for silver, 0.5g for platinum or 1g for palladium. A pair of 10mm twisted hoops is comfortably under 7.78g, so a £6.00 sterling silver earring can be described as silver, sold as silver and carry no mark whatever, entirely lawfully. A £100 Thomas Sabo bracelet almost certainly is not under it, and should be marked. The compulsory marks are three: the sponsor's mark showing who submitted the article, the assay office mark showing where it was tested, and the fineness mark. Date letters and pictorial town marks are optional extras. Trading Standards enforces all of it.

Vintage gold, the 1950 line, and whose job it is to prove it

This is where the two halves of the page meet. Old jewellery frequently carries no hallmark, and the law makes room for that: an article manufactured before 1950 may be described and sold as precious metal without a hallmark, provided the seller can show it is of minimum fineness and that it has had no improper alteration since 1950. The fineness floors for the exemption are 375 parts per thousand for gold and 800 for silver. Platinum articles made before 1 January 1975 are exempt, and palladium before 1 January 2010, which is the year palladium was brought into the hallmarking regime at all. Several narrower pre-1975 categories also sit outside it, including certain rings, filigree work and jeweller's settings.

Read that against how ASOS's vintage pages are structured and the practical point is obvious. The burden of proving the date and the fineness falls on the seller. On a vintage listing, the seller is the boutique. ASOS's own terms are unambiguous about this: where goods are sold and shipped by an ASOS Brand Partner, you enter into a contract to buy with that partner, and your contract with ASOS Payments UK Limited covers only the payment. Where an item is sold by a partner but shipped by ASOS, ASOS.com Limited (company number 3584121) is the seller. So if you are buying a £180 vintage gold chain described as gold, the firm that has to stand behind that description is whichever boutique's name sits on the listing, and the question to ask before paying is which it is.

The returns mechanics follow the same split and are more generous than you might expect. Brand Partner returns are free and go direct to the brand rather than to ASOS, with up to 10 calendar days for the parcel to be received and processed and up to 15 for some brands. Sending one to ASOS instead delays the refund. The policies themselves, though, are at each brand's discretion rather than set centrally, which on a one-of-a-kind item is the part worth reading first.

The notice that should be on the page, and was not

There is a second hallmarking duty, and it is the one with a visible gap. Anyone dealing in precious metals must display an approved Dealer's Notice, explaining the marks and the exemption weights, in a conspicuous position. The current version became compulsory on 1 May 2020, issued by the British Hallmarking Council, which supervises the four UK assay offices. The format is prescribed: printed in black and white, clearly legible, A4 portrait at 210mm x 297mm or larger, reproduced in its entirety with no amendments, additions or deletions. The 2020 revision added a differentiated Birmingham mark for articles marked outside the UK — a vertical anchor and compass in a cut-cornered rectangle, mandatory since 1 April 2019 — and a generic type 2 Convention conformity mark.

The Council's published position is that the Act reaches internet sales and that premises includes a website, so dealers supplying precious metal articles online should carry the notice on the site. Searching ASOS's terms and conditions and its customer care index on 1 October 2026 turned up no Dealer's Notice and no statement about precious metals, fineness or hallmarks anywhere. That is an absence found in the two places a reader would look, and it is reported as exactly that rather than as a finding: a large site has many pages, the duty's application to a general retailer selling mostly plated jewellery is not something this page can settle, and a question for ASOS or for Trading Standards is the honest next step. It needs confirming.

One more piece of wording deserves a mention while we are on the subject of what labels promise. Reclaimed Vintage is an ASOS house brand, sold under the ASOS Brands heading for both women and men, and it is new clothing rather than second-hand. Its brand page carries navigation and no explanation at all of what is reclaimed or vintage about it. Shoppers looking for actual old clothes want the Vintage category and the boutiques in it, not this.

What the recall register shows about the names on these listings

The Office for Product Safety and Standards publishes every alert, report and recall, and jewellery is a recurring entry: a search of the register on 1 October 2026 returns 69 jewellery reports. Nickel is the dominant hazard, because nickel in prolonged skin contact is a sensitiser and the limits are low.

One of those reports concerns a brand that ASOS sells. Product safety report 2512-0348, published 9 January 2026, covers the Lost Souls 'S' Initial Necklace — gold-coloured necklaces with S-shaped pendants, identifier 152384001, country of origin China. The nickel content exceeded the maximum permissible limit, the risk was recorded as serious chemical, the breach was recorded against the REACH Enforcement Regulations 2008, it was notified by local authority Trading Standards, and the affected products were destroyed. Lost Souls appears as a brand on ASOS's women's jewellery pages. Two things need saying with that. The report does not name ASOS, and it concerns a specific consignment rather than a retailer's stock — a destroyed consignment is the system working, not a product on sale. But a shopper filtering 2,310 pieces of inexpensive jewellery has no way of telling which brands have ever been caught by the register, and that is the useful thing to know.

The same register also carries MISSOMA stud earrings (2509-0310, 17 October 2025, recalled from end users) and a Bees Jewellery hand chain, bangle and earrings (2601-0411, 3 February 2026, destroyed). Checked directly against the register on 1 October 2026, the keyword asos itself returns nothing.

Who actually owns the brands in your basket

Topshop sits in the brand list on ASOS's women's jewellery pages and across its clothing, and most shoppers assume ASOS owns it. It mostly does not. On 5 September 2024 ASOS announced a joint venture in which Heartland — the Holch Povlsen family's business, which also owns Bestseller, with more than £4bn of sales across its channels and around 2,800 retail stores in over 30 countries — took 75% of Topshop and Topman for £135m in cash, valuing the two brands at £180m. ASOS Holdings Limited kept 25%. Net consideration to ASOS was expected at roughly £118m after fees and Nordstrom's pro-rata share, and the deal was guided to knock £10m to £20m off FY25 EBITDA before becoming accretive.

What ASOS kept is the part that affects a shopper. It holds design and distribution rights for both brands and pays royalties to go on marketing and selling them online, with exclusive wholesale distribution rights in the UK and North America, and it said at the time that it intended to relaunch Topshop.com within six months of completion. So buying Topshop on ASOS is buying a brand ASOS designs and distributes under licence and owns a quarter of. Nothing about that is hidden, and nothing about it changes your consumer rights, which run against the seller named on the listing exactly as they do on any other item. It is simply not what the brand name implies.

ASOS's own financial year ended on 31 August 2026 and the full-year results had not been published when this page was written, with the latest releases on its corporate site running to an Adaptive collection on 24 September 2026, a creative partnership with stylist Law Roach on 19 September and a London Fashion Week event on 18 September. Anyone weighing up how long a boutique's listing or a loyalty balance is likely to last should read those results when they land rather than relying on this page for it.

Common questions

Does ASOS sell second-hand clothes?

Yes, but none of it is ASOS's own stock. Women's Vintage on asos.com carried 1,012 tops and 537 dresses on 1 October 2026, priced from £21.60 to £189.00, all of it held and posted by independent boutiques such as 28 Vintage, Second Wave Vintage and Messina Hembry. The separate marketplace.asos.com site closed after a migration announced on 23 January 2025 and completed by April 2025, and the address now returns a 302 redirect to the main site.

Can I sell my old clothes to ASOS?

Not to ASOS. The route is ASOS X Thrift+, launched with 30,000 free bags on 6 June 2022 and still running. You fill a free bag, return it at no cost, and earn 10% of the sale price on items selling at £4.99 or less, 20% from £5 to £49.99 and 40% above £50. You can opt to take cash monthly once your balance passes £5.00, but credits earned before opting in cannot be converted. Items unsuitable for resale go to charity shops or recycling and are not returned.

Why does ASOS jewellery say gold tone instead of gold?

Because of the Hallmarking Act 1973. Describing an article in trade as made of gold, silver, platinum or palladium without a hallmark is an offence, so wording such as gold tone, silver tone, 18k plated or 14k gold plated keeps a listing outside the Act. Where ASOS does claim precious metal it says so directly: a search for sterling silver returned 240 styles from £4.00 to £255.00.

Should a cheap silver item from ASOS carry a hallmark?

Not necessarily. Hallmarking is only required above 1g for gold, 7.78g for silver, 0.5g for platinum and 1g for palladium. A £6.00 pair of 10mm sterling silver hoops will fall below 7.78g, so it can lawfully be sold as silver with no mark. A heavier piece such as a £100 925 silver bracelet should carry three compulsory marks: the sponsor's mark, the assay office mark and the fineness mark.

Who do I complain to about a vintage item bought on ASOS?

The boutique named on the listing, in the first instance. ASOS's terms state that where an item is sold and shipped by a Brand Partner you contract with that partner, with ASOS Payments UK Limited (13337408) handling only the payment; where a partner's item is shipped by ASOS, ASOS.com Limited (3584121) is the seller. Brand Partner returns are free and go direct to the brand, taking up to 10 calendar days to be received and processed and up to 15 for some brands.

Is a vintage gold piece without a hallmark legitimate?

It can be. An article manufactured before 1950 may be described and sold as precious metal with no hallmark, provided the seller can prove it is of minimum fineness — 375 parts per thousand for gold, 800 for silver — and has had no improper alteration since 1950. Platinum made before 1 January 1975 and palladium before 1 January 2010 are also exempt. The proof is the seller's responsibility, which on an ASOS vintage listing means the boutique's.

Our verdict

As a womenswear retailer ASOS is doing something few of its size bother with: 51 adaptive pieces at £32 to £65 in XS to 3XL, co-created rather than consulted on, is a real range rather than a gesture. On the second-hand question that this category is compared on, the answer is better than the brand's reputation suggests and more complicated than its pages admit. There is genuine vintage here — 1,012 tops, 537 dresses, 80s Yves Saint Laurent next to £21.60 unbranded — but it is boutique stock sold through ASOS, with boutique returns policies, and marketplace.asos.com now just redirects to the front page. Selling your own clothes means a third party, Thrift+, and a 20% band that turns a £20 dress into £4. The jewellery is where to read carefully: 2,310 styles of gold tone and plated, 240 described as sterling silver, and a 7.78g exemption that lawfully lets a £6 silver hoop carry no mark at all. Worth buying from, with the boutique's name and the metal wording checked first.
