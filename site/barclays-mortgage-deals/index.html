Barclays Mortgage Deals: 5.03% After Two Rate Rises in One Week

Rates and terms checked 5 October 2026 · Mortgage Deals · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Barclays advanced £17.7bn of new mortgages in the first half of 2026, up from £15.4bn a year earlier, and its UK mortgage balances stood at £176.7bn on 30 June 2026 against £166.8bn twelve months before. Those are the bank's own interim figures, and everything on this page was checked on 5 October 2026.

The pricing has moved recently and in one direction. Barclays withdrew a market-leading 4.75% two-year fix and a 4.93% five-year fix on 29 September 2026, raising the two-year range by 30 basis points to 5.05% and the five-year to 5.03%, then repriced 62 further products upward on 2 October — its second increase inside a week. Across the ten biggest lenders, the average cheapest two-year remortgage fix moved from 4.68% at the start of September to 5.11% by the end of it. A rate anyone noted in August is no longer on sale.

What Barclays does better than most of the high street is lend against somebody else's money. Mortgage Boost puts a relative's income on the application without putting their name on the title deeds. Family Springboard was priced at 4.99% for 95% of a purchase price and 5.19% for the whole 100% in the June 2026 range. And since 8 April 2025 a council or housing association tenant exercising the Right to Buy can borrow the entire discounted price with no deposit of their own.

A year of repricing, rung by rung

Barclays reprices often, and the direction reversed in the autumn. Taking the bank's own announcements in order through 2026, the two-year fix at 60% loan to value with an £899 fee went from 3.57% to 3.65% on 5 February, to 4.25% on 19 March in a round that touched 62 residential products at once, down again to 4.30% on 19 June and to 4.25% on 10 July, before the September rises took the whole ladder past 5%.

The 9 July round was the deepest cut of the year: reductions of up to 0.66%, which took the fee-free two-year fix at 90% loan to value from 5.45% to 4.79%, the Green Home version of the same product from 5.35% to 4.69%, and the fee-free two-year fix at 95% loan to value to 5.11%. The 7 August round cut the ten-year fix at 60% loan to value with a £999 fee by a full 50 basis points, from 5.62% to 5.12%, and introduced a Great Escape three-year remortgage fix at 4.88% with a £50,000 minimum and a £2m maximum loan.

Then it turned. On 7 September the two-year fix at 90% loan to value with an £899 fee rose from 4.96% to 5.14%, the fee-free equivalent from 5.11% to 5.29%, the two-year at 95% from 5.35% to 5.53%, the three-year at 90% from 5.04% to 5.22%, the five-year at 90% from 4.85% to 5.03% and the five-year at 95% from 5.30% to 5.48%. Three weeks later the 4.75% and 4.93% headline deals went, and the 2 October round followed. David Hollingworth of L&C Mortgages put it down to rising funding costs and warned of more to come.

Bank Rate itself has not moved. The Monetary Policy Committee held at 3.75% on 17 September 2026 on a 6–3 vote, with Megan Greene, Catherine Mann and Huw Pill all preferring 4%, against August CPI of 3.1%. The next decision is 5 November 2026. Fixed rates are priced off swaps rather than Bank Rate, which is why Barclays could raise its range twice in a week with the base rate unchanged.

£37,500 of income, £270,000 of mortgage: the Mortgage Boost sum

Mortgage Boost is a joint borrower sole proprietor arrangement, and Barclays publishes the arithmetic rather than describing it. A buyer earning £37,500 with a £30,000 deposit could borrow a maximum of £168,375, enough for a £198,375 home. Add a parent earning the same £37,500 to the application and the maximum becomes £270,000, enough for a £300,000 home. That is £101,625 of extra borrowing, a rise of 60%, from a person who will not own a square foot of the property.

Run the two sums against income and something useful falls out. On one income Barclays stretched to 4.49 times earnings. On two it lent 3.6 times the combined £75,000 — a lower multiple, not a higher one, because the deposit stayed at £30,000 and the purchase price rose, pushing the loan to 90% of value. The booster's income buys a bigger loan; it does not buy a better income multiple. Anyone modelling a boost on the assumption that two incomes attract the same 4.49 times will be £68,000 out.

The obligations are real and worth reading before the call. Boosters are jointly and severally liable from completion, which means Barclays can pursue either party for the whole debt. Every booster is credit checked, the mortgage appears on their credit file, and each one has to take independent legal advice. Boosters are named on the mortgage and not on the deeds, so they carry the liability without the asset. Mortgage Boost also cannot be combined with Family Springboard, Right to Buy, the First Home Fund, Help to Buy Equity or the Mortgage Guarantee Scheme — it is one route or the other. Sian McIntyre, managing director of life moments at Barclays, framed the product around how much harder a first purchase has become; the mechanism is older than the framing, and the figures are the part to check.

The deposit somebody else puts up

Family Springboard is the other side of the same idea: instead of a relative's income, their savings. In the range Barclays announced on 19 June 2026 the five-year Springboard fix was 4.99% fee-free for 95% of the purchase price and 5.19% fee-free for the full 100%, cut from 5.32% and 5.54%. A 100% advance at 5.19% against a standard 95% five-year fix at 5.09% that same month is a 10 basis point premium for needing no deposit at all, which is a narrow gap by the standards of high loan-to-value lending.

The Right to Buy product, launched on 8 April 2025, is the most generous thing on the shelf and the least advertised. Barclays will lend up to 100% of the discounted price, provided that figure does not exceed 90% of the open market valuation, so the statutory discount does the work of a deposit. A tenant with a 40% discount is treated as having a 40% deposit and priced at 60% loan to value — the cheapest rung of the ladder rather than the dearest. Higher-value homes are capped more tightly, at 100% of the discounted price but no more than 80% of open market value, and the thresholds are published: houses valued at £640,000 or above and flats at £310,000 or above. Above those lines a deposit is still needed. Lee Chiswell, head of mortgages, described the point as removing the deposit barrier entirely, and on the published terms that is what it does for a tenant under the value caps.

Barclays is also lending at the top of the range far more than it was. Loans above 90% loan to value were 4.4% of new UK home lending in the first half of 2026, against 1.6% in the same period of 2025 — roughly £780m against £250m. Balances above 90% are now 2.1% of the book, up from 1.7% at December 2025.

What Premier status is worth, measured on one day

Barclays runs a Premier range alongside the core one, and the gap between them is measurable because both move in the same announcement. On 7 September 2026 the Premier five-year fix at 90% loan to value rose from 4.65% to 4.83% while the standard five-year at the same loan to value rose from 4.85% to 5.03%. That is a flat 20 basis point discount for Premier customers on identical terms. On a £250,000 loan over 25 years, 20 basis points is in the region of £27 a month.

Premier pricing shows up elsewhere in the range too. The two-year tracker Barclays introduced on 19 June 2026 at 3.99% with a £999 fee up to 60% loan to value was a Premier product, and in the 18 June round the Premier two-year fix at 75% loan to value was 4.47% against 4.50% for the standard product with an £899 fee. Existing borrowers have a third range again, the Existing Mortgage Customer Reward: on 7 August its two-year fix at 60% loan to value was 4.66% with a £999 fee, and its ten-year fix at 75% was 5.12% with a £749 fee — the lowest product fee in any Barclays range this year.

Barclays does not publish the Premier qualifying test on any page this review could read, so the income or balance needed to reach that 20 basis points could not be confirmed and is worth asking about before an application is built around it.

£177.8bn falls due by Christmas, and most of it will never be shopped

Barclays publishes an analysis of the CACI Mortgage Market Database, and the June 2026 edition, using data to March 2026, found £177.8bn of UK mortgages maturing in the second half of 2026 — £154.5bn residential, up 5.1% on 2025, and £23.3bn buy-to-let. December alone carries £38bn of residential maturities against £16.2bn in November. By region the residential total splits £33.6bn in London, £31.1bn in the South East, £18.5bn in the East of England, £9bn in Scotland and £2bn in Northern Ireland.

What happens to maturing loans is the single most revealing statistic in this market. UK Finance's half-year figures for 2026 show £143bn of internal product transfers against £41bn of external remortgaging — three and a half times as much money staying put as moving. Its December 2025 forecast put the full year at £261bn of product transfers against £77bn of remortgaging, and 2027 at £272bn against £80bn. Total gross lending was £145bn in the first half against a £300bn full-year forecast, of which £82bn was house purchase and £5bn buy-to-let.

A product transfer is also the one route where nobody has to look at the wider market. Under MCOB 4.8A.10R a lender can vary a rate on an execution-only basis provided there is no additional borrowing beyond fees and it shows the customer every product they are eligible for. MCOB 4.8A.14R then requires the firm to say plainly that it is not assessing suitability and that the customer loses the protections an advised sale carries. Those rules are a floor, not a recommendation: a borrower clicking through a switcher screen has been told, in terms, that nobody checked whether the deal suits them. Execution-only is barred outright for a statutory Right to Buy, for debt consolidation and for shared equity agreements under MCOB 4.8A.7R, and high net worth customers are exempted from parts of it under MCOB 4.8A.9R.

For scale, the Office for National Statistics put the average UK house price at £272,000 in June 2026, with annual growth of 2.0%, down from 3.0% in May: £293,000 in England, £213,000 in Wales, £195,000 in Scotland and £202,000 in Northern Ireland in the second quarter. A 10% deposit on the English average is £29,300, which is almost exactly the £30,000 in Barclays' own Mortgage Boost illustration.

Who is actually lending, and what the complaints record shows

The lender is Barclays Bank UK PLC, firm reference 759676, company number 09740322, incorporated on 19 August 2015 and registered at 1 Churchill Place, London E14 5HP, with accounts to 31 December 2025 and SIC code 64191. That entity is the ring-fenced UK bank created out of the old Barclays Bank PLC, and it is the counterparty on a residential mortgage. The FCA's register is a JavaScript application that does not return firm data to an automated request, so the firm reference above was taken from a published FCA final notice rather than from the register; it is worth two minutes to check it yourself.

That final notice, dated 14 July 2025, fined Barclays Bank UK PLC £3,093,600 for breaches of Principle 3 and SYSC 6.1.1R over account opening checks for WealthTek. It concerns client money controls in another part of the bank, not mortgage lending, and it should not be read as evidence about either.

On complaints, the independent numbers are thinner than they look. The Financial Ombudsman Service received 53,600 new complaints in the quarter to 30 June 2026, upholding 26% of them — 30% once motor finance commission cases are stripped out. First charge residential mortgages accounted for 1,291 of those complaints with 26% upheld, buy-to-let 153 at 21% and lifetime mortgages 71 at 13%. Mortgages are a quiet product at the Ombudsman compared with credit and motor finance, and that is worth saying plainly. The FCA's own aggregate data for the second half of 2025, published on 15 May 2026, recorded 1.74m complaints across financial services, with home finance falling 3.8% from 78,616 to 75,658.

Barclays publishes its own UK complaints return every six months, broken down by product group. The January to June 2026 page refuses automated access, so the home finance complaint count, the rate per 1,000 accounts, the eight-week closure rate and the uphold rate could not be read for this review. Those are the figures that would place Barclays against its peers, and they need confirming directly.

Four documents that would not open, and one rating that does not exist

A review is only as good as the paperwork behind it, so here is what could not be read. Barclays' own customer rate sheet, its intermediary residential and buy-to-let rate sheet dated 2 October 2026, its criteria and packaging guide and its complaints data pages all return a 403 to an automated request, and repeated attempts over the course of this review did not change that. Every Barclays rate quoted above therefore comes from the bank's own dated repricing announcements as carried by the mortgage trade press, not from a rate table and not from a comparison site. The live sheet may have moved again since 2 October.

Three specific gaps follow from that, and they matter more than the rates do. First, the follow-on rate: no Barclays page reachable for this review states what a fixed deal reverts to, or whether that rate is capped against Bank Rate. A lender whose reversion rate is capped at a stated margin and a lender whose reversion rate is uncapped are selling different products, and Barclays' answer could not be established. Second, the early repayment charge ladder: the percentage payable in each year of a two, five or ten-year fix was not readable, and on a ten-year product at 5.12% that is a material unknown. Third, the tariff of charges — telegraphic transfer, arrears visits, redemption administration — could not be verified either. Ask for all three in writing before you sign, and treat any figure you are quoted verbally as provisional.

On independent ratings, be clear which kind of absence this is. Defaqto does not rate mortgage ranges at all, and Fairer Finance's mortgages customer-experience table returned "Sorry, there are no ratings to show here just yet" when checked on 5 October 2026. No rating exists to report, which is different from a poor one.

The credit signals in Barclays' own half-year numbers are mixed and belong here rather than higher up. Average loan to value on new lending held at 70%, but the whole book rose from 54% to 57%, lending above 90% loan to value nearly tripled as a share of new business, and the credit impairment charge rose from £237m to £338m year on year. None of that is alarming for a book of this size; all of it is the shape of a lender reaching further up the loan-to-value ladder, and a borrower at 95% should know they are part of that shift.

  • Family Springboard was 4.99% at 95% loan to value and 5.19% at 100%, both fee-free, in the June 2026 range
  • Premier customers got a flat 20 basis point discount on the five-year 90% loan to value product in the 7 September 2026 repricing — 4.83% against 5.03%
  • Existing borrowers have their own range with the cheapest product fee seen all year, £749 on the ten-year fix at 75% loan to value
  • Barclays reprices frequently and publishes each change with the product, the loan to value and the fee, so the direction of travel is checkable
  • A ten-year fix at 60% loan to value was cut 50 basis points to 5.12% on 7 August 2026, which is a genuine long-term option few lenders price keenly
  • Green Home versions of the core products carried a visible discount, 4.69% against 4.79% on the two-year 90% loan to value fix in July 2026

Common questions

What is the cheapest Barclays mortgage rate right now?

The most recent figure that can be verified from Barclays' own announcement is 5.03% on the five-year fix, effective 29 September 2026, replacing a 4.93% deal; the two-year equivalent went to 5.05%. Barclays then repriced 62 further products upward on 2 October 2026 without publishing the individual rates, and its own rate sheet refuses automated access, so treat 5.03% as a floor rather than today's number and ask for the current sheet.

How much more can Mortgage Boost let me borrow?

On Barclays' own illustration, a buyer earning £37,500 with a £30,000 deposit could borrow £168,375. Adding one person earning another £37,500 took that to £270,000 — £101,625 more, a rise of 60%. The booster is named on the mortgage, not on the title deeds, is jointly and severally liable for the whole balance, is credit checked, has the mortgage recorded on their credit file and must take independent legal advice.

Can I get a Barclays mortgage with no deposit?

In two situations. Family Springboard was priced at 5.19% fee-free for 100% of the purchase price in the June 2026 range, with a helper's savings standing behind it. And since 8 April 2025 a Right to Buy tenant can borrow 100% of the discounted price, capped at 90% of open market value, with the statutory discount doing the work of a deposit — though homes valued at £640,000 or more, or flats at £310,000 or more, are capped at 80% of open market value and still need a deposit.

Is it worth switching to a new Barclays deal or remortgaging elsewhere?

UK Finance recorded £143bn of internal product transfers against £41bn of external remortgaging in the first half of 2026, so most borrowers stay. An internal switch is quick and, under MCOB 4.8A.10R, can be done execution-only with no additional borrowing — but MCOB 4.8A.14R means the lender has told you it is not assessing whether the deal suits you. With £177.8bn of UK mortgages maturing in the second half of 2026 and £38bn of it in December alone, it is worth at least pricing the external market before accepting the switcher screen.

Who regulates Barclays mortgages and where do I complain?

The lender is Barclays Bank UK PLC, firm reference 759676, authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Complain to Barclays first; if you are not satisfied, or eight weeks pass, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which upheld 26% of the 1,291 first charge residential mortgage complaints it received in the quarter to 30 June 2026.

Our verdict

Barclays is at its most useful when a borrower needs somebody else's income or somebody else's deposit. The Mortgage Boost figures are published rather than hinted at, the Right to Buy product prices a statutory discount as a deposit, and Family Springboard will lend the full purchase price for 20 basis points over its own 95% product. Against that, the rate position is unstable — two increases in the week to 2 October 2026, with the 4.75% two-year and 4.93% five-year headline deals withdrawn on 29 September — and the documents that would let a borrower price the whole deal rather than the first few years, the reversion rate and the early repayment charge ladder, are not readable from outside. Go to Barclays for what it will lend and on whose income; get the follow-on rate and the charge schedule in writing before you sign anything.
