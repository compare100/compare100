Barclays Personal Loan: the Rate You Only See Once You Bank There

Rates and terms checked 10 October 2026 · Loans · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Barclays advertises its Greener Home Loan at 6.3% APR representative on £7,500 to £15,000 over two to five years, with up to £250 cashback, and it is the only personal loan rate the bank publishes as readable text anywhere on its site. Figures re-checked on 10 October 2026 against barclays.co.uk, where the same deal is advertised at 6.3% on the two greener-homes pages and at 6.5% on the main loans page.

Barclays lends between £1,000 and £50,000, repaid over two to five years. The quote is the selling point and the bank says so in its own words: “With some lenders, you won't know your actual rate until you've applied. And if you're not happy with the APR you're offered, your credit rating could still be affected even if you don't take out the loan. We're different – in many cases we can give you a personalised price quote up front, with no impact on your credit score.” Settle early and the charge is “a fee equal to 30 days' interest on the amount you're repaying”, which is a good deal less than the law lets a lender take; change your mind altogether inside 14 days and there is no settlement fee and, if you repay within a week of opening, no interest either. There is also a price guarantee: find a like-for-like unsecured loan at a lower APR within 30 days of signing and Barclays will “reduce the interest rate to produce an APR equal to the competing offer”.

The condition is that you already bank there. The provisional offer and the personal rate are for people who hold “a current account with us” — the loan pages also accept “a Barclays account – such as a Barclaycard” — together with Online Banking or Barclays Mobile Banking. Somebody arriving from a comparison page with neither cannot obtain a Barclays rate at all, and the representative example on the bank's open loan pages was still being served with its numbers missing on 10 October 2026, twenty-seven days after it was first found that way. That is worth knowing before you spend a click on it.

Two Barclays pages, two different rates for one loan

The Greener Home Loan rate is tied to a narrow slice of the product, and on 10 October 2026 Barclays was quoting it at two different levels. The dedicated product page at barclays.co.uk/sustainability/greener-homes/greener-home-loan and the greener-homes hub above it both head the offer “6.3% APR Representative on loans of £7,500 - £15,000 over 2-5 years, (your rate may differ*)”. The general loans page at barclays.co.uk/loans carries the identical sentence with “6.5%” in front of it. Both were read twice. Two of the bank's own pages therefore disagree by two tenths of a point on the one personal loan rate it publishes, and the figure used here — 6.3% — is the one on the page built for the product rather than the one on the listing page above it. On a £10,000 five-year advance the gap between the two is worth roughly £50 of interest: small, but it is the number a shopper is being asked to compare on.

Either way the rate reaches only part of the range. You get to it by selecting “Greener home” as the purpose in the application, and it is quoted for £7,500 to £15,000 over two to five years. Borrow £1,000, or borrow £40,000, and neither published figure tells you anything.

For the ordinary Barclayloan the picture is thinner still. On barclays.co.uk/loans, /loans/personal, /loans/calculator and /loans/home-improvement-loan, the representative APR was again served as “0 APR Representative on loans of 0 over 2-5 years” and the example beneath it as “APR Representative based on a loan of repayable over months at an interest rate of pa (fixed)”. The figures are plainly injected by the page's own scripts rather than absent from the product, and a shopper in an ordinary browser may well see them fill in — but they are not in the page as delivered, and no Barclays page that could be opened on 13 September or 10 October 2026 stated the standard representative APR or the maximum rate. Treat that as unestablished rather than as a gap in the product, and ask for the number before you apply. The maximum advance is at least published: “You can apply to borrow up to £50,000” on the home improvement page, and “between £1,000 and maximum of £50,000” on the greener loan page, which settles a minimum that could not be established in September.

One line on the loan calculator is worth keeping whichever rate you are shown: “The representative APRs shown are based on all loan applications made by Barclays customers.” A representative APR has to be available to at least 51% of the people who take the advertised product under CONC 3.5, so the other 49% can be charged more. Barclays repeats the point itself — the rate offered “will be based on your personal circumstances, the loan amount and the repayment term”.

The document the law promises you, and the moment it arrives

Every regulated loan in Britain comes with a standard disclosure form, and most borrowers have never heard of it. The Consumer Credit (Disclosure of Information) Regulations 2010 were made on 28 March 2010 and came into force on 30 April 2010. Regulation 3(2) says that “In good time before the agreement is made, the creditor must disclose to the debtor, in the manner set out in regulation 8, the pre-contract credit information”, and regulation 8(1) says that information “must be disclosed by means of the form contained in Schedule 1”. Schedule 1 is headed Pre-contract credit information (Standard European Consumer Credit Information), and it is the one place a borrower sees the rate, the total amount payable, the duration, the charges and the early settlement terms set out to a fixed pattern that can be laid alongside another lender's.

The timing matters as much as the content. “In good time before the agreement is made” is not the same as before you apply: it lands inside the application journey, once Barclays has decided what to charge you. So the comparison the form was designed to make possible happens at the point you are most committed, and on a product whose public pages carry one rate at two different values and no rate table at all for the standard loan, it is the first document with reliable numbers on it. Barclays publishes no specimen version of it for a personal loan that could be found on either 13 September or 10 October 2026, and the full terms of its price guarantee, linked from the loan page as “See our full price guarantee terms and conditions”, would not open on either date. Neither absence is proof that the documents are hard to get once you are in the application; both mean you cannot read them first.

A soft search now, a hard one later

The no-impact quote is real, and it is worth understanding what it does and does not settle. A quotation search leaves a footprint only you can see; the full application leaves one every other lender can. Between the two sits the assessment the FCA requires, and the rules are more demanding than most people expect.

CONC 5.2A.4R obliges a firm to carry out a creditworthiness assessment before entering into a regulated credit agreement or significantly increasing the credit provided. CONC 5.2A.10R makes the firm weigh two different things: “the risk that the customer will not make repayments under the agreement by their due dates (credit risk)” and the risk to the customer of not being able to make them at all. CONC 5.2A.12R(5) puts it in the borrower's terms — the question is whether repayments can be made “without the repayments having a significant adverse impact on the customer's financial situation”. CONC 5.2A.14R stops the lender counting any guarantee, indemnity or security when it judges that second risk. All four have carried the date 1 November 2018 since the affordability rules were rewritten.

The practical effect on this particular loan is that Barclays is assessing you partly from data it already holds. Your salary credits, your standing orders and your overdraft behaviour are all in the same bank, which is one honest explanation for why the personalised quote can be produced without a credit search, and why the product is closed to people who do not bank there. Barclays states the residual risk plainly: “All loans are subject to status” and borrowing is “Subject to application, financial circumstances and borrowing history”.

Fourteen days to change your mind, and the price of borrowing more

Two separate exits are worth keeping apart. The first is withdrawal, which is free: Barclays' own help page gives you “within 14 days of receiving your confirmation letter” to pull out, in writing, by phone on 0800 800 300 or in a branch, with no reason required and no early settlement fee. You then have “30 days to repay the loan amount, plus any interest” — and the useful part, which almost nobody mentions, is that “[i]f you repay us in full within 7 days of your loan being opened, we won't charge any interest”. A loan taken out and cleared inside a week costs nothing at all.

The second is settlement proper. “You have the right to repay your loan early, in part or full, at any time,” says the loan page, and that right comes from section 94 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 rather than from Barclays. What varies between lenders is the compensation they take for it. Barclays charges “a fee equal to 30 days' interest on the amount you're repaying, as well as any other interest that's due”. Under the Consumer Credit (Early Settlement) Regulations 2004, regulation 5 puts the settlement date 28 days after the borrower's notice and regulation 6 lets the creditor push it back by a further 30 on an agreement running more than a year, and most mainstream lenders take the whole 58. Barclays taking 30 is roughly half the industry maximum, and on a £10,000 balance at a mid-single-digit rate that is a difference of tens of pounds rather than hundreds — small, but in the borrower's favour and rarely mentioned.

The same fee is where a top-up quietly costs money. Borrow more from Barclays and it does not add a second agreement: “we set up a new loan for the remaining balance plus the extra amount you want to borrow” and pays off the first one, which means the 30 days' interest falls due on the loan being settled. The bank also warns that “the new loan may have a different interest rate from your initial loan”, so a top-up can reprice the original borrowing at whatever the bank is charging on the day, and you cannot do it immediately — “You'll need to have had your loan with us for at least three months.” The combined ceiling is £50,000. Barclays itself points at the alternative: “If you just want to take out a second loan instead of topping up, that could be an option too.” Anyone with a cheap existing loan and a small extra need should price that route first.

The cashback, and the twenty-year gap inside its own small print

The £250 attached to the headline is the top of a three-rung ladder, and the product page prints the whole thing: “£50 cashback on a loan up to £1,999”, “£100 cashback on a loan between £2,000-£4,999” and “£250 cashback on a loan of £5,000+”. The money is not automatic. “To claim the cashback reward, at least 90% of the loan proceeds must be spent on eligible home improvements”, the invoice has to come from a VAT-registered installer or tradesperson, solar and low-carbon heating installers “should be MCS certified”, and “[f]rom the day your loan application is accepted, you have 6 months to claim”. It is payable once per loan and once per property, only on your main home rather than a buy-to-let, second home or holiday home, and only if you are not in breach of the agreement; once the receipts are accepted the money arrives in your current account “in one go within 30 days”. Note the shape of that deal on the lowest rung: a £1,000 loan with £900 of qualifying work behind it returns £50, which is 5% of the advance back.

Seventeen measures qualify, and the list is specific: air source and ground source heat pumps, biomass pellet boilers, solar thermal, solar photovoltaic panels, solar battery storage, loft and pitched roof, flat roof, cavity wall, room-in-roof, underfloor, suspended wooden floor and solid wall insulation, A-rated double or triple glazing replacing single glazing, secondary glazing, new insulated external doors and an electric car charging point. “Spray foam insulation is excluded.”

One of those bullets does not say the same thing in both places, and a householder could spend money on the strength of the wrong one. The product page qualifies the door measure as “[n]ew insulated external doors (replacing single-glazed or solid doors installed before 2022)”. The offer's own terms and conditions, linked from it, qualify the identical measure as “replacing single-glazed or solid doors installed before 2002”. Both were read twice on 10 October 2026. Twenty years separates the two, and the terms are the document that governs a claim, so anyone replacing a door fitted in, say, 2010 is eligible on one Barclays page and ineligible on the other. Ask which applies before you order the doors, in writing.

Who the money actually comes from

The lender is Barclays Bank UK PLC, firm reference 759676 — the ring-fenced retail bank, a different authorised entity from Barclays Bank PLC (122702), which houses the investment bank. The distinction is not academic housekeeping: the help page telling you how to withdraw from a Barclayloan asks for the notice to be posted to “Barclays Bank plc, PO Box 362, M1 3BN”, which is not the entity named as lender on the product. Send it to the address the page gives, keep a copy, and telephone to confirm.

Barclays UK reported £220.8bn of loans and advances to customers at amortised cost and £245.6bn of customer deposits at 30 June 2026, with £1,767m of profit before tax for the half year, a net interest margin of 3.70%, a return on average allocated tangible equity of 20.1% and 14.1 million digitally active customers excluding Tesco Bank. Those are half-year figures and the next set is due at the end of October 2026, so they are the most recent audited picture rather than a current one.

The regulator has been in the file twice in recent years. On 16 July 2025 the FCA fined the group £42m over financial crime risk management, split as £3,093,600 against Barclays Bank UK PLC — reduced from £4,419,500 — and £39,314,700 against Barclays Bank PLC, reduced from £56,163,900; Barclays also agreed a voluntary payment of £6.3m to WealthTek clients facing a shortfall. The older penalty is the one that bears directly on a loan, and it is in the next section.

A borrower's protections are worth stating precisely, because one of them does not exist here. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme covers deposits, not borrowing; being a Barclays depositor changes nothing about a Barclays loan. What you do get is the Financial Ombudsman Service, free to use, whose decisions bind the bank if you accept them — and which has already been asked about this product.

If the payments stop

This is the part of a loan page most lenders would rather nobody read, and it is where Barclays has history. On 15 December 2020 the FCA fined Barclays Bank UK PLC, Barclays Bank PLC and Clydesdale Financial Services Limited £26,000,000 for failures in the treatment of customers in financial difficulty between April 2014 and December 2018, affecting at least 1,530,000 customer accounts. The penalty carried a 30% settlement discount from £37,223,500, breached Principles 3 and 6, and followed more than £273m of redress paid since 2017. Mark Steward, then the FCA's Executive Director of Enforcement and Market Oversight, said that “[c]onsumers should feel reassured that their lender will work with them to help resolve any financial difficulties”.

The rules he was enforcing are in CONC 7 and they are short enough to quote. CONC 7.3.4R: “A firm must treat customers in or approaching arrears or in default with forbearance and due consideration.” CONC 7.3.5BR requires a firm to “take all reasonable steps to ensure that any repayment arrangements agreed with customers are sustainable”. CONC 7.3.9R forbids a policy of refusing to negotiate with a customer developing a repayment plan; CONC 7.3.10R forbids pressing a customer to clear the debt in one or very few payments, within an unreasonably short time, or by selling property or borrowing more; CONC 7.3.11R requires the lender to suspend active pursuit of the debt for a reasonable period while a plan is being worked out. Those are entitlements, not favours, and they are worth naming on the phone.

Barclays' own support pages are a mixed picture, and twenty-seven days has not improved them. The dedicated page for a customer struggling with a Barclayloan or overdraft would not open on 13 September and would not open on 10 October 2026, which may be a fetching problem rather than a broken link. The general money-problems page did open on both dates, and it still names “The Money Advice Service” — a brand that stopped existing on 30 June 2021, when the Money and Pensions Service folded it together with The Pensions Advisory Service and Pension Wise into MoneyHelper — offering “free, impartial information and tools on how to manage your money”. The same page still carries the line “Check the government site for the latest advice and support on the coronavirus.” StepChange, the other organisation it names, is very much still there, still free, and described accurately.

The price guarantee, as an ombudsman read it

Matching a rival's APR is an unusual promise and it deserves testing rather than repeating. The Financial Ombudsman Service has published a decision on exactly this, reference DRN8017479, decided by ombudsman Rebecca Connelley against Barclays Bank Plc. A customer had applied for a loan elsewhere at a lower rate and expected Barclays to match it. The ombudsman found the bank had been entitled to insist on an actual loan agreement from the other lender rather than a completed application form, and declined to order the lower rate; the bank was told to pay £50 for distress and inconvenience because there was “enough evidence to conclude that the bank misrepresented the price match promotion”.

Read alongside the promise itself, that decision is the instruction manual. The claim window is 30 days from the date the Barclayloan agreement was signed, the comparison has to be like-for-like and unsecured, and the evidence has to be a real agreement rather than an indicative quote or a submitted application. Which means anyone intending to rely on the guarantee has to take out a second loan agreement, with a second lender, inside a month of taking the first — a sequence with real consequences for a credit file. The remedy if it works is a rate cut and recalculated monthly repayments, not a refund.

Where unsecured lending sits this quarter

The Bank of England's Credit Conditions Survey for 2026 Q3, published on 8 October 2026, found the opposite of the quarter before it on two of the four measures that matter to a borrower. Lenders reported that the availability of unsecured credit to households decreased in Q3, at a net balance of −11, and expected it to increase slightly in Q4; overall demand for unsecured lending was unchanged in Q3 and was expected to increase slightly in Q4; default rates on total unsecured lending increased in Q3 and were expected to increase again in Q4; and overall unsecured lending spreads were unchanged in Q3 and were expected to be unchanged in Q4. In Q2 the same survey had reported availability rising and spreads narrowing, so the direction of travel between July and September was tighter supply at a flat price with bad debts still climbing.

That is the commercial logic behind Barclays keeping this product inside its own banking app. When lenders are pulling back on availability and watching defaults rise, a bank that can see an applicant's salary credits and standing orders can price more finely than one buying applications from a comparison table — and it does not need to widen its spread to do it.

Independent scoring is thin on credit products. Defaqto rates insurance, not lending, so there is no star rating or ribbon to point at on any personal loan. Smart Money People, which does cover credit, lists the Barclays personal loan at 4.51 out of 5 from 108 individual reviews — against 4.32 from 4,963 for the current account, 3.56 from 95 for the fixed rate mortgage and 4.06 from 12 for car finance. The loan's score and review count are unchanged since 13 September; the mortgage score has moved from 3.44 to 3.56 on the same 95 reviews. No “last reviewed” date is shown against any individual product, only “Last reviewed within 7 days” for the bank as a whole, so treat the loan score as a modest sample of unknown vintage rather than a verdict.

Common questions

Can I get a Barclays loan without banking with Barclays?

Not on the same terms. The provisional offer and the personalised quote are described as being for people who hold “a current account with us” plus Online Banking or Barclays Mobile Banking, and the loan pages also accept “a Barclays account – such as a Barclaycard”. Barclays states that borrowing is “Subject to application, financial circumstances and borrowing history”. If you hold neither account, expect to open one first or to borrow elsewhere.

What is the Barclays personal loan rate?

The only rate Barclays publishes as readable text is for the Greener Home Loan, and on 10 October 2026 two of its own pages disagreed about it: 6.3% APR representative on the greener-homes product page and hub, 6.5% on barclays.co.uk/loans, both for £7,500 to £15,000 over two to five years. The standard Barclayloan's representative APR is served by the page's scripts and was blank on every page that could be opened, so ask for your figure in the application or in writing and do not rely on either published number.

What does it cost to pay a Barclays loan off early?

“A fee equal to 30 days' interest on the amount you're repaying”, on top of the interest already due. Section 94 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 gives you the right to settle at any time, in part or in full, and the Consumer Credit (Early Settlement) Regulations 2004 let a lender charge for up to 58 days. Barclays taking 30 is at the cheaper end of the market. Withdrawing inside the 14-day cooling-off period is different again: no settlement fee, 30 days to repay, and no interest at all if you clear it within 7 days of the loan opening.

How much can I borrow and for how long?

Between £1,000 and £50,000, including any existing Barclays loan you are topping up, repaid over two to five years. A 10-year term for “selected existing customers” appeared on Barclays' loan pages in September 2026 but could not be found on 10 October 2026, so treat five years as the published maximum and ask if you need longer. A top-up needs the existing loan to have run at least three months.

How does the £250 greener home cashback work?

It is £50 on a loan up to £1,999, £100 between £2,000 and £4,999 and £250 at £5,000 or more. At least 90% of the loan has to be spent on eligible improvements at your main home, the invoice must come from a VAT-registered installer, solar and low-carbon heating installers should be MCS certified, and the claim has to be made within 6 months of the application being accepted. It pays once per loan and once per property, and the money reaches your current account in one go within 30 days of the confirmation email.

Will checking my rate hurt my credit score?

Barclays says it can give “a personalised price quote up front, with no impact on your credit score” in many cases. The full application is different and will leave a search other lenders can see. The assessment behind both is governed by CONC 5.2A, which requires the bank to weigh the risk of you not repaying against the risk that repaying would have “a significant adverse impact on the customer's financial situation”.

What happens if I cannot keep up the repayments?

CONC 7.3.4R requires Barclays to treat you “with forbearance and due consideration”, CONC 7.3.5BR requires any arrangement agreed to be sustainable, and CONC 7.3.11R requires the bank to suspend active pursuit of the debt for a reasonable period while you put a plan together. Missed payments will still reach your credit file. Free help is available from StepChange and from MoneyHelper, the service that replaced the Money Advice Service on 30 June 2021.

Our verdict

For somebody who already banks with Barclays, this is one of the least stressful ways to borrow: a real rate on screen before any credit search, £1,000 to £50,000, fourteen days to walk away for nothing, a 30-day price guarantee and an early settlement charge at roughly half what the regulations permit. For everybody else it is close to unquotable, because the personal rate lives behind the app and the published pages carry a representative example with no numbers in it — and the one rate Barclays does publish statically is now showing as 6.3% on its greener-homes pages and 6.5% on its main loans page. Get the figure confirmed in writing, open the pre-contract credit information the moment it appears in the application, check the APR and the total amount payable against a rate you have been offered elsewhere, and remember that the guarantee needs a rival's signed agreement rather than a screenshot.
