Blackcircles.com Tyres Review: Fitted Prices, the Garage Network and the Small Print

Rates and terms checked 12 October 2026 · Tyres · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

A 205/55 R16 tyre starts at £50.68 on Blackcircles.com, and that figure is the price on the car rather than the price in a box. The site states what sits inside it: “Fully fitted price includes VAT, delivery, fitting, balancing, new rubber valve & disposal of old tyres.” Four of them come to £202.72, with nothing to pay at the garage.

The company has been doing this since 2001 and it has been owned by Michelin since 2015, when the French tyre maker paid £50 million for it. Blackcircles says it has served more than two million customers and sold 36 million tyres. It owns no garages at all: you choose a fitting partner from a network the company puts at more than 2,200 across the UK, book a slot, pay online, and an independent business you have probably never dealt with does the half-hour of work. That arrangement is the reason the price can be a single number — the fitting is bought wholesale and bundled, not added at the counter — and the listings are honest about the product too, with the fuel, wet grip and noise grades printed against every tyre.

One thing to know before you book. Blackcircles puts the average gap between ordering and fitting at 48 hours, so this is not the route to a tyre this afternoon; a mobile appointment at home or work is usually included in the same fully fitted price, but it runs on the same lead time.

What £50.68 buys, and who turns the wheel nuts

Search by registration or by the three numbers on the sidewall and the 205/55 R16 page returns 21 distinct tyres. The bottom of the ladder on 12 October 2026 ran £50.68 for a Wanli Sport Macro SA301 in 91V, £55.21 for a Dynamo Street-H MH01 91V, £61.64 for a Triangle ReliaX Touring TE307 in 94W, and £66.66 for a Kumho Ecowing ES31 91H, with a Kumho 91V and a Yokohama BluEarth ES32 91V tied at £69.99. A separate brand table starts lower still, quoting Royal Black from £49.99, Westlake from £50.44 and Mazzini from £51.26, though it gives no model, load index or label grades to go with them.

Each listing carries the three graded figures a buyer is entitled to see. The Wanli is C for fuel and B for wet grip at 70 decibels; the Dynamo is D and C at 71; the Kumho is B and B at 69; the Yokohama is C and B at 68, which is the only A on the noise scale in the cheap half of the list. On a car tyre both letter scales now bottom out at E rather than G, and one step on the wet grip scale is worth several metres of stopping distance in the rain, so paying £16 more for the Kumho buys a measurable difference rather than a brand name.

The arithmetic that makes this kind of retailer worth a click is the fitting. Kwik Fit, which does own its centres, states that its tyre discounts “exclude charges for fitting, balancing, valve and disposal” and prices that work at £20.00 per tyre in one place on its own page and £20.50 in another, neither dated. Call it £82 on a set of four. Blackcircles has the same work inside the number on the screen, so £202.72 for four of the cheapest 205/55 R16 is the whole bill, and the only cash transaction at the garage is none. The company says so plainly: “nothing to pay on the day of your fitting.”

From Peebles to Clermont-Ferrand

The company was incorporated on 16 October 2001 as Online Tyre Store Limited, became Black Circles Limited four weeks later on 12 November, and took its present name on 26 September 2005. Mike Welch founded it in Peebles in the Scottish Borders, and it was among the first British businesses to sell a tyre online for fitting at a local garage. The registered office is now 1 Tanfield in Edinburgh and the registered activity is the retail trade of motor vehicle parts and accessories.

Michelin bought it in 2015 for £50 million. The person-with-significant-control register names Compagnie Générale des Établissements Michelin of 23 Place des Carmes-Déchaux, Clermont-Ferrand — a société en commandite par actions governed by French law — holding 75% or more of the voting rights since 6 April 2016. Blackcircles still sells every brand rather than only its parent's, which is worth saying because the only credit offer on the site is tied the other way: PayPal Credit at 0% for up to 12 months on spending of £500 or more, but on Michelin tyres specifically, against a representative 23.9% APR variable.

Unlike several of the online tyre shops it competes with, this one files full accounts. Thirty-two pages made up to 31 December 2025 went in on 23 September 2026, following the same for 2024 on 1 October 2025 and 2023 on 18 September 2024. A buyer who wants to know whether the company taking the money will still exist when a guarantee is claimed can read the answer rather than guess at it. For scale behind it, Michelin reported revenue of €12.7 billion for the six months to 30 June 2026, up 0.5% at constant exchange rates and down 2.6% as reported, with segment operating income of €1.45 billion at 11.4% of sales against 11.1% a year earlier, and free cash flow before acquisitions of €282 million against minus €102 million in the first half of 2025. The consumer tyre division alone turned over €6,926 million.

Four answers to one question: how many garages?

The network is the product here, and the company cannot settle on its size. On the fitting-network page itself the body text says “over 2,000 garages spread throughout the country” while the page's own meta description says “over 1,500 tyre fitting centres in the UK” and the site navigation above both says “Over 2,200 tyre fitters across the UK”. The about page says 2,200-plus and calls them fitting partners rather than centres. The mobile fitting page says over 2,000. None of the four figures is dated.

Whichever is right, the scale is real. For comparison, the National Tyre Distributors Association — the trade body for the whole British tyre trade, formed in 1930 — has more than 300 member companies between them representing over 2,000 tyre retail outlets. Blackcircles claims a booking network roughly the size of an entire national trade association's shopfront estate, and says 94% of the UK population lives within an eleven-minute drive of one of them, Northern Ireland included.

What the company publishes about quality is thinner than what it publishes about reach. A subset called Premier Garages, selected through a programme begun in 2010, is said to have “exceeded our rigorous criteria on capabilities, facilities and customer feedback” and to offer the highest standards of workmanship. For the other two thousand the site sets out no standard, no audit cycle and no minimum equipment at all, and there is no published way of telling from a search result whether the garage you have just booked has ever been inspected. A third strand, Blackcircles Pitstop, sits at what the company calls key Tesco stores and shopping centres, again without saying who owns or staffs it.

Two guarantees, one ladder, and a date in 2024

There are two products with similar names, and the free one is the problem. The Lifetime Guarantee was introduced in October 2021 and promises a replacement “where a tyre demonstrates a defect due to the manufacturing process or the materials used”, on car, van, 4x4 and SUV tyres from 22 named brands including Michelin, Continental, Goodyear, Pirelli, Bridgestone and Hankook. What you get depends on the tread left when the fault is assessed: 6mm or more brings a free replacement of the same model, 5mm to 5.99mm an 80% discount, 3mm to 4.99mm 60%, and 1.6mm to 2.99mm 40%, each against the price originally paid. Motorbike and truck tyres are out, and so is anything bought elsewhere.

Its own page then says: “Please note: Lifetime Guarantee is no longer active on orders placed after 12:00pm on 04/09/2024.” That is more than two years ago. The same page still carries the heading “Peace of mind, at no extra cost” and still says selected brands “now come with a Lifetime Guarantee, free of charge!”, and the about page still advertises the guarantee as a current reason to buy. A shopper reading either of those in October 2026 is reading about a benefit the company has withdrawn.

The paid Tyre Guarantee is live and it is the one worth understanding. It costs £6.99 a tyre where the pre-discount fitted price is under £49.99, then £7.99, £8.99, £10.99, £12.99, £14.99, £16.99, £20.99 and £21.99 as the tyre gets dearer, and it has to be bought for every tyre in the order. It covers accidental damage for 12 months from fitting and manufacturing defects for the tyre's legal life, which it defines as ending when the tread drops below 1.6mm. Inside the year it allows two puncture repairs with a £20 contribution from you on each, and one discounted replacement per tyre on exactly the same tread ladder as the free guarantee. Damage caused during fitting or removal is excluded, as are low pressures, overloading, snow chains, track use, malicious damage and fire.

Notice what is not stated. The page says Blackcircles provides the cover itself. It names no insurer, gives no Financial Conduct Authority firm reference and does not describe the product as insurance. The comparable product at Kwik Fit's counter names London General Insurance Company Limited, firm reference 202689, which brings the Financial Ombudsman Service and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme with it. Here, a disputed claim on a £21.99-a-tyre product is a contract dispute with the retailer and nothing more. Cancellation is 14 days from purchase, by emailing a form.

Clause 9.8 meets section 57 of the Consumer Rights Act

The terms of sale are clear that the fitting is subcontracted: the Fully Fitted Service means Blackcircles arranging fitting “using third party service providers”, and clause 7.1.2 refers to “the relevant tyre fitting agreement between Blackcircles.com and the relevant fitting centre”, which takes precedence where one exists. That agreement is not published, so the document actually governing the work on your car is one you cannot read.

Three clauses then narrow what the company answers for. Clause 9.4 requires a defect to be notified in writing within 48 hours of delivery or fitting, or within 24 hours of discovery where it was not apparent on reasonable inspection — a tight window for a fault that announces itself as a vibration at 60 miles an hour a week later. Clause 9.5 says the Blackcircles fitter must be given the chance to inspect and rectify any claimed fitting damage, and that work done by anyone else, at a garage the company has not designated or without written agreement, “will not be covered”; so a kerbed alloy cannot simply be repaired locally and invoiced. Clause 9.8 caps the company's entire liability at the price of the goods and excludes liability for loss arising from “the supply or fitting of the Goods”, apart from death or personal injury caused by negligence.

That last exclusion runs into statute. Section 49 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 treats every contract to supply a service as including a term that the trader must perform it with reasonable care and skill, and section 57(1) says a term is not binding on the consumer to the extent that it would exclude the trader's liability under section 49. Section 57(3) and 57(4) reach further, catching terms that restrict such liability, make a remedy conditional on something onerous, or stop the consumer recovering the price paid. Because Blackcircles sells the fitting as part of its own service rather than introducing you to a garage, section 49 bites on Blackcircles, and the remedies in sections 54 to 56 — repeat performance within a reasonable time and at the trader's cost, or a price reduction — are the consumer's whatever clause 9.8 says. The clause is in the contract; the statute says it is unenforceable to that extent. Anyone told otherwise on the phone should quote the section number.

Above the Head of Customer Care there is nobody

Clause 11.7 puts the contract under the law of Scotland and the non-exclusive jurisdiction of the Scottish courts, wherever in the UK the customer lives and whichever garage does the work. The published escalation route stops inside the business: a customer care line on 01721 725 050, a WhatsApp number, and an invitation to write to Tracy Price, Head of Customer Care, who “will make sure your concerns are fully reviewed and put right as quickly as possible”. Naming a senior person is more than most retailers do.

Beyond that there is nothing. No ombudsman, alternative dispute resolution body, trade association or approved code scheme is named on the terms of sale, the help centre, the customer service pages or the contact page. The sector has a scheme and it is a good one: The Motor Ombudsman runs four codes of practice, all approved under the Chartered Trading Standards Institute's Approved Code Scheme, including a Service and Repair Code that covers exactly the work a fitting partner does. It can award up to £10,000 or the value of the vehicle, it gives a business up to eight weeks to issue a final response before it will look at a case, and it takes an average of 90 days to decide one; an adjudicator's view can be appealed by either side, and an ombudsman's final decision binds the business if the consumer accepts it.

Accreditation is applied for, paid for annually and spot-checked on site, and the scheme is blunt about the consequence of not joining: “We will not be able to take on a complaint about a business which is not accredited to a Code of Practice.” No accreditation for Blackcircles could be found, and it is not obvious how an online retailer that performs no work in its own premises would fit a garage code in any case. The practical effect is the same either way. If the company's final answer does not satisfy you, the next step is the small claims route in Scotland, not a free adjudication.

Mobile fitting, 48 hours and the £20 to send a tyre back

Mobile fitting is the quietly strong part of the proposition. The company says “the price of a mobile tyre appointment is included in the fully fitted price in most instances”, with an extra charge flagged on the fitter's listing before booking where one applies, typically in remote areas. A van comes to your home or to a workplace that allows access to the car, provided there is room to work safely, and the fitter installs the tyres, checks the balancing, fits a new rubber valve and takes the old tyres away for recycling. Evening and weekend slots exist. Allow about 30 minutes a tyre.

Cancellation is more generous than the law requires. The Consumer Contracts Regulations give 14 days on a distance sale; clause 8.1 allows 30 days from the day after delivery, or any time before the arranged fitting date, provided the tyres are unused and in the same condition. The cost of getting them back is yours, and a collection carries a “fixed return fee of £20” which the clause applies inside the fourteen-day cooling-off period as well as outside it. Return them yourself within 30 days of asking to cancel and that fee does not arise. Faulty, damaged or wrong goods are different: there the entitlement is a repair, replacement or refund under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and no return fee applies. Anything beyond the statutory minimum, clause 8.2 says, is “entirely at the discretion of Blackcircles.com”.

Two smaller numbers from the same document. Delivery and fitting charges vary by location and by fitting centre, and some areas including the Isle of Man may carry a surcharge, so the headline price is not uniform across the country. And late payment on a trade or account order attracts interest at four per cent a year above the Bank of Scotland base rate.

Where it wins

  • The advertised price is the fitted price: VAT, delivery, fitting, balancing, a new rubber valve and disposal of the old tyre are all inside it, and there is nothing to pay at the garage
  • £50.68 for a fitted 205/55 R16 is competitive once the £20.00 to £20.50 a tyre that a fitting charge costs elsewhere is taken into account
  • Mobile fitting at home or work is included in the same price in most cases, rather than being a paid extra or conditional on buying four tyres
  • 94% of the population is said to be within an eleven-minute drive of a fitting partner, Northern Ireland included
  • Fuel efficiency, wet grip and noise grades are shown against every tyre in the listing, so the cheap options can be compared on something other than price
  • 30 days to cancel an unused order, against the 14 the distance-selling rules require
  • Owned by Michelin since 2015 and files full 32-page accounts, most recently made up to 31 December 2025
  • The tread-depth credit ladder on a guarantee claim is published as exact percentages rather than left to discretion

Where it falls short

  • The free Lifetime Guarantee is still advertised as a current benefit on the about page while its own terms page says it stopped applying to orders placed after 12:00pm on 4 September 2024, more than two years ago
  • No ombudsman, alternative dispute resolution body or approved code scheme is named anywhere on the site, and the terms send a dispute to the Scottish courts wherever the customer lives
  • The paid Tyre Guarantee names no insurer and gives no FCA firm reference, so a disputed claim carries no Financial Ombudsman route and no compensation scheme behind it — unlike the equivalent product sold at a competitor's counter
  • The size of the fitting network is given four different ways across three of the company's own pages: over 1,500, over 2,000 and over 2,200, none of them dated
  • Nothing is published about how the roughly two thousand ordinary fitting partners are vetted, audited or equipped; only the Premier Garage subset has any stated criteria, and a search result does not say which is which
  • Clause 9.4 asks for a defect to be reported in writing within 48 hours of fitting, which is a short window for a fault that only shows itself at speed
  • Clause 9.8 purports to exclude liability for loss arising from the supply or fitting of the tyres, when section 57(1) of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 says a term excluding liability for failing to perform a service with reasonable care and skill is not binding on the consumer
  • The fitting agreement between Blackcircles and each garage takes precedence under clause 7.1.2 but is not published, so the document governing the work is one the customer cannot read
  • A 48-hour average lead time from order to fitment rules the site out for an urgent replacement
  • Returning an unwanted order costs £20 if the company collects it, and the clause applies that fee inside the statutory cooling-off period as well as outside it

Common questions

Is the price on Blackcircles.com the final price?

For a fully fitted order, yes. The site states that the fully fitted price includes VAT, delivery, fitting, balancing, a new rubber valve and disposal of the old tyres, and that there is “nothing to pay on the day of your fitting”. The cheapest 205/55 R16 on 12 October 2026 was £50.68, so a set of four was £202.72 with no charge at the garage. Two caveats sit in clause 4.4 of the terms: delivery and fitting charges vary by location and by fitting centre, and some areas including the Isle of Man may carry a surcharge.

Does Blackcircles fit the tyres itself?

No. It owns no fitting centres. The terms describe the Fully Fitted Service as Blackcircles arranging fitting “using third party service providers”, and the company describes its network as the largest group of independently owned garages in the UK, put at over 1,500 on one page, over 2,000 on another and over 2,200 on a third. A selected subset called Premier Garages has published criteria; the rest have none stated. Because the fitting is sold as part of Blackcircles' own service, section 49 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 makes Blackcircles responsible for performing it with reasonable care and skill.

Is the Lifetime Guarantee still available?

Not on a new order. The Lifetime Guarantee page says the cover “is no longer active on orders placed after 12:00pm on 04/09/2024”, and tyres bought before it launched in October 2021 were never covered either. The same page and the about page both still present it as a free benefit, which is why it is worth checking rather than assuming. Claims on orders placed inside that window are still handled, on a tread ladder of a free replacement at 6mm or more, 80% off between 5mm and 5.99mm, 60% off between 3mm and 4.99mm and 40% off between 1.6mm and 2.99mm.

What does the paid Tyre Guarantee add?

Accidental damage cover for 12 months from fitting, which the free guarantee never included, plus manufacturing defect cover to the end of the tyre's legal life at 1.6mm of tread. It runs from £6.99 a tyre where the fitted price is under £49.99 up to £21.99 above £250, across nine bands, and must be bought for every tyre in the order. Inside the year it allows two puncture repairs with a £20 contribution each and one discounted replacement per tyre on the same tread ladder. Damage caused during fitting or removal is excluded. No insurer is named and no FCA firm reference is given, so a disputed claim is a contract matter with the retailer rather than one the Financial Ombudsman Service could look at.

Who do I complain to if the fitting damages my wheel?

Blackcircles first, and clause 9.5 requires its own fitter to be given the chance to inspect and rectify the damage before anyone else touches it — repairs arranged independently will not be covered. Report it in writing quickly, because clause 9.4 asks for notification within 48 hours of fitting, or within 24 hours of discovery for a defect that was not apparent on inspection. If the answer does not satisfy you, there is no ombudsman: no alternative dispute resolution scheme is named anywhere on the site, The Motor Ombudsman cannot take a complaint about a business that is not accredited to one of its codes, and clause 11.7 points to the Scottish courts. Section 57(1) of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 still prevents clause 9.8 from excluding liability for poor workmanship.

How soon can the tyres be fitted?

Allow two days. The company puts the average lead time from ordering to fitment at 48 hours, and says a quicker supply can sometimes be organised. Fitting takes about 30 minutes a tyre, and evening and weekend appointments are available at both garages and mobile slots. For a tyre needed the same day, a retailer holding stock in its own centres is the better route.

Our verdict

As a way of buying tyres this works, and the reason it works is the single number. £50.68 fitted for a 205/55 R16, £202.72 for four, VAT and fitting and balancing and the valve and the disposal all inside it, and no card machine at the garage — that is a genuinely cleaner transaction than a tyre priced in a box with the labour to come, and the mobile option being included in most cases rather than sold as an upgrade is better than the market standard. Michelin's ownership since 2015 and 32 pages of filed accounts mean the company behind the guarantee can be checked. The caution is about what happens when something goes wrong, because that is where owning no garages shows. The work is done by one of two thousand independent businesses whose vetting is undescribed, under an agreement you cannot read, with a 48-hour notification window, a liability cap at the price of the tyres and no ombudsman above the company. Section 57 of the Consumer Rights Act makes the fitting exclusion unenforceable whatever the clause says, so the legal position is stronger than the contract reads — but enforcing it means the Scottish courts rather than a free adjudication. Buy on the fitted price, book a Premier Garage if one is near you, read the tread ladder before paying £6.99 a tyre for the guarantee, and do not count on the Lifetime Guarantee the site is still advertising.
