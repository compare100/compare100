boohoo Women's Fashion: Delivery, Returns and the Preloved Rail

boohoo charges £3.99 for standard delivery, £2.99 to an InPost locker and £9.99 a year for unlimited Premier delivery, and it gives you 21 days to send something back — a week longer than the 14 days the law insists on. Returns cost £1.99 a parcel, deducted from the refund. Every figure here was read on 13 October 2026 from boohoo.com's own delivery, returns and terms pages.

The shop behind those prices is older than the brand suggests. Boohoo.com UK Limited was incorporated in Manchester on 27 February 2006 and traded as Wasabi Frog Limited until 14 April 2014; it still files from 49-51 Dale Street, M1 2HF. It is now one of five brands inside Debenhams Group, which describes itself as a platform carrying more than 25,000 partner brands, and which reported £795.6m of Youth Brands gross merchandise value before returns in its results for the year to 28 February 2025. That scale is why a £9.99 annual delivery pass and a seven-option delivery menu exist at this end of the market at all.

One thing is worth knowing before you fill a basket: not everything on boohoo.com is sold by boohoo. Third-party sellers, which the terms call Concessionaire Brands, contract with you directly, with DBZ Marketplace Online Limited (company number 13135555) acting as their "disclosed agent and not as principal". Those items are returned to the brand that supplied them rather than to boohoo, and the refund comes from the brand. Check the seller name on your order confirmation before you post anything back.

Seven ways to get a parcel, and the sum that decides whether Premier is worth it

boohoo publishes a seven-line delivery menu for the UK rather than the usual two, and the cheapest line is not the obvious one. A 24/7 InPost locker costs £2.99 and is quoted at about 3 working days. Standard delivery is £3.99 and quoted at about 4 working days, Monday to Saturday. An Evri ParcelShop drop is also £3.99 on a 4 working day estimate. Express is £4.99 and promises 3 working days, which makes the locker option both cheaper and, on boohoo's own stated timings, no slower. Next day is £5.99 with a cut-off of 12am, seven days a week. Northern Ireland standard is £4.99 on about 5 working days, and a bulky item is £4.99 on 5 to 7.

Premier is £9.99 for 12 months and covers standard, express, next day and Northern Ireland standard delivery. The arithmetic is ours rather than boohoo's, and it is simple: three standard orders at £3.99 come to £11.97, so the subscription has paid for itself on the third parcel. On next day it pays for itself on the second, because two orders at £5.99 come to £11.98. Anyone ordering monthly is better off with it by a wide margin; anyone ordering twice a year is not.

Two conditions sit underneath that. Subscriptions bought after 12am on 02.03.2023 carry a £20 minimum spend for free next day delivery, with free standard delivery still applying below £20. And the three cheapest or most specialised lines — the £2.99 InPost locker, the £3.99 Evri ParcelShop drop and the £4.99 bulky option — are not marked free with Premier, so the pass does not cover the single cheapest way to receive a boohoo order. To boohoo's credit the subscription does not roll over silently: the terms say it does not auto-charge for a second year, an email reminder goes out as the 12 months run down, and there is a 14-day cancellation window by email to customerservices@boohoo.com.

Twenty-one days, £1.99 a parcel, and the fourteen days you have regardless

boohoo gives 21 days from the day the order arrives to send something back, which is seven days more than the statutory minimum and more generous than several larger rivals. The charge is the trade-off: "a returns charge of £1.99 per parcel will be deducted from your refund amount", in the words of clause 10.5 of the terms. Send three parcels back from one order and that is £5.97 gone. Concession items work differently and better — a single £1.99 covers all of them regardless of how many parcels or partners are involved — while boohoo's own items are charged per parcel, so a mixed order can attract both.

Premier holders get one free return per order and pay for any further return against the same order. Once a parcel reaches the warehouse boohoo quotes up to 14 days to process the refund and up to 5 more days for a bank to show it, so the worst case from posting to money back is close to three weeks. A refund taken as a gift card is paid at 120% of the return value, which is a real 20% uplift for anyone who was going to spend it there anyway, though the card expires 90 days from receipt and the uplift is not offered to customers who paid with a gift card in the first place. Collection of a large electrical item costs a further £50.

The hygiene exclusions are longer than most: fashion face masks, cosmetics and beauty products, pierced jewellery, swimwear and lingerie where a hygiene or product seal has been broken, plus vitamins and supplements, medicines and toiletries. Clothing and footwear must come back unworn and unwashed with the original labels attached.

Set that against the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013, which give a 14-day right to cancel an online order running from the day after the last item in it arrives, a refund due within 14 days of boohoo being told or of the goods coming back, and the basic outbound delivery charge refunded in full. The regulations forbid restocking and cancellation fees outright, but they do allow a retailer to make the customer bear the cost of return provided it said so up front, which is what the £1.99 is and why it is lawful. The exception matters more than the rule: on a faulty item, or one not as described, no return charge is properly payable at all, because the Consumer Rights Act 2015 remedy is not a change of mind. boohoo's own terms accept the principle, promising repair, replacement or refund on a faulty product, with a right to ask for evidence after 14 days.

A cancellation paragraph written for a customer in the EEA

The returns page explains the statutory right in these words: "If you are a customer in the European Economic Area (EEA) and you order an item online", you get "14 calendar days to cancel your order because you have changed your mind", and "This cancellation period starts from the day you have received all of the items in your order." It adds that "For the avoidance of doubt, your right to cancel does not apply to an in-store purchase."

The drafting has not kept up with the shop. boohoo.com's contracting company is registered in England and Wales, the United Kingdom is not a member of the European Economic Area, and the right a British shopper is exercising comes from the 2013 Regulations named above rather than from any EEA instrument — which the policy does not cite anywhere. The right is not in doubt and nothing here suggests boohoo refuses it; what is missing is any sentence telling a UK customer that the 14 days are theirs by law and sit underneath the 21-day goodwill policy. A shopper reading only that paragraph could reasonably conclude the cancellation right is for someone else.

It is worth knowing which of the two you are using, because they behave differently. Cancelling under the Regulations inside 14 days entitles you to the basic outbound delivery charge back as well as the price. Returning under boohoo's 21-day policy does not, and costs £1.99.

The company in the middle has had four names in five years

Where a boohoo order includes a third-party item, the terms name DBZ Marketplace Online Limited as the seller's agent. The register tells the rest of that story. Company 13135555 was incorporated on 14 January 2021 as Acraman 550 Limited, renamed Debenhams.com Online Limited eleven days later on 25 January 2021, renamed Debenhams Brands Limited on 30 March 2023, and renamed DBZ Marketplace Online Limited on 26 November 2024. It files from the same Manchester address as boohoo, 49-51 Dale Street, under the same mail-order SIC code 47910.

boohoo's documents have not converged on one of those names. The terms and conditions use "DBZ Marketplace Online Limited" at clause 2.2 and "Debenhams DBZ Limited" elsewhere in the same document; the returns policy heads its third-party section "Products bought from DBZ Brands:" and tells you to "return the item direct to the Concessionaire Brand that supplied the item"; and the help pages carry "Debenhams.com Online Limited", which the register shows was that company's name until March 2023. Two of the four names a shopper may meet are former names of one company, which is checkable in a minute on Companies House and not explained anywhere on boohoo.com.

None of this is hidden and none of it changes your rights, because the law makes the disclosed seller responsible either way. It changes the practical question of where a parcel goes and who owes the refund, so the useful habit is to read the seller name on the order confirmation rather than assuming boohoo sold you the dress. The same company number is the one named in Annex A of the green claims undertaking below, under the Debenhams Brands name it carried in March 2024.

What Boohoo Group actually promised the competition regulator

This is the most substantial document anyone has published about how boohoo describes its clothes, and almost nobody reads it. The Competition and Markets Authority began reviewing environmental claims in fashion retail on 10 January 2022, opened a case against three businesses on 29 July 2022, published it on 26 January 2023 and closed it on 27 March 2024 when all three signed formal undertakings. The CMA's own figures put Boohoo's 2022 global sales "in excess of £1.9 billion" with 20 million active customers worldwide, and the three businesses together on £4.4 billion of UK fashion sales a year.

The undertaking is given by Boohoo Group plc, Jersey registered number 114397, registered at 12 Castle Street, St Helier JE2 3RT with a UK principal place of business at 49/51 Dale Street, Manchester M1 2HF. It binds the five companies in Annex A — Boohoo.com UK Limited, Prettylittlething.com Ltd, KarenMillen.com Limited, Nasty Gal Limited and Debenhams Brands Limited — and it is expressly not an admission that anyone broke the law. The legal basis is Part 8, section 219 of the Enterprise Act 2002 in relation to the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008.

What it commits boohoo to is unusually concrete for a voluntary document. Paragraph 1 bars new misleading environmental claims; paragraph 2 gave 2 months to remove or amend the ones already up. Paragraph 4 covers misleading icons, logos and imagery and paragraph 6 covers filter and navigation names, so a menu item called something green is itself a claim. Paragraph 7 requires the inclusion criteria for any environmental range to be published clearly and prominently, and paragraph 8 requires a summary of those criteria, including minimum thresholds, to travel with every purchasable item in the range. Paragraph 10 bans vague fabric language such as "eco-fabrics" in favour of objective characteristics, and paragraph 11 forbids labelling a product as part of a range it does not fully qualify for.

The two paragraphs a shopper can test on a product page are 18 and 19. Paragraph 18 bars a recycled or organic claim where the garment contains non-qualifying fibres in more than a negligible proportion, with fasteners, buttons, zips and threads left out of the calculation. Paragraph 19 requires the specific percentage of recycled or organic fibre, or at least the minimum percentage, evidenced by supplier due diligence or testing. Paragraphs 14 to 16 do the same job for target-based claims, paragraph 17 for claims covering only part of a product's life cycle, paragraphs 20 to 22 for third-party accreditations, and paragraphs 23 and 24 for overall-impact and comparative claims.

Then there are the enhanced consumer measures, which are the operational half. Paragraph 26(b)(ii) required spot checks on a representative sample of supplier certificates within 3 months and at least yearly after that; 26(b)(iii) required confirmations from existing suppliers inside the same 3 months; 26(c) says a claim must come down if a due diligence failure is not remedied or an error is not rectified within 10 working days. Paragraph 26(e) is the one with teeth: spot checks on at least 5% of new listings carrying an environmental claim, at least weekly, starting within 5 working days. Paragraph 26(h) gives 5 working days to pull or fix a misleading claim once boohoo knows about it. Internal documents were due within 6 months under paragraph 27, drafts to the CMA within 2 months under 28, and staff training by the same 6-month date and annually thereafter under paragraph 29.

Ready for the Future, and the clock that ran out in March 2026

The range all of that attaches to was called Ready for the Future, named as such in the CMA's own press notice of 27 March 2024. It is not on boohoo.com now. The site's /womens/looks/sustainability path, which is where an environmental edit would sit, returned no environmental range at all on 13 October 2026: no range name, no inclusion criteria, no stated recycled or organic percentage, and nothing in the navigation described as eco, sustainable, recycled or organic. Whether the range was withdrawn, renamed or simply moved could not be established from boohoo's own pages, and this page does not assert which.

The reporting machinery has a published end, and it has passed. Records had to be kept until the Reporting End Date, which the undertaking sets at 2 years after signature — 27 March 2026. The reporting periods ran 6 months, then a second 6 months to the one-year mark, then 12 months to the end; a declaration report was due within 20 working days of the first period under paragraph 34, further reports within 20 working days of each period under paragraphs 35 to 37, and spot check reports within 10 working days of each 3-month spot check period under paragraph 38. Annex B carries the whole timetable from "Immediately" to the two-year mark.

Two things follow, and the second is the one that matters to a shopper. The reporting obligations have run their course, and whether the CMA still treats any part of them as live is not something this page can settle. But the substantive conduct paragraphs, 1 to 24, carry no expiry date in the document at all. The ban on unquantified recycled claims, the requirement to publish range criteria, the prohibition on green filter names: none of that was time-limited, and all of it is still a commitment on the record. The sector guidance the CMA published afterwards, on 18 September 2024, deliberately sets very few numerical thresholds, so the undertaking remains the most specific thing boohoo has signed on the subject. If a recycled claim ever reappears on a boohoo listing without a percentage next to it, paragraph 19 is the sentence to quote.

The Preloved rail: eight subcategories and no explanation

boohoo does publish a second-hand department, which is more than the brand's reputation would lead you to expect. It runs to eight subcategories — Women's Preloved, Women's Preloved Shoes, Preloved Accessories, Preloved Fashion, Preloved Clothing, Preloved Jewellery, Preloved Bags and Men's Preloved — introduced on the site with the line "Introducing Preloved products here at boohoo!" The copy is aimed squarely at designer resale: the clothing page talks about "preloved Gucci outfits" and "pre-loved designer clothing", the bags page names Dior, Fendi and Gucci and promises "preloved designer bags and second-hand bags for timeless style at irresistible prices", adding that "Each piece has a story - and now it's ready for a new one with you."

What those pages do not carry is any explanation of the scheme. No partner, platform or consignment seller is named on any of the three pages read on 13 October 2026. Nothing states how items are sourced, who authenticates a designer bag, or how condition is graded — there is no equivalent of the four-step grading ladders used elsewhere in resale. The returns policy makes no mention of pre-owned goods anywhere, so a buyer is left to assume the standard 21 days and £1.99 apply to a second-hand Fendi bag on the same terms as a £8 top, and the hygiene exclusions list says nothing about used clothing either.

Two limits on what can be said about the size of it, stated plainly rather than guessed. No item count and no price could be read from any of the Preloved pages, and that is not evidence the rail is empty: boohoo.com serves no product grid at all to an automated read, which was confirmed by checking the ordinary women's dresses department, where no product or price appeared either. So the depth of the Preloved stock needs confirming by eye. And in the other direction the position is clear: there is no route on boohoo.com to buy a second-hand boohoo garment, no take-back or recycling scheme, and no sell-back — boohoo buys nothing from its customers. A boohoo dress reaching a second owner does so somewhere else entirely.

Paying in instalments stopped being unregulated on 15 July 2026

Fast fashion and deferred payment grew up together, and the rules changed this summer. From 15 July 2026 the Financial Conduct Authority regulates deferred payment credit, the formal name for interest-free buy now, pay later. Firms could register for the temporary permissions regime between 15 May 2026 and 1 July 2026 and then have 6 months from the start of the regime to apply for full authorisation. Agreements entered into before 15 July 2026 remain exempt, so the protections attach to new plans rather than old ones.

The FCA set out what that buys a shopper in its statement of 11 February 2026. Lenders must carry out proportionate checks that a customer can afford to repay before the option is offered. They must support customers in financial difficulty and, where appropriate, point them to free debt advice. Consumers get upfront information on payment dates, amounts and what happens if a payment is missed. The whole sector comes under the Consumer Duty, and a shopper who thinks something has gone wrong can complain to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which was not available on these agreements before. For scale, the FCA puts the market at £0.06bn in 2017 and over £13bn in 2024, with 20% of UK consumers — 10.9 million adults — having used it in the 12 months to May 2024.

boohoo's side of this is thinner than it should be. Its own documents refer only to a generic "buy now, pay later" service provider rather than naming one, and the returns wording passes the refund straight out of its hands: boohoo will "notify the relevant service provider that you have returned your Products to us", after which "the relevant service provider will process your full or partial refund as applicable, in accordance with its terms and conditions", with the note that "different refund processing times may apply" and an instruction to "review the applicable terms for your chosen service provider". Stack that on top of boohoo's own 14 days to process and up to 5 days for the bank, and a returned instalment order has two queues to clear. The payment page itself lists Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Maestro, Visa Electron and Apple Pay by icon, and names no instalment provider at all.

If it goes wrong, the escalation route runs out quickly

boohoo's terms do the ordinary things properly. Clause 1.1 names the operator, clause 4.1 warns that product images "are for illustrative purposes only" and that it "cannot guarantee" how a screen renders colour, clause 5.1(a) requires a customer to be at least 18 in England and Wales, and clause 8.5 reserves the position "where there is a pricing error that is obvious and unmistakable". Clause 16.1 accepts responsibility for "loss or damage you suffer that is a foreseeable result of our breach" while excluding unforeseeable loss, which is the standard formulation.

What is absent is the next step. No alternative dispute resolution body and no ombudsman is named in the terms or the returns policy, and boohoo is not on the published member list of RetailADR, the retail sector's certified ADR scheme and a trading name of Consumer Dispute Resolution Ltd, company number 09189773. Neither Boohoo.com UK Limited nor Debenhams appears on it, while ASOS, Footasylum, Boux Avenue, Damart, Hobbs, Miss Selfridge, Oliver Bonas, TK Maxx, Topshop and Topman all do (checked 13 October 2026). A customer who exhausts boohoo's own complaints handling is left with the county court or a card chargeback rather than a free independent decision.

Two registers are worth knowing about, and one of them reads in boohoo's favour. The Office for Product Safety and Standards publishes every product safety alert, report and recall in the UK, and a search on the keyword boohoo returned 0 reports on 13 October 2026. That is an absence rather than a clean bill of health, since reports name the product and not always the retailer, but it is a free check any shopper can run in thirty seconds. The other register is Companies House, where Boohoo.com UK Limited files audit exemption subsidiary accounts supported by a statement of guarantee from its parent: the parent guarantee and the notice of agreement to exemption from audit were filed on 4 March 2026 and the accounts for the year to 28 February 2025 followed on 21 March 2026; the next set, to 28 February 2026, is due by 30 November 2026. There is no auditor's opinion on the trading company's own numbers, which is lawful and common in a group structure, and worth knowing if you are wondering who stands behind a refund.

Common questions

How much does it cost to return something to boohoo?

£1.99 per parcel, deducted from the refund rather than paid up front, under clause 10.5 of boohoo's terms. Items from a third-party concession brand are charged a single £1.99 between them however many parcels or partners are involved, so a mixed order can attract both charges. Premier members get one free return per order and pay for any further return against that order. Collection of a large electrical item costs a further £50. No return charge should be applied to a faulty item or one that is not as described, because that is a Consumer Rights Act 2015 remedy rather than a change of mind.

How long do I have to send a boohoo order back?

21 days from the day you receive the order, which is boohoo's own policy and seven days more than the law requires. Separately, the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 give you 14 days to cancel an online order, counted from the day after the last item arrives, and cancelling that way entitles you to the basic outbound delivery charge back as well as the price. Once a return reaches boohoo it quotes up to 14 days to process the refund and up to 5 further days for a bank to show it.

Is boohoo Premier worth £9.99?

It depends on how often you order, and the sum is short. Three standard deliveries at £3.99 cost £11.97, so Premier is ahead from the third parcel; two next-day deliveries at £5.99 cost £11.98, so it is ahead from the second. It covers standard, express, next day and Northern Ireland standard delivery for 12 months, but not the £2.99 InPost locker, the £3.99 Evri ParcelShop drop or bulky delivery. Subscriptions bought after 12am on 02.03.2023 need a £20 minimum spend for free next day. boohoo says it does not charge automatically for a second year.

Does boohoo sell second-hand clothes?

It publishes a Preloved department with eight subcategories, and the copy is about pre-owned designer pieces rather than used boohoo stock — Gucci, Dior and Fendi are the labels it names. No supplier, platform or authentication partner is identified on those pages, no condition grading is published, and the returns policy does not mention pre-owned goods. There is no route on boohoo.com to buy a second-hand boohoo garment, and no scheme of any kind for selling your old clothes back or recycling them through boohoo.

Who am I actually buying from on boohoo.com?

For boohoo's own stock, Boohoo.com UK Limited, company number 05723154, registered at 49-51 Dale Street, Manchester M1 2HF. For a third-party item the contract is with the seller, which the terms call a Concessionaire Brand, and DBZ Marketplace Online Limited (company number 13135555) acts as that seller's disclosed agent and not as principal. Those items go back to the brand that supplied them and the brand pays the refund, so it is worth reading the seller name on the order confirmation before posting anything.

What did boohoo promise the CMA about its green claims?

Boohoo Group plc signed a formal undertaking on 27 March 2024 under Part 8, section 219 of the Enterprise Act 2002, closing a case opened on 29 July 2022. Twenty-four conduct paragraphs cover misleading claims, imagery, filter names, fabric descriptions and accreditations. The two most testable are paragraph 18, which bars a recycled or organic claim where non-qualifying fibres are more than negligible, and paragraph 19, which requires the specific or minimum percentage to be stated. It was given without any admission of wrongdoing, and the reporting obligations ran to a date 2 years after signature.

Who can I complain to about boohoo if customer services cannot help?

There is no free independent scheme covering the shop. boohoo names no alternative dispute resolution body or ombudsman in its terms or returns policy and does not appear on RetailADR's published member list, where ASOS, Hobbs, Oliver Bonas, TK Maxx, Topshop and Topman do. That leaves a card chargeback or the county court for the purchase itself. Since 15 July 2026, though, an interest-free instalment plan used to pay for an order is regulated credit, so a complaint about the credit can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Our verdict

On the things a shopper decides on before paying, boohoo is better than its reputation: a £2.99 locker delivery, a £9.99 annual pass that pays for itself on the third order, and 21 days to change your mind against a legal minimum of 14. The £1.99 per-parcel return charge is the price of that, it is lawful because it is disclosed, and it is worth remembering that it should never be charged on a faulty item. On the question this category is compared on — how many ways there are to buy the same clothes second-hand — the answer is one, and it is a designer resale rail with no partner named, no grading published and no returns wording of its own, while there is no route to a second-hand boohoo garment and nothing boohoo will buy back. The most interesting document in the whole picture is the undertaking Boohoo Group plc signed on 27 March 2024, which still commits it, with no expiry, to putting a percentage next to any recycled claim and publishing the criteria for any environmental range. The reporting clock behind it ran out on 27 March 2026 and the range it was written about is no longer on the site, which makes those paragraphs easier to forget and more useful to quote. Buy on the delivery terms, not on the green copy, read the seller name on your order confirmation, and if you pay in instalments remember that since 15 July 2026 the Financial Ombudsman Service is finally available on the credit even though it is not available on the shop.
