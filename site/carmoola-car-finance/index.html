What £12,500 costs on each of the two plans

Carmoola publishes a representative example for both products, and the difference between them is the whole argument about which to take. On hire purchase, £12,500 over 60 months at a representative 14.9% APR with a £0 deposit gives a monthly payment of £290, a total cost of credit of £4,901 and a total amount payable of £17,401, including the £1 option-to-purchase fee. Pay the last instalment and the car is yours, with — the lender's phrase — "no mileage limits" along the way. Both examples were unchanged when re-read on 11 October 2026.

On PCP, the same £12,500 over 48 months at the same 14.9% gives 47 payments of £267 followed by an optional final payment of £5,001. The total cost of credit is £5,050 and the total amount payable £17,550. The monthly figure is £23 lower and the total is £149 higher, and at the end you either find £5,001, hand the car back, or start a fresh agreement on a different car. That final payment is set from the predicted value of the car at the end of the term, which Carmoola calls the Guaranteed Minimum Future Value. Hand the car back and any mileage over the agreed allowance is charged — its own guidance says the rate "can reach 15p per mile, but it can vary" — along with anything beyond fair wear and tear.

The 14.9% is a representative rate, not a promise. Carmoola's eligibility checker states "personalised rates between 9.9% and 29.9% APR", and under the FCA's consumer credit rules in CONC 3.5 a representative APR only has to be available to at least 51% of the customers who take out the advertised product. Half of accepted applicants can be paying more than the number on the poster and the advert is still compliant. Worth knowing before you play with the calculator: its default illustration prices £12,500 at 9.9% APR for £221.00 a month, the very bottom of the published ladder, five percentage points below the rate the same site advertises as representative. On the £12,500 over 60 months in the formal example, the gap between 9.9% and 29.9% runs to thousands of pounds, so the figure that matters is the one in your own quote.

Who can borrow, and on what sort of car

The personal criteria are light: 18 or over, UK resident, proof of identity and address, and a credit and affordability check. Carmoola says it weighs current affordability rather than the credit score alone. The initial budget comes from a soft search that other lenders cannot see, and the hard credit check runs only after you have signed your agreements and are ready to buy — generous, unusual, and worth understanding before you sign rather than after.

The vehicle rules do far more filtering, and the lender sets them out as a plain list: "Petrol, diesel (from Sept 2015 only), hybrid or electric cars", "Cars sold at one of our approved dealerships (no private sellers, unfortunately)" and "Used cars (no vans, motorbikes or new cars)". On hire purchase the car must be less than 15 years old by the end of the agreement with less than 100,000 miles on the clock. On PCP the bar rises sharply: under 10 years old by the end of the agreement and under 70,000 miles, which rules out a large slice of the cheaper end of the forecourt. Annual mileage on a PCP is usually set between 6,000 and 12,000 miles.

The September 2015 diesel line is not arbitrary. It is the point at which Euro 6 became the standard for new registrations, and Euro 6 is exactly what Transport for London requires of a diesel car to avoid the Ultra Low Emission Zone daily charge — petrol cars only need Euro 4. A diesel that fails Carmoola's date test is generally the same diesel that would cost its owner a daily charge to drive into central London, so the rule protects the resale value of the security as much as anything else. On the other side of that trade, the market is moving the lender's way: diesel was the weakest fuel in the last used-car quarter SMMT has published, down 7.2% on 620,045 sales, while battery electric rose 67.0% to 110,761.

The £7.5bn redress scheme: three doors, and Carmoola has none of them

The defining story in UK car finance is money going backwards, and where a lender stands in relation to it is the most useful thing a review can establish. On 28 July 2020 the FCA announced a ban on discretionary commission models — arrangements that let a dealer or broker raise the interest rate a customer paid and be paid more for doing it — and the ban took effect on 28 January 2021, with the regulator estimating it would save customers £165 million a year.

The redress scheme followed in policy statement PS26/3 on 30 March 2026. It reaches finance taken out between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024, makes 12.1 million agreements eligible, and the FCA puts total redress at £7.5bn on an assumption that 75% of eligible consumers claim, at an average of around £830 an agreement. Read the scope closely and there are exactly three doors into it, and all three are held open by somebody standing between the lender and the buyer. The first is a discretionary commission arrangement, which the FCA describes as the case where "their dealer or broker set the interest rate to earn more commission". The second is commission of "at least 39% of the total cost of credit and 10% of the loan". The third is a tied arrangement, where "the dealer or broker was using one lender or gave one lender the right of first refusal". Commission of £120 or less before 1 April 2014, or £150 or less from that date, is excluded outright.

Carmoola's agreements sit inside the scheme's date range — it began lending in March 2022, comfortably between April 2007 and November 2024 — so it is not the dates that keep it out. What keeps it out is that every one of those three doors runs through a dealer or a broker, and in a Carmoola purchase neither arranges the credit: the customer applies in the app, the loan is approved before a car is chosen, and the garage is paid by Faster Payments like any other seller, usually the same day. No intermediary means no commission to mark up, to measure against the total cost of credit, or to tie to one lender.

The scheme itself, meanwhile, is not running. On 2 July 2026 the Upper Tribunal ordered a partial suspension on terms agreed with four challengers — Consumer Voice, represented by Courmacs Legal, together with Volkswagen Financial Services, Mercedes Benz Financial Services and Crédit Agricole Auto Finance. Until the Tribunal process concludes, lenders are "not required to calculate or pay redress, or send communications about compensation owed under the scheme". Everything else still binds: firms must keep identifying relevant complaints and agreements, gather commission and disclosure data, and answer complainants who are not owed compensation. The hearing is listed for 14 to 18 December 2026 or 16 to 26 February 2027, and the FCA expects payments to begin in 2027 if the scheme is upheld and not appealed. The dates that matter to a reader with a complaint in: an agreement from 1 April 2014 onwards, complained about by 30 June 2026, should get an answer by 18 November 2026; an older agreement complained about by 31 August 2026, by 18 January 2027; anything later, within five months. The separate complaint-handling pause under DISP App 5 expired on 31 May 2026.

Ending it early: what the 1974 Act gives you

Hire purchase and PCP are both regulated agreements under the Consumer Credit Act 1974, and that statute hands the borrower two exits that most finance advertising never mentions. Section 99(1) says that "at any time before the final payment by the debtor under a regulated hire-purchase or regulated conditional sale agreement falls due, the debtor shall be entitled to terminate the agreement by giving notice". Section 100(1) then caps what that costs: the borrower is liable "to pay to the creditor the amount (if any) by which one-half of the total price exceeds the aggregate of the sums paid and the sums due".

In practice that means once you have paid half the total amount payable, you can hand the car back and walk away owing nothing further. On the £17,401 hire purchase example, half is £8,700.50 — reached at around instalment 30 of 60. Two conditions bite. Section 100(4) allows the lender to add a sum where the borrower "has contravened an obligation to take reasonable care of the goods", so a damaged car still costs money. And section 100(3) lets a court order a smaller payment where the lender's actual loss was less, which cuts the other way.

Settling in full rather than terminating is the other route, and section 94 of the same Act gives the right to do it at any time with a rebate of interest. Carmoola confirms that "Early Settlement Quotes are valid for 28 days", and that "other fees may apply and you will still be charged interest" — without saying anywhere what those fees are. A 28-day quote on a figure that keeps accruing is a narrow window, and it is the one number in this part of the product the lender does publish.

The used market it is lending into

Carmoola only funds second-hand cars, so the health of that market is the health of its book — and the half of it that matters most to a lender has turned down. The Finance & Leasing Association's July 2026 release, published on 23 September 2026, reports its members financing 117,156 used cars in the month, down 7%, on advances of £1,819m, down 8%. Over the twelve months to July 2026 that is 1,382,326 used cars, down 2%, on £21,628m, flat at 0%. New car finance went the other way entirely: 740,677 cars and £21,143m over the same twelve months, both up 14%, and 54,313 cars on £1,549m in July alone, both up 10%. FLA members financed over 85% of private new car registrations in 2024. Geraldine Kilkelly, the association's Director of Research and Chief Economist, called it "a consumer car finance market that remains broadly resilient".

Do the division and the useful fact falls out. Used volumes fell 2% over the year while the total advanced held still, so the average amount financed per used car rose by roughly the same 2%, to about £15,650. That is the signature of rising prices on a shrinking number of transactions, and it is also the exact environment in which fixing the rate for the whole term — which both Carmoola products do — is worth more than it is in a falling one. Three months earlier, on the May 2026 release, used finance was down 5% on both volume and value and new finance up 12%, so both halves of the picture have moved since.

Unit sales, as opposed to finance, are the other half of the same paragraph, and here the most recent quarter SMMT has published on its own site is still Q2 2026, released on 11 August 2026: 2,009,318 used cars changed hands, up 0.7%, with 4,025,550 in the first half, up 0.2%. Battery electric reached 110,761, up 67.0%, for a 5.5% share; hybrids took 6.5% on 130,825 sales, up 31.2%, while plug-in hybrids slipped 6.8% to 22,856. Petrol at 1,121,243 and diesel at 620,045 still account for 86.7% between them. Superminis lead on 31.7% and lower-medium cars on 26.6%. SMMT had not published a Q3 figure on its own used-car pages when this page was checked, so nothing from that quarter is quoted here.

Sell My Car, and the first payment the site admits moving between it and a dealer

Carmoola now runs a second service alongside the lending, and it answers a question the finance side of the business has always left open. Its legal hub now carries Sell My Car customer terms, last updated 23 March 2026, and a separate set of dealership terms. The service "helps you find dealers in our network who may want to buy your car", and clause 1.4 states plainly that it is not a consumer credit product.

For the seller it is free, and the terms say so twice: clause 11.2 reads "Carmoola does not charge you any listing fee, success fee, commission or other customer fee for using the Service". For the dealer it is not. Clause 6.4 reads "Carmoola charges participating dealers a standard fee for completed sales", and clause 2.5 calls it a "fixed fee from the dealership purchasing your vehicle". The amount is nowhere in the document. That is the first place on the whole site where money is acknowledged to move between Carmoola and a dealership, and the shape of it is the right shape: a fixed fee per completed sale cannot be flexed by the size or the rate of anything, which is precisely the defect the FCA's 2021 ban was aimed at.

Two things a seller should know before using it. The contract is not with Carmoola on either count: clause 2.6 says "the contract for the purchase of your vehicle is between you and the purchasing dealer, not between you and Carmoola", and clause 17.5 says "these Terms are between you and Moola Asset Management Limited only" — a different legal entity from the lender. Moola Asset Management Limited is company 14712041, incorporated on 7 March 2023 at the same NW1 8HS address, with SIC code 70221 for financial management and Carmoola Group Limited recorded as its controlling party since 20 November 2024. It was dormant for the year to 31 December 2023, filed total exemption accounts for 2024 on 23 September 2025, and on 11 October 2026 Companies House showed it carrying the warning "Accounts overdue": its accounts for the year to 31 December 2025 were due by 30 September 2026 and nothing has been filed since a confirmation statement on 23 March 2026. The lender filed on time; the entity behind the free service has not.

What the site does not publish

For a lender that leans on transparency, the pre-sale paperwork is thin, and it was no thicker on 11 October 2026 than it was a month earlier. The commission promise has been reworded since this page was written, and the new version is weaker. No middlemen trying to make more money by giving you a worse deal", which is a principle rather than a disclosure. Three smaller things. That August 2025 piece is the only commentary the site carries on the biggest story in its market, and it closes by noting that the FCA "has said it will announce its intentions to consult on such a scheme by Monday, 4th August, 2025" — fourteen months before this page was checked, and two regulatory stages behind where the scheme now stands. The complaints numbers, now that they can be read

Complaints go to 020 3773 3901 between 8am and 8pm daily, to complaints@carmoola.co.uk, or by post to 1 Whittlebury Mews West, London NW1 8HS, and the lender promises a guaranteed update after four weeks and a written response within eight weeks. After that a customer has six months from the final response letter to take the case to the Financial Ombudsman Service on 0800 023 4567. To its credit Carmoola publishes its own half-yearly complaints returns, which most lenders of its size do not — and unlike the last time this page was written, both reports now open and can be read.

They are not comfortable reading. In the six months to 30 June 2026 Carmoola opened 2,294 complaints, a rate of 314 per 1,000 accounts or loans, closed 8.6% of them within three days and a further 80% after three days but within eight weeks — the two columns are separate, so 88.6% inside eight weeks — and upheld 44%. In the preceding half, to 31 December 2025, it opened 1,262 at 180 per 1,000, closed 3% within three days and 81% after three days but within eight weeks, and upheld 48%. So the volume has grown 82% in six months and the rate per 1,000 accounts by three quarters, while the uphold rate has eased four points and the speed of the quick wins has nearly trebled. Two things in Carmoola's favour are stated in its own notes: it records "all customer concerns as complaints, including issues relating to the quality of vehicles supplied under hire purchase agreements" even though vehicle quality is "not directly within Carmoola's control" — a wider net than the rules oblige — and the three-day and eight-week percentages exclude anything caught by a complaints-handling pause.

That pause is the figure a reader should sit with. Of the 2,294 complaints opened in the first half of 2026, 1,606 are recorded as paused, against 782 of 1,262 in the half before. The footnote explains why: "Complaints relating to motor commission arrangements are currently on hold while wider industry guidance is being finalised." Seven in ten of this lender's complaints in that half are commission complaints it cannot resolve. Being outside the redress scheme's three doors, as above, does not mean being outside the queue: the FCA's unsuspended rules still require Carmoola to identify those complaints, gather the commission and disclosure data behind them, and tell each complainant who is not owed compensation — by 18 November 2026 or 18 January 2027 depending on the agreement date — while the Upper Tribunal decides whether the scheme stands at all.

Common questions

Am I covered by the car finance compensation scheme?

Not through a Carmoola agreement, and the reason is the scheme's scope rather than its dates. PS26/3 of 30 March 2026 reaches 12.1 million agreements taken out between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024, and there are three ways in: a discretionary commission arrangement, commission of at least 39% of the total cost of credit and 10% of the loan, or a tied arrangement. All three need a dealer or broker arranging the credit, and in a Carmoola purchase neither does. The scheme is also partly suspended: on 2 July 2026 the Upper Tribunal froze the obligation to calculate or pay redress pending a hearing listed for 14 to 18 December 2026 or 16 to 26 February 2027, with payments expected in 2027 if it is upheld. If you have an older agreement arranged in a showroom, that one is worth raising with the lender who wrote it.

Does Carmoola pay dealerships commission?

On finance, no page on the site says either way. The dealership page states that "Carmoola doesn't receive commission from customer purchases" and "doesn't offer preferential rates for using Trusted Dealerships" — both about money coming in, not going out. The only document that names a payment to a dealer is the Sell My Car terms of 23 March 2026, where clause 6.4 says Carmoola "charges participating dealers a standard fee for completed sales" and clause 2.5 calls it a fixed fee. The amount is not published. In practice the dealer plays no part in the credit application: the loan is approved first and the garage is paid by Faster Payments like any other seller.

How many complaints does Carmoola get, and does it publish them?

It does, every six months, and both reports are readable. In the six months to 30 June 2026 it opened 2,294 complaints, a rate of 314 per 1,000 accounts or loans, closed 8.6% within three days and 80% after three days but within eight weeks, and upheld 44%. In the half to 31 December 2025 the figures were 1,262 complaints at 180 per 1,000, 3% and 81%, and 48% upheld. Of the 2,294, some 1,606 are paused as motor commission complaints awaiting the Upper Tribunal. Carmoola also records vehicle-quality disputes as complaints even though the cars are not its own, which inflates the count in its favour.

What credit score do I need?

Carmoola does not publish an acceptance threshold or a minimum score. Its eligibility checker says personalised rates run between 9.9% and 29.9% APR, and that it weighs current affordability and the overall financial profile rather than the score alone. You need to be 18 or over and a UK resident. The initial budget comes from a soft search that other lenders cannot see, so checking costs nothing; the hard credit check is run only after you have signed.

Can I hand the car back before the end?

Yes. Section 99 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 lets you terminate a regulated hire purchase or conditional sale agreement at any time before the final payment falls due, and section 100 caps the liability at one half of the total price less what you have already paid. On the £17,401 hire purchase example that is £8,700.50, reached at around the thirtieth of sixty payments. The lender can add a sum if the car has not been reasonably looked after. Settling in full instead is a section 94 right, and Carmoola's settlement quotes are valid for 28 days.

How much can I borrow, and over how long?

Hire purchase runs from £2,000 to £40,000 in £500 steps and PCP from £2,000 to £55,000, both over terms offered in nine lengths from 12 to 60 months. No deposit is required, though the calculator accepts up to £20,000. The representative example is £12,500 over 60 months at 14.9% APR fixed, giving payments of £290 and a total amount payable of £17,401 including the £1 option-to-purchase fee.

What happens at the end of a PCP agreement?

On the published example you make 47 payments of £267 and then choose one of three things: pay the optional final payment of £5,001 and keep the car, hand it back, or start a fresh agreement on a different car. That £5,001 is set from the car's predicted value at the end of the term, which Carmoola calls the Guaranteed Minimum Future Value. Handing it back triggers charges for mileage above the agreed allowance, usually set between 6,000 and 12,000 miles a year and charged at a rate Carmoola says "can reach 15p per mile, but it can vary", and for any damage beyond fair wear and tear.

Will Carmoola buy my car as well as finance one?

It will introduce you to a dealer who might. Sell My Car, whose customer terms were last updated on 23 March 2026, "helps you find dealers in our network who may want to buy your car". It is free to you — no listing fee, success fee or commission — and the dealer pays a fixed fee on a completed sale. Two things to note: the sale contract is between you and the buying dealer rather than Carmoola, and the terms themselves are with Moola Asset Management Limited, company 14712041, a separate entity at the same address. It is not a credit product and carries none of the protections of one.

Our verdict

Carmoola is the rare car finance company whose structure answers the thing that has gone wrong with car finance, and re-reading the scheme's own scope makes the case better rather than worse: all three routes into the FCA's £7.5bn redress scheme run through a dealer or a broker, and a Carmoola purchase has neither. The rate is fixed, the only named fee on the agreement is £1, the budget is good for 30 days, and the new Sell My Car service charges the dealer a fixed fee and the seller nothing. Against that, 14.9% is representative rather than typical, the ceiling is 29.9%, the calculator shows you 9.9%, and no fee schedule or specimen agreement is published for anyone to read before applying. Its own complaints return is the hard part: 2,294 complaints in six months at 314 per 1,000 accounts, 44% upheld, and 1,606 of them frozen as commission complaints until the Upper Tribunal rules in December 2026 or February 2027. Being outside the scheme is not the same as being outside the argument. If your car is a used petrol, hybrid, electric or post-2015 diesel from a dealer, this is a strong option and the quote costs a soft search. If it is a van, a bike, a new car or a private sale, look elsewhere.
