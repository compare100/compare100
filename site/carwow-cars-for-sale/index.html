Carwow Cars for Sale

Nothing. That is the fee Carwow charges a private seller — and the company is unusually direct about naming who it thinks does charge, stating on its own selling page that "unlike other sites like WeBuyAnyCar and Motorway, it's always completely free to sell your car with Carwow. No hidden costs, no admin fees, nothing."

The model behind that claim is an auction rather than a classified advert. You submit the car, it goes into a daily sale, and 6,000+ dealers bid against each other for it. You set the reserve. If a bid clears it, the winning dealer arranges collection and pays — potentially the same day. A car can go from listed to sold in 24 hours.

Since launching car selling in 2021, Carwow says it has facilitated 122,000+ used car sales worth £1.6bn, and it has helped more than 2.5m people buy or sell since 2011. Roughly one in ten new cars registered privately in the UK is bought through the platform.

Why the seller pays nothing, and who does

The money comes from the dealer side. Winning bidders pay Carwow for access to the auction and the vehicle, which means the seller's headline price is the price. There is no deduction on collection, no listing fee and no admin charge subtracted from the transfer.

Compare that structurally with a fixed-price instant buyer, where the quoted figure is frequently revised downward at inspection, and with a classified advert, where you pay upfront for exposure regardless of whether the car sells. Carwow's arrangement removes both risks for the seller and shifts the commercial pressure onto the dealers competing for stock.

The trade-off is control. In a private classified sale you negotiate directly and can hold out indefinitely. In an auction you find out what the trade will pay this week — a number that reflects dealer margins, not private-buyer sentiment, and one that will usually sit below what a patient private sale achieves.

Setting a reserve and the 24-hour clock

The reserve is yours to set, and it is the single most important decision in the process. Set it too high and the auction closes without a sale; set it too low and you are contractually exposed to a bid you regret.

The mechanics worth knowing before you start:

  • Bidding runs as a daily auction, so you are not waiting weeks for a result.
  • A sale can complete in as little as 24 hours from listing.
  • If the car does not sell first time, Carwow offers a two-day extension rather than making you relist from scratch.
  • The winning dealer collects the car, and same-day payment is available.

Photographs and an honest description do most of the work on price here. Dealers are bidding from your listing rather than a physical inspection, and a bid that has to be revised downwards when the car is collected is a wasted day for everyone.

Cars Carwow will not take

The exclusions are narrow but absolute. Carwow does not accept insurance write-offs, and it does not accept former service vehicles such as ambulances and taxis.

What is notable is what is not excluded. Carwow states no minimum or maximum value, no age cap and no mileage limit. A high-mileage twelve-year-old hatchback and a nearly new performance car are both eligible, which is a wider gate than several rivals apply.

If your car has a Category S or N marker, this is not the route. A specialist salvage-aware buyer or a private sale with full disclosure will serve you better, and attempting to conceal the marker would unwind the sale in any case.

Auto Express, evo and the media machine behind the site

In February 2024 Carwow acquired Autovia, bringing Auto Express, Carbuyer, evo, DrivingElectric and the Driver Power owner satisfaction survey under the same roof. Those titles reach around 15m car owners a month, on top of Carwow's own 2.5m monthly users and a YouTube channel that drew 1.1bn views in 2023.

No other provider in this comparison owns both a marketplace and the enthusiast magazines that review the cars sold on it. For a buyer, it means the review, the reliability survey data and the transaction live in one ecosystem. It is also worth naming the tension: a business that earns from dealer transactions also publishes the editorial verdicts, and readers should weigh recommendations with that in mind.

One figure to treat carefully — the sell-my-car page cites 6,000+ bidding dealers, while the February 2024 Autovia announcement referenced 4,500+ dealer partners. Both are Carwow's own numbers from different dates; the older figure is the one to quote if you need a dated source.

The gap in the offering: used-car classified depth

Carwow's centre of gravity is new-car discounting and car selling. It does not publish a used-car listing count that stands comparison with Auto Trader's 457,000 average live cars or Cazoo's 300,000 listed monthly.

If you are hunting for a specific used car — a particular trim, colour and gearbox within forty miles — Carwow is the wrong first stop. Its strength is in the other direction of travel: getting dealers to compete for your business, whether that means bidding for the car you are disposing of or discounting the new one you are ordering.

Where it wins

  • Genuinely free for the seller — no listing fee, no admin fee, nothing deducted at collection
  • 6,000+ dealers bidding competitively rather than one buyer making a take-it-or-leave-it offer
  • You set the reserve, so there is a floor you control
  • Sale possible within 24 hours, with a two-day extension if the first auction fails
  • Dealer collects the car and same-day payment is available
  • No stated minimum or maximum value, age or mileage limit
  • Owns Auto Express, Carbuyer, evo, DrivingElectric and the Driver Power survey — research and transaction in one place
  • Strong track record: 122,000+ used car sales worth £1.6bn since 2021

Where it falls short

  • Trade bids will generally come in below a patient private sale — you are selling to the motor trade, not to an end user
  • Thin as a used-car search destination; no published listing count comparable to Auto Trader or Cazoo
  • Insurance write-offs and ex-service vehicles such as taxis and ambulances are refused outright
  • Dealer-count figures differ between Carwow's own sources (6,000+ on the selling page, 4,500+ in the Feb 2024 release)
  • Owning the magazines that review the cars it profits from selling creates an editorial conflict worth keeping in view
  • An auction result is a market test, not a guaranteed sale — the car may not clear your reserve at all

Common questions

Is selling through Carwow really free?

Yes for the private seller. Carwow states there are no hidden costs and no admin fees, and it earns from the dealer who wins the auction rather than from you. The figure you agree is the figure you receive.

What happens if nobody bids above my reserve?

The car does not sell, and Carwow offers a two-day extension to give more dealers a chance to bid. If it still fails to clear, the usual fix is to review the reserve against the bids you did receive — those bids are the market's answer on what the trade will pay.

Will Carwow accept a Category S or Category N car?

No. Insurance write-offs are excluded, as are former service vehicles such as ambulances and taxis. A write-off is better sold privately with full disclosure or through a salvage-aware specialist.

Can I use Carwow to search for a used car?

You can, but it is not where Carwow is strong. It publishes no used-car listing count comparable to Auto Trader's 457,000 average live cars, and its focus is new-car discounting and selling. Start a used search elsewhere and use Carwow for the disposal side.

How quickly will I get paid?

The winning dealer collects the car and same-day payment is available, with the whole process capable of completing within 24 hours of listing. Confirm the payment method and timing with the buying dealer before you hand over the keys.

Our verdict

Carwow is the strongest option on this list if you are selling a car and want competitive bidding without paying a penny for it, or if you are buying a new car and want dealers to compete on discount. The combination of a seller-set reserve, free entry, 24-hour turnaround and doorstep collection is hard to beat for anyone who values speed and certainty over squeezing out the last few hundred pounds. It is the wrong choice if you are searching for a specific used car, where Auto Trader's 457,000 live listings make it a far more productive place to start. It is also no use at all if your car is a Category S or N write-off, an ex-taxi or an ex-ambulance, or if you have the patience and the time to sell privately and want the higher price that usually brings.
