Comfort Insurance Motorhome Breakdown Cover Review

Rates and terms checked 29 October 2026 · Breakdown Insurance · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Comfort Insurance has been arranging cover for motorhomes and campervans for more than 30 years, and its M/C Assist breakdown cover pays up to £10,000 per claim for covered costs, in the UK and in Europe, with individual benefit limits inside that. It has stayed at it long enough to build a reputation the big direct insurers have never bothered to compete for. The business trades from Comfort House on Goresbrook Road in Dagenham, its flagship Horizon motorhome and campervan policy is underwritten by Aviva, and that policy took a Defaqto 5 Star rating in March 2025. The firm has been voted Insurance Provider of the Year by What Motorhome magazine on twelve occasions, and says it paid 98.3% of claims in 2025.

The breakdown cover reviewed here is the add-on that sits alongside that policy. It starts at £96 a year, it carries UK and European assistance in the same package rather than selling Europe separately, and it allows up to six separate breakdowns in a twelve-month period. Roadside repair, recovery, help at the house and a misfuelling service are all in the standard product, and there is a single UK number for callouts on 0330 303 1192, with +44 1206 812 868 for anything that happens abroad.

One thing to know before you get as far as a quote, because it decides whether this page is any use to you at all: Comfort will not sell this cover on its own. Its wording is blunt about it — breakdown is only available "if it is added to your Comfort Insurance motorhome or campervan policy at the time you purchase or renewal and cannot be bought as a stand-alone policy". If your motorhome is insured somewhere else, you cannot buy the assistance here, and if you skip it at renewal you are waiting until the next one.

Six callouts, one hour at the roadside and a £10,000 claim limit

The policy wording splits the product into UK cover (the United Kingdom, the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man) and European cover. Both come with the same premium, which is unusual, because most breakdown sellers price the Channel crossing as an extra.

Across both halves, the insurer pays covered costs up to £10,000 per claim, and the cover runs to a maximum of six breakdowns in any period of insurance. Within that £10,000, individual benefits carry their own limits, and those are the figures worth knowing.

In the UK, the operator sends help to the roadside and, if the vehicle cannot be fixed within an hour, recovers you, your passengers and the vehicle to the nearest suitable garage, or to your home or original destination if a same-day repair is not possible. If the vehicle cannot be repaired the same day and you are at least 20 miles from home, the insurer pays up to £300 towards alternative transport, plus up to £150 for one person to go back and collect the repaired vehicle. Where a night in a hotel makes more sense than onward travel, emergency overnight accommodation, including breakfast, is covered up to £500 per claim. You pay for alternative travel and accommodation first and claim it back with receipts, and the operator has to authorise it in advance.

In Europe the benefit limits are higher: up to £750 towards alternative transport, up to £200 for two people to return and collect the vehicle, and up to £1,000 per claim for emergency overnight accommodation. If the vehicle cannot be repaired within 48 hours, or by your planned return if that is later, the vehicle, you and your passengers are repatriated home or to your original destination. In countries outside the EEA, such as Albania, Serbia and Turkey west of the Bosphorus, services are on a pay and claim basis.

Misfuelling is covered too. The wording provides for a drain and flush at the roadside or at a suitable garage, and the most the insurer will pay under the misfuelling section is the cost of 10 litres of the correct fuel and no more than £250 in total for each claim. Comfort puts the scale of the problem at over 150,000 UK motorists a year, which is its own figure rather than an official one, but the service is useful in a vehicle with a large tank and a filler cap at chest height.

Six breakdowns in twelve months sounds generous until you meet an older base vehicle with a temperamental starter. It is a real cap, and the seventh callout in a bad year is yours to pay for.

Dagenham, Bristol and Colchester: three firms behind one phone call

Three separate companies stand behind a single callout, and each does something different.

Comfort itself is Victor Millwell Insurance Agency Ltd, authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority under firm reference number 300281 — a number you can check on the FCA Register. It is the broker: it arranges the cover, holds the premium and administers the policy from Comfort House, 8 Goresbrook Road, Dagenham RM9 6UR. Its terms of business confirm it advises on the sale rather than simply presenting options, and that premiums are held either in an insurance broking bank account or under risk transfer from the insurer.

The risk itself sat with DAS Legal Expenses Insurance Company Limited, registered in England as company number 103274 at DAS House, Quay Side, Temple Back, Bristol BS1 6NH. That name has since moved. ARAG Group completed its purchase of DAS UK from ERGO Versicherung AG on 16 January 2024, in a deal covering roughly £190 million of gross written premium and around 800 employees as at 2022, and the two businesses now trade under the ARAG name — ARAG plc holds FRN 452369 and ARAG Legal Expenses Insurance Company Limited holds FRN 202106. Comfort's own breakdown page has kept pace, and now refers to "our breakdown partners ARAG and Call Assist".

Call Assist Ltd is the operational half: company number 03668383, based at Axis Court, North Station Road, Colchester CO1 1UX, running what it describes as around 426 recovery operators across the UK and the Republic of Ireland with a separate network covering the rest of Europe. It is the firm that decides which local recovery operator gets sent to a layby on the A30, and it maintains dedicated networks for vehicles that a standard flatbed cannot handle.

For completeness: the insurance policy this cover attaches to is a different arrangement again. The Horizon motorhome and campervan policy is underwritten by Aviva, a relationship Comfort traces back to the mid-1990s when the insurer was still Norwich Union and Comfort was designing its first motorhome scheme.

Crossing the Channel on the same certificate

European assistance being standard rather than optional is the strongest single argument for buying this add-on at the same time as the policy. The European territory listed in the wording runs well beyond France and Spain, taking in most EEA countries plus Andorra, Gibraltar, Monaco, San Marino, Switzerland and Vatican City. Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, North Macedonia, Montenegro, Serbia and Turkey west of the Bosphorus are also covered, but on a pay and claim basis with the operator's authorisation first.

That matters because the underlying Comfort motorhome policy carries a 365-day Green Card, so the insurance itself does not run out at ninety days the way a mainstream car policy's foreign-use clause does. A driver who takes six months in Portugal over the winter has insurance for the whole trip and assistance for the whole trip, from one broker, on one renewal date. Very little else in the motorhome market is arranged that way.

The limits abroad are the ones set out earlier: £10,000 per claim overall, up to £1,000 for emergency accommodation and up to £750 towards alternative transport, plus repatriation if the vehicle cannot be fixed within 48 hours. They are pitched at getting you home, or on your way, rather than at replacing the holiday.

Two smaller conditions are worth writing on the back of the certificate. Claims for a breakdown in the first 48 hours of the initial period of insurance are excluded, so cover effectively starts two days after the policy does. And recovery is not available at all if the vehicle can be repaired at the scene within an hour; the assistance provider makes that call, not the driver.

The charges that sit outside the premium

Comfort publishes its fee schedule openly in its terms of business, which is more than a good many brokers manage, and the numbers are middling rather than punitive for a specialist.

New business or renewal carries a £35 charge. A mid-term change — a new address, a change of driver, adding a partner after a wedding — is another £35. Cancelling inside the 14-day cooling-off period costs £40; cancelling after it costs £45. A failed direct debit adds £20. Those sit on top of the insurer's premium and on top of any commission Comfort earns from the insurer, which the same document confirms it does.

The breakdown cover itself carries a 14-day cooling-off right from purchase or renewal, with a full refund if cover has not started or a proportionate refund if it has. After that you can still cancel at any time, but there is no refund of the breakdown premium. Comfort charges a £10 administration fee, plus Insurance Premium Tax where applicable, on a breakdown cancellation. The insurer can cancel for a valid reason, such as non-payment or suspected fraud, and will normally give at least 7 days' notice.

Should the firm fail, the Financial Services Compensation Scheme covers 90% of a claim with no upper limit for general insurance arranged this way, rising to 100% for compulsory motor insurance. That distinction catches people out: the motor third-party element of the policy is protected in full, the breakdown add-on is protected at 90%.

Vehicle size and what the wording says

Comfort's breakdown page says the cover has "no limits on the length or weight of your vehicle". The policy wording covers a UK registered vehicle registered with the DVLA as a motorhome, motor caravan, campervan or van with side windows, and notes that DVLA size limits are 12 metres long and 2.55 metres wide. Those are the general legal limits for a vehicle of this kind on UK roads rather than an extra restriction added by Comfort, and the wording sets no weight limit.

Towing is treated separately. An attached caravan or trailer is included if it has a standard towing hitch and is no more than 7.6 metres (25 feet) long, so anyone towing a long car trailer or caravan behind a motorhome should check that figure.

One note on documents. Comfort's policy document archive page lists breakdown documents only up to August 2023, but the current M/C Assist wording and product information document, for policies taken out or renewed on or after 1 April 2024, are linked from Comfort's motorhome breakdown cover page. Those are the documents this review now uses.

One further piece of context, since the sales page mentions weight: weight matters more to your licence than to this policy. A driver who passed the car test after 1 January 1997 is limited to 3,500kg, and anything heavier needs the C1 entitlement. Plenty of six-berth coachbuilts sit within a few hundred kilograms of that line once they are loaded.

What the national complaints figures say about this product

Roadside assistance is a quietly troublesome product line across the whole market, and the Financial Ombudsman Service's annual complaints data — published on 21 May 2026 for the 2025/26 year — is the place to see it. The Ombudsman took 214,600 new complaints across every financial product that year and upheld 30% of the ones it resolved. Roadside assistance insurance produced 950 new complaints, of which it upheld 44%.

That is a small line by volume and it says nothing about Comfort specifically, which is far too small to appear in it. But the gap is instructive: car and motorcycle insurance ran at 13,420 complaints and a 35% uphold rate over the same period, and commercial vehicle insurance at 1,429 and 44%. Complaints about assistance products are upheld appreciably more often than the average complaint about anything, which suggests the disputes tend to be about whether a promise made at the point of sale was kept.

Comfort handles complaints from Dagenham on 020 8984 0777. Anything about the breakdown policy itself goes to ARAG's Customer Relations Department on 0117 917 1561, and from there to the Financial Ombudsman Service at Exchange Tower, London E14 9SR on 0800 023 4567 or 0300 123 9123. That escalation is free and the deadline is six months from the firm's final response.

A note on independent ratings: Comfort's Defaqto 5 Star rating, announced on 3 March 2025, applies to its Horizon motorhome and campervan insurance policy. Comfort does not present it as a rating of the M/C Assist breakdown cover, and we could not find an independent star rating for this assistance product.

Common questions

Can I buy Comfort breakdown cover without insuring my motorhome with Comfort?

No. The cover is only available as an addition to a Comfort motorhome or campervan insurance policy, and only at the point you take that policy out or renew it. There is no standalone version and no mid-term purchase.

How many breakdowns does it cover in a year?

Up to six in any twelve-month period. That is a genuine limit rather than a fair-usage clause, so a seventh callout in a bad year would be at your own cost.

How large a motorhome can go on the policy?

The wording covers UK vehicles registered with the DVLA as a motorhome, motor caravan, campervan or van with side windows, and notes the DVLA size limits of 12 metres long and 2.55 metres wide. There is no separate weight limit in the wording. A towed caravan or trailer must have a standard towing hitch and be no more than 7.6 metres (25 feet) long.

Is European cover included or an extra?

Included. The wording has a European section as standard, covering most EEA countries plus Andorra, Gibraltar, Monaco, San Marino, Switzerland and Vatican City, with Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, North Macedonia, Montenegro, Serbia and Turkey west of the Bosphorus on a pay and claim basis. The overall limit is £10,000 per claim, with up to £1,000 for emergency accommodation and up to £750 towards alternative transport.

What happens if I put the wrong fuel in?

Misfuelling is part of the standard cover. The operator can arrange a drain and flush at the roadside or at a suitable garage. The most the insurer will pay under the misfuelling section is the cost of 10 litres of the correct fuel and no more than £250 in total for each claim. The benefit pays for the drain and flush, not for parts or repairs if the wrong fuel has caused damage.

Am I covered straight away?

No. A breakdown in the first 48 hours of the initial period of insurance is not covered, so cover effectively begins two days after the policy starts.

What does it cost to cancel or change the policy?

Comfort's terms of business set out £40 to cancel the main policy within the 14-day cooling-off period and £45 afterwards, £35 for a mid-term change, £35 on new business or renewal, and £20 if a direct debit fails. For the breakdown cover itself, you can cancel within 14 days of purchase or renewal for a full or proportionate refund; after that you can still cancel but there is no refund, and Comfort charges a £10 administration fee plus any Insurance Premium Tax.

Who do I complain to if a callout goes wrong?

Start with Comfort in Dagenham on 020 8984 0777, or with ARAG's Customer Relations Department on 0117 917 1561 for the breakdown policy itself. If you are not satisfied after eight weeks or a final response, you can take it free of charge to the Financial Ombudsman Service on 0800 023 4567. The Ombudsman upheld 44% of the 950 roadside assistance complaints it resolved in 2025/26.

Our verdict

For anyone already insuring a motorhome or campervan with Comfort, this is an easy addition: £96 buys UK and European assistance on one certificate, six callouts, misfuelling cover and a £10,000 per claim limit across both. The European benefits in particular, with up to £1,000 of emergency accommodation, £750 towards onward travel and repatriation if a repair takes more than 48 hours, suit the long continental trips motorhome owners actually take, and pair neatly with the 365-day Green Card on the main policy. The individual benefit limits are the figures to read: £300 for alternative travel and £500 for a night's accommodation in the UK, and £250 for misfuelling. And because the cover is add-on only, the decision is really a decision about the insurance policy: if the Comfort quote is competitive on the motorhome itself, the breakdown cover is worth adding, and if it is not, this add-on is not available to you on its own.
