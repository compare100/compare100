Cynergy Bank Savings Accounts: Rates, Bonus Trap and Verdict

Rates and terms checked 7 October 2026 · Savings Accounts · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Cynergy Bank’s one-year Fixed Rate Bond pays 5.05% AER, read from Cynergy’s own rates page on 7 October 2026, and it is now the only competitive thing the bank sells to a new customer. The Online Easy Access Account — the bonus-inflated headline that put Cynergy on every best-buy table — now carries the line “available for our existing customers only”. If you do not already hold one, you cannot open one.

That one-year rate beats every other term Cynergy offers, including the five-year at 4.30%. The curve is not merely flat, it is inverted, and sharply: locking money away for five times as long pays you 75 basis points less.

There is a second structure to understand before comparing Cynergy with anyone else. Every bond and fixed cash ISA is published at two rates: an advertised rate for new money and a loyalty rate, up to 0.85 percentage points higher, available only when a maturing Cynergy account funds it. On the two and three-year bonds the gap is 80 and 85 basis points — 4.20% advertised against 5.00% and 5.05% loyalty. Any single-figure quote for a Cynergy bond is therefore telling you only half the story, and quite possibly the wrong half.

The easy access account, and why it still matters

Cynergy’s easy access pricing is now a closed book, but it is worth reading if you are already in it. The account pays 4.15% AER with a bonus for the first twelve months and 2.15% AER without it. Both figures are Cynergy’s own: the bank used to publish only the headline, leaving the underlying rate to be worked out by subtraction, and it now prints both side by side. That is a straightforward improvement in disclosure and it confirms the two-point gap.

So an existing customer who opened the account on its old 4.55% headline should treat month eleven as a deadline. Once the bonus falls away the money is sitting at 2.15%, which is behind Close Brothers’ unconditional 3.85% and barely ahead of Aldermore’s 2.25%. Because the account is closed to new customers, you also cannot reset the clock by opening a fresh one — the usual bonus-hopping move is off the table here.

  • Status: closed to new customers
  • With bonus: 4.15% AER, first 12 months
  • Without bonus: 2.15% AER, published by Cynergy
  • Withdrawals: unlimited and free, no notice, no rate penalty

The Online ISA at 4.15% AER variable is still open, takes £1 and accepts transfers in. It now pays exactly what the easy access account pays with its bonus attached, tax-free rather than taxable, which makes it the more sensible home for anyone near their personal savings allowance.

An inverted bond ladder, and two rates for every rung

Minimum £1,000, maximum £1,000,000, with a 14-day funding window and no withdrawals once the term is underway. Interest is paid into the bond and cannot be paid away, which rules the product out for anyone drawing an income from their capital.

The rates, advertised first and loyalty second:

  • 6 months — 4.57% / 4.58%
  • 1 year — 5.05% / 5.06%
  • 15 months — 4.40% / 4.65%
  • 18 months — 4.40% / 4.65%
  • 2 years — 4.20% / 5.00%
  • 3 years — 4.20% / 5.05%
  • 4 years — 4.25% / 4.26%
  • 5 years — 4.30% / 4.35%

Read the advertised column on its own and the conclusion is blunt: take the one year at 5.05% and nothing else. The 15 and 18-month bonds pay 65 basis points less than the one-year for holding your money longer, and the two and three-year pay 85 less. There is no term at which a new saver is rewarded for committing beyond twelve months.

The loyalty column changes the shape completely — 5.00% at two years and 5.05% at three make the ladder behave normally — but those rates need a maturing Cynergy account to fund them, so they are a reason to stay rather than a reason to arrive. Judge the bank on the advertised column, then treat loyalty as what it is: a retention offer you may qualify for in a year.

Cash ISAs

The one-year Fixed Rate Cash ISA is 4.05% AER advertised and 4.75% loyalty, with a £500 minimum and the £20,000 subscription cap. The 70-point gap is the widest in the range. Closing early costs 180 days’ loss of interest, and it is not a flexible ISA — money taken out cannot be replaced without using fresh allowance.

Notice accounts

Three terms, all with a £500 minimum: 35 day at 4.12%, 95 day at 4.15% and 120 day at 4.18% AER. These were the weakest part of this page when it was last written, quoted from a third-party table and flagged as indicative. They are now read from Cynergy’s own rates page, and the 120-day account at 4.18% is the best rate Cynergy will pay you without locking the money away at all.

Awards, complaint volumes and the loyalty catch

Cynergy carries better independent recognition than its size suggests. The Defaqto Banking Award 2026 for Instant Access Cash ISAs is a consistency measure rather than a snapshot: a product has to sit in the top fifteen for ten of twelve months to qualify, and only 29 products out of 2,114 assessed made the cut across all categories. It is also a Which? Recommended Provider.

On complaints, the Financial Ombudsman Service recorded 83 new cases against Cynergy for the period 1 July 2025 to 31 March 2026 — the second-lowest volume of the five providers in this cluster. Per-firm uphold percentages are published only inside an FOS spreadsheet we could not access, so the proportion of those cases decided in the customer’s favour is unverified.

The loyalty structure deserves one more line, because it has grown since this page was first written. It is no longer a footnote about maturing bonds: it is a second published rate on every bond and every fixed ISA, and on the two and three-year bonds it is worth more than the entire gap between Cynergy and the best rate in this comparison. New money is, by design, offered the worse of the two.

Living with a branchless bank

Cynergy is an online operation: accounts are opened and run through the website or app, and the terms say plainly that the accounts are “not suitable for savers who are unable to use Online Banking”. Deposits and withdrawals move through a nominated UK current account. Reported friction points cluster around slow telephone support at peak times and app-based authentication that some customers find fiddly.

Eligibility is straightforward — you must be 18 or over and UK resident for tax purposes. Existing customers get a Fast Track Apply route inside online banking, which removes most of the re-keying when opening a second or third account, and is also how the loyalty rates are reached.

Deposits are protected by the FSCS up to £120,000 per eligible depositor, and Cynergy states that each holder of a joint account is entitled to compensation up to that limit. One correction to what this page said before: Cynergy does not publish a “£240,000 joint” figure, nor an explicit statement that its licence is unshared, so neither is asserted here. If you hold money with more than one brand, check the licence yourself on the FSCS register before assuming the limits stack.

Where it falls short

  • The Online Easy Access Account is closed to new customers — the rate this bank was known for is not available
  • The advertised bond ladder is inverted: 5.05% at one year against 4.20% at two and three, and 4.30% at five
  • Loyalty rates up to 0.85 points higher go only to maturing customers, so new money is offered the worse rate by design
  • Existing easy access customers drop to 2.15% once the 12-month bonus ends, below Close Brothers’ unconditional 3.85%
  • Fixed rate bonds pay interest into the bond only — no option to have it paid away, so unsuitable for income
  • Fixed cash ISA is not flexible, and early closure costs 180 days’ interest
  • The 1-year fixed cash ISA is only 4.05% advertised, against 4.75% for a loyalty customer
  • No branches; support is online and telephone only, with reported delays at peak

Common questions

Can I still open a Cynergy easy access account?

No. Cynergy's Online Easy Access Account states that it is available for existing customers only. If you want easy access with Cynergy you are limited to the Online ISA at 4.15% AER, or a notice account at up to 4.18%.

What is the difference between Cynergy’s advertised and loyalty rates?

Advertised rates are for new money; loyalty rates need a maturing Cynergy account to fund them. The gap is up to 0.85 percentage points — the two-year bond is 4.20% advertised against 5.00% loyalty, and the three-year 4.20% against 5.05%. Quote the advertised rate when comparing Cynergy with another bank, because that is the one you can actually get on day one.

Why does Cynergy pay less for a longer bond?

On the advertised rates it does, and there is no good reason for a saver to accept it. The one-year bond pays 5.05% AER while the five-year pays 4.30% and the two and three-year 4.20%. An inverted curve like that usually means the bank wants short-dated money and is not competing for long. Take the one year, or go elsewhere for the longer term.

Can Cynergy pay my bond interest into my current account?

No. Interest on fixed rate bonds is paid annually into the bond itself and cannot be paid away. Savers who need a regular income from their capital should look at providers that pay interest away — Close Brothers pays annually and at maturity to a nominated account.

Is Cynergy Bank a UK bank and is my money protected?

Yes. Cynergy was Bank of Cyprus UK until it was rebranded in 2018 following a change of ownership, and it is a UK-authorised bank. Eligible deposits are covered by the FSCS up to £120,000 per eligible depositor, with each holder of a joint account entitled to that limit. Cynergy does not publish a statement that its licence is unshared, so check the FSCS register if you hold money with more than one brand.

Our verdict

Cynergy is a different proposition than it was two months ago, and a narrower one. The easy access account that made its name is closed to new customers, so what is on offer to a new saver is a bond ladder whose only competitive rung is the one-year at 5.05% AER. Take that rung and the bank is worth the £1,000; look past it and the 15-month, 18-month, two and three-year bonds all pay less for a longer commitment, which is not a trade anyone should accept. The 120-day notice account at 4.18% is a quietly decent place for money you can plan four months ahead for, and the Online ISA at 4.15% is reasonable if your savings interest is near your personal savings allowance. It remains the wrong bank for income savers, since bond interest cannot be paid away, and for anyone who wants a branch. Existing easy access customers have one job: find out when the bonus ends, because 2.15% afterwards is beaten by Close Brothers paying 3.85% with no conditions at all.
