Dunelm Furniture Review: Returns, Delivery and the Guarantee Ladder

Rates and terms checked 27 October 2026 · Furniture · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Dunelm gives you 28 days to change your mind about a piece of furniture, twice the 14 days the cancellation regulations require, and it will send a van to collect anything that weighs more than 15kg and measures over 120cm in any direction. Sales reached £1,825m in the 52 weeks to 27 June 2026, up 3.1%, across 204 stores in the UK and Ireland.

It is also one of the few homeware retailers on the high street that makes part of its own range rather than buying it in. Dunelm bought Bellbird Ltd in 2001 and turned it into its Made to Measure manufacturing centre, and the company you contract with — Dunelm (Soft Furnishings) Ltd, number 2129238 — still carries SIC code 32990, other manufacturing not elsewhere classified, alongside its two retail codes. The curtain hanging in the window was cut in Leicestershire.

That manufacturing is also the one part of the shop where the 28 days does not exist. Made to Measure and Made to Order products sit outside the change-of-mind policy entirely: Dunelm will take a Made to Measure item back only if it is faulty, damaged or has not been made to the specification, and its own help pages say a Made to Measure cancellation has to be raised with your consultant before the shop closes on the day you placed the order. Made to Order furniture gets a seven-day goodwill window instead. If you are shopping for blinds rather than a bookcase, that is the number that matters.

Twenty-eight days, fourteen days, and the day the shop shuts

Three separate clocks run on a Dunelm order and they are not the same length. The longest is the voluntary one: the returns page offers returns and exchanges “within 28 days of purchase*”, with the asterisk pointing at an extended seasonal window. That window is currently open — anything bought between 12 October and 24 December 2026 can be returned until 31 January 2027, on proof of purchase. Dunelm did not have to offer any of this.

Underneath it sits the right nobody can take away. On an order placed online or by telephone the terms confirm that your rights “entitle you to cancel your order and obtain a full refund if you request one from the moment you place your order” and that the window runs “until 14 days from the day of delivery of the goods”. The same clause adds the part shoppers routinely miss: “you are responsible for the cost of returning the goods to us”. On a flat-packed wardrobe that is not a stamp.

The third clock is measured in hours. Because a Made to Measure blind is cut for one window, the returns policy says that “Each Made to Measure item or Custom Cut item are one of a kind and made just for you (and your window)” and that Dunelm “can only accept returns if they’re faulty, damaged, or they haven’t been made to the correct specification”. The help pages put the cancellation deadline at close of business on the day of the order, through the consultant who took it. Made to Order furniture is treated slightly more generously, with a seven-day goodwill change period before the piece goes into production, and the terms state plainly that “the Dunelm 28 day ‘change of mind’ returns policy will not apply to Made to Order items”.

Several categories are carved out of the 28 days whether they are bespoke or not: personalised items, cut-to-length fabric, perishable goods, ex-display and “less than perfect” products, live plants, gift cards, and paint and paint-your-own ranges. A second list turns on seals rather than customisation and covers mattresses, toppers, protectors, duvets and covers, pillows and pillowcases, bedsheets, throws and blankets, toilet seats and cosmetics — all of which have to come back unused, in the original packaging, with any hygiene or security seal intact. Once a mattress is out of its wrapper the policy is blunt: it “cannot be returned unless they are faulty”.

Getting a sofa back out of the house

The 28 days is only useful if there is a mechanism behind it, and on a large item that mechanism is a collection rather than a car boot. Dunelm states that “Large item furniture cannot be returned to any of our Dunelm Stores” and that “Home collections are available for items that weigh more than 15kg and measure over 120cm in any direction”. Those two numbers are the dividing line between a twenty-minute errand and a booked appointment, and they are published, which is more than several furniture retailers manage.

Booking one is less tidy. The policy says the fastest route to a refund is to arrange a collection, and the only two ways to do that are Live Chat or the Contact Us form — there is no self-service return portal, and no customer service email address is published anywhere on the contact page, which states that the addresses it does list are “for corporate and administrative enquiries only”. Live Chat runs Monday to Friday 8am to 8.30pm, Saturday 8am to 5pm and Sunday 9am to 4pm, with a virtual assistant outside those hours. The telephone line, 0345 165 6565, is open Monday to Saturday 8am to 5pm and Sunday 9am to 4pm.

The faulty route is the better one and it is worth knowing the difference. Within 30 days of receipt a faulty sofa, mattress or furniture item can be rejected outright for a full refund; after 30 days the remedy becomes a repair or replacement under the Consumer Rights Act. Where a faulty furniture item cannot be repaired, Dunelm says it “will arrange to collect the item free of charge” — language it does not use about a change-of-mind collection, where no charge is published either way. Repairs are assessed in the home by an independent expert, and on an order over £199 photographs are required before the visit is arranged.

Two smaller clauses are worth reading before you order. The delivery charge comes back “only if the whole order is damaged or faulty”, so one broken chair out of four leaves the £12.95 where it is. And purchases made in store, including Click and Collect orders, “can only be returned in-store” — which collides with the large-item rule, because a large item bought on Click and Collect cannot go back to the store that handed it over. Refunds take 7 to 10 working days to the original payment method, or 3 to 5 working days on a card once the goods arrive back.

What £3.95, £12.95 and £600 actually buy

Dunelm publishes its delivery ladder in full, which makes the thresholds easy to plan around. Standard UK delivery is £3.95 and free over £60, although furniture and large items are excluded from that threshold. Express is £5.95, again excluding furniture and large items, and is not offered to remote areas. Furniture and large items are a flat £12.95 with dates available Monday to Saturday, and Made to Order furniture is charged at the same £12.95 against a stated lead time of 5 to 10 weeks. Click and Collect is free, same day, and advertised “in as little as 2 hours”.

Spend £600 or more on qualifying large or furniture products and the two-person delivery service comes free. That is the line most sofa orders will clear, and it buys a service Dunelm runs itself rather than subcontracting: delivery “to your room of choice, up to the 2nd floor”, with a contact-free doorstep alternative if you prefer. The ceiling is literal — flats, apartments and townhouses are served to a maximum of the second floor “without the aid of a serviceable lift”.

The access rules are where a furniture order goes wrong, and Dunelm is clearer about them than most. An address judged unsafe may not get room-of-choice delivery, in which case the item is left on the doorstep or held until a safe location is agreed. If nobody is in, the item returns to the depot and has to be rebooked, and the page is explicit that items cannot be left in a safe place. There are no deliveries on public or bank holidays, and remote areas carry longer lead times.

Dunelm’s own satisfaction measurement says this is the part of the business it has been working on. The results for the year to 27 June 2026 report customer satisfaction up 2.4 percentage points overall, and then break it down by channel: stores up 3 points, Click and Collect up 6 points, and home delivery down 1 point. The commentary splits the difference the same way, recording that “satisfaction with our own two-person furniture delivery service improved” while “performance across third-party courier services declined during the year”. The service Dunelm staffs got better and the one it buys in got worse, and a furniture buyer over £600 is in the first group.

The measuring is the product, and Dunelm owns the factory

The made-to-measure curtains listing returned 414 results on 27 October 2026, starting at £62.10 and with other designs opening at £90, £99 and £118.80, from “hundreds of fabrics, colours and patterns”. Up to five fabric samples are free, the appointment is free, and the quote is described as “free, no-obligation”. Lead times run from “as little as 7 days”, which against the 5 to 10 weeks on Made to Order furniture is a different kind of operation.

It is a different operation because Dunelm built it. The 2001 purchase of Bellbird Ltd created the Made to Measure manufacturing centre, and the 2026 results record that “Made-to-Measure window treatments again grew strongly”, with “tech-supported growth drivers implemented in Made-to-Measure”. No figure is attached to either sentence, so the growth is a claim rather than a number, but the manufacturing is in the public registry: Dunelm (Soft Furnishings) Ltd holds SIC 32990 for manufacturing alongside 47510 for retail sale of textiles and 47910 for mail order and internet retail.

The commercially interesting part is what Dunelm charges to take the measuring risk off you. Measuring over video is free and “backed by our MeasureSafe guarantee”, which “covers any Made to Measure products measured through video assisted or in-home Check Measure” and promises that “If incorrectly measured, we’ll put it right, for free”. An in-home Check Measure by a fitter costs £40, redeemable against fitting. Fitting itself starts “from just £99 per order”, is paid when the order is placed rather than on completion, and comes with a 12-month guarantee on the workmanship; the fitter will also take the packaging away and remove the old window dressings on request.

Set that against the tolerance written into the returns policy, which allows 1cm of variance from the specification before an item counts as wrongly made. Measure the window yourself and anything inside a centimetre is your problem, on a product with no change-of-mind right. Let Dunelm measure it and MeasureSafe moves that risk across. The free video option makes that protection available at no cost, which is a genuinely good arrangement — and it is why the £40 and the £99 are better read as insurance against a non-returnable purchase than as service charges.

From a market stall in Leicester to 204 stores

Dunelm’s own history page dates the business to September 1979, when Bill Adderley and his wife Jean took on pitch D45 at Leicester market with their savings in discounted curtains, Marks and Spencer seconds and slippers, and reported a 600% profit inside a month. The first shop opened in Churchgate, Leicester in 1984; Will Adderley became managing director in 1996; the first Syston head office and warehouse followed in 1999; the website became fully shoppable in 2005; and the company listed on the London Stock Exchange in 2006. The registry dates the listing vehicle precisely — Dunelm Group plc, company 04708277, was incorporated on 23 March 2003 as Tooley Limited, renamed Dunelm Group Limited ten days later, and became a plc on 3 October 2006.

The current numbers come from the preliminary results of 8 September 2026, covering the 52 weeks to 27 June 2026. Sales of £1,825m were up 3.1% on the £1,771.0m of the year before. Gross margin rose 10 basis points to 52.5%. Profit before tax was £211m, described as in line with the prior year. Free cash flow improved to £155m from £127m. The estate stood at 204 stores across the UK and Ireland, with new openings in Wandsworth and Kingston-upon-Thames, and digital participation rose 2 points to 42% of sales.

Market share, measured on GlobalData UK data for the 12 months to June 2026, moved up 10 basis points to 7.9%. That is the figure worth holding on to, because it puts the company’s size in proportion: the largest specialist homeware retailer in the country still sells under eight pence in every pound spent in the category, which is why the terms a shopper actually gets are set by competition rather than by dominance. Shareholders took an ordinary dividend of 45.5p against 44.5p, plus a special dividend of 25.0p, for 70.5p declared in total against 79.5p the year before. Customer numbers “remained broadly stable across the year”, which is a flat line stated plainly rather than dressed up.

Spreading a furniture order over twelve months

Dunelm offers 12 months interest free on spends of £300 or more, with other finance options opening at £20, and the lender is named: the credit “is provided by the lender Creation Consumer Finance Ltd”, with Dunelm stating that it “is a credit broker, not a lender”. Klarna and PayPal sit alongside it. The eligibility conditions are published in full — applicants must be 18 or over, UK resident for three years or more, and on the 12-month offer “you’ll need an income of at least £10,000 a year”, all subject to status.

What the page does not carry is a representative APR. For a 12-month interest-free product that is less serious than it sounds, because at 0% there is nothing for an APR to measure, but the figure is the one number that lets a shopper compare the offer against a credit card if they fail the income test or want longer than a year, and it is absent. No firm reference number is given for the lender either, so a shopper who wants to check the credit provider on the Financial Conduct Authority register has to find it elsewhere.

The practical point for a furniture buyer is that the two thresholds do not line up. Free two-person delivery arrives at £600 and the interest-free credit opens at £300, so the sort of order that clears the delivery threshold comfortably clears the credit one; a single armchair or a cabinet may clear neither. Worth checking which side of £300 and £600 a basket falls on before adding a cushion to push it over.

Ten years, twenty-five years, and the five things both of them cover

Dunelm keeps a public guarantee library, which is rarer than it should be: a single index page lists the ranges that carry cover and states that “some of our own-brand buys come with a guarantee of between 2 and 25 years”, adding that the products “are tested way beyond what’s required”. The documents themselves are served as PDFs and can be read before buying. Most homeware retailers publish a badge and nothing behind it.

Read the two furniture documents side by side, though, and the numbers stop meaning what the badges suggest. The 10-year internal frame guarantee covers the Main Range Sofa Collection and Made to Order Upholstery for products bought from September 2018. The 25-year guarantee covers the Edited Life and Natural History Museum made-to-order ranges from September 2021. Both cover exactly five things, in the same order and the same words: “Loose joints”, “Timber breakage”, “Suspension spring and webbing breakage”, “Spring and webbing coming loose” and “Broken legs”. Both exclude exactly the same list, including the sentence that matters most — “It does not apply to fabric, fillings, cushions and mattresses including sofa beds” — plus “The metal mechanism for a recliner chairs or sofa beds is not included”, which the 25-year version extends with “This includes electric recliners”. Both are dated January 2023 and both carry the same typing error, promising nothing “If the timber is exposed to extreme moisture withing the home”.

So the difference between ten years and twenty-five years is the number on the cover, and neither number reaches a single component a customer touches. A sagging cushion, a flattened filling, a worn cover and a failed recliner motor are all outside both documents; a snapped leg is inside both. Dunelm is not alone in writing a frame guarantee this way, and a 25-year frame promise is still worth having — but a shopper choosing the Edited Life range over the main range on the strength of fifteen extra years is buying fifteen extra years of cover on the skeleton.

Four conditions apply across the furniture documents and all four narrow them further. Cover “only applies to the original purchaser of the item”, so it does not survive a house move with the sofa in it, and “any second-hand products that are still within their 25 Year Guarantee are excluded”. Domestic use only, within England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland and Jersey. Care instructions must have been followed, and proof of purchase is required — on a 25-year promise that means keeping a receipt until 2051. And the final remedy is not money: an independent service technician assesses the item at home, repairs it there if possible, offers a replacement or like-for-like if not, and where no replacement exists “you will be offered a credit for use with Dunelm” on a pre-loaded gift card, because “The Guarantee has no cash value and is non-transferable”. Ex-display and clearance items are outside all of it.

Two documents in the library are also not finished. The 2-year guarantee on Dunelm own-brand electrical heating and cooling products says in its “What do we offer?” section that a replacement “or credit will only be given strictly in accordance with the terms and conditions of the Lincoln Sliding Wardrobe Hardware Guarantee” — a different product’s paperwork, quoted in the operative clause of a live consumer document, checked twice on 27 October 2026 to be sure it was not a fetch artefact. And the Made to Measure guarantee page, which should cover the 414-product bespoke range, names exactly one thing: “Perfect Fit shutters - 5 Year guarantee”. There is no published guarantee length for a made-to-measure curtain, roller blind or Roman blind anywhere on it.

Who you are actually buying from, and who hears a complaint

Two companies share one brand. Dunelm Group plc (04708277) is the listed holding company and carries SIC 70100, activities of head offices. The contract is with Dunelm (Soft Furnishings) Ltd (02129238), incorporated on 8 May 1987 as Dunelm Soft-Furnishings Limited and renamed on 5 January 2005, registered at the Dunelm Store Support Centre on Watermead Business Park in Syston. Both filed their most recent accounts to 28 June 2025. Nothing about that is concealed — the terms, the contact page and the order paperwork all name the trading company and its number — but it is the entity to put on a letter, not the plc.

The terms themselves are short and mostly even-handed. Title passes on delivery “provided that we have processed and received payment in full” and risk “passes to you when the products are delivered”, which is the orthodox pairing. A pricing error is handled in the customer’s favour to the extent that the order is cancelled and refunded in full rather than repriced upwards. The limitation of liability clause is explicitly confined to non-consumer purchases and preserves liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence and for fraudulent misrepresentation. One drafting oddity: the main law clause says you “irrevocably submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of England”, while the gift card terms on the same site submit to the non-exclusive jurisdiction of the English courts. Two clauses, one website, opposite words.

The gap that matters is what happens after a complaint fails. Neither the terms and conditions nor the contact page sets out a complaints procedure, and neither names an ombudsman, an arbitration service or any alternative dispute resolution scheme. The only dispute process written into the terms concerns alleged intellectual property infringement in user-generated content. The furniture trade does have a scheme: the Furniture and Home Improvement Ombudsman has operated since 1992, describes itself as “independent, not-for-profit, government-approved”, provides “impartial Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) services” and makes its outcomes binding on members — but membership is something a business chooses, and two attempts to open the consumer-facing member directory on 27 October 2026 returned errors, so whether Dunelm is a member could not be established here. What can be said is that Dunelm points no customer towards it. A furniture dispute that Dunelm will not resolve goes to the county court, which is the ordinary position in goods retailing and still worth knowing before a £600 order.

One last thing a reader should know before browsing rather than buying. Dunelm’s category listings are built client-side, and three furniture listings — sofas and armchairs, sofas, and made-to-order sofas — all returned “0 results are available” to a plain page request on 27 October 2026, while the made-to-measure curtains listing returned its full 414 products with prices. The delivery and returns pages load without trouble. It is a browsing quirk rather than a trading problem, and it is why the prices quoted above come from the Made to Measure range and the service pages rather than from a sofa.

Where it wins

  • 28 days to change your mind, against the 14 the regulations require, and currently extended to 31 January 2027 on anything bought from 12 October 2026
  • Home collection is published with a threshold attached — over 15kg and over 120cm in any direction — rather than left to a phone call
  • A faulty furniture item that cannot be repaired is collected free of charge, and within 30 days of receipt it can be rejected outright for a full refund
  • Free two-person delivery to the room of your choice over £600, run by Dunelm itself; its own satisfaction measure says that service improved this year while bought-in couriers got worse
  • A public guarantee library of readable PDFs covering 2 to 25 years, which most homeware retailers do not publish at all
  • Measuring over video is free and carries the MeasureSafe promise, which moves the risk of a mismeasured non-returnable blind off the customer at no cost
  • Made to Measure is manufactured in-house, from the Bellbird business bought in 2001, with fitted orders turned round in as little as 7 days

Where it falls short

  • The 10-year and 25-year frame guarantees cover the same five faults and exclude the same list, and neither reaches fabric, fillings, cushions, mattresses or a recliner mechanism — so the extra fifteen years apply only to the frame
  • Both frame guarantees run to the original purchaser only, require proof of purchase, pay out in Dunelm credit rather than cash and are non-transferable, which on a 25-year promise means holding a receipt until 2051
  • The Made to Measure guarantee page covers one product — Perfect Fit shutters at 5 years — and publishes no guarantee length for a made-to-measure curtain or blind, which is most of the 414-item range
  • The 2-year guarantee on own-brand heating and cooling products binds its own remedy to the terms of the Lincoln Sliding Wardrobe Hardware Guarantee, a different product altogether, in a document dated January 2023
  • A change-of-mind collection on a large item has no published charge either way, and can only be booked through Live Chat or a web form; there is no self-service returns portal and no customer service email address
  • Neither the terms nor the contact page sets out a complaints procedure or names any ombudsman or ADR scheme, so an unresolved furniture dispute has nowhere to go but court
  • No representative APR is published alongside the 12-month interest-free credit, and the lender is named without a firm reference number
  • Both frame guarantee documents carry the same typing error, excluding timber exposed to extreme moisture “withing the home”, which suggests neither has been proofread since January 2023

Common questions

How long do I have to return Dunelm furniture?

28 days from purchase under Dunelm’s own change-of-mind policy, with a seasonal extension to 31 January 2027 for anything bought between 12 October and 24 December 2026. Separately, an order placed online or by telephone can be cancelled under the regulations up to 14 days from the day of delivery, and on that route you pay the cost of sending the goods back. Made to Measure and Made to Order items sit outside the 28 days altogether.

Will Dunelm collect a sofa I have changed my mind about?

Yes. Home collections are offered for items weighing more than 15kg and measuring over 120cm in any direction, and large furniture cannot be taken back to a store instead. The collection has to be booked through Live Chat or the Contact Us form. Dunelm publishes no charge for a change-of-mind collection, so ask for the figure when you book; it does state that a faulty furniture item that cannot be repaired is collected free of charge. Refunds take 7 to 10 working days, or 3 to 5 working days on a card once the item is back.

What does the 10-year sofa guarantee actually cover?

Five things: loose joints, timber breakage, suspension spring and webbing breakage, spring and webbing coming loose, and broken legs. It applies to the internal frame of the Main Range Sofa Collection and Made to Order Upholstery bought from September 2018. It does not apply to fabric, fillings, cushions or mattresses including sofa beds, and it excludes the metal mechanism of a recliner or sofa bed. The 25-year guarantee on the Edited Life and Natural History Museum ranges covers and excludes exactly the same items. Cover runs to the original purchaser only, needs proof of purchase, and pays in Dunelm credit where no repair or replacement is possible.

How much is Dunelm delivery on a large item?

£12.95 for furniture and large items, on dates from Monday to Saturday, and the same £12.95 on Made to Order furniture against a lead time of 5 to 10 weeks. Spend £600 or more on qualifying large items and the two-person delivery service is free, delivered to your room of choice up to the second floor without a lift. Standard delivery on smaller items is £3.95 and free over £60, express is £5.95, and Click and Collect is free on the same day in as little as two hours.

Can I cancel a Made to Measure blind order?

Only immediately. Because each item is cut for one window, Dunelm accepts a Made to Measure return only if it is faulty, damaged or has not been made to the specification, and it allows 1cm of tolerance before an item counts as wrongly made. Its help pages put the cancellation deadline at close of business on the day you ordered, through the consultant who took the order. Made to Order furniture is more forgiving, with a seven-day goodwill change period before production starts.

Does Dunelm measure the windows for me?

Yes, and the video version costs nothing. A video-assisted Check Measure is free and an in-home Check Measure by a fitter is £40, redeemable against the fitting charge. Either route brings the MeasureSafe guarantee, which covers any Made to Measure product measured that way and promises that an incorrect measurement will be put right free of charge. Fitting itself starts from £99 per order, is paid when you place the order, and carries a 12-month guarantee on the workmanship. Dunelm does not publish the MeasureSafe terms or a duration for it.

Who do I complain to if Dunelm will not help?

There is no escalation route published. Dunelm’s terms and its contact page set out no complaints procedure and name no ombudsman, arbitration service or alternative dispute resolution scheme. The customer service line is 0345 165 6565, open Monday to Saturday 8am to 5pm and Sunday 9am to 4pm. The furniture trade does have a voluntary scheme, the Furniture and Home Improvement Ombudsman, which has operated since 1992 and makes its decisions binding on members, but Dunelm does not point customers towards it and its membership could not be confirmed from the directory on 27 October 2026. Beyond Dunelm’s own process, the remaining route is the county court.

Our verdict

Dunelm is the straightforward choice in this category on the two things a furniture buyer can check before paying: 28 days to change your mind rather than 14, and a published collection threshold of 15kg and 120cm instead of a conversation. At £1,825m of sales across 204 stores on a 7.9% market share it is big enough to run its own two-person delivery and honest enough to publish that the service improved while its bought-in couriers got worse. The Made to Measure side is better still, because the free video measure and the MeasureSafe promise take the risk off a product that cannot be returned, and the 2001 Bellbird purchase means the thing arriving at your window was made by the company selling it. Where it is weaker is on the promises that run longest. The 10-year and 25-year frame guarantees are the same document with a different number on it, neither covers a cushion, a cover or a recliner motor, and both pay in Dunelm credit to the original buyer only. Treat the frame cover as what it is, note that no guarantee length is published for a made-to-measure curtain at all, and remember that a dispute Dunelm will not settle has no ombudsman behind it. Buy the sofa on the 28 days and the £600 delivery threshold, not on the twenty-five years.
