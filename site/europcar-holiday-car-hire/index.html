Europcar Car Hire Review: The Excess, The Damage Matrix and What It Costs

Rates and terms checked 4 November 2026 · Holiday Car Hire · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Europcar's standard damage and theft excess is £2,000 on a small car and £2,200 on a large one, and Premium Protection takes it to £0 for drivers aged 26 and over at up to £60.50 a day. Both figures come from Europcar's own Tariff Guide, updated 30 September 2026 under the reference 453B1-25-09-26.

That document is the reason this page can do arithmetic at all. Europcar Group UK Limited prices every standard charge a renter can meet in a dated guide, publishes a Light Damage Charges Schedule — updated 13 October 2026 — that puts a fixed price on a scratch according to its length, and defines fair wear and tear in centimetres. Across much of UK car hire the equivalent numbers arrive with the bill rather than before the booking.

Figures here were checked on 4 November 2026 against those two documents, the Terms and Conditions of Hire updated 1 June 2026 (453B-41-06-26), Your Guide to a Smooth Journey, Companies House and Europcar Mobility Group's own management report for the year to 31 December 2025. Where two Europcar pages give different answers, both are named below.

The excess, and the three ways to shrink it

Europcar sells protection in three named tiers, and its own comparison table labels them by what they leave behind: Basic is “Full excess”, Medium is “Reduced excess” and Premium is “Zero excess”. All three include damage to and theft of the car, vandalism, overhead and underbody damage, and natural disasters. Glass, lights and tyres are covered on Medium and Premium only — so on the cover that comes with the car, a kerbed alloy or a chipped windscreen is the renter's.

The Tariff Guide of 30 September 2026 sets the full excess at £2,000 on a small car and £2,200 on a large one, up to £4,000 on premium models and up to £1,900 on a van. Medium brings it down to £450 in Category A and £350 in Category B. Premium brings it to £0, and the promotional page adds the condition the Tariff Guide does not: the driver has to be 26 or over.

The daily maximums are published too. Premium Protection runs £60.50 for a small car and £73.50 for a large one in Category A, or £54.50 and £66.00 in Category B. Medium is £47.00 and £58.00 in Category A, £42.50 and £52.00 in Category B. Collision Damage Waiver bought on its own is £26.00 a day across all groups, Value Cover for glass, lights and tyres is £21.00 for cars and £22.00 for vans, and Interior Protection is £5.

Put the two sets of numbers together and the trade is clear. A week in a small car with Premium Protection at the maximum rate costs £423.50, which is about 21% of the £2,000 it removes; the cover stays cheaper than the excess until day 33. On a van the choice does not exist in the same form — Premium Protection is not sold on vans at all, and the Tariff Guide's van premium band is £850 to £1,050 rather than nothing.

A scratch is priced by the centimetre

Your Guide to a Smooth Journey (reference 669/24/01/25A) says what Europcar will not charge for. A scratch that penetrates the paint is wear and tear up to 2cm; light surface scratching up to 15cm; a dent up to 2cm, unless it breaks the paint or sits on the swage line; a chip on a windscreen or headlamp up to 1cm; and scuffing up to 2cm of combined length on the outer rim, alloy or hub cap. Damage inside the outer rim, a distorted wheel and kerbed tyres are not.

The Light Damage Charges Schedule picks up exactly where that stops. Its bands are Scratch 2–5cm, 5–15cm and over 15cm; Dent 2–5cm, over 5–15cm and over 15cm; a flat charge of 110 for “Scuffed” in every one of the six passenger categories; and, for “Heavy Scuffed”, the code DE — no price from the independent source, so a Desktop Engineer sets it. Ten columns cover Mini/Economy, Compact, Intermediate/Standard, Fullsize/Premium, Luxury and Passenger Van, then Small, Medium, Large and Special Van.

The cheapest line on the schedule is 37 and the dearest 1,741, for a dent over 15cm on a large van. The document prints neither a currency symbol nor a VAT note, which in a UK schedule means pounds but is worth asking about at the desk. Read as pounds, a 2.1cm scratch — the first chargeable millimetre past wear and tear — comes to £87 once the £50 Light Damage Administration Charge is added, which is more than a day of Premium Protection on the same car.

Two timings matter more than any of the prices. Damage you find that was not recorded at pick-up has to be reported within 24 hours of the rental start, on 0800 0280 999; a damage invoice has to be queried within 14 days of the date it was sent. And the £50 administration charge is waived altogether if the damage is paid for at the station on return, which is worth more than the cheapest line on the schedule.

Four years of tariff guides, read side by side

Europcar leaves superseded tariff guides on its own servers, which almost nobody in this sector does, and that makes the movement in its prices provable rather than asserted. The guide of 1 December 2022 (453B1/17/12/22) is still readable alongside the current one of 30 September 2026.

The excess moved first. A small car's full excess went from £1,600 to £2,000, up £400 or 25%. A large car's went from £2,100 to £2,200, up 4.8%. A van's went from £1,500 to £1,900, up 26.7%. The reduced excess did not move at all: £450 and £350 then, £450 and £350 now.

The cover moved further. Premium Protection in Category A went from £41.24 to £60.50 a day on a small car, up 46.7%, and from £47.48 to £73.50 on a large one, up 54.8%. Collision Damage Waiver on its own went from £14.28 to £26.00, up 82.1%. Roadside Assistance Plus went from up to £8.33 a day to up to £19.00, which is 128%. The young driver surcharge went from £46.41 to up to £79.00 a day, up 70.2%, and the additional driver fee from £16.66 to £19.10, up 14.6%, with its cap moving from £166.60 to £191.00 — still exactly ten days.

The administrative charges rose in step. Light damage administration went from £40 to £50 and serious damage administration from £80 to £100, both up 25%; the engineer's charge from £40 to £45; third party administration from £40 to £50; no-show from £102 to £134; late cancellation from £45 to up to £54. Three things held still: the valet charge at £45, the DVLA contact charge at £4.75, and the Europdrive pack, up only 4.9% to £312.00.

One line shows what happens when a rate is updated and its cap is not. A child seat or booster cushion was £15.47 a day in 2022 with a cap of £154.70 — ten days exactly. The daily rate is now £15.70 and the cap is still £154.70, so it bites at 9.85 days. The satellite navigation line, at £17.84 a day capped at £178.40, still comes to ten days on the nose.

The charges that land before anyone looks at the paintwork

Fuel is the largest of them. Bring a petrol, diesel or hybrid car back short and the refuelling calculation in section 18 of the terms is (pump price per litre × 2.5) multiplied by the litres needed to fill the tank — a 150% premium on the fuel itself. On a 40-litre shortfall at a pump price of £1.35 a litre, that is £135.00 against £54.00 at the forecourt. The Full Tank Option bought at the counter pre-authorises a tank and is processed in full if the car comes back short. An electric car is recharged at the weighted average rapid and ultra-rapid price index plus £0.15 per kilowatt, multiplied by what it needs to reach 80%.

Time is next. There is a 29-minute grace period after the return time; Late Check-in, bought at collection for up to £30, buys two hours. An agreed extension is £10 and an unauthorised one £25, and a car not returned with no contact for 24 hours is reported to the police as stolen. The maximum hire period is 84 days.

Then location and distance. A Location Charge of up to 20% of the rental applies at airports, major ferry terminals, railway stations and some city sites — up to £60 on a £300 week. Excess mileage is 30p a mile for cars and 24p for vans; on a hire of 28 days or more the allowance can be as low as 30 miles a day, which turns 500 extra miles into £150. Diesel vans carry an AdBlue charge of 3.5p a mile.

The loss-of-use formula deserves its own sentence, because it is where a modest repair becomes an expensive one. On serious damage Europcar charges one day for taking the car in, one day for every four hours or part of repair labour, and one day for returning it to the fleet. An eight-hour repair therefore bills four days of rental income on top of the repair. On a total loss the clock runs until the car is sold for salvage, capped at 60 days under the Damage Management Policy. On tyres, windscreens and essential items loss of use is capped at a single day.

Two more that catch people out. Pay with a card billed in another currency and there is 3.25% commission on top of the exchange rate “determined by UBS and Credit Suisse on the day of payment” — £65 on a £2,000 excess charge. And the deposit takes up to 10 working days to come back to the card, with the authorisation itself expiring 30 days after it is taken.

Seven names on one company number

The contract is with Europcar Group UK Limited, company 01089053, at 1 Great Central Square, Leicester LE1 4JS, registered under SIC 77110. It was incorporated on 1 January 1973 as Swan National Limited, and the Companies House record reads as a tour of the British rental trade: Swan National Rentals Limited from 31 December 1978, Eurodollar (UK) Limited from 25 May 1993, National Car Rental Limited from 22 January 1998, ANC Rental Corporation (UK) Limited from 25 February 2000, Vanguard Rental (UK) Limited from 15 December 2003, PremierFirst Vehicle Rental UK Limited from 4 June 2007, and the present name since 18 December 2008.

The company is active and current with its filings: full accounts for the year to 31 December 2025 were filed on 22 September 2026, the latest confirmation statement was made on 6 March 2026, and a statement of capital of £85,378,223 was registered after an allotment of shares on 28 December 2023.

Above it sits Europcar Mobility Group SA, trading since 1949 and taken private by a consortium of Volkswagen, Attestor and Pon Holdings in a deal Volkswagen announced as closed on 5 July 2022. The group's management report for 2025 gives revenue of €3,321.8m, down 2.5%; adjusted corporate EBITDA of €163.6m, up 62.3%; and a net loss of €332.0m. Behind those sit 75.2m rental days, an average fleet of 269,000 vehicles at 76.6% utilisation, revenue of €985.1 per vehicle per month and an average rental of 7.6 days — which works out at about €44 of revenue per rental day. The Dutch business was sold to Pon on 1 June 2025.

The group has also narrowed. Volkswagen's own 2022 release named five brands under Europcar Mobility Group — Europcar, Goldcar, InterRent, Ubeeqo and Buchbinder. The group's current site describes three: Europcar, Goldcar and Fox, across 130+ countries with 8,300 employees. That matters to a renter for one reason, which the next section covers.

What renters scored it, and what the regulator extracted in 2015

Which? is the only independent assessment that reaches this sector — Defaqto, Fairer Finance and the Financial Ombudsman Service do not cover car hire at all. Its survey of 3,637 panel members and members of the public, run in February and March 2026 and covering hires since February 2024, gives Europcar a customer score of 67%: joint ninth of the sixteen brands scored, ahead of Thrifty on 66%, Budget and Hertz on 65% and Avis on 64%, behind Sixt on 68% and Enterprise on 74%. AutoReisen led on 93%. The star breakdown is four for vehicle condition, four for customer service, three for value and for the collection process, and two for clarity of rental conditions. The average price reported across the survey was about £60 a day, and one in eight respondents said they were charged more than they expected.

The uncomfortable number in that table belongs to the same owner. Goldcar finished last of the sixteen on 48%, 19 points below Europcar, and Goldcar has been part of Europcar Mobility Group throughout. A reader choosing Europcar is not choosing Goldcar, but the two are run from the same place, and the gap is a fair question to ask about group standards.

The sector's regulatory history is short and specific. In October 2015 the Competition and Markets Authority and its European counterparts secured commitments from five firms — Avis-Budget, Enterprise, Europcar, Hertz and Sixt, about two thirds of EU private car rentals — on upfront price comparability, pre-booking detail on waivers and insurance, inspection transparency, and a fair chance to dispute a damage charge before being billed. The completion report of March 2019 records Europcar, including Goldcar, as having finished in June 2019.

If a dispute outlasts Europcar's own process, the escalation is the BVRLA, which is approved by government as a consumer ADR body under the Alternative Dispute Resolution for Consumer Disputes Regulations 2015 and by the Chartered Trading Standards Institute. Its code requires a member to acknowledge a complaint within five working days and resolve it within 15, with conciliation opening inside 30 days and a Conciliation Committee decision within 30 working days of referral. A customer may refer a dispute to it; a member may not refer one against a customer.

Five places Europcar's own documents disagree

The excess. Europcar's Premium Protection landing page says UK rentals include a Damage Liability Waiver with an excess “from £1,000”, and that Selection category vehicles “start from £2000 excess”. The Tariff Guide of 30 September 2026 gives £2,000 for a small car and £2,200 for a large one. The guide is dated and referenced and the promotional page is neither, so the guide is the number to plan around — but a renter who priced the risk off the promotion is £1,000 out.

The Selection range. The terms of 1 June 2026 set a minimum driver age of 25 or 30 for Selection vehicles, and the promotional page quotes a Selection excess. The current Tariff Guide has no Selection row at all; it prices premium vehicles up to £4,000 instead. A Selection renter cannot look up their own excess in the document the contract points them to.

Returning an electric car. Section 18.3 of the terms says an electric vehicle is supplied with a minimum 80% charge and must be returned with a minimum 80%. Your Guide to a Smooth Journey says it is supplied with at least 80% and returned with at least 20%. The two cannot both be right, and the gap between them is 60 percentage points of charging bought at the index price plus £0.15 a kilowatt.

How long a complaint takes. Section 23 of the terms promises a response within 10 working days. The Customer Complaints Policy on the same site promises 7 working days.

How big the network is. One Europcar UK page claims “more than 3,300 car hire locations” in “over 150 countries”. Its own UK locations page says “3835 Europcar locations in over 140 countries” and “200 Airports around the world”. The group's corporate site says 130+ countries. None of the three is dated, and the UK branch count is not published anywhere a renter can find it.

Two smaller things, both checkable in a minute. Europcar's own footer links its accessibility statement under the words “Accessibility: non compliant”. And the Damage Management Policy page, which exists to tell a renter how to report damage, carries a download link whose email address is a placeholder rather than the damage hotline printed beside it.

Where it wins

  • Prices every standard charge in a dated Tariff Guide — 30 September 2026, reference 453B1-25-09-26 — rather than leaving it to the counter
  • Publishes a fixed-price Light Damage Charges Schedule, updated 13 October 2026, so a scratch has a known price before you hire
  • Defines fair wear and tear in measurements: scratches through the paint up to 2cm, surface scratching up to 15cm, dents up to 2cm, glass chips up to 1cm and 2cm of combined wheel scuffing are not charged
  • The £50 Light Damage Administration Charge is waived if you pay at the station on return
  • Premium Protection removes the excess entirely for drivers 26 and over, and Value Cover adds glass, lights and tyres for £21.00 a day
  • Which? customer score of 67% with four stars for both vehicle condition and customer service, ahead of Avis, Hertz, Budget and Thrifty
  • Delivery and collection quoted at the same £25 each way on the sales pages and in the Tariff Guide, plus £2.00 a mile
  • 10% off all year through Privilege For You, up to 15% off UK car hire for students and recent graduates

Where it falls short

  • The standard excess on a small car has risen from £1,600 in the Tariff Guide of 1 December 2022 to £2,000 in the guide of 30 September 2026 — up 25% — while Premium Protection to remove it went from £41.24 to £60.50 a day, up 46.7%. Collision Damage Waiver alone rose 82.1%, from £14.28 to £26.00.
  • Europcar's own Premium Protection page quotes a UK excess “from £1,000” where the current Tariff Guide gives £2,000 on a small car, and the promotional page carries no date or reference to settle it.
  • The terms of 1 June 2026 and the promotional pages both refer to Selection vehicles, but the 30 September 2026 Tariff Guide has no Selection row, so a Selection renter cannot look up the excess their contract makes them liable for.
  • Two live documents give opposite instructions for returning an electric car — a minimum 80% charge in the terms, a minimum 20% in Your Guide to a Smooth Journey — and recharging is billed at the rapid-charging index plus £0.15 a kilowatt.
  • Van renters cannot buy the excess down to nothing: Premium Protection is not sold on vans, and the Tariff Guide's van premium band is £850 to £1,050 against a standard van excess of up to £1,900.
  • The refuelling calculation is the pump price per litre multiplied by 2.5, a 150% premium on fuel, which on a 40-litre shortfall at £1.35 a litre is £135.00 against £54.00 at the forecourt.
  • The Light Damage Charges Schedule prices damage to the centimetre but prints no currency symbol and no statement of whether its figures — from 37 to 1,741 — include VAT, and its “Heavy Scuffed” row has no price at all, only a referral to a Desktop Engineer.
  • No ombudsman covers car hire. The only escalation is BVRLA conciliation, which the terms mention at section 24.2, and the site's two statements of how long a complaint takes do not agree — 7 working days on the complaints policy, 10 in the terms.
  • Goldcar, part of the same group, finished last of sixteen brands in the Which? survey of February and March 2026 on 48%, 19 points below Europcar.

Common questions

What is the excess on a Europcar hire in the UK?

The Tariff Guide updated 30 September 2026 gives a maximum standard damage and theft excess of £2,000 on a small car, £2,200 on a large one, up to £4,000 on premium models and up to £1,900 on a van. The Medium package reduces it to £450 in Category A or £350 in Category B, and Premium Protection takes it to £0 for drivers aged 26 and over. Note that Europcar's Premium Protection landing page says “from £1,000” instead; the dated Tariff Guide is the document the terms point to.

How much does it cost to take the Europcar excess to zero?

Premium Protection costs up to £60.50 a day on a small car and £73.50 on a large one in Category A, or £54.50 and £66.00 in Category B. A seven-day small-car hire at the maximum rate is £423.50, about 21% of the £2,000 excess it removes. It is not available on vans, and the driver must be 26 or over.

What will Europcar charge for a small scratch?

Nothing, if it is within fair wear and tear: a scratch through the paint up to 2cm, light surface scratching up to 15cm, a dent up to 2cm away from the swage line, a glass or headlamp chip up to 1cm, or up to 2cm of combined wheel scuffing. Past that, the Light Damage Charges Schedule of 13 October 2026 charges by band — 2–5cm, 5–15cm or over 15cm — with its lowest line at 37 and its highest at 1,741. A £50 Light Damage Administration Charge is added, but waived if you pay at the station on return.

What happens if I bring a Europcar back without a full tank?

The refuelling charge is the pump price per litre multiplied by 2.5, applied to the litres needed to fill the tank, so a 40-litre shortfall at £1.35 a litre costs £135.00 rather than £54.00. The Full Tank Option bought at the counter pre-authorises a tank instead and is taken in full if the car comes back short. An electric car is supplied with at least an 80% charge and recharged at the rapid-charging price index plus £0.15 a kilowatt.

How old do I have to be to hire from Europcar in the UK?

22, with a full licence held for at least 12 months. A young driver surcharge of up to £79.00 a day applies to drivers aged 22 to 25, and the Selection range needs a driver of 25 or 30 depending on the car. The maximum age is 75 on Orkney and 79 on Jersey and Guernsey. UK licence holders must generate a DVLA Share Driving Licence passcode, valid for 21 days; if Europcar has to check another way it charges £4.75.

Who do I complain to about a Europcar damage charge?

Query the invoice within 14 days of the date it was sent, through the instructions in the damage invoice pack or on 0371 384 0235. Europcar's complaints policy promises a response within 7 working days, although its terms say 10. If the final answer does not satisfy you, the escalation named at section 24.2 of the terms is the BVRLA, a government-approved ADR body whose code requires acknowledgement within five working days and resolution within 15. There is no ombudsman for car hire.

Our verdict

Europcar is the UK hire company that will tell you in advance what it is going to charge you. The Tariff Guide of 30 September 2026 prices every standard fee, the Light Damage Charges Schedule of 13 October 2026 prices a scratch by its length, and Your Guide to a Smooth Journey says in centimetres what will be let go. That is more disclosure than most of the sector manages, and it is why the arithmetic above is possible at all. The price of that transparency is that the numbers are not small: a £2,000 excess on a small car, £60.50 a day to remove it, £87 for the smallest chargeable scratch once administration is added, and a refuelling formula at two and a half times the pump price. Four years of Europcar's own guides show the exposure up 25% and the cover up 46.7%. A renter who reads the two documents before booking, buys Premium Protection on anything but a van, brings the car back full and photographs it at both ends has priced the hire properly. One who takes the “from £1,000” on the promotional page at face value has not.
