Eurostar Tickets: The £0 Booking Fee, the 75-Minute Rule and Two Compensation Ladders

Rates and terms checked 4 November 2026 · Train Tickets · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Eurostar charges nothing at all to sell you a ticket on eurostar.com or in its own app. Book the same seat by telephone or at a station counter in Eurostar Standard or Eurostar Plus and a service fee of £12 per transaction applies, quoted as €14 or $20 in the other currencies, and waived for anyone travelling in Premier or holding a Carte Blanche or Étoile membership. Adult fares on the London routes start at £39 one way to Paris, Brussels, Lille, Amsterdam or Rotterdam, priced on the purchase of a return.

The company behind that fare carried 20 million passengers in 2025, about 500,000 more than the year before and a 3% rise, on a fleet of 51 high-speed trains serving more than 20 destinations in five countries. Revenue passed €2bn, up 1.7%, with EBITDA of €337m and gross debt down to €520m after a €130m repayment in April 2026. London to Amsterdam grew fastest at 18.3%, and the group has committed €2bn to as many as 50 double-deck Alstom Celestia trains, the first of them due in service from May 2031.

One practical thing decides whether this ticket suits a given trip, and it is not the price. A Eurostar journey from London St Pancras involves a frontier, so the published advice is to arrive 75 minutes before departure in Standard or Plus, and the gates shut 30 minutes out. In Premier those figures are 45 and 15. Nobody boards a Eurostar five minutes before the whistle, and a plan that assumes otherwise is the one thing that will cost a reader their fare.

What the fee page charges for, and what it leaves alone

Eurostar publishes its charges in one place, and the list is short. Booking on the website or in the app costs nothing on top of the fare. The £12 service fee attaches to the channel rather than the ticket, so it lands on a telephone or counter booking in Standard or Plus and never on a Premier one; the fees page says plainly that to avoid it you should “use eurostar.com or the Eurostar app to make and manage bookings”. Changing the date or time is also free in every class, in Standard and Plus up to an hour before the original departure and in Premier up to 48 hours after it, with any difference in fare payable either way. Upgrading a Standard ticket to Plus is allowed; upgrading it to Premier is not.

The money sits in the two charges a passenger only meets when something changes. A refund on a Standard or Plus ticket costs £25 per person for each leg, or £50 per person to cancel a return outright, and it is available only seven or more days ahead of the departure date. Inside that week the ticket has no refund value at all, and a ticket exchanged to a later date less than seven days out stops being refundable from that moment on. The second charge is for arriving late: miss the gates, get refused boarding and ask for a seat on a later service, and that rebooking costs £44 in Standard or Plus on top of any fare difference. Premier escapes both — it is refundable with no fee until two days after the train has gone, and it carries a same-day boarding guarantee onto any other available service, though not to a particular seat, class or meal.

Published one-way starting fares, each based on the purchase of a return, run £39 Standard, £70 Plus and £245 Premier to Paris; £39, £70 and £229 to Amsterdam and Rotterdam; and £39, £70 and £209 to Brussels and Lille. On the continental routes the entry prices are lower — Brussels to Cologne from £16.50 Standard and £78 Premier, Paris to Brussels from £25.50 and £113. Group bookings of ten or more change free up to 31 days out and recover 80% of the fare at that point, 50% between 30 and nine days, and nothing from eight days. Eurostar SNAP, the cheap last-minute fare where the passenger picks a half-day rather than a train, is “non-exchangeable, non-refundable and non-transferable”.

Seventy-five minutes at St Pancras is part of the ticket

Fares on a rail route are set by the industry and arrive identical wherever they are bought, which is why the booking fee is the only figure worth comparing between sellers. On an international service the second real variable is time, and Eurostar publishes it station by station. At London St Pancras International the advice is to arrive 75 minutes before departure in Standard or Plus and 45 in Premier, with gates closing 30 and 15 minutes out respectively, and 60 minutes with a 30-minute gate for anyone travelling with assistance. Paris Gare du Nord and Amsterdam Centraal both ask for 75 to 90 minutes in the cheaper classes. Brussels Midi/Zuid and Rotterdam Centraal want 45 to 60, and Lille Europe 60. Premier at Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Lille and Brussels drops to 30 minutes. Eurostar labels all of it guidance that can move, and tells passengers to read the time printed on the downloaded ticket.

Add the two numbers together and the cheapest London to Paris fare asks for about an hour and a quarter at the station before a two-and-a-quarter-hour train, which is the honest way to compare it against a flight rather than against a domestic service. Premier buys back half an hour of that, which on a day return is a larger part of the value than the seat or the meal.

Luggage is generous by the standards of anything that flies. Standard and Plus carry two pieces plus a small daypack or handbag, Premier three plus the small bag, and the conditions of carriage put the size limit at two large items “not exceeding 85cm in length”. Registered luggage sent separately has a maximum authorised weight of 30kg a piece, and an extra item costs €30. The conditions also price a set of behaviours a passenger would rather not test: a €200 fine for smoking, a minimum €60 for damage to a train, and €10 for every minute of delay a passenger causes with a £60 floor.

Two compensation ladders, and the one that pays more is a voucher

A late Eurostar is not handled under the arrangements a British passenger will recognise from a domestic operator. Eurostar's own passenger rights page names European Regulation No. 2021/782 on rail passengers' rights and obligations, in force for its services from 7 June 2023, and the thresholds come from there: 25% of the ticket price for a delay of 60 to 119 minutes and 50% for 120 minutes or more, measured between departure and final destination.

What the conditions of carriage add is a second, better ladder for anybody willing to be paid in Eurostar credit. Choose an e-voucher and the scale becomes 30% for 60 to 119 minutes, 60% for 120 to 179, and 75% for 180 minutes or more, against 25% and 50% in money. Eurostar says so itself, describing the voucher route as a higher level of compensation than the Regulation requires. The arithmetic is worth doing before clicking: on a £70 Plus fare, three hours late, the choice is £35 in cash or £52.50 to spend with the same company inside a year. There is one carve-out that is easy to miss — the e-voucher option is not available where the delayed journey did not pass through the Channel Tunnel, so a Brussels to Cologne passenger is on the cash ladder whether they want to be or not.

Two clocks run alongside. An e-voucher claim and a partial refund claim must both be made within three months of the delay, and a voucher once issued has to be redeemed within 12 months. Separately from money, a delay of 60 minutes or more obliges Eurostar to make arrangements for assistance, accommodation or snacks where it can, and if no re-routing has been offered within 100 minutes of the scheduled departure a passenger may arrange their own by train, coach or bus and claim back “the necessary, appropriate and reasonable costs incurred”. That self-help right is unusually clear, and it is the single most useful thing on the page for anyone stuck at Gare du Nord.

Who actually hears a complaint Eurostar has turned down

This is where an international ticket diverges furthest from a domestic one, and it is the part of the paperwork least likely to be read before buying. A complaint has to reach Eurostar within three months of the date of the incident. The company promises an acknowledgement within one month and, on complex cases, an update every 10 days. Its own charter reports that 95.22% of complaints were answered inside 30 days in the year to 31 March 2026, on an average customer wait of six days, which is a good number and one almost no transport operator publishes. Claims worth under €4 are not reimbursed at all.

If the answer is no, the escalation route is not the body a British rail passenger would expect. The conditions of carriage name Médiation SNCF Voyageurs, London TravelWatch and the European Union's online dispute resolution platform. The terms are governed by English law with the parties submitting to the jurisdiction of the English courts, which sits oddly beside a French mediator, and the practical effect is that a passenger has to work out for themselves which of three routes covers the leg they are complaining about.

The third of those routes no longer exists. The European Commission discontinued the ODR platform at ec.europa.eu/odr on 20 July 2025 under Regulation (EU) 2024/3228, replacing it with a directory of national dispute resolution bodies rather than a successor site. Eurostar's conditions still point there. That is a dead end printed in a live contract, and it has been dead for more than a year.

Eurostar grades itself, and its own page shows two different grades

Nobody independent scores a rail ticket seller. Defaqto rates insurance, Fairer Finance does not reach transport, Which? publishes nothing that ranks operators, and the regulator's complaints release gives a national rate without breaking it out per operator. What exists instead is Eurostar's own customer charter, which doubles as the service quality report the Regulation obliges it to publish, and it is unusually full.

For April 2025 to the end of March 2026 it reports 83.3% of trains arriving within 15 minutes of schedule against a 2026 target of 87%, and 73.1% arriving less than five minutes late. It logs 73,209 complaint contacts over the same twelve months, and breaks them down by theme: aftersales exchanges and refunds 32.7%, multi-issue 20.7%, train disruption 13.5% and compensation 6.5%. That the largest single category is people trying to change or cancel a ticket is the most telling figure on the page, and it sits directly on top of the £25 refund fee and the seven-day cliff.

Two things on that page do not hold up. There are two versions of the report published on it at once, and they disagree: one sets a 2026 punctuality target of 87% and reports 83.3% for April 2025 to March 2026, the other sets a 2025 target of 78% and reports 78.1% for the same stated period. Both are Eurostar's own words about the same twelve months, and nothing on the page reconciles them, so a reader cannot tell which figure is current. The second is the cleaning target: onboard cleanliness satisfaction was set at 87.5% for 2025 and came in at 87.3%, and the target published for 2026 is 85.5% — lower than the score actually achieved. A monthly cleaning audit below 95% is meanwhile described as unacceptable, so the standard the trains are measured against and the standard passengers are asked to expect are moving in opposite directions.

Points, and how much has to be spent before they are felt

Club Eurostar is free to join and earns 1.2 points for every £1 spent on train tickets, or one point per €1 or $1, which quietly makes sterling the best currency to pay in. Train and hotel packages halve that to 1.2 points per £2. Points expire 24 months after the most recent purchase, and a single point in the account keeps the balance alive.

The tiers are where the arithmetic matters, because status is earned on spend rather than journeys: 500 status points for Avantage, 2,900 for Carte Blanche and over 5,000 for Étoile. At 1.2 points a pound that is roughly £417 of tickets for the first rung, about £2,417 for the second and more than £4,167 for the third. On the cheapest £39 London to Paris fare, Avantage is nine single journeys away and Carte Blanche more than fifty. Reward points, as opposed to status points, do carry a tier bonus — none at Classique, 25% at Avantage, 50% at Carte Blanche and 75% at Étoile — and the two upper tiers are also the memberships that escape the £12 telephone and counter fee. For an occasional traveller the scheme is a rounding error on the fare; for a regular commuter to Brussels it is the reason to book direct rather than through an agent.

The company whose name is on the ticket

The seller is Eurostar International Limited, company number 02462001, registered at 6th Floor, Kings Place, 90 York Way, London N1 9AG, with VAT number GB991292001 and the SIC code 49100 for interurban passenger rail. It is older than the service it runs: incorporated on 22 January 1990 as Transeurope Track Limited, renamed European Passenger Services Limited that August, Eurostar (U.K.) Limited on 1 October 1996, and only taking its present name on 31 December 2009. Its accounts were last made up to 31 December 2025 and the company is active.

Above it sits Eurostar Group, a Brussels holding company formed to put the Eurostar and Thalys businesses under one roof, owning all the shares in Eurostar International Limited and in THI Factory SA. Its shareholders are SNCF Voyages Développement with 55.75%, the Canadian pension manager CDPQ with 19.31%, Belgium's SNCB with 18.50% and funds managed by Federated Hermes Infrastructure with 6.44%. The practical consequence for a passenger is the one visible in the complaints section: the operator is a British company, the controlling shareholder is French, the mediator named in the contract is French, and the contract itself is governed by English law.

The group's stated ambition is 30 million passengers by 2030, measured against the 19 million carried in 2019 and the 20 million carried in 2025. Reaching it depends on the Alstom order, on the new cross-channel terminal at Amsterdam that Eurostar says triples capacity on that route, and on the three announced extensions to London–Frankfurt, London–Geneva and Amsterdam or Brussels to Geneva, none of which yet has a launch date.

One depot, and what changes around 2030

Eurostar has had the Channel Tunnel passenger market to itself since 1994, and the reason is a shed in east London. International trains have to be maintained at Temple Mills, Eurostar operates it, and until there was room in it nobody else could run. The Office of Rail and Road found on 5 June 2025 that there was enough capacity there “for one additional operator or for Eurostar to grow”, and on 30 October 2025 it awarded that room to Virgin Trains Europe Holdings Limited, whose application it judged to have the strongest prospects of making best use of the space. Three rival bids were turned down: Evolyn Mobility, Gemini Trains and Trenitalia France. Eurostar's own expansion plan for the depot was considered in the same decision.

Nothing changes for a ticket bought today. Virgin still has to reach a commercial agreement with Eurostar as the depot's operator, the regulator cannot compel it to run any particular service, and its own plan points at 2030, with roughly £700m of investment and 400 jobs attached to it. The regulator was explicit that access to the depot is a first step rather than a service. What it does mean is that the only UK rail market with a single operator has a date on which that may stop being true, and a reader comparing fares in 2030 will have something to compare them with.

Where it wins

  • No booking fee of any kind on eurostar.com or in the Eurostar app — the fare is the price
  • Date and time changes are free in every class, up to an hour before departure in Standard and Plus and up to 48 hours after it in Premier
  • Premier is refundable with no fee until two days after the booked train has left, and carries a same-day boarding guarantee onto any other available service
  • The e-voucher compensation ladder pays 30%, 60% and 75%, above the 25% and 50% the Regulation requires in cash
  • If no re-routing is offered within 100 minutes of the scheduled departure, a passenger can arrange their own transport and reclaim the reasonable cost
  • Luggage is two large items up to 85cm plus a small bag in Standard and Plus, and three in Premier, with no airline-style weight fee on hand baggage
  • Complaint handling is published and measured: 95.22% answered within 30 days in the year to 31 March 2026, on an average wait of six days
  • Adult fares from £39 one way on every London route, and from £16.50 between Brussels and Cologne

Where it falls short

  • A Standard or Plus ticket has no refund value at all inside seven days of departure, and outside that window the £25-per-leg fee takes most of the cheapest fare — a £39 single cancelled eight days out returns £14
  • The conditions of carriage still send a dissatisfied passenger to the European Union's online dispute resolution platform at ec.europa.eu/odr, which the European Commission discontinued on 20 July 2025 under Regulation (EU) 2024/3228
  • Two versions of the service quality report sit on the same customer charter page, one reporting 83.3% of trains within 15 minutes for April 2025 to March 2026 and the other 78.1% for the same stated period, with nothing reconciling them
  • The onboard cleanliness satisfaction target published for 2026, 85.5%, is lower than the 87.3% actually scored at the end of 2025
  • Arriving after the gates close costs £44 in Standard or Plus on top of any fare difference, and the gates shut 30 minutes before departure at St Pancras
  • No independent body rates the service — Defaqto, Which? and Fairer Finance publish nothing here, and no per-operator complaints rate exists for the route
  • The fares page gives the child discount for ages four to eleven as approximately 30% in one section and approximately 50% in another, and neither figure applies in Premier

Common questions

Does Eurostar charge a booking fee?

Not online. Booking on eurostar.com or in the Eurostar app carries no booking or service fee at all, so the fare you see is the price you pay. A booking made by telephone or at a station counter in Eurostar Standard or Eurostar Plus attracts a service fee of £12 per transaction, quoted as €14 or $20 elsewhere. Premier passengers and Club Eurostar members at Carte Blanche or Étoile level are not charged it.

Can I get a refund on a Eurostar ticket?

On Standard and Plus, only if you ask seven or more days before the departure date, and it costs £25 per person for each leg or £50 per person to cancel a full return. Inside seven days the ticket is not refundable, and exchanging a ticket to a later date less than seven days out makes it non-refundable from then on. Premier tickets are refundable with no fee until 48 hours after the original departure time. Eurostar SNAP fares cannot be refunded, exchanged or transferred at all.

How early do I need to get to the station?

At London St Pancras International, 75 minutes before departure in Standard or Plus and 45 minutes in Premier, with the gates closing 30 and 15 minutes before the train leaves. Paris Gare du Nord and Amsterdam Centraal ask for 75 to 90 minutes in the cheaper classes, Brussels Midi/Zuid and Rotterdam Centraal 45 to 60, and Lille Europe 60. Passengers travelling with assistance are asked for 60 minutes everywhere. Eurostar treats these as guidance and prints the applicable time on the downloaded ticket.

What does Eurostar pay if the train is late?

Compensation starts at a 60-minute delay between departure and final destination. Taken in money it is 25% of the fare for 60 to 119 minutes and 50% from 120 minutes. Taken as an e-voucher it rises to 30%, then 60% for 120 to 179 minutes, and 75% at three hours or more, though the voucher option is unavailable if the delayed journey did not pass through the Channel Tunnel. Claims must be made within three months, and a voucher has to be spent within 12 months of issue.

Who do I complain to if Eurostar says no?

Eurostar first, within three months of the incident. Beyond that its conditions of carriage name Médiation SNCF Voyageurs and London TravelWatch, and also the European Union's online dispute resolution platform — which the European Commission shut down on 20 July 2025, so that third route is no longer available. The Rail Ombudsman, which handles domestic British operators, is not named in Eurostar's terms. Claims worth under €4 are not reimbursed.

How much luggage can I take?

Two pieces plus a small daypack or handbag in Standard and Plus, and three pieces plus the small bag in Premier. The conditions of carriage limit a large item to 85cm in length. Luggage registered to travel separately is capped at 30kg per piece, and an additional item beyond the allowance costs €30 on the continental routes.

Our verdict

On the one figure that genuinely varies between sellers of a rail ticket, Eurostar is as cheap as it gets: £0 to book online, with the £12 charge reserved for the telephone and the ticket window. Free date changes in every class and a Premier fare that stays refundable two days after the train has gone are better terms than most airlines on the same city pairs. The costs are elsewhere — in the seven-day refund cliff and the £25 fee that renders a £39 fare close to worthless once cancelled, in the £44 charge for missing a gate that shuts half an hour before departure, and in the 75 minutes the cheaper classes are asked to spend at St Pancras. The compensation regime is more generous than a domestic operator's if a voucher is acceptable, and the complaints machinery is unusually well documented, but it points in part at a dispute service that closed in July 2025 and at a French mediator under an English-law contract. Book direct, read the arrival time on the ticket rather than the one in your head, and treat anything cheaper than Premier as a fare you cannot get back.
