Expedia Hotels

Rates and terms checked 8 November 2026 · Hotels · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Expedia's own London hotels page opens at £59 a night, including taxes and fees: one night at the Otherwander Soho Pod Hotel, an adults-only property, for 9 to 10 November 2026, read on 8 November 2026. That matters more than it sounds, because the two largest hotel platforms on this site publish no city lead-in price at all. Expedia prints a number before you type a date.

The business launched in 1996 as a division of Microsoft, floated in 1999 and was spun out as Expedia, Inc. in 2005, becoming Expedia Group in 2018. In the three months to 30 September 2026 it put $34,008 million of travel through its platforms and booked 115.7 million room nights. Its London listings run to 230 five-star hotels, 2,760 four-star, 6,429 three-star and 4,726 two-star.

One structural fact decides how much of this page applies to you, and it is worth knowing before you click. Expedia sells two legally different products from the same search box. A room on its own is a hotel booking. A room bought with a flight is a package under the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018, which brings Air Travel Organiser's Licence number 5788 with it and makes Expedia answerable for the whole holiday rather than the room. The £59 room carries neither.

The £59 room, and what that price already includes

Read on 8 November 2026, the London hotels page carried the line “Find hotels in London from £59” and backed it with a priced list rather than a search box. The cheapest was the Otherwander Soho Pod Hotel at £59 for the night of 9 to 10 November. Above it, on the same page and the same dates: a&o London Docklands Riverside at £62, Easyhotel London Victoria at £74, Zedwell Piccadilly Circus at £104, Point A London Kings Cross St Pancras at £108, the Royal National Hotel at £110, Wombat's City Hostel at £115, the Strand Palace at £128, The Tower Hotel by Thistle at £139, the Riu Plaza London Victoria at £142, Park Plaza London Riverbank at £160, Park Plaza Westminster Bridge at £200 and The Clermont London, Victoria at £216.

Every one of those figures was shown including taxes and fees. That is the compliance point behind the pricing, and it is a recent one: the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 brought in a prohibition on drip pricing in April 2025, the practice of advertising one figure and adding charges as a shopper moves through the checkout. The same Act lets the Competition and Markets Authority decide for itself whether consumer law has been broken rather than litigating through the courts, with fines of up to 10% of global turnover for an infringement, 5% for breaching undertakings and 1% for failing to provide information. In its own account of the first year, published on 17 April 2026, the CMA recorded investigations into 14 businesses with 2 settled, £760,000 ordered in consumer refunds, £4.7 million of fines, 157 advisory and warning letters and 46 information notices. The drip-pricing case was the AA Driving School and BSM, which admitted liability and was fined £4.2 million.

Cancellation is the other thing the listing page sells on, and Expedia is looser about it than about price. The page offers a fully refundable filter and the promise to “Book hotels with free cancellation”, while its own frequently asked questions say that “Most bookings are refundable if canceled within 24-48 hours of the scheduled arrival”. Most, and a window with two different numbers in it, is a policy to check per room rather than a feature of the site.

Add a flight and the booking changes legal category

A hotel room bought by itself from Expedia is a travel service. A hotel room bought with a flight is a package, and the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 then govern it. Expedia says so plainly in its booking conditions, last revised on 26 October 2026: “We provide full financial protection under the UK ATOL scheme for our flight-inclusive Packages”, under Air Travel Organiser's Licence number 5788. Its own help centre names that licence as Expedia, Inc.'s.

The test it gives the reader is refreshingly concrete. “If you do not receive an ATOL Certificate then the booking will not be ATOL protected”, and if a certificate arrives but some parts of the trip are missing from it, “those parts will not be ATOL protected”. A package without a flight in it, and money paid directly to Expedia for a linked travel arrangement, are covered a different way: insurance against Expedia's insolvency, “arranged by International Passenger Protection Limited” and underwritten by Liberty Mutual Insurance Europe SE.

What the Regulations add beyond insolvency is the part most readers never see. On a package the organiser carries the price as well as the performance. Expedia may pass on a price increase only by notice on a durable medium “at the latest 20 days before the start of the package”, and where an increase exceeds 8% of the total price the traveller gains the rights set out in regulation 11. If the organiser cannot get travellers home because of unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances, it must pay for necessary accommodation “for a period not exceeding 3 nights per traveller” — a limit that does not apply at all to travellers with reduced mobility, pregnant travellers, unaccompanied minors or anyone needing specific medical assistance, provided Expedia was told at least 48 hours in advance.

The organiser named for all of this is Travelscape, LLC, trading as Expedia Travel, at 5000 W. Kearney Street, Springfield, Missouri 65803. Two Expedia pages describe the money side of bundling differently: the holidays landing page states that “You can save up to 10% when you bundle your flight + hotel, flight + car or hotel + car together”, while the package savings page quantifies nothing at all, saying only that savings are “based on the price of the hotel + flight booked together” against the same components booked separately, with prices drawn from “the lowest price found within the past 48 hours” and quoted per person per stay on the cheapest return flights with two people sharing the cheapest double room. One figure is a ceiling, the other is absent.

Three times the price is the ceiling, and it is the lowest the law allows

Expedia's package clause reads: “Expedia Travel's liability to you in connection with your Package will be limited to a maximum of three times the cost of your Package”, except in cases involving death, personal injury or damage caused intentionally or with negligence. Set against the hotel platforms this site has already looked at, that is a large number. One caps its liability at the value of a booking fee it never sizes; another at the amount the customer paid.

It is also, to the character, the floor the law sets. Regulation 16(6) of the 2018 Regulations allows a package travel contract to limit the organiser's compensation provided the limit does not apply to “personal injury or damage caused intentionally or with negligence” and does not “amount to less than 3 times the total price of the package”. Expedia has written the statutory minimum into its contract. That is lawful and it is more than its competitors offer, and it is not a concession.

Three further limits sit alongside it and all three are in the Regulations rather than in Expedia's drafting. The organiser escapes compensation where it proves the failure is attributable to the traveller, to an unconnected third party whose act was unforeseeable or unavoidable, or to unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances. Where an international convention caps what an airline or a carrier would owe, the same cap passes through to the organiser. And none of it reaches a hotel-only booking: outside a package, Expedia's terms limit it to direct damages that were reasonably foreseeable, actually suffered and directly attributable to its actions, with no monetary figure stated anywhere. On the £59 room there is no number to point at, only the undertaking that “we do not limit or exclude our liability for fraud or personal injury or death (resulting from our negligence)”.

What One Key pays back, and on which part of the trip

One Key is the loyalty scheme Expedia advertises hardest and the only part of the proposition with a published price list. Under terms effective 8 April 2026, an eligible Expedia booking earns “0.2% in OneKeyCash per £1 spent (excluding taxes and fees)” on flights and 2% on hotels, short-term rentals, activities, packages and car hire. A pound of OneKeyCash buys a pound of discount, so the arithmetic is unusually easy to do: a £300 flight returns about 60p, and the same £300 spent on a room returns about £6 — except that the earn base strips out taxes and fees while the price Expedia shows you includes them, so both figures come in below 2% and 0.2% of the number on the screen.

Status is counted in Trip Elements, and a Trip Element needs an eligible travel component of £20 or more, excluding taxes and fees. Five of them in a calendar year makes Silver, fifteen makes Gold and thirty makes Platinum. Tier is worth something specific: on what Expedia calls Preferred Inventory, Blue members earn nothing at all, Silver 3%, Gold 4% and Platinum 6%. Status lasts for the remainder of the membership year in which it was earned, the next full calendar year, and through 28 February of the year after that. Base OneKeyCash survives as long as a member makes a booking or a redemption at least once every 18 months.

The list of things OneKeyCash cannot pay for is longer than the list of ways to earn it. It “cannot be used on any Pay Later bookings, or when using buy now pay later payment methods”, nor on previously paid bookings, packages or insurance, nor on resort fees, security fees, deposits, fuel charges, cancellation fees, change fees, seat choice, baggage or flight changes. It “cannot be used or refunded for cash in any currency and has no cash value”. A reader who follows the bundling advice in the section above therefore earns 2% on a package and cannot spend a penny of it on the next one.

Two further benefits carry conditions worth reading before relying on them. Price Drop Protection checks the price of a booked flight every 24 hours until departure and pays the difference as OneKeyCash once the trip is complete, but it is limited to Gold and Platinum members signed in to the UK Expedia app, applies only to flights “expressly identified as including Price Drop Protection prior to booking”, excludes card fees, checked baggage and seat selection from the comparison, excludes flight and hotel combinations and Member Prices entirely, and is subject to a cap and frequency limits that are shown before booking but published nowhere. Member Prices offer “instant discounts of 10% or more on over 500,000 stays worldwide” to One Key members and app users who are logged in, excluding taxes and fees, subject to blackout periods and possible minimum stays. The same page's own description gives the figure as over 100,000 hotels worldwide, and nothing reconciles the two counts.

Expedia says in writing that paid placement moves properties up the list

Most platforms in this trade either stay silent on ranking or bury it. Expedia publishes a sort order page and states the awkward part of it in one sentence: for accommodation, “we also consider how much we're paid when you book and stay at a property”, and “we may feature properties that offer more commission or compensation higher in the search results listing”. The qualifier is that compensation weighs in “when determining the relative order of properties with similar offers in our search results”, and the same page draws a line the reader can use: “We do not show airlines that offer commission or compensation higher in the search results listing.” Suppliers may also buy advertising, which “will be marked clearly as an “Ad” (Advertisement) or similar”.

The scale of the lever is published too, on the advertising side of the business rather than the consumer side. Accelerator is described as a pay-per-stay tool where a property can “set your additional margin to between 1%-25%” and is “only charged when a traveler stays at your property”, with no budget required. Expedia's own incrementality data from 2022 claims properties running Accelerator alongside its TravelAds product see nearly 25% higher gross booking value and nearly 30% higher net room nights than TravelAds alone. A hotel can therefore hand over up to a quarter more margin to climb the page, and nothing on the listing you are reading says whether this one has.

The other factors Expedia lists are the ordinary ones, and there are a lot of them: location relative to the search, price and offers measured against other listings on the site and against other sites, price history, guest review scores, past bookings, and for rentals the host's own metrics, with cleanliness, staff and service, property condition, availability and cancellation and refund rates folded in. Ordering is also the question the regulator came to this sector about. The CMA opened its consumer law investigation into hotel booking sites on 27 October 2017, began enforcement action on 28 June 2018, and published undertakings on 6 February 2019 from six sites covering promotions, scarcity messaging, hidden charges and the disclosure of how commission affects search results. Expedia, ebookers and Hotels.com appear in a single document among them, alongside Agoda Company Pte. Ltd, two Booking.com entities and trivago N.V.; Expedia Group owns Hotels.com and ebookers, and holds a stake in trivago. The CMA published compliance principles on 26 February 2019, required the changes in place by 1 September 2019, and on 13 September 2019 signed up 25 further booking sites, meta-search engines, hotel chains and rental platforms to the same principles. The case is now closed.

Who takes your money, and whose courts hear the argument

Three companies stand behind a British Expedia booking and none of them is obvious from the website. The terms are written by Expedia, Inc., a Washington corporation at 1111 Expedia Group Way W, Seattle, WA 98119. Packages are organised by Travelscape, LLC in Springfield, Missouri. And the company that takes payment on a United Kingdom booking is Travel Partner Exchange UK Limited, which the terms name without a company number or an address.

Companies House fills that in. Travel Partner Exchange UK Limited is company 13444092, incorporated on 8 June 2021 with a statement of capital of USD 25,000, registered at 6th Floor One London Wall, London EC2Y 5EB, under SIC code 82990 for other business support service activities. It has filed accounts for a small company for every period since the one ended 31 December 2021, the most recent made up to 31 December 2025 and filed on 20 July 2026. Small-company accounts carry no profit and loss account, so there is no published turnover for the entity that handles British payments, and the terms add that where one of these companies takes the money, “the law governing that payment transaction will be the laws of the location of such company”.

The headline jurisdiction, though, is the best of any hotel platform reviewed on this site. “These Terms are governed by the laws of England and Wales”, and “You consent to the exclusive jurisdiction and venue of the English courts in all disputes”, with an express option for residents of Scotland or Northern Ireland to choose their own law and courts instead. Two competitors in the same category put the customer's contract under Singapore law, and a third routes disputes to individual arbitration in New York.

What is missing is the step between a complaint and a court. Expedia asks customers to “raise any complaints within 30 days after travel”, gives a UK number on 020 3788 0445 at local rate, and takes written complaints at Expedia Customer Relations, P.O Box 70720, London EC1P 1GW with a copy to its support address. No ombudsman is named, no alternative dispute resolution scheme is named, and no ABTA membership is claimed anywhere in the booking conditions. For insolvency the terms point at the Civil Aviation Authority and International Passenger Protection, and for delayed and cancelled flights at Regulation (EC) 261/2004. The ATOL number itself could not be independently confirmed for this review: the CAA's own ATOL holder search is built in JavaScript and returns nothing to a plain fetch, so 5788 is Expedia's published figure and a reader who wants it verified should run that search in a browser.

The scale behind the search box, and where it is growing

Expedia Group files quarterly with the Securities and Exchange Commission, which makes the money question answerable from audited numbers rather than marketing copy. In the three months to 30 September 2026 gross bookings were $34,008 million, up 11% on the same quarter of 2025, of which lodging accounted for $24,875 million. Revenue was $4,938 million, up 12%. Operating income rose 28% to $1,326 million, net income attributable to the group was $1,030 million, and adjusted EBITDA was $1,689 million at a 34.2% margin. Booked room nights were 115.7 million against 108.2 million. Free cash flow for the nine months was $3,627 million, the quarterly dividend is $0.48 a share, and about 2 million shares were bought back for $576 million in the quarter. Full-year guidance was raised to between $131.4 billion and $131.9 billion of gross bookings and between $16.37 billion and $16.43 billion of revenue.

The interesting line is the split. Consumer revenue was $3,065 million, up 6%. The business-to-business arm, which supplies hotel inventory to other companies' websites and apps, was $1,708 million, up 23% — growing close to four times as fast as the consumer brand. The trivago segment added $165 million, up 20%. Direct selling and marketing cost $2,184 million in the quarter, equal to 6.4% of gross bookings, and the consumer half of that spend was flat year on year at $1,028 million while the business-to-business half rose 23% to $1,048 million. A shopper looking at a room on Expedia should read that as the trade moving quietly behind the scenes: the same inventory increasingly reaches travellers through somebody else's booking form.

Expedia's own published scale figures, drawn from internal data dated January 2026, are 70 or more countries and destinations, more than 3.5 million stay options and more than 200,000 activities and experiences. It gives no total property count for London, which is why the star-rating breakdown in the first section is the closest thing to one.

Where it wins

  • Publishes a London lead-in price of £59 a night including taxes and fees, where two larger rivals publish no city starting price at all
  • Flight-inclusive packages are covered by ATOL 5788 and come with an ATOL Certificate listing exactly which parts are protected
  • Packages without a flight, and money paid for linked travel arrangements, are insured against Expedia's insolvency through International Passenger Protection Limited, underwritten by Liberty Mutual Insurance Europe SE
  • On a package Expedia Travel accepts liability up to three times the cost of the holiday, which is far above the caps the comparable platforms set
  • Terms are governed by the laws of England and Wales with the exclusive jurisdiction of the English courts, and Scottish and Northern Irish residents may choose their own
  • A UK telephone number on 020 3788 0445 and a London postal address for complaints
  • States in its own legal pages that compensation affects the order of accommodation results, and that it does not do the same for flights
  • Advertised placements are required to be marked clearly as an advertisement
  • A fully refundable filter and free cancellation on a large part of the London list
  • Price increases on a package need 20 days' notice on a durable medium, and an increase above 8% of the total price triggers the traveller's statutory rights

Where it falls short

  • The £59 lead-in price is one night, one date and one adults-only property. Expedia's own page title moved between £40, £57, £59 and £69 across repeated readings, so the figure is a live quote rather than a standing rate, and there is no published floor price for London that holds from one day to the next.
  • A hotel-only booking has no ATOL protection and no insolvency cover. The insurance arranged through International Passenger Protection reaches non-flight packages and money paid directly to Expedia on a linked travel arrangement, which leaves the single room, the product most readers come for, outside both schemes.
  • The three-times-the-package cap is the statutory minimum. Regulation 16(6)(b) forbids a contractual limit amounting to less than three times the total price, so Expedia has adopted the lowest figure the Regulations permit and nothing more.
  • Outside a package there is no monetary cap at all, only a promise of direct damages that were reasonably foreseeable, actually suffered and directly attributable to Expedia's actions. On the £59 room there is no number a reader can plan around.
  • No ombudsman and no alternative dispute resolution scheme is named anywhere in the booking conditions, and no ABTA membership is claimed. The route is an internal complaint raised within 30 days of travel, and then the English courts.
  • Accelerator lets a property add between 1% and 25% to the margin it pays in order to be lifted up the results, and nothing on an individual listing discloses whether that property is paying. Expedia's sort order page states that compensation is a factor; the search result itself does not.
  • Price Drop Protection is restricted to Gold and Platinum members signed in to the UK app, pays in OneKeyCash rather than cash, pays only after the trip is complete, and specifically excludes flight and hotel combinations and Member Prices, which are the two offers Expedia promotes hardest. Its cap and frequency limits are shown at booking and published nowhere.
  • Flights earn 0.2% in OneKeyCash against 2% on rooms, a tenth of the rate on what is usually the largest line of the bill, and OneKeyCash cannot be spent on a package at all even though a package earns it.
  • Member Prices are advertised as 10% or more on over 500,000 stays in the page heading and on over 100,000 hotels worldwide in the same page's own description, with nothing reconciling the two. The discount also excludes taxes and fees, while the prices shown on the listing pages include them.
  • The bundle saving is quantified on one Expedia page as up to 10% and left entirely unquantified on another, so a reader cannot tell what adding a flight is actually worth before running two searches.
  • Travel Partner Exchange UK Limited, which takes payment on United Kingdom bookings, files accounts for a small company and has done so for every period since 2021. There is no published turnover, profit or staff figure for the entity handling British money.
  • The company that organises a package is a Missouri limited liability company, and the terms provide that the law of a payment transaction follows the location of whichever company took the payment, which cuts across the English law clause that governs everything else.
  • The ATOL number cannot be checked from the regulator's side without a browser. The CAA's ATOL holder search is JavaScript-rendered and returns nothing to a plain fetch, so 5788 rests on Expedia's own disclosure.
  • Expedia's frequently asked questions describe most bookings as refundable if cancelled within 24 to 48 hours of arrival, a two-figure window on a page that elsewhere advertises free cancellation outright.

Common questions

Is an Expedia hotel booking ATOL protected?

No. ATOL 5788 covers flight-inclusive packages booked on Expedia.co.uk, and Expedia's own test is the certificate: “If you do not receive an ATOL Certificate then the booking will not be ATOL protected”, and where a certificate omits part of the trip, that part is not protected either. A room booked on its own is a travel service rather than a package. Packages without a flight, and money paid directly to Expedia for a linked travel arrangement, are instead insured against Expedia's insolvency through International Passenger Protection Limited, underwritten by Liberty Mutual Insurance Europe SE.

What does One Key actually pay back on a hotel booking?

2% in OneKeyCash per £1 spent on an eligible hotel, excluding taxes and fees, and 0.2% on a flight. One pound of OneKeyCash gives a pound off a later booking. On Preferred Inventory the rate depends on status: Blue earns nothing, Silver 3%, Gold 4% and Platinum 6%, with Silver reached at 5 Trip Elements in a calendar year, Gold at 15 and Platinum at 30, and a Trip Element requiring an eligible travel component of £20 or more. OneKeyCash has no cash value, cannot be spent on a package or on insurance, and expires unless the member books or redeems at least once every 18 months.

Can I cancel an Expedia hotel room for free?

Sometimes, and it depends on the room rather than on Expedia. The London listing page offers a fully refundable filter and advertises free cancellation, while Expedia's own frequently asked questions say most bookings are refundable if cancelled within 24 to 48 hours of the scheduled arrival. The terms are set by the property and appear in the rules and restrictions at booking, so the cancellation line is a per-room fact. On a package, Expedia warns that some elements, a flight in particular, may carry a 100% change or cancellation charge.

Who do I complain to if an Expedia booking goes wrong?

Expedia first, and then the courts. The terms ask customers to raise complaints within 30 days after travel, give a UK number on 020 3788 0445 at local rate and a postal address at Expedia Customer Relations, P.O Box 70720, London EC1P 1GW. No ombudsman and no alternative dispute resolution scheme is named, and the booking conditions claim no ABTA membership. The terms are governed by the laws of England and Wales with the exclusive jurisdiction of the English courts, and residents of Scotland or Northern Ireland may choose their own law and courts.

Does paying Expedia more move a hotel up the search results?

Expedia says it can. Its sort order page states that for accommodation “we also consider how much we're paid when you book and stay at a property”, and that it “may feature properties that offer more commission or compensation higher in the search results listing”, weighing compensation between properties with similar offers. Its advertising arm sells the mechanism openly: Accelerator lets a property set an additional margin of between 1% and 25% and charges only when a traveller stays. The same page states that Expedia does not rank airlines by the commission they pay. The disclosure of how commission affects ordering was one of the points covered by the undertakings six booking sites, Expedia among them, gave the Competition and Markets Authority on 6 February 2019.

How big is Expedia?

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Expedia Group reported gross bookings of $34,008 million, revenue of $4,938 million and 115.7 million booked room nights, with lodging accounting for $24,875 million of bookings. Consumer revenue of $3,065 million grew 6% while the business-to-business arm grew 23% to $1,708 million. Direct selling and marketing took $2,184 million, or 6.4% of gross bookings. The group publishes internal figures of more than 3.5 million stay options across 70 or more countries and destinations, dated January 2026.

Our verdict

Expedia is the rare hotel platform that prints a starting price, writes its ranking rules down, and submits to English law and the English courts, and on a flight-inclusive holiday it is the only one of its peers that brings an ATOL licence and a liability figure with three digits of headroom. The catch is that none of that follows the booking most people make. A £59 room in Soho is a travel service with no licence behind it, no insolvency cover and no cap on anything, and the £1 or so of OneKeyCash it returns is not the reason to be there. If the trip needs a flight anyway, buying it in the same basket moves the whole thing inside the Package Travel Regulations and is the single most valuable thing this site does for a reader. If it does not, Expedia is a well-priced, transparently ranked shop window where you should still read the cancellation policy on the room rather than the promise on the page.
