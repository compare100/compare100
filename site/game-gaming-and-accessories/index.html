GAME Gaming and Accessories: The Prices, and Who Sells Them to You Now

Rates and terms checked 14 November 2026 · Gaming and Accessories · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

GAME's own category pages were printing 783 products under Games on 14 November 2026, 81 controllers, 670 discounted lines in the gaming sale and 183 titles in video games clearance starting at £7.00 for EA FIFA 23. The cheapest wired Switch controller was £10.00 and the cheapest digital code £8.39. Every figure here was read off game.co.uk or a public register on that date.

That alone is worth saying, because a lot of big retailers no longer print a price a reader can find. GAME does: each tile carries the current price, most carry the previous price, and many carry the discount as a percentage — £12.00 against £24.99 on a PowerA Switch controller, £17.99 against £29.99 on a Turtle Beach headset, £7.00 against £64.99 on a four-year-old football game. There is an Age Rating filter, a price-band filter, 379 collection points across the United Kingdom and Ireland, and an interest-free way to spread a bigger purchase over three months.

Two things about the shop have changed, and they belong up here rather than further down. The company whose name is on the website is not the company selling you the goods. GAME Retail Limited, company 07837246, has been in administration since 24 February 2026, with Lauren Wentworth and James Saunders of Kr8 Advisory Limited appointed. The seller named in game.co.uk's own footer and terms is Frasers Group Trading Limited, company 03406347, FRN 980401, which was called SportsDirect.com Retail Limited until 30 March 2026.

And the shops themselves are gone as shops. Frasers Group's own half-year results announcement for the 26 weeks to 26 October 2025 describes the “replacement of standalone Game stores with Game concessions situated inside larger Sports Direct stores”, and its full-year announcement for the 52 weeks to 26 April 2026 puts a 4.7% fall in UK Sports revenue down to “planned declines in Game UK standalone stores and Studio Retail”. The 379 entries in GAME's store finder are counters inside other people's shops. If you were planning to drive to a GAME branch, or to take a bag of discs in and trade them, both of those plans need rethinking before you click anything.

379 entries in the store finder, and what they are counters inside

The A to Z directory at game.co.uk/stores/all listed 379 entries on 14 November 2026 — 378 unique, because “Irvine Riverway” appears twice — spread across the United Kingdom and Ireland. Read by the suffix on each name, 55 end in SD, 29 in HE, 17 in DW, 12 in RP, 11 in FRA and 5 in HOF, one reads “Bray SD IRL”, and 249 carry no suffix at all.

Frasers Group's own documents explain what those entries are. The half-year announcement for the 26 weeks to 26 October 2025 states the “replacement of standalone Game stores with Game concessions situated inside larger Sports Direct stores”. The full-year announcement for the 52 weeks to 26 April 2026 records UK Sports revenue down 4.7%, “largely driven by planned declines in Game UK standalone stores and Studio Retail”, while UK Sports store numbers across the division rose from 785 to 794 — a shrinking GAME inside a growing estate. The same document notes the disposal of the Game Spain business on 19 March 2025.

So the directory is a list of host shops, and that is the gap in it: it does not say which fascia each counter sits in. “Oxford Street”, “Aberystwyth” and “Leeds Centre” carry no tag of any kind, so a reader cannot tell from GAME's own store finder whether the nearest counter is inside a Sports Direct, a House of Fraser or a Frasers. The list is also unpolished enough to be worth a sentence: “Bournemouth - Ringwwod Road”, “Northhampton - Kettering”, “Carrick on ShannonHE” and “ChristChurch SD” are all transcribed as they appear. Before setting out, ring the number for the location rather than trusting the entry.

Two companies, five weeks apart

GAME RETAIL LIMITED is company 07837246, incorporated on 7 November 2011 as MACSCO 36 LIMITED, renamed BAKER ACQUISITIONS LIMITED on 30 March 2012 and GAME RETAIL LIMITED on 26 April 2012, with SIC code 47540 for the retail of electrical household appliances. Its administration began on 24 February 2026. The filing history runs: AM01, appointment of an administrator, filed 9 March 2026; a change of registered office the same day, from Unit A, Brook Park East, Shirebrook to c/o Kr8 Advisory Limited in Manchester; AM03, a 44-page statement of administrator's proposal, on 28 April 2026; AM06, notice of deemed approval of proposals, on 1 June 2026; and AM10, a 27-page administrator's progress report, on 5 October 2026. Its last accounts were made up to 27 April 2024. The return for the year to 30 April 2025 was due by 30 April 2026 and the register marks it overdue.

FRASERS GROUP TRADING LIMITED is company 03406347, active, incorporated on 21 July 1997 as SPORTS SOCCER LIMITED. It became SPORTS WORLD INTERNATIONAL LIMITED on 30 September 2003, SPORTSDIRECT.COM RETAIL LIMITED on 28 April 2008, and FRASERS GROUP TRADING LIMITED on 30 March 2026 — five weeks after the administration started. Its SIC code is 47789, “Other retail sale of new goods in specialised stores”, rather than GAME Retail's 47540. Its last accounts were made up to 27 April 2025 and the next are due by 31 January 2027.

This matters for one practical reason. If something goes wrong with an order, the question of which company you contracted with decides where a faulty-goods claim, a chargeback or a card-scheme dispute is aimed, and both names are visible on GAME's own site — one in the footer and terms as the seller, the other as the brand on every page. A purchase from an active company with current accounts and a purchase from a company in administration are not the same transaction, and nothing on game.co.uk sets out the relationship between the two.

One smaller detail from the same register: FRASERS GROUP CREDIT BROKING LIMITED, company 13191369, was dissolved on 7 July 2026. The group no longer has a dedicated credit-broking company; the trading company you buy from does the broking itself, which is exactly what the Frasers Plus wording says.

The help centre keeps a farewell page for three things

GAME's help centre still carries the closure notice for its loyalty scheme, and it is unusually precise. “GAME REWARD and GAME ELITE shall be closing on 31 July 2024”, ending at 23:59 that day. Points could be earned “but only until 23.59 on 15 July 2024 after which date purchases will not earn you any GAME Reward points”. Members were promised a final balance email “by 16 July 2024”. Redemption needed a minimum of 400 points a time, and after the closure date “Any GAME Reward points remaining on your GAME Reward account following the Closure Date shall be reset to zero”.

The same index carries “GAME Elite Service Ending FAQ's”, “When will my Elite Membership end” and “What will happen with Value Guarantee” — three discontinued services with a goodbye page each, sitting in a help centre of roughly 120 articles.

What is harder to find is the thing the brand is most associated with. GAME's own HTML sitemap runs to several hundred category links — Consoles and Video Games, Digital Gift Cards, PC Gaming, Tech, LEGO, Toys and Games, Trading Cards, Retro Gaming Consoles, Retro Arcade Machines, 23 separate deals pages and a Black Friday tree — and not one of them is a pre-owned, second-hand or trade-in category. The help centre index has no trade-in article either. The 437-product Consoles and Video Games listing returns none of the words “pre-owned”, “preowned”, “used” or “trade in”. That is a statement about what is published rather than an announcement GAME has made: on the evidence of its own two indexes, there is nowhere on the site to sell a disc back, and nothing on the site that says so in words. Anyone counting on a trade-in should ring a counter first.

Paying over three months runs through a 1962 finance company

The credit offer on game.co.uk is Frasers Plus, promoted on the home page as “Buy now, Pay in 3, interest free. Representative APR 29.9% (Variable)”. The dedicated page sets out who is who: “Frasers Group Financial Services Limited (FRN: 311908)” is “authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority as a lender”, while “Frasers Group Trading Limited (FRN: 980401), trading as 'Game', is acting as a credit broker not a lender”. Pay in 3 spreads the cost over three monthly instalments with “Nothing to pay at checkout”; longer plans run “6-36 months”, and the 29.9% representative APR applies “If you choose to pay over 6 months or longer”. The account earns “1 point for every £1 you spend”. The published eligibility is “Aged over 18, UK resident, Income over £7,500”, with the warning that “Missed payments may affect your credit score”.

The lender is older than the shop by three decades: FRASERS GROUP FINANCIAL SERVICES LIMITED is company 00718151, incorporated on 16 March 1962, at Express House, Petre Road, Clayton Business Park, Accrington BB5 5JB.

Frasers Group publishes the scale of it in its own full-year announcement for the 52 weeks to 26 April 2026: “£340.0m of retail sales were made on Frasers plus in FY26 (FY25: £195.0m)”, the business “ended FY26 with 1.1m active customers (FY25: 0.6m)”, and it “accounted for 20.5% of UK online sales, compared to 12.0% at FY25”. The group says it is “currently exceeding its 15% target on yield”, holds a long-term ambition of “£1bn+ in sales”, and that Frasers Group Financial Services and Visa have “partnered to launch a new UK credit and payments solution in FY27”. One pound in five of the group's British online sales now goes on its own credit account.

For a gaming order, the arithmetic cuts the other way. A £7.00 clearance game and a £10.00 controller are not credit purchases. The price filter on the Consoles and Video Games listing puts 46 of its 437 products at £10 or under, 109 between £10 and £20, and only 17 above £250 — so the credit offer really only engages at the console, monitor and gaming-chair end of the range, which is a small slice of what GAME actually sells.

A shop with shelves in a market that moved off them

Ukie's UK Games Market Valuation for 2025 puts total British consumer spending on games at £8,764m, up 7.4% on £8,158m. Software accounts for £6,026m of that, up 7%, and the split inside it is the whole story of why a chain of games shops closed its shops. Digital console spending was £2,490m, up 9.2%; mobile £2,067m, up 7.9%; digital PC £1,150m, up 1.0%; and boxed software £319m, down 1.0% — the only line in the table that shrank. Boxed discs and cartridges are 5.3% of software spending and 3.6% of the whole market, and digital console alone is 7.8 times their size.

Hardware came to £2,172m, up 3%: consoles £811m (up 12%), gaming PCs £790m (up 11%), console game accessories £420m (down 9%) and virtual reality £151m (down 26%). Game culture — the merchandise, the books, the figures — was £566m, up 42%, the fastest-growing line of the lot.

GAME's own listing has already followed that money. Break the 437 products on its Consoles and Video Games page down by platform and you get Nintendo Switch 23, PS5 14, PC 9, Xbox 4, PS4 3 and Xbox One 2, with 27 multi-format — against Merchandise 69 and Electronics 15. By category it is Gaming Accessories 123, Headsets 48, Indoor Lighting 48, Charging and Cables 44, Controllers 43, Nintendo Switch Accessories 26, Lamps 17, VR 16, Wheels 15 and Gaming Chairs 9. A page headed Consoles and Video Games is mostly neither. The separate Games listing is where the titles live, at 783 products.

Two things follow for a shopper. The accessories that dominate the shelf are the one hardware line in retreat, down 9% while the consoles they plug into rose 12% — and a shrinking accessory market on a growing installed base is the condition in which very cheap unbranded kit multiplies. And lamps, wall lights and merchandise sitting inside a console category is not padding: Game Culture grew 42% last year, and it is the part of this business with the clearest wind behind it.

Where the risk sits in a cheap gaming order

The rulebook that reaches a shop rather than a manufacturer is the General Product Safety Regulations 2005, SI 2005/1803, in force on 1 October 2005 and revoking the 1994 regulations it replaced. Regulation 8 puts the duty on the seller directly: a distributor “shall act with due care in order to help ensure compliance with the applicable safety requirements”, must not supply or offer to supply a product it knows or should presume, as a professional, is dangerous, and must help monitor safety by passing on risk information, keeping records that trace a product's origin, and cooperating with producers and enforcement authorities. Regulation 29 gives a due diligence defence to a firm that took all reasonable precautions. Regulations 11 to 15 give enforcement officers suspension, marking, warning, withdrawal and recall notices to use.

Read the Office for Product Safety and Standards register for “games console” and it returns 96 notices, of which almost all are wooden fishing games, playpens and magnetic board games. Exactly one is a games console: Product Recall: 620-in-1 Classic Mini Games Console Entertainment System, reference 2012-0209, unbranded, made in China, barcode WI001110049377FPL, rated 110-250VAC in with a 5VDC 500mA console input. It “presents a serious risk of electric shock and fire” from inadequate insulation, insufficient creepage and clearance distances in the transformer and power supply, and a poorly built unfused plug whose live pins can be touched while it is being pushed in or pulled out. It breached the Electrical Equipment (Safety) Regulations 2016 and the Plugs and Sockets (Safety) Regulations 1994, was recalled from end users on 15 January 2021, and the marketplace that listed it took the listing down. GAME's own sitemap carries a Retro Gaming Consoles category and a Retro Arcade Machines category, which is the same shelf.

The newest notice that touches this category is not electronics at all. Product Recall: Junbright Ltd Vittanly Gaming Chair sold via Amazon and AliExpress, reference 2607-0300, models DJY002BK in black and DJY003WT in white, made in China, dated 11 August 2026, carries a medium risk of fire “because the cover fabric and sheet foam does not meet flammability requirements”, breaching Schedule 5 Part 1 of the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 as amended. It was recalled from end users by Junbright Ltd and notified by Local Authority Trading Standards. GAME lists nine gaming chairs inside its console category and keeps a page for them.

Neither notice is a GAME product, and that is the point rather than an aside. In both cases the hazard was not the console or the chair as a thing — it was the power supply moulded into the cheapest version of one and the foam stuffed into the cheapest version of the other. The register's own answer to “is gaming kit dangerous” is that the branded hardware barely appears on it.

What the site will tell you, and the sentence that stops halfway

Credit where it is due first. Prices render in GAME's own HTML, which is not true of every big retailer, and they come with their history attached: £24.99 from £34.99 at 25% off on a PowerA Advantage Plus controller, £17.99 from £29.99 at 40% off on a Turtle Beach Rematch Core headset, £12.00 from £24.99 at 50% off on a PowerA wired Switch pad. There is also a second price on some lines — “Frasers Plus Price £110.00” against £129.99 on the PDP RIFFMASTER wireless guitar controller, and £500.00 against £519.99 on a PS5 Digital Edition 825GB bundle — with a whole Frasers Plus Exclusive Prices section, split into games-and-consoles and toys-and-board-games. It is a lower price for paying on the house credit account, and the site is open about it.

Then there is the sentence. On every product and category page the delivery line reads, in full, “This product will be delivered by , and may arrive separately to other items in your basket” — carrier and date missing, the template rendered with the variable empty. It appears on the clearance pages, on the controller listings and on a £519.99 console bundle alike.

The charge behind it could not be read. help.game.co.uk served its sitemap and served exactly one article — the GAME Reward closure FAQ — and then refused every other article and section across nine attempts on 14 November 2026, among them Delivery Options, GAME Returns Policy, the 14 Day Right to Change Your Mind, Click and Collect, Declaration of Conformity, What will happen with Value Guarantee, and both the Delivery Methods and Delivery FAQ's sections. The basket and checkout paths are disallowed in robots.txt. So this page does not quote GAME's delivery charge, its returns window, its non-returnable list or its refund timescale, because it could not read them, and will not guess at figures a shopper would act on. Ask before ordering, and ask in writing if the order is large.

The filters are worth knowing about too. The Games listing holds 783 products, and its Age Rating facet classifies 94 of them — 26 at 18, 25 at 3, 21 at 7, 11 at 16, 9 at 12 and two at 0 — while the Platform facet covers about 96, with a “Show 4 More” option left unopened. An age rating is a legal label on the physical box rather than a website feature, so this is a navigation point and not a compliance one; but a parent filtering 783 titles by rating is reading an index that reaches roughly one title in eight.

One last tell, for anyone who wonders how far the platform is now shared. game.co.uk's robots.txt blocks /au/, /us/ and /gb/ regional folders, /api/*, /DesktopModules/*, .axd resources and /genericscripts/*, points crawlers at /sitemap.xml — and carries a single “Allow: /cartier” exception punched through its own “/cart*” block. A rule written for a jeweller, living in a games shop's robots file, is what a shared retail platform looks like from the outside.

Where it wins

  • Prices are published and readable: 783 products under Games, 437 under Consoles and Video Games, 81 controllers, 670 sale lines and 183 clearance titles all carried a price on 14 November 2026.
  • Most tiles carry the previous price and the discount percentage beside the current one — £7.00 against £64.99 on EA FIFA 23, £12.00 against £24.99 on a PowerA Switch controller, £17.99 against £29.99 on a Turtle Beach headset.
  • There is a genuinely cheap bottom to the range: 46 of the 437 console-category products were £10 or under, and 109 sat between £10 and £20.
  • Pay in 3 is interest free with nothing to pay at checkout, and the page offering it names both the lender (Frasers Group Financial Services Limited, FRN 311908) and the broker (Frasers Group Trading Limited, FRN 980401) with their FCA reference numbers.
  • Frasers Plus Price is a real second price rather than a voucher code: £110.00 against £129.99 on one controller and £500.00 against £519.99 on a console bundle, with its own section of the site.
  • 379 collection points across the United Kingdom and Ireland is a wider counter network than the brand ran as a standalone chain.
  • A digital code needs no delivery at all and the site promises it “in under 24 hours”, from £8.39 for 1,720 Minecraft Minecoins.
  • The seller is an active company with current accounts — Frasers Group Trading Limited, 03406347, last accounts made up to 27 April 2025 — rather than the entity in administration.
  • Age Rating is offered as a filter at all, alongside platform, brand, category, style and price bands.

Where it falls short

  • The delivery charge, the returns window, the non-returnable list and the refund timescale could not be read. help.game.co.uk refused every one of those articles across nine attempts on 14 November 2026, and the basket and checkout are blocked in robots.txt, so none of it can be quoted here.
  • Every product and category page renders the delivery promise with the variable empty: “This product will be delivered by , and may arrive separately to other items in your basket”. A £519.99 console bundle gets the same blank as a £7.00 clearance game.
  • No pre-owned, second-hand or trade-in category appears anywhere in GAME's own sitemap, its help centre index or its 437-product console listing. The part of the business the name is most associated with is absent, and nothing on the site explains its absence.
  • Two company names are visible without the relationship between them being set out anywhere: GAME Retail Limited (07837246) has been in administration since 24 February 2026, while the seller in the footer and terms is Frasers Group Trading Limited (03406347), renamed from SportsDirect.com Retail Limited on 30 March 2026.
  • The A to Z store directory lists 379 entries without saying which shop each counter sits inside, duplicates “Irvine Riverway”, and carries misspellings including “Bournemouth - Ringwwod Road”, “Northhampton - Kettering” and “Carrick on ShannonHE”.
  • The Frasers Plus page publishes a 29.9% representative APR and no representative example, no fee schedule, no late payment charge and no indication of credit limits.
  • The Age Rating filter classifies 94 of the 783 titles in the Games listing, so filtering by rating hides most of the catalogue rather than narrowing it.
  • GAME Reward and GAME Elite closed at 23:59 on 31 July 2024 with unredeemed points reset to zero, and there is no replacement scheme of GAME's own: points now accrue at 1 per £1 on a group credit account, which means taking credit to earn them.
  • GAME Retail Limited's accounts for the year to 30 April 2025 were due by 30 April 2026 and the register shows them overdue, so there are no recent published figures for the business the brand was built on.

Common questions

Which company am I actually buying from on game.co.uk?

Frasers Group Trading Limited, company number 03406347, FRN 980401, registered at Unit A, Brook Park East, Shirebrook NG20 8RY. The site's own footer and terms name it as the seller, “trading as 'Game'”. That company was called SportsDirect.com Retail Limited until 30 March 2026, and before that Sports World International Limited and, on incorporation in July 1997, Sports Soccer Limited. The separate company GAME Retail Limited, 07837246, has been in administration since 24 February 2026. Keep your order confirmation: the name on it decides where a faulty-goods claim or a card dispute is aimed.

Are there still GAME shops I can walk into?

There are 379 entries in GAME's own A to Z store directory covering the United Kingdom and Ireland, but they are concessions rather than branches. Frasers Group's half-year announcement for the 26 weeks to 26 October 2025 describes the “replacement of standalone Game stores with Game concessions situated inside larger Sports Direct stores”, and its full-year announcement to 26 April 2026 attributes a 4.7% fall in UK Sports revenue to “planned declines in Game UK standalone stores”. GAME's directory does not say which shop each counter is inside, so ring the location before travelling.

Can I still trade in or buy pre-owned games at GAME?

Nothing on the site says you can. GAME's own HTML sitemap lists several hundred categories and none of them is pre-owned, second-hand or trade-in; the help centre index of roughly 120 articles has no trade-in article; and the 437-product Consoles and Video Games listing returns none of those words. That is what is published rather than a statement GAME has made, so if a trade-in is the reason you are going, ask a counter directly first.

What does delivery cost?

This page cannot tell you, and would rather say so than guess. GAME's delivery terms live on help.game.co.uk, which served its own sitemap and one article on 14 November 2026 and then refused every delivery, returns and collection article across nine attempts. The basket and checkout paths are blocked to crawlers. The product pages themselves print the delivery sentence with the carrier and date missing. Put a cheap item in a basket and look at the checkout total before ordering, and treat the £7.00 clearance price as the item price rather than the delivered price.

What happened to my GAME Reward points?

GAME Reward and GAME Elite closed at 23:59 on 31 July 2024. Points stopped being earned at 23:59 on 15 July 2024, members were sent a final balance email by 16 July 2024, redemption needed a minimum of 400 points, and the closure FAQ states that any points left on an account after the closure date “shall be reset to zero”. There is no replacement GAME scheme. The only points on offer now are one per pound spent on a Frasers Plus credit account.

Is Frasers Plus really interest free?

The three-month plan is: the site offers “pay in 3 for interest free”, spread over three monthly instalments with nothing to pay at checkout. The longer plans are not — the 29.9% APR representative (variable) applies, in the site's own words, “If you choose to pay over 6 months or longer”. The lender is Frasers Group Financial Services Limited, FRN 311908, a company incorporated on 16 March 1962; Frasers Group Trading Limited acts as credit broker. Published eligibility is being over 18, a UK resident and having income over £7,500. No representative example, fee schedule or late payment charge is published on that page, so ask for the pre-contract information before signing.

Our verdict

As a shop to buy from, GAME is in better shape than its headlines suggest: the prices are there to read, the discounts at the bottom of the range are real, the bigger purchases can be spread over three months for nothing, and there are 379 counters to collect from. Judge it on that and it compares well with retailers whose websites will not show a shopper a number at all. What a buyer has to go in knowing is that the transaction is not quite the one the brand implies. The company behind the name has been in administration since 24 February 2026; the company taking the money was called SportsDirect.com Retail Limited five weeks before that; the branches are counters inside other Frasers shops; the loyalty scheme closed in July 2024; and there is no trade-in counter anywhere in the site's own index. None of that makes a £7.00 game a bad buy. It does mean checking the delivery charge and the returns terms before you commit — which this page could not do for you, because GAME's own help centre would not open — and keeping the order confirmation, because the name on it is the one that matters if anything goes wrong.
