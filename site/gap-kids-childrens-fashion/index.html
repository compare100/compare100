Gap Kids in the UK: £10 to £35, Run by Next, and the VAT Line Under the Price

Rates and terms checked 14 November 2026 · Children's Fashion · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Gap's children's pages open at £10 for a baby logo pique polo shirt and run to £35 for a pair of Winnie the Pooh denim dungarees, across 506 baby and toddler lines, 270 girls' lines and 247 boys' lines listed on gap.co.uk on 14 November 2026. Collection is free at more than 500 shops. Every price and document below was read that day on Gap's own UK pages and on HMRC's.

The shop behind the website is not the one most people picture. The footer of gap.co.uk reads “© 2026 Next Retail limited trading as Gap”, the terms name Next Retail Limited of Desford Road, Enderby, Leicester, and NEXT plc announced the arrangement on 17 September 2021 as a joint venture it owns 51% of, with Gap Inc. holding the other 49%. Seven Gap stores are left in Britain; the other 44 Gap shopfloors are concession areas inside Next branches, and the 500-plus collection points are Next shops. Anyone driving to a retail park expecting a Gap store will in all likelihood be walking into a Next.

The other thing worth knowing before spending anything is that almost nothing on these pages carries VAT at all. Children's clothing and footwear is zero-rated in the United Kingdom, which is a large part of why a £10 polo shirt can be £10. The relief has edges, measured in centimetres rather than birthdays, and Gap's largest children's size sits within a few centimetres of one of them.

Where the £10 lines are, and what a multipack works out at

The baby and toddler department carried 506 lines on 14 November 2026 and it holds the cheap end of the range. The least expensive garment on the first page of it was a navy blue logo pique polo shirt at £10. Above that, a relaxed logo hoodie is £18, a vintage soft Brannan Bear hoodie £20, a floral zip-up hoodie £22, a sherpa quarter-zip fleece and a Brannan Bear crew-neck jumper £25 each, a sherpa logo one-piece sleepsuit £30, and the Winnie the Pooh denim dungarees £35. Shop-by-age links on that department run from 0–3 months to age 4–5.

Multipacks are where the arithmetic turns. A five-pack of long-sleeve Brannan Bear bodysuits is £25, which is £5 a bodysuit; a three-pack of short-sleeve embroidered T-shirts in sizes six months to five years is £20, or £6.67 each. Against a £10 single polo shirt, buying in threes and fives is the only real discount structure on the children's pages that is not time-limited.

Denim is priced in a tight band. Baby and toddler jeans listed 30 products, from £20 for blue slim-fit pull-on stretch jeans and medium-wash pull-on baggy jeans, through £22 for black cotton pull-on slim jeans and £25 for most of the rest, to £30 for blue barrel horseshoe jeans. Nothing in that category was shown with a was-and-now price.

The older departments are a few pounds dearer and the ladder is the same shape. Girls' clothing listed 270 lines: £18 relaxed logo joggers, £25 relaxed logo hoodies, £30 sherpa-lined zip-up hoodies and oversized crew-neck jumpers, £35 easy pull-on baggy jeans. Boys' clothing listed 247: £18 joggers, £20 vintage logo hoodies, £25 zip-up hoodies, £30 for the sherpa-lined versions and for a Grinch logo sweatshirt. Both departments offer age 4–5, 6–7, 8–9, 10–11 and 12–13 and stop there. New account holders are offered 10% off a first order, which the site's own meta description carries.

The 20% that is not in the price of a child's coat

Children's clothes in Britain are zero-rated for VAT, and the rules that decide it are in HMRC's VAT Notice 714. Four conditions have to be met at once: the item is clothing or footwear, it is not made of fur, it is designed for young children, and it is suitable only for young children. The measurements behind all of that are built on “the size of an average child up to the eve of his or her fourteenth birthday”, so the test is a size test and not an age test. Nothing on a label about who a garment is for settles it.

The garment table is the main route and it is specific. A garment is measured flat with the buttons fastened, the chest taken 2.5cm below the armhole and doubled, the waist taken across the fastened waistband and doubled. Shirts and knitwear qualify to a 104cm chest for boys and 105cm for girls; jackets and waistcoats to 109cm and 110cm; top coats and outerwear to 114cm and 115cm; dresses to a 98cm chest; skirts to a 71cm waist; trousers and shorts to 72cm for boys and 71cm for girls; underwear and swimwear to an 88cm or 89cm chest; nightwear to 105cm and 106cm. Elasticated waistbands are measured at full stretch, and an unfastened garment can still qualify up to a stretched waist of 85cm for boys or 90cm for girls.

Footwear has its own ceilings. Boys' shoes qualify up to and including size 6½, unisex shoes to 5½, girls' court shoes to size 3, and other girls' shoes to size 3 and then from 3½ to 5½ so long as the heel height does not exceed the depth of the sole by more than 4cm. HMRC gives the conversions too: boys' 6½ is a US 7 and an EU 40, girls' 3 is a US 4½ and an EU 35½, girls' 5½ is a US 7 and an EU 38½. Where someone needs two different sizes, the pair qualifies if the smaller shoe does.

The oddities are worth knowing because they are where a parent gets caught. Fur puts a garment straight onto the standard rate if it is wholly or partly made of skin with the fur still attached, although a fur trim is fine unless it covers more than a fifth of the surface area. Cycle helmets are zero-rated whatever their size, while other protective helmets stop at 59cm. Alice bands, hair ribbons, scrunchies, sports headbands, sun visors and ear muffs are accessories rather than clothing and carry the full 20%. Lifejackets qualify to a 52kg maximum body weight, teen bras to a 34B, tights to a waist-crotch-waist measurement of 51cm lightweight or 56cm heavyweight, and socks to boys' shoe size 6½ or girls' and unisex 5½. A one-size garment sold to both children and adults cannot be zero-rated at all.

School uniform gets no relief of its own and follows the ordinary children's rules, with one exception: where there is a specific agreement with a school that takes only under-14s, and the garment is unique to that school by design — a prominent badge, piping in the school colours — it can be zero-rated whatever its size. A garment also worn by other groups, Scouts among them, does not qualify. Gap's schoolwear listings appear in searches of its own domain, but every schoolwear path tried on 14 November 2026 was closed to reading, so none of this could be checked against its own uniform pages.

Gap's biggest children's size against HMRC's biggest child

Gap publishes its own body measurements, and setting them beside HMRC's is the quickest way to see where the relief runs out. Its boys' chart runs 4–5 years at 99–114cm tall and a 58–61cm chest, 6–7 years at 114–130cm, 8–9 at 130–137cm and a 69cm chest, 10–11 at 137–145cm and 71cm, 12 years at 145–152cm and 74cm, and 13 years at 152–170cm tall, a 76cm chest and 44–54kg. The girls' chart is close behind: 12 years at 145–152cm and a 76cm chest, 13 years at 152–163cm, an 80–84cm chest and 42–52kg.

HMRC has a second route for larger sizes. A garment can be zero-rated whatever it measures if it was designed from body measurements at or below a height of 163cm, an 84cm chest, a 70cm waist, 85cm hips, a 59cm arm and a 77cm inside leg for boys, or 161cm, 85cm, 69cm, 90cm, 57cm and 76cm for girls. Gap's largest boys' size is cut for a child up to 170cm, which is 7cm past that figure, and the largest girls' size reaches 163cm against 161cm. The chest measurements, by contrast, are comfortably inside: a 76cm boys' body chest becomes a flat garment measurement a long way below the 104cm limit on a shirt.

So on the garment route nothing in this range looks close to the line, and the zero rate on a Gap hoodie is not in any real doubt. The interesting edge is the overlap at the top of the chart, where the 13-year size and an adult extra-small cover almost the same body, and HMRC's rule that a garment sold to both children and adults cannot be zero-rated bites on exactly that overlap. Which side of it any particular line falls is the retailer's call, made before the price is printed.

A shopper cannot audit it either way. All gap.co.uk's terms say about the subject is that “the prices shown are inclusive of any value added tax or sales tax”, so a £35 pair of jeans does not disclose whether nothing or a fifth of it is tax. The practical consequence is simple enough: a child who outgrows age 12–13 moves into adult sizes, and at that point the same hoodie starts carrying 20% VAT.

Getting the parcel, and the cost of sending it back

Standard courier delivery to a home address is £4.95 per delivery. Delivery to an Evri Parcel Shop is £3.50. Delivery to a store is free, and click and collect covers “500+ locations nationwide, including any Next or Gap store”. The home page also advertises next-day delivery to the door. There is no basket value at which home delivery becomes free: the only free home delivery written into the terms ran from 8am on 26 June 2026 to 8am on 29 June 2026 and needed a £70 minimum order, which is 72 hours in a year.

Returns are more generous than the law requires and the terms point that out themselves. Goods can go back within 28 days from the day they were delivered, which the page describes as “14 days longer than the consumer law right of cancellation”, in new and unused condition with the tags attached. Taking them into a store is free. A courier collection from home is charged at £3.50 and taken out of the refund. The statutory cancellation right sits underneath all of that: 14 days beginning the day after the goods arrive, with the goods returned within 14 days of giving notice, and made-to-order or customised goods, opened toiletries and cosmetics and pierced jewellery outside it.

Put the two charges together and the shape of this account becomes clear. A £10 polo shirt ordered to the door and sent back by courier costs £4.95 to get and £3.50 to return — £8.45 on a £10 garment, or 85% of its price. Collected from a Next store and returned to one, both legs are free and the shirt costs £10. On a department whose cheapest lines are £10 to £25, the collection point is not a convenience, it is the difference between a sensible purchase and a silly one.

Two smaller terms are worth reading before clicking. Promotions do not stack: where more than one applies, “only the one with the best cash saving will apply to your order”. And an investigation fee of “no less than £35” can be charged where an item comes back substituted or incorrectly returned, which is a large number on a £25 parcel.

The company on the invoice, and the one that is not in the accounts

Next Retail Limited, company number 04521150, was incorporated on 29 August 2002 as EVER 1889 LIMITED, renamed NEXT NEAR EAST LIMITED on 3 October 2002 and took its present name on 25 March 2003. It is active, registered at Desford Road, Enderby, Leicester LE19 4AT, and carries SIC codes 47710 for retail sale of clothing in specialised stores, 47721 for footwear, 47789 for other new goods and 47910 for mail order and internet retail. Its accounts to 31 January 2026 were filed on 9 October 2026 as audit exemption subsidiary accounts, supported by a parent company statement of guarantee filed on 20 May 2026 — so the company that takes a Gap order publishes no audit opinion of its own, and the audited numbers sit at NEXT plc instead.

The joint venture itself is harder to see. NEXT plc's 17 September 2021 announcement sets out the structure plainly: NEXT takes 51% and Gap Inc. 49%, NEXT manages the UK and Eire business as franchise partner, the online business runs on the NEXT Total Platform, Gap-branded shop-in-shops open at selected locations and click and collect starts in 2022. Lord Simon Wolfson, NEXT's chief executive, said NEXT was “delighted at the prospect of its Total Platform supporting GAP”; Mark Breitbard, then chief executive and president of Gap Global, described partnering with “one of the UK's leading online clothing retailers”. Gap Inc.'s net sales for its 2020 financial year were given as $13.8 billion.

Five years on, the word Gap does not appear in NEXT plc's results for the half year to July 2026, nor anywhere in its annual report and accounts to January 2026. Total Platform Services is reported in detail — client online sales of £122m, up 21%; total income £40m, up 16%; profit £8m, up 23%, at a 19.6% margin — and the Investments section covers the Reiss holding, raised by 0.3% to 74.3%. The Gap UK business's own sales and profit are published nowhere a shopper can read them.

Scale: an American brand on a Leicester platform

Gap Inc. is the smaller half of the arrangement at group level and the Gap brand is its best-performing part. In the 13 weeks to 1 August 2026 the group's net sales were $3,651m, down 2% on $3,725m, with comparable sales down 1%. Gap brand net sales were $844m, up 9%, and Gap brand comparable sales rose 10% against 4% a year earlier. Old Navy fell 4% to $2,061m, Banana Republic rose 1% to $478m, and Athleta fell 12% to $264m. Reported gross margin was 52.8%, flattered by a tariff recovery benefit; stripping that out gives 41.4%, up 20 basis points. The group ran 2,471 company-operated stores out of nearly 3,500 locations in about 35 countries, roughly 1,000 of them operated by franchise partners — and the United Kingdom is one of those franchises.

The operator is the larger business in Britain. NEXT plc's six months to July 2026 produced group sales of £3,540m, up 9.0% on £3,249m, and group profit before tax of £569m, up 10.5%, with post-tax earnings per share of 370.4p. Online UK sales were £1,370m, up 8.0%, online international £774m, up 26.4%, and 16.2m customers ordered online over the preceding 12 months, up 14%. Retail store sales were £895m, down 0.4%, with eight new stores and two re-sites planned for the year and six of them open. The shape of it matters for anyone buying children's clothes: the parcel comes out of a growing online operation, and the shop it is collected from comes out of a store estate that is flat.

What cannot be read before you buy

A fair amount of Gap UK's own customer-service material could not be opened on 14 November 2026. The help centre at gapuk.zendesk.com, which Gap's own pages link to both for arranging a return and for frequently asked questions, refused to load at all. The delivery page at /customer-service/delivery, the /help index, the FAQ page and the shipping-and-handling page reached from Gap's own customer-service navigation all answer “Sorry, the category you requested might have moved or no longer exists.” So do /shop/kids/clothing, /shop/baby/shoes, /shop/girls/shoes and the school-uniform path, while /shop/baby/clothing, /shop/girls/clothing and /shop/boys/clothing work normally. The /shop/childrens index and the faceted listing paths are closed by the site's robots file. Every delivery and returns figure on this page therefore comes from the terms and conditions page, which does work, rather than from the help article a shopper would be sent to.

That terms page is marked “Correct as of 05/02/2026” and still prints a Black Friday discount code that expired on 1 December 2025 and a 25%-off offer that expired on 9 February 2026. The size guide carries clothing, jeans and baby charts and no footwear chart at all, although the children's shoe pages exist — which is unfortunate on a retailer whose shoes sit either side of a VAT threshold defined in shoe sizes.

Outside ratings are thin as well, and in a way that says something about the structure. Which?'s baby and child shops survey, fieldwork in July and August 2025, covered 36 brands using 6,099 experiences reported by 2,317 people, with a category average customer score of 80%. Next scored 83% on 276 responses, John Lewis and Marks & Spencer 82% each, Amazon 81% on 532 responses, JoJo Maman Bebe 76% and The Range last on 74%. Gap is not in the table. The company that runs the UK business has a score; the brand on the label does not.

The official safety record is clean and worth reading carefully. Searching the Office for Product Safety and Standards alerts register for “Gap” returns 24 reports and not one of them is a Gap garment — the word matches other text in unrelated entries, which is a reminder that a keyword search of that register proves less than it appears to. What the register does hold for children's clothing is a small cluster from 2022: an Olly & Molly junior playsuit and a Minoti children's playsuit both recalled on 11 July 2022, and two models of a Luke 1997 junior hoodie recalled on 10 August 2022. Nothing against this retailer, and a short list of recalls across a whole category says more about how few listings get tested than about how safe the shelves are.

Common questions

Is Gap UK the same company as Gap in America?

No. gap.co.uk is operated by Next Retail Limited, company number 04521150, registered at Desford Road, Enderby, Leicester LE19 4AT, with VAT number GB 179 7658 90. Its footer reads “© 2026 Next Retail limited trading as Gap” and its terms say the contract is formed between Next and the customer when the goods are dispatched. NEXT plc announced the arrangement on 17 September 2021 as a joint venture it owns 51% of, with Gap Inc. holding 49%.

How much is delivery, and can I get it free?

Standard courier delivery to a home address is £4.95 per delivery, delivery to an Evri Parcel Shop is £3.50, and delivery to a store is free with collection available at more than 500 Next or Gap shops. There is no order value at which home delivery becomes free. The only free home delivery written into the terms was a promotion running from 8am on 26 June 2026 to 8am on 29 June 2026 with a £70 minimum order.

What does it cost to return something?

Nothing if it goes back into a store, which can be done within 28 days of the day of delivery provided the item is new, unused and still has its tags. A courier collection from home is £3.50, taken out of the refund. The statutory cancellation right is separate and shorter: 14 days beginning the day after delivery, with the goods returned within 14 days of giving notice. An investigation fee of “no less than £35” can be charged where an item is substituted or incorrectly returned.

Do children's clothes from Gap include VAT?

Children's clothing and footwear that meets HMRC's size tests in VAT Notice 714 is zero-rated, so for most of this range there is no VAT in the price. The test is a measurement test, not an age test: it is built on the size of an average child up to the eve of a fourteenth birthday, and the garment tables allow a boys' shirt up to a 104cm chest, a dress up to 98cm, a skirt to a 71cm waist and boys' shoes to size 6½. Gap's terms say only that “the prices shown are inclusive of any value added tax or sales tax”, so the rate inside a given price is not disclosed.

What sizes does the children's range cover?

From up to 7lb and 0–3 months at the bottom to age 12–13 at the top. Baby and toddler shop-by-age links run from 0–3 months to age 4–5, and the girls' and boys' departments offer 4–5, 6–7, 8–9, 10–11 and 12–13. Gap's own charts put 13-year boys at 152–170cm tall with a 76cm chest and 13-year girls at 152–163cm with an 80–84cm chest. There is nothing above that, so a larger child moves into adult sizing.

Can I buy Gap children's clothes in a shop?

In 51 places, on the store locator read on 14 November 2026: 7 Gap stores, including the Oxford Street flagship marked for reopening in October 2026, and 44 Gap concession areas inside selected Next branches. Click and collect is wider than that, covering more than 500 locations including any Next or Gap store, which is how most people will actually pick up an order.

Has Gap UK been rated by an independent body?

Not in the obvious place. Which?'s baby and child shops survey, with fieldwork in July and August 2025 covering 36 brands and 6,099 shopping experiences reported by 2,317 people, does not include Gap. Next, the company that operates the UK business, scores 83% in that table against a category average of 80%. A keyword search of the Office for Product Safety and Standards alerts register for Gap returns 24 reports, none of which involves a Gap garment.

Our verdict

As a children's clothing shop this is a straightforward and reasonably cheap one: £10 to £35 across 1,023 children's lines, multipacks that bring a bodysuit down to £5, and free collection at more than 500 shops with nothing to spend first. The thing to get right before ordering is the carriage. Home delivery is £4.95 whatever the basket, and a courier collection of a return is another £3.50, which on the cheap end of this range is most of the price of the garment; collect in a Next store and return it there and both legs are free. Read the website as what it is — an American brand operated in Britain by Next Retail Limited under a 51/49 joint venture, with seven standalone shops and 44 concessions — and the gaps make sense: the help centre that will not open, the dead customer-service links, the terms page six months out of date. The one genuine piece of good news is the tax. Children's clothing and footwear is zero-rated, the whole of this range sits well inside HMRC's garment measurements, and a parent is getting the same hoodie 20% cheaper than the adult version on the next rail. That stops the day a child outgrows age 12–13, which on Gap's own chart is a boy of 170cm, and it is worth knowing before the next size up arrives.
