Habitat Furniture Review: Prices, Delivery and the Two Guarantees

Rates and terms checked 14 November 2026 · Furniture · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Habitat lists 142 sofas, from a £240 two-seater to a £1,095 velvet corner chaise, and it has been putting contemporary design into ordinary British front rooms since 1964. A Chesterfield velvet three-seater is £695, a Milford tan leather four-seater £1,050, and the cheapest thing in the range — the Nora two-seater — is £240. Every figure on this page was checked on 14 November 2026 against Habitat's and Argos's own help pages and product listings.

There is no Habitat shop to walk into. Habitat is a trading name of Argos Limited, and the brand reaches shoppers through Argos's 201 standalone stores, 466 Argos outlets inside Sainsbury's supermarkets and 466 collection points — 1,133 points of presence as at 28 February 2026. That matters before you order rather than after: the delivery slot, the returns window, the guarantee and the phone number on a Habitat sofa all belong to Argos.

The money side is published and easy to check. Large item delivery is £9.95, small items are £4.95 and free over £100, same-day Fast Track starts at £7.95 with other slots from £4.95, and £40 buys twelve months of Argos Plus delivery on orders of £20 or more. Click and Collect is free and the order is held for seven days. Drivers ring ahead with a two-hour window and a 30-minute warning, and for a sofa they will carry it to the room you choose.

What £240 buys, and what arrives in a box

The sofa listing is unusually easy to read because Habitat prints a single price against each model rather than a "from" figure. On 14 November 2026 the 142 sofas ran from the Nora two-seater at £240 and the Poppy at £285 through the Della three-seater at £400, the Julien fabric three-seater at £695 and the Chesterfield velvet three-seater at £695, to the Milford tan leather four-seater at £1,050 and the Julien corner chaise at £1,095. The Habitat x Morris & Co. Merton two-seaters, in Sunflower and Acanthus, sit at £725 — and those collaborations are the part of the range Sainsbury's singled out in its own results, which credited "design-led collaborations in Habitat" with a positive market share performance.

Discounting is real rather than permanent. The Elmdon corner sofas were £864 against a £1,080 was-price, the Turnmill four-seater chaise £537.50 from £1,075 and the Betsy boucle three-seater £348.75 from £450, with site-wide codes FURN20 and FURN30 advertised as up to 30% off selected furniture. Habitat also badges some products under a Price Guarantee, promising that "prices won't go lower before Christmas" on those lines and a voucher for the difference if they do.

The most practical thing in the range is the Sofa in a Box line — the Mina two-seater at £250, the Finney at £380 and the Betsy boucle three-seater at £348.75. A sofa that arrives flat and is assembled in the room solves the problem that decides most sofa purchases, which is whether the thing will go round the turn of the stairs at all. It is also a direct descendant of what the shop did in 1971, when Habitat started selling furniture off take-away racks for the customer to build.

Thirty days to change your mind, and £30 off the refund

Habitat gives 30 days from the date of delivery or collection to request a return on a change of mind, which is more than twice the 14 days the law requires on a distance sale, and purchases made between 23 September and 25 December 2026 can go back until 31 January 2027. Small items can be handed in at any Argos store or any Argos inside a Sainsbury's. Refunds are processed within 14 days of the goods coming back.

Large items are different, and the number is worth knowing before you order. Habitat's returns page states that "for large home delivered items, a non-refundable collection charge of £30 will be applied to orders returned from 12 June 2025, unless the product is faulty or has been delivered directly by one of our suppliers". So the sofa that cost £9.95 to deliver costs £30 to take away again, three times over. Habitat does refund the delivery itself — "you're entitled to receive a refund of the standard delivery charge where the full order is returned" — so the net cost of changing your mind on the £240 Nora is the £30, which is 12.5% of what you paid for it.

The charge is lawful and the regulation it sits under is worth reading, because almost nobody quotes it. Under regulation 35 of the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013, where a trader "has offered to collect" returned goods it becomes the trader's responsibility to collect them, and paragraph (8) says the consumer "is not required to bear any cost of collecting goods" — unless the trader offered collection and "the consumer has agreed to bear the costs of the trader doing so". That agreement is the whole basis of the £30. The same regulation cuts the other way too: paragraphs (5) and (6) mean that if a trader fails to tell you in advance that you will pay to send something back, the contract is treated as making the trader pay instead. Habitat publishes the figure, so it is on the right side of that line — but a reader who never opens the returns page will meet it as a deduction from a refund.

Ten years on one help page, fifteen on another

Habitat's frame guarantee is genuinely long, and the company states it twice at two different lengths. The page at habitat.co.uk/help/sofa-guarantee says that "there is a 2 year guarantee for the fabric and 10 years for the frame", covering "any damage found on the frame, internal springs, webbing or feet, that has been caused by a manufacturing defect" on Home and Habitat branded products. The page at help.argos.co.uk/help/enjoy-our-15-year-frame-guarantee says that armchairs and sofas "are covered for 15 years, from the date of purchase", with the same two years on fabric, the same frame-springs-webbing-feet wording, and the qualification that it "applies to Habitat branded products only" bought on or after August 2024.

Both are the same company's own help centres and the gap between them is five years on the most expensive thing in the shop. Habitat's own help index makes it harder rather than easier: it carries two links side by side labelled "10 year guarantee" and "15 year guarantee", and the second one leads to a sofa-buying guide that contains no guarantee terms at all — checked twice on 14 November 2026. Which document a claim is actually read against is not something a shopper can work out from Habitat's own pages, and it is the kind of thing worth settling on the phone before the money leaves your account.

What both versions agree on is the exclusions and the charge. Seat pads and back cushion fillings are outside the guarantee. So are sofa beds, recliners, selected occasional chairs and the entire Made To Order range, which means the sofas you wait longest and pay most for carry the least frame cover — and because regulation 28(1) of the 2013 Regulations removes the cancellation right on goods made to a consumer's specification, a made-to-order sofa has neither the frame guarantee nor the change-of-mind return. And either guarantee costs £60 to use: there is a call-out charge for an inspection, refunded only "if the fault is covered by your guarantee". On the £240 Nora that is a quarter of the purchase price staked on the engineer agreeing with you.

Beyond the guarantee there is Argos Care, which Habitat sells as insurance rather than warranty. It is "provided by Domestic & General Insurance PLC" (company 485850, Wimbledon), brokered by Argos Limited under FCA reference 713206, and for furniture it covers accidental damage from day one including spills, rips, tears, burns, scratches on leather and damage caused by pets, then frame and structural defects once the manufacturer's guarantee runs out. Cancel inside 45 days and you get a full refund. No price is published anywhere on the Habitat pages — the terms refer to pricing tables in separate leaflets — so the only way to know what the cover costs is to reach the checkout.

A brand with 1,133 front doors and none of its own

Habitat had three shops in the UK when the Competition and Markets Authority looked at it. The clearance decision on Sainsbury's purchase of Home Retail Group, given on 22 July 2016 and published on 16 August 2016, records in one line that "HRG also owns the Habitat brand which has three stores in the UK", footnoted as the position in April 2016, and concluded that the merger was not expected to result in a substantial lessening of competition. Those three shops are gone. What replaced them is Argos's estate: 201 standalone stores, 466 Argos outlets inside Sainsbury's supermarkets and 466 collection points as at 28 February 2026, across a group running 609 supermarkets and 885 convenience stores.

On the group's own numbers, Habitat is a small part of a large machine. Argos took £4.1bn of sales in the 52 weeks to 28 February 2026, up 0.7% in what Sainsbury's called "a highly competitive market", inside retail sales of £29,992m, up 4.3%, and underlying pre-tax profit of £718m. Sainsbury's own general merchandise sales fell 3.2% over the same year. Of Habitat itself the statement says one thing: that "re-launched Chad Valley and design-led collaborations in Habitat resulted in positive market share performances".

One date does not line up, and it is Habitat's own. The heritage timeline on habitat.co.uk says "2017: The Sainsbury's Group acquired the UK arm of the brand, along with Argos", while the regulator cleared that purchase in July 2016 and names Home Retail Group as the owner in April 2016. Both are the organisation's own published records and they are a year apart; the CMA document is the dated one.

There is a second Habitat, and a UK reader can easily land on it. The brand outside Britain trades at habitat-design.com and says on its own heritage page that it was "relaunched at the start of 2024 by the Vente-unique.com group", an online furniture seller trading since 2006 in France and Europe. That site does not mention the UK at all. Two unrelated companies, one name, one founder in the story — and only the Argos one will deliver to a British address or honour an Argos guarantee.

From a broadsheet catalogue in 1966 to a sofa in a box

The heritage is not decoration; it explains the range. Habitat's own story page dates the business to 1964, when Terence Conran and a group of friends set out "to bring London a new, European way to shop for contemporary homeware". The first printed broadsheet catalogue came in 1966, illustrated by Juliet Glynn Smith, and Habitat By Post launched the same year so that people nowhere near a shop could buy from it — which is, allowing for sixty years, exactly what the website does now.

Then 1971 and the take-away racks: flat-pack furniture a customer carried home and assembled, which the company's own timeline calls the flat-pack revolution. The Basic Habitat range followed for a market hit by recession, and the Housepack service furnished a three-bedroom house in one go. The 1970s prints were commissioned from four British artists including David Hockney and Peter Blake; the 1980s brought the Graffiti sofa, a cookware range with the chef Michel Guerard, the 1985 City Living collection and fabrics and wallpapers made with the Victoria and Albert Museum.

The V&A returned the compliment in 1994 with Selling Lifestyle, 30 Years of Habitat for the thirtieth anniversary. The Habitat Art Club sold work by Tracey Emin and Gillian Wearing; 1999 brought the 20th Century Legends re-editions; a heart was added inside the wordmark in May 2002; and the VIP collection launched in 2004 with 22 products. The 2024 60 Years of Design collection was made with Sebastian Conran, Felix Conran, Margo Selby and Tord Boontje. A shopper paying £725 for a Morris & Co. two-seater is buying into a line of collaborations the shop has been doing since the Hockney prints.

Three sofa ranges that went back for failing a match test

Habitat keeps its own recall notices online, and the furniture ones are specific. The Jackson velvet two-seaters, three-seaters and cuddle chairs in Black, Orange and Artichoke — nine catalogue numbers running from 9426946 to 9578612 — were recalled under case 2302-0155, published 8 March 2023, after sale between 14 March 2022 and 28 February 2023. The regulator's own entry gives the reason without euphemism: a "serious risk of fire" because "the fabric cover fails to meet the requirements of the relevant standard BS5852", and records that "the product does not meet the requirements of the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988". The sofas were made in Romania and the notifier was local authority Trading Standards.

The Isla velvet sofas and armchairs in Green, Teal and Pink (case 2303-0001) and the Eton fabric corner sofas and footstool in Grey and Charcoal (case 2303-0003) were published the same day on the same grounds. Habitat's own notices, updated 3 March 2023, tell owners they may keep using the item provided it is kept away from sources of fire and to ring 0800 011 3462 to arrange "delivery of an alternative model or refund", with the product still available for collection. Two more recent Habitat recalls on the public register are not furniture: a Seed Pod Bowl Filler (2509-0160, 23 September 2025) and the Mango Wooden Taper Candle Holders sold through Sainsbury's (2512-0270, 7 January 2026), where the fix is to "return it to your local Sainsburys store for a full refund".

Five reports in total under the Habitat name, three of them upholstery, all three traced to the same cover-fabric test. That the company publishes them itself, with catalogue numbers and a freephone line, is to its credit; that upholstery is where the failures clustered is the reason the labels under the cushions exist.

Who hears a complaint about a Habitat sofa

The answer is Argos, and then nobody in particular. Habitat's contact page gives 0345 640 2020, open 8am to 8pm seven days a week, Facebook Messenger, @HabitatUK, a SignVideo British Sign Language service free from 8am to 6pm on weekdays, and a live chat team you reach by going to argos.co.uk. It names no ombudsman, no alternative dispute resolution scheme and no escalation route at all, and neither do the Argos terms and conditions, which promise only to aim at resolving a Trusted Seller dispute "within 21 working days".

The furniture trade has a free scheme for exactly this. The Furniture and Home Improvement Ombudsman's member list runs to several hundred names and includes Heal's, DFS, Dreams, Sofology, Bensons for Beds, Furniture Village, Marks and Spencer, Wickes and B&Q. Checked on 14 November 2026, neither Argos, Habitat nor Sainsbury's appears anywhere in it. A Habitat buyer in dispute has the shop, then the small claims track, and nothing in between — where a Heal's buyer, shopping another London furniture name on that list, has a conciliation service that costs them nothing.

Two services that look like Habitat's are other people's. Furniture assembly is handed to Airtasker, which the page describes as a platform connecting customers with independent "Taskers", where the price "varies depending on the type of task, your budget and the offers you negotiate" and the only hard number is that tasks "must be above £50 to qualify" for the discount code — an offer the page itself says was "valid until 30th October 2026", a fortnight before this check. Old furniture removal goes to Clearabee, at an unstated "competitive price", with no mattress recycling, booked on Clearabee's own site, and with Argos stating plainly that it is "not responsible for any service issues". Neither is a criticism of the furniture. Both mean that if the sofa arrives and the assembly or the disposal goes wrong, the company you bought from is not the company you ring.

Where it wins

  • 142 sofas listed with a single clear price each rather than a "from" figure, starting at £240 for the Nora two-seater and topping out at £1,095
  • 30 days to request a change-of-mind return, more than double the 14 days a distance sale legally requires, extended to 31 January 2027 for anything bought between 23 September and 25 December 2026
  • A long frame guarantee by any standard — 10 years on Habitat's own help page and 15 on the Argos one — covering frame, internal springs, webbing and feet
  • Large item delivery at £9.95 with two-person handling to the room of your choice, a two-hour window and a 30-minute warning call
  • 1,133 Argos points of presence for free collection as at 28 February 2026, including 466 outlets inside Sainsbury's supermarkets, with orders held for seven days
  • Sofa in a Box models from £250 that arrive flat in a box, which is the honest answer to a narrow staircase
  • Recall notices published openly on Habitat's own site with full catalogue numbers and a freephone line, matching the public safety register entry for entry
  • £40 of Argos Plus buys a year of same-day, next-day and standard delivery on orders over £20, which pays for itself on the fifth large-item delivery

Where it falls short

  • A non-refundable £30 collection charge applies to any large home-delivered item returned on a change of mind since 12 June 2025 — three times the £9.95 it cost to deliver, and 12.5% of the price of the cheapest sofa in the range
  • Nowhere on the delivery or returns pages is there a published weight or size threshold for what counts as a "large item", which is the single fact that decides both the £9.95 delivery charge and the £30 collection charge
  • Two of the same company's help centres state the frame guarantee at 10 years and 15 years, and Habitat's own help index links to a page labelled "15 year guarantee" that carries no guarantee terms whatsoever
  • The Made To Order range is excluded from the frame guarantee altogether, and regulation 28(1) of the 2013 Regulations removes the cancellation right on made-to-specification goods — so the dearest sofas have neither cover nor a change of mind
  • Using either guarantee costs a £60 call-out charge up front, refunded only if the inspection finds a fault the guarantee covers
  • No price is published for Argos Care, for Clearabee's furniture collection or for Airtasker assembly, and the assembly page's own promotional offer expired on 30 October 2026
  • No ombudsman or alternative dispute resolution scheme is named on any Habitat or Argos page, and Argos Limited is not a member of the Furniture and Home Improvement Ombudsman, where Heal's, DFS, Dreams, Sofology and Furniture Village all are
  • Three Habitat upholstery ranges — Jackson, Isla and Eton — were recalled on 8 March 2023 for cover fabric that failed the BS5852 test required by the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988

Common questions

Is Habitat the same company as Argos?

In Britain, yes. Habitat's own footer states that "Habitat is a trading name of Argos Limited", company 01081551, registered at 33 Charterhouse Street, London EC1M 6HA, and Argos Limited is owned by J Sainsbury plc. The Habitat business trading at habitat-design.com in Europe is a different company altogether — its own heritage page says the brand there was "relaunched at the start of 2024 by the Vente-unique.com group" and it does not mention the UK.

What does it cost to send a Habitat sofa back?

£30. Habitat's returns page says that "for large home delivered items, a non-refundable collection charge of £30 will be applied to orders returned from 12 June 2025, unless the product is faulty or has been delivered directly by one of our suppliers". You have 30 days from delivery to request the return, and refunds are processed within 14 days of the goods coming back. Small items can be handed in free at any Argos store or any Argos inside a Sainsbury's.

How long is the Habitat sofa guarantee — 10 years or 15?

Both figures are published by the same company and we could not reconcile them. habitat.co.uk/help/sofa-guarantee says "there is a 2 year guarantee for the fabric and 10 years for the frame"; help.argos.co.uk says sofas and armchairs "are covered for 15 years, from the date of purchase" for Habitat-branded items bought on or after August 2024. Both exclude seat pads, back cushion fillings, sofa beds, recliners and the Made To Order range, and both charge a £60 call-out refunded only if a covered fault is found. Ask for the length in writing before you buy.

Can a Habitat sofa be delivered to an upstairs room?

Yes. Argos's delivery FAQ says that for bigger items the driver will contact you on the day with an estimated slot and "they'll safely take your order to your room of choice", with a two-hour window and a call 30 minutes out. Someone has to be in to accept it, because larger and higher-priced items need a photograph or signature and cannot be left. If access is the problem rather than the carrying, the Sofa in a Box models from £250 are built in the room.

Who can I complain to if Habitat will not put things right?

Habitat gives one number, 0345 640 2020, open 8am to 8pm seven days a week, and that is the end of the published route: no ombudsman and no alternative dispute resolution scheme is named on any Habitat or Argos page. The Furniture and Home Improvement Ombudsman runs a free conciliation service for the trade, but checked on 14 November 2026 neither Argos, Habitat nor Sainsbury's was in its member list, while Heal's, DFS, Dreams, Sofology, Bensons for Beds and Furniture Village all were. Beyond the shop, the remaining route is the small claims track.

Have any Habitat sofas been recalled?

Three ranges. The Jackson, Isla and Eton upholstery ranges were recalled on 8 March 2023 under cases 2302-0155, 2303-0001 and 2303-0003, after sale between 14 March 2022 and 28 February 2023, because the cover fabric failed BS5852 and so breached the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988. Habitat's own notice lists every catalogue number and asks owners to call 0800 011 3462 for a replacement model or a refund, keeping the sofa away from any source of fire in the meantime.

Our verdict

Habitat is a 1964 design brand that now lives inside a catalogue retailer, and the practical consequence is that the furniture is Habitat's while the delivery, the returns window, the guarantee and the complaints line are Argos's. On price it is strong and legible: 142 sofas with a real price on each, £240 to £1,095, large-item delivery at £9.95 and two-person handling to the room you pick. On returns it is better than the law requires for 30 days and then charges £30 to collect a large item, which is the number to decide on before you order rather than after. The frame guarantee is the best thing on offer and the least clearly stated — ten years on one of the company's own help pages, fifteen on another, a £60 call-out either way, and nothing at all on the Made To Order range. Buy a Sofa in a Box or something you are confident about, use a collection point if one is near you, and ring 0345 640 2020 to get the guarantee length confirmed in writing before you pay.
