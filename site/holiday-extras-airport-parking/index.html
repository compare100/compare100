Holiday Extras Airport Parking: Prices, Fare Types and What the Voucher Is Worth

Rates and terms checked 11 September 2026 · Airport Parking · Compare100 editorial team

Holiday Extras has been selling the bits of a holiday that happen before the flight since 21 January 1983, when it was incorporated in Kent as Apple Booking Company Limited. It became ABC Holiday Extras Limited in September 2001 and took its present name on 1 May 2003, and it still trades from the same address on the Ashford Road at Newingreen, outside Hythe. Gerry Pack started it to make booking an airport hotel less painful; in 1995 it put the first airport parking booking system in Britain online, and it now says it handles more than 8 million travellers a year through 8,000 travel agents and around 4,000 partnership agreements.

The ownership is unusual enough to be worth knowing before you spend anything. A management buyout in 2018 moved a majority of the shares into an Employee Benefit Trust, and in 2024 Gerry and Carol Pack sold their remaining six million shares to Holiday Extras Investments Limited after 42 years, taking the founding family out of the share register entirely. The group is now held between that trust and a management team led by Matthew Pack, group chief executive since January 2015. Nobody is extracting a dividend to a private equity house, which is not something most of this trade can say.

What you are buying is a booking, not a car park. Holiday Extras sells space in other companies' car parks and puts them in a price ladder next to each other. On 11 September 2026 the Gatwick page opened at £5.74 a day, with Purple Parking and Cophall both at £45.95 for eight days, APH Park & Ride at £57.92, and meet and greet from Meteor and I Love at £71.00 against ACE Parking at £94.50. Heathrow ran from Maple Parking's Express Park & Ride for terminals 2 and 3 at £11.33 a day, or £90.62 for the week, up to MBW Meet and Greet at terminal 5 at £15.86 a day. Purple Parking, which the group itself owns, sat in the middle of that list rather than at the top of it.

Three fare types, and the voucher that sits behind two of them

Almost every complaint about airport parking comes down to somebody buying the cheapest fare and assuming it behaves like the dearest one. Holiday Extras sells three, and the differences are set out plainly enough on its own help pages.

A fully flexible booking, which the company brands Flextras, can be amended or cancelled right up to the time you were booked to arrive. Cancel one and you do not get your money back: you get a voucher for the full value, redeemable against a future booking. A Saver fare can also be cancelled for a voucher, but the voucher runs for a shorter period and the company states flatly that it cannot offer a cash refund on saver products at all. A Supersaver, sold under the label Can't Cancel, is what it says — no amendment, no cancellation, no voucher, no refund.

The voucher is the part that catches people. It can be spent in instalments, and any unused balance keeps the original expiry date rather than resetting. You can convert one to cash, but only within 14 days of the voucher being issued, and every such conversion carries a £10 processing fee. So a £45.95 week at Gatwick cancelled on a flexible fare and taken as cash comes back as £35.95, and only if you act inside a fortnight. Leave it longer and the money stays in the Holiday Extras system until it expires.

Three airports break the pattern and the headline does not mention them. Parking at Manchester, Stansted and East Midlands can only be cancelled up to midnight four days before arrival, not up to the arrival time itself. Transfers close 24 hours ahead and car hire 48 hours ahead.

No amendment fee is a real advantage, and it is unusual

The clearest thing in Holiday Extras' favour is a charge it does not make. Its own help page says: "We don't charge any amendment fees. When you change your booking you'll only need to pay the difference if the price has altered." Changing a car registration or a name costs nothing. The parking trade generally charges for this — a mid-term change of £20 is ordinary in adjacent markets, and the broker fee schedules published elsewhere in travel and insurance run from £20 to £100 for changes and cancellations.

The site also states there are no hidden booking fees. That matters more than it sounds, because a non-refundable card booking fee is the standard complaint against this trade, and it is exactly the practice the Competition and Markets Authority named when it turned its attention to unavoidable charges at checkout. A firm that has removed both the amendment fee and the booking fee has removed the two charges most likely to turn a £45.95 advertised price into something else at the payment screen.

Against that, the cheap fares carry their inflexibility in the price rather than in a fee, which is a more honest way of doing it but produces the same outcome for a customer who has to cancel.

Where the price promise stops

"Never Beaten on Price" appears on every parking page. The conditions behind it are narrower than the phrase suggests, and they are published, so they can be checked.

You have 24 hours from making and paying for the booking to claim. The cheaper product you found must be, in the company's words, "exactly the same in every respect, including being provided by the same supplier as you have purchased from Holiday Extras, with the same cancellation terms". A rival selling the same car park on a stricter cancellation basis does not count. You submit a screenshot through a form, the company answers within three days, and a successful claim is refunded to the card.

The exclusions are long. The guarantee does not cover London City or Republic of Ireland hotels and parking, hotel packages including coach or rail, coach travel, insurance, overseas car hire, lounges or transfers, theatre and theme park breaks, payment surcharges or cancellation protection. Prices conditional on buying something else, and prices from loyalty or rewards schemes, are out too. On the products it does cover, the promise is genuine; it is just a good deal smaller than a banner reading "never beaten on price" implies.

One arm is regulated, and the one you are buying is not

Holiday Extras is two quite different businesses wearing one name, and only one of them has a regulator.

Holiday Extras Cover Limited (company 05058680) is authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority under firm reference 828848. The travel insurance it arranges is underwritten by Great Lakes Insurance UK Limited (company 13436330, firm reference 955859, of 1 Fen Court in the City), which is authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority and regulated by both. Buy that policy and you get the whole apparatus behind it: a regulated intermediary, a regulated insurer, and a free route to the Financial Ombudsman Service if the claim goes wrong. Cover starts from 10p a day on the company's own stated basis — one traveller, average age 45, Europe, bronze annual cover, priced on 22 April 2026 — and the headline extras are £25 back for a delayed flight and round-the-clock GP access.

The parking booking sitting in the same basket has none of that. There is no FCA permission over a car park, no ombudsman for a scratched wing, and no compensation scheme if the operator stops trading. What you have instead is the Consumer Rights Act 2015 duty to perform a service with reasonable care and skill, the ordinary law of bailment once an attendant takes your keys, and the county court. Two purchases, one checkout, two completely different levels of protection — and nothing on the payment page says so.

The ATOL logo, and what the company itself says about it

Credit first, because this is better than most of the trade manages. Holiday Extras publishes an explanation of the ATOL scheme that is accurate and unusually blunt about its own position: ATOL protects flights and air holidays, and "as a holiday extras provider, Holiday Extras is not eligible to be ATOL protected". Very few travel businesses volunteer the limits of their own bonding.

The awkward part is that the ABTA and ATOL logos appear on the company information page and on the about pages, where that caveat does not. A customer who never reaches the FAQ could reasonably finish a booking believing their parking is bonded. It is not. The five-digit ABTA membership number, which is the thing that would let anyone verify the membership independently through ABTA's own member search, is not published on the company information page either — that needs confirming directly with ABTA rather than assumed from a logo.

ABTA membership would matter, because it brings an alternative dispute resolution route with a stated aim of responding within 28 days. That is more than a standalone car park operator offers, where no ADR scheme exists at all. It is worth establishing which of your bookings it covers before you need it.

Small print that has not been read for a while

Two things on the site suggest the legal pages are not reviewed as often as the prices.

The booking conditions for the company's theatre breaks accept responsibility "as an organiser under the Package Travel, Package Holidays and Package Tours Regulations 1992". Those Regulations are marked as revoked on the statute book. They were replaced by the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018, which widened the definition of a package, invented the linked travel arrangement, and tightened the insolvency protection an organiser has to hold. Citing the old instrument does not reduce anyone's rights — the current Regulations apply whatever the terms say — but it is a live contract page referring to law that has been off the books for eight years.

The second is smaller and more likely to affect you. The customer FAQ says a cancellation voucher must be used "within 2 years of issue". The dedicated cancellation charges page says a flexible booking's voucher runs for three years and a Saver's for 18 months. Both pages are the company's own, and they cannot all be right. If you are holding a voucher, work to the shortest figure and ring before you rely on it.

The same theatre breaks terms also say: "All of our packages are non-cancellable, non-refundable and non-amendable." That sits at some distance from the "cancel for free" message carried across the parking pages, and a customer moving between products on one site would have no reason to expect the switch.

What the prices actually looked like

Prices move daily, so treat these as a snapshot taken on 11 September 2026 rather than a quote. The hub page advertised parking from £5 a day, with eight days at Gatwick from £45.89, Heathrow from £89.95 and Manchester from £49.99, and hotel-and-parking bundles from £33. The individual airport pages, loaded the same day, opened at £5.74 a day at Gatwick and £11.33 at Heathrow. That is normal drift on a live inventory rather than a contradiction, but it does mean the number on the hub page is a floor across a season and not a price you can hold the company to.

The more useful figure is the spread. At Gatwick the gap between the cheapest park and ride at £45.95 for eight days and the dearest meet and greet at £94.50 is a little over double. At Heathrow the ladder ran from £90.62 a week to £126.90. Meet and greet is not a small premium on this evidence, and the eight-day park and ride price at Gatwick was roughly half the Heathrow equivalent for the same service.

Holiday Extras says it covers 19 UK airports and hundreds of approved car parks, including the Irish airports, and it puts competitors' products above its own in the results where they are cheaper. That last point is the strongest argument for using it: a booking agent that ranks a rival ahead of a car park it owns is behaving like a comparison site rather than a shop window.

Where it wins

  • No amendment fees at all — changing dates, times, names or a car registration costs only the price difference
  • No booking fee, which removes the charge most often added at the payment screen in this trade
  • Flexible fares can be cancelled right up to the booked arrival time at most airports
  • Competitors' car parks are listed above the group's own where they are cheaper, including Purple Parking, which it owns
  • 19 UK airports and hundreds of car parks in one search, from £5.74 a day at Gatwick on 11 September 2026
  • Employee-owned through a benefit trust since 2018, with no external investor to pay
  • Trading since January 1983 and the first company to sell airport parking online in Britain, in 1995
  • The travel insurance arm is separately FCA authorised, firm reference 828848, so that purchase carries an ombudsman route

Where it falls short

  • Cancelling a flexible booking returns a voucher, not your money — cash is only available within 14 days of issue and always costs a £10 processing fee
  • Saver fares cannot be refunded in cash under any circumstances, and Supersaver bookings cannot be cancelled, amended or exchanged for a voucher at all
  • The company's own pages disagree about how long a voucher lasts: 2 years on the FAQ, 3 years for flexible and 18 months for Saver on the cancellation page
  • "Never Beaten on Price" runs for only 24 hours, requires an identical product from the identical supplier on identical cancellation terms, and excludes lounges, transfers, overseas car hire, coach travel and insurance
  • Parking at Manchester, Stansted and East Midlands closes to cancellation four days before arrival, which the site-wide "up to the day before" message does not mention
  • The theatre breaks booking conditions still cite the Package Travel, Package Holidays and Package Tours Regulations 1992, revoked and replaced in 2018
  • ABTA and ATOL logos appear on the company information page without the caveat, published elsewhere on the site, that a holiday extras provider is not eligible for ATOL protection
  • No ombudsman, ADR scheme or compensation scheme covers the parking itself, and the five-digit ABTA number is not published where a customer would look for it

Common questions

Can I get my money back if I cancel Holiday Extras parking?

Not usually as cash by default. Cancelling a fully flexible booking produces a voucher for the full value. You can swap that voucher for a cash refund, but only within 14 days of it being issued and always with a £10 processing fee deducted. Saver bookings produce a voucher and no cash refund at all, and Supersaver bookings produce nothing — they cannot be cancelled or amended.

Does Holiday Extras charge a booking fee?

Its parking pages state there are no hidden booking fees, and its help pages say it charges no amendment fees either — a change costs only the price difference, if there is one. That makes it one of the few operators in this market where the price on the results page is the price at the payment screen.

How long do I have to claim on the price guarantee?

24 hours from making and paying for the booking. The cheaper offer must be the same product from the same supplier on the same cancellation terms, evidenced by a screenshot submitted through the claim form. Holiday Extras answers within three days and refunds the difference to the card used. Lounges, transfers, overseas car hire, insurance, coach travel and theatre breaks are excluded.

Is my Holiday Extras parking booking financially protected?

The company states on its own site that as a holiday extras provider it is not eligible for ATOL protection, which covers flights and air holidays. There is no ombudsman for a parking dispute. Your rights come from the Consumer Rights Act 2015, which requires a service to be performed with reasonable care and skill, and from the law of bailment once staff take your keys. Travel insurance bought from Holiday Extras Cover Limited is a separate matter and does carry an ombudsman route.

Who owns Holiday Extras?

An Employee Benefit Trust holds the majority, alongside a management team led by group chief executive Matthew Pack. A management buyout in 2018 moved most of the shares into the trust, and in 2024 founders Gerry and Carol Pack sold their remaining six million shares to Holiday Extras Investments Limited, ending 42 years of family ownership.

Which airports does Holiday Extras cover?

It lists 19 UK airports and hundreds of car parks, including Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted, Manchester, Birmingham, Luton, East Midlands, Bristol, Liverpool, Leeds Bradford, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Newcastle, Cardiff, London City, Southampton and Aberdeen, along with Dublin, Shannon and Belfast.

Our verdict

A booking agent worth using for the two charges it has taken out rather than the guarantee it advertises. No amendment fee and no booking fee are real money, the ladder at Gatwick opened at £5.74 a day, and listing Purple Parking among rivals rather than above them is the behaviour of a comparison site. Read the fare label before you pay: only the flexible one gives you anything back, and even then it is a voucher with a £10 exit charge, not a refund.
