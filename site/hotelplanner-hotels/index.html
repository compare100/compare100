HotelPlanner: Group Hotel Rates and How the Bidding Works

Rates and terms checked 22 September 2026 · Hotels · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

HotelPlanner's London hotel list opens at $60 a night — a Travelodge out at Dagenham East — and the company puts the average three-star London group rate it booked over the past 365 days at $111.36. Both figures come from HotelPlanner's own city pages, read on 22 September 2026. The business has been selling rooms since 2003, says it works with 100,000 group-friendly hotels, and runs its European operation from Level 5a Maple House on Tottenham Court Road with a UK freephone line on 0800 808 5603.

One thing is worth knowing before you click, because it decides whether the site is any use to you. For a single room, HotelPlanner behaves like any other booking site: pick a hotel, pay, done. For a group it does not sell you anything at all — you fill in a form, a planner is assigned, hotels bid against each other for your block, and you compare and haggle online before choosing. The lowest-rate guarantee attaches at nine rooms a night or more, and on most group bookings you settle up with the hotel directly rather than with HotelPlanner. If you want an instant confirmation for a Friday night, that is a different product from the one this page is about.

The company you are dealing with is Reservations Group, LLC of 11780 U.S. Highway One, Palm Beach Gardens, Florida, trading as HotelPlanner.com. The UK arm, HotelPlanner Limited, is company 08235172, incorporated on 1 October 2012. One genuinely good thing sits in the small print for British readers: where most booking platforms cap what they will pay you at the amount you handed over, HotelPlanner's terms cap its liability to a UK or EU consumer at the greater of three times the value of the booking or €2,500. That is a more generous ceiling than its larger rivals publish.

Nine rooms is the line, and crossing it changes the price you are shown

Almost every hotel site sells you the rate the hotel has loaded that morning. HotelPlanner's group side runs the transaction backwards: your requirement goes out to matching properties, they quote against it, and the quotes land in one place where you can push back on them. The company describes the result plainly enough — "Our private rates provided to you are typically lower than online rates offered to the general public" — and the process costs nothing to start: "Pay the hotel separately. It's FREE with no obligations!"

The guarantee that sits behind the marketing is narrower than the headline, and reading it is worth a minute. HotelPlanner says it "will meet or beat any price on your group's travel needs when reserving 9 rooms per night or more at a hotel of equal or greater quality". Three conditions, all of them doing work: nine rooms, per night, and a like-for-like property. The free rooms offered to whoever is organising the trip carry their own qualifier — they apply "to participating hotels only and required group size may vary among hotels" — so the coordinator's free bed is a hotel-by-hotel matter, not a HotelPlanner policy.

The two averages the site publishes give you a sense of what actually clears. London three-star group rooms averaged $111.36 over the past year and Manchester $90.30, which puts the advertised "up to 70%" saving in proportion: it is a ceiling drawn from somebody's best week, not a number to budget on. Set those averages against the $60 London lead-in on the individual side of the site and the group route is not automatically cheaper per room — it is cheaper for a block of rooms that a hotel would rather fill in one go than one at a time.

The commission is printed, which is rarer than it sounds

Booking platforms tend to treat what they earn as a trade secret. HotelPlanner puts it in its terms: a 10% referral fee on business that actualises, dropping to 7% for hotels that take a preferred membership, and rising to 12% where a travel agent brings the group. Agents who register get "a flat rate equal to a 10% referral fee on hotel room revenue produced", paid once HotelPlanner has itself been paid by the hotel, and the hotel's own fee falls due within 21 days of the group's departure date.

That tells a group organiser something useful. The bids you receive already have a commission of up to a tenth built into them, and the hotel knows it — which is the honest explanation for why a direct call to the same property sometimes beats the platform, and why it often does not, since the hotel is paying for a sales channel it would otherwise have to staff itself. Publishing the rate is a point in the company's favour, and it is the sort of thing a reader can check against any quote they are given.

There is one charge pointed the other way. Cancel a non-refundable booking and HotelPlanner says it "will honor your request for a refund if full waiver is obtained, but reserves the right to charge you 3% transaction fee". Getting a hotel to waive a non-refundable rate is a favour, and a 3% fee for extracting it is not unreasonable — but it is money you will not see coming unless you have read that clause.

A Florida company with a London office, and a fair bit of history

Reservations Group has been at this for 23 years, and the scale it claims now is substantial: around 2,000 call centre staff, 36,000 destinations, more than 1 million groups booked and $2bn of projected gross hotel sales for 2025, on revenue growth the company puts at 351% since 2022. It placed 917th on the Inc. 5000 list of fast-growing American private companies announced on 18 August 2026.

It came close to being a public company and is not one. In August 2021 HotelPlanner and Reservations.com agreed a three-way merger with Astrea Acquisition Corp, a blank-cheque vehicle, at an enterprise value of $567.1m, with more than $120m of cash expected onto the balance sheet and 2022 revenue forecast at around $170m. The deal was called off on 14 February 2022, the three parties saying only that terminating it "is the best path forward for all parties". No reason was given then and none has been given since, so the projections in that filing are the last detailed financial picture anybody outside the company has had.

The UK entity is smaller than the London address suggests. HotelPlanner Limited files total exemption full accounts — the small-company regime, which means a balance sheet and no profit and loss account, so no turnover, no booking volume and no UK customer count is on the public record. Its registered office moved to Tottenham Court Road on 30 April 2025, and its SIC code, 82990, is the catch-all for business support services rather than anything travel-specific.

It quietly runs other people's group desks

Type a group request into Hotels.com and the page you land on is not run by Hotels.com. Its group travel terms name "Reservations Group Incorporated, operating under the name HotelPlanner" at the same Palm Beach Gardens address, and cap the hotel voucher rebate on offer at $250 per group stay. Expedia runs a separate group booking domain on the same pattern. The company also says more than 4,000 affiliates use its technology, and it has held the group hotel contract for sports and tourism bodies including the United Soccer League and the LPGA.

For a reader comparing group tools, that matters more than any marketing claim on this page: several of the "different" group booking forms on the internet are the same engine wearing different clothes. Going straight to HotelPlanner removes a layer, and the quotes you get back are drawn from the same pool either way.

The phones are answered by software now

HotelPlanner was an early and unusually committed adopter of voice AI. It announced an end-to-end AI voice reservationist in late 2024 handling around 10,000 calls a day in 15 languages, trained on 8 million recorded customer calls, and launched the product commercially as Reservations.ai on 22 October 2025. By that launch the company said the AI was driving 40% of HotelPlanner bookings, working alongside the human call centre rather than replacing it — co-founder John Prince's framing was that "our human call centre team can look after customers with the more complicated and higher value bookings".

The practical reading for a group organiser: a straightforward block of rooms will likely be quoted and confirmed by a machine, quickly, at any hour. Anything with a wrinkle in it — a split stay, a late rooming list, a contract the venue wants changing — is where you should ask for the human planner the site promises to assign you, and where you will find out how good that assignment is.

What a UK group booking is actually worth

Group hotel bookings in Britain are largely business events, and the sector is bigger than most people assume. The Culture, Media and Sport Committee's report of 6 July 2026 put UK business events at £33.6 billion a year, over half of the £61.7 billion generated by the wider events industry, and noted that 38% of business events take place outside London. Domestic conferences and meetings alone ran to 1.08 million events with 95.4 million delegates and £19.3 billion of value in 2024.

The inbound side is where the money per head sits. VisitBritain counts 6.8 million inbound business visits in 2024 worth £5.9 billion, of which the meetings and events segment accounts for 2.6 million visits and £2.8 billion. A delegate arriving from outside Europe spends an average of £1,824; one from within Europe £872; a UK delegate £328. The committee also flagged the uncomfortable backdrop — Britain ranked 113th of 119 countries on travel and tourism cost competitiveness in 2024 — which is the reason a hotel would rather discount a block of nine rooms than watch the conference go to Lisbon.

Where the small print sends a British customer

The terms you accept are Florida's. Disputes "shall be resolved exclusively through final and binding arbitration in Palm Beach County, Florida, rather than in court", with a class action waiver attached and a window of 30 days from first accessing the site in which to reject arbitration in writing. UK and EU consumers are treated better than most — they are "encouraged but not required" to arbitrate — but the alternative the terms point them at is the European Commission's Online Dispute Resolution platform, and that platform was switched off on 20 July 2025 under Regulation (EU) 2024/3228. It had not covered UK consumers since Brexit in any case. The signpost leads nowhere, and there is no named UK or EU alternative dispute resolution scheme in its place.

Nor is there a trade body standing behind the booking. A hotel booked on its own is not a package under the Package Travel Regulations, so there is no ATOL certificate and no ABTA bonding, and no ABTA membership number appears anywhere on HotelPlanner's site. On the group side the money usually goes to the hotel rather than the platform, which is convenient until something goes wrong: a company that never took your payment has very little it can refund, and the generous-looking liability cap is measured against a booking value it may never have handled.

One fee deserves reading twice. The terms describe "Packaged Bundled Costs" as "a mandatory charge retained by ResGroup as compensation for those services and benefits", included in the total price displayed. Including it in the displayed total is the right side of the law — showing a price that grows at the checkout has been prohibited since the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024's consumer provisions came into force on 7 April 2025, backed by fines of up to 10% of global turnover. But the clause never says how large the charge is or what it buys, so the one number a customer would want from it is absent. For the record, HotelPlanner was not among the firms the Competition and Markets Authority took action against over hotel booking displays in September 2019.

Three numbers on the same website that do not match

The group threshold changes depending on which page you are standing on. The home page asks "Need 5+ rooms per night?" and then promises "Guaranteed Lowest Group Rates" when "reserving 10 rooms per night or more"; the London, Manchester and Group Rate pages all say 9. Three minimums, one company, and no way for a reader to know which applies to them until a planner tells them.

The inventory count moves about too. The home page title offers "250K+ Hotels", the body of the same page says "100,000 group friendly hotels worldwide", the travel agent page says "over 100,000 hotels and meeting venues", and the partnership copy says "1M+ properties". Those may all be true of different things — directly contracted hotels, group-capable hotels, total bookable properties — but nothing on the site explains which is which.

Then there is the matter of the register. HotelPlanner Limited received a first Gazette notice for compulsory strike-off on 4 March 2025. It filed two years of overdue accounts — for 2023 and 2024 — on 28 March, and the strike-off action was discontinued on 29 March 2025. The company was never in any danger of vanishing, since the trading entity is American and the UK company is an office rather than the till. It is still a fair thing to know about the London address printed on the contact page, and the accounts filed to clear it tell a reader nothing, because the small-company regime does not require a profit and loss account.

Where it wins

  • Group pricing is genuinely negotiated rather than listed — hotels bid for the block and you can push back on the quotes online, at no cost and with no obligation
  • The commission is published: 10% as standard, 7% for preferred hotels and 12% on travel agent bookings, which lets you judge any quote you are given
  • The liability cap for UK and EU consumers is the greater of three times the booking value or €2,500 — more generous than the 'what you paid us' cap most booking platforms use
  • Real UK contact points: a London office on Tottenham Court Road, a group freephone on 0800 808 5603 and a 24-hour line
  • 23 years of trading, around 2,000 call centre staff and over 1 million groups booked — this is an established operator, not a shell
  • The AI booking line answers quickly at any hour and handles around 10,000 calls a day in 15 languages

Where it falls short

  • The dispute clause sends you to Florida: binding arbitration in Palm Beach County under Florida law, with a class action waiver and only 30 days from your first visit to opt out
  • The alternative dispute route offered to UK and EU consumers is the EU Online Dispute Resolution platform, which was shut down on 20 July 2025 and never covered UK consumers after Brexit — no replacement scheme is named
  • The group minimum is stated as 5, 9 and 10 rooms per night on different pages of the same website, and the inventory as 100,000, 250,000 and 1 million properties
  • 'Packaged Bundled Costs' is described as a mandatory charge retained by the company, but the terms never say how much it is or what it pays for
  • On most group bookings you pay the hotel directly, so the platform has taken no money from you and has correspondingly little to give back if the booking fails
  • Prices on the UK city pages are quoted in US dollars, and no ABTA membership or ATOL cover applies to a hotel-only booking
  • The UK company files small-company accounts with no profit and loss account, and picked up a first Gazette strike-off notice on 4 March 2025 before clearing two years of late filings

Common questions

How many rooms do I need before HotelPlanner is worth using?

The lowest-rate guarantee attaches at nine rooms per night or more at a hotel of equal or greater quality, which is the figure given on the city and Group Rate pages. The home page separately invites enquiries from five rooms up and elsewhere quotes ten, so the safe reading is that five will get you a quote and nine is where the guarantee bites. Below that you are better off on the individual side of the site, where London rooms start at $60 a night.

Do I pay HotelPlanner or the hotel?

On group bookings the usual arrangement is that you settle with the hotel directly — the site's own words are "Pay the hotel separately". HotelPlanner is paid by the hotel afterwards, at 10% of the room revenue as standard, 7% for preferred-member properties and 12% where a travel agent is involved, due within 21 days of the group's departure. Individual reservations made through the site are paid for in the usual way at the time of booking.

What protection do I have if something goes wrong?

A hotel booked on its own is not a package, so there is no ATOL certificate and no ABTA bonding. HotelPlanner's terms cap its liability to a UK or EU consumer at the greater of three times the booking value or €2,500, which is better than most platforms offer, but they also route disputes to arbitration in Palm Beach County, Florida under Florida law. The alternative route the terms name for European consumers, the EU Online Dispute Resolution platform, closed on 20 July 2025. If you paid by credit card, the usual UK card protections still apply to your own payment.

Is the 'up to 70%' saving real?

Treat it as a ceiling rather than an expectation. The numbers HotelPlanner publishes itself are more useful: its average three-star London group rate over the 365 days to 22 September 2026 was $111.36, and Manchester was $90.30. The saving on any particular block depends on how badly the hotel wants to fill those nights, which is the whole point of putting the request out to bid.

Who actually runs HotelPlanner?

Reservations Group, LLC of Palm Beach Gardens, Florida, trading as HotelPlanner.com and operating since 2003. The UK arm is HotelPlanner Limited, company number 08235172, incorporated on 1 October 2012 and registered at Level 5a Maple House, 149 Tottenham Court Road, London W1T 7NF. The group also powers the group booking services of some larger travel brands, and it agreed and then abandoned a US stock market listing through a $567.1m merger with Astrea Acquisition Corp, terminated on 14 February 2022.

Our verdict

HotelPlanner is a specialist doing something the big booking sites do badly: putting a block of nine or more rooms out to competitive bid and letting the organiser haggle. It has been at it since 2003, it publishes the commission it takes, and it quietly runs the group desks behind several household-name travel brands. The reservations are about paperwork rather than competence — Florida arbitration, a dead European dispute link, a mandatory charge of unstated size, and a group threshold the site states three different ways. For a wedding block, a conference or a team trip it is well worth putting a request through and seeing what comes back, since it costs nothing to ask. For one room on one night, a straightforward booking site will serve you better.
