iD Mobile SIM Only Review: Plans, Bill Cap and Rollover

Rates and terms checked 20 November 2026 · SIM Only · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

iD Mobile sells SIM-only plans from £6 a month, and that £6 buys 6GB on a 30-day rolling contract, 8GB over 12 months or 10GB over 24 months. Every plan on the shop page carries unlimited UK minutes and texts, 5G at no extra cost, data that rolls over into the next month, and a bill cap you set yourself. Figures checked 20 November 2026 against iD Mobile’s own pages.

Two of those features are free and neither is standard. The bill cap lets you hold out-of-plan spending to any figure between £0 and your credit limit, adjusted in the app whenever you like, and unused data rolls over once into the following month and is spent first. The price ladder is also graded rather than flat: at £7 a month, a 24-month commitment buys 30GB against 10GB on a rolling month, which is three times the data for the same money. The provider’s own price-match wording claims it was cheaper on 100.0% of 195 like-for-like plans matched between 10 April and 10 July 2026.

Two things to know before you click. iD Mobile runs on Three’s masts, and no page on which you can buy a SIM says so — the network is named nowhere in the shop or in the Post Pay terms, which refer only to the Network. And a SIM-only plan here is a credit product in the sense that matters: iD’s own page states that for a Pay Monthly SIM only deal you will need to pass a credit check, so the cheapest rung is not open to everybody.

What £6, £7 and £8 buy across three contract lengths

Most networks sell one plan on one term. iD Mobile sells the same three entry prices on all three of its terms, so the cost of committing is published rather than implied. On the SIM-only shop page on 20 November 2026 the standard ladder read:

  • £6 a month: 6GB rolling, 8GB over 12 months, 10GB over 24 months
  • £7 a month: 10GB rolling, 20GB over 12 months, 30GB over 24 months
  • £8 a month: 40GB rolling, 50GB over 12 months, 60GB over 24 months

Read down rather than across and the reward for signing up is consistent in direction but wildly uneven in size. At £7 the 24-month plan is worth three times the rolling one. At £8 it is worth half again. At £6 the gain is 4GB, which on an entry plan is the difference between running out and not. The rolling ladder also climbs much higher on its own: £10 buys 80GB, £12 buys 120GB, £14 buys 200GB, £15 buys 250GB and £16 buys 300GB, none of which has a standard equivalent on the longer terms.

Unlimited data runs the other way, and it is the clearest number on the page. It is £18 a month on a rolling contract, £17 over 12 months and £16 over 24 — so two years of lock-in is priced at £2 a month, or £48 over the term. The same £16 buys 300GB on a rolling month or unlimited over 24, which is the honest way of saying that at the top of the ladder the commitment buys headroom rather than volume. Set any of it against Ofcom’s measured average of 11.8GB a month per mobile data user and most of the ladder is sold above the need it serves.

Alongside the standard rungs sit half-price introductory plans, and these are the ones to read twice. A 12-month 150GB plan is advertised at £6.00 per month and then £12.00 per month from your January 2027 bill; a 24-month 100GB plan is £5.00 and then £10.00 per month from your April 2027 bill. The discount is 50% of the monthly charge for the first three or six bills and the standard rate applies from month four or seven. The retailer prints both numbers and the date, which is more than the rules require, and the arithmetic is worth doing anyway: the 24-month 100GB plan costs £30 for its first six months and £180 for the remaining eighteen.

The bill cap is real, and the credit limit beside it is not a cap at all

Bill capping is the feature iD Mobile leads on and it does what the page says. It is described as a money-saving feature on all iD Mobile plans, it is set and adjusted in the app or in My Account at any time, and it can be put anywhere between £0 and your credit limit. Run out of an allowance and keep using the phone and you are charged at out-of-plan rates only until the cap is reached; at the cap, or with the cap at £0, calls, texts and data that carry an extra charge simply stop. You can then raise the cap up to your credit limit or buy an add-on.

What makes this worth a section is the distinction iD’s own terms draw next to it. The credit limit is a separate thing: clause 4.11 of the Post Pay terms lets iD set a limit on the charges you may incur in a calendar month, and clause 4.13 then states that the Credit Limit does not act as a cap to prevent further Charges arising. So one of the two numbers on your account stops the bill and the other only measures it. Networks are routinely criticised for blurring exactly that line, and here the paperwork separates them in plain words.

The cap has limits a reader should know. Your standard monthly cost and optional extras, including add-ons or paper billing, sit outside the capped amount, so a £0 cap does not mean a £0 bill — it means a bill of exactly your line rental. The capping page lists no other exclusions at all: premium-rate calls and texts, direct-to-bill purchases and charity donations are the charges that most often defeat a cap elsewhere, and none of them is addressed. Roaming is handled: with a £0 cap you can still use the phone in the 50 inclusive destinations while you have allowance left, and nowhere else. There is no preset menu of cap amounts and no statement of whether a cap is applied by default, so a customer who never opens the app is a customer with no cap set.

Rollover, and the number behind unlimited that nobody publishes

Data rollover is the second free feature and the footnote is admirably short: unused monthly data rolls over once into the next month and is used first, rollover data expires after one month, and it is available on all iD Mobile pay-monthly plans. Used first is the part that matters, because it is the difference between a rollover that works and one that quietly expires behind a fresh allowance. Rolling over once also means the benefit does not accumulate: a light month followed by two more light months banks nothing by the third.

Against that, iD Mobile publishes no fair-use number for unlimited data in the UK at all. There is no stated gigabyte ceiling, no tethering device limit and no hotspot restriction anywhere on the SIM-only pages, the capping page or the roaming policy. The only hard figure attached to the word unlimited is the 30GB roaming cap, and that applies abroad. A reader cannot establish what unlimited means on a British SIM, which is an absence rather than a trap, and it is a fair thing to say plainly.

Minutes and texts are described as unlimited throughout, with the promotional footnote adding only that any additional usage not included within your monthly allowances will be charged as standard. The per-minute, per-text and per-megabyte UK rates are not in the Post Pay terms either: clause 1.1 defers them to a separate List of Charges on the website. Reconnection is one of the few charges the contract does name — clause 8.10 sets it at £10.00 where the services have been temporarily suspended.

Roaming in 50 destinations, and the two-step surcharge behind it

The roaming claim is the boldest thing on the site: roam in 50 destinations, which the page says is more than any other UK network. The structure underneath is a 30GB monthly cap for customers who joined on or after 21 June 2023, with customers who joined or upgraded on or before 20 June 2023 able to use their full allowance. On top of that sits a promotion the fair usage page describes in its own words: from 1 October 2024 and until further notice, all customers can use their full UK data allowance in Roam Free destinations, with iD promising to notify customers when the promotion ends and the 30GB roaming data limit is reinstated. So the published limit and the live position differ, the provider says so, and the concession has no end date.

Go past 30GB when the cap is in force and the charge arrives in two steps rather than one. A surcharge of £3 per GB applies for the remainder of your data allowance, including any rolled-over data or add-ons, and after that you pay the standard out-of-plan rate of 10p per MB. Clause 4.2.1 of the Post Pay terms prints the conversion itself: £0.10 per Mb, equivalent to £102.40 per GB. A provider doing that arithmetic on the reader’s behalf is rarer than it should be. Clause 4.2 also sets a data roaming spend limit of £45 excluding VAT per month, and clause 4.3 adds a £0.25 per MB surcharge on data used beyond the reasonable data allowance in the EEA. Calls and texts abroad are 3.2p per minute and 1p per text.

Two conditions sit behind the word free. The fair usage policy applies to all Roam Free destinations except Ireland. And spend 63 days or more in those destinations over a 4-month period and further charges apply, because the feature is for UK residents who travel occasionally rather than people living abroad. iD texts at 80% and 100% of both the monthly allowance and the 30GB roaming limit, and gives 14 days’ notice before charges are applied.

Who you are actually buying from, and the masts nobody mentions

There are two companies here and only one of them is on your contract. Clause 1.1 of the Post Pay terms defines we, iD Mobile and us as Currys Group Limited, company 504877, registered at 1 Portal Way, London W3 6RS, and names it the supplier of the services. Meanwhile ID MOBILE LIMITED exists as company 09304672, was incorporated on 11 November 2014, is active, carries SIC code 61900 for other telecommunications activities, and has never changed its name. Its registered office moved from 1 Portal Way to Currys Newark Campus, Long Hollow Way, Newark NG24 2NH on 28 July 2026. Nothing improper follows from any of that, but a customer chasing a contractual question needs the name on the agreement rather than the name on the SIM.

The register also explains why no standalone trading figures exist. ID MOBILE LIMITED last filed audit exemption subsidiary accounts made up to 3 May 2025 on 31 October 2025, running to 22 pages, alongside a parent-company statement of guarantee and 225 pages of consolidated parent accounts. Full accounts were filed every year up to the period ending 29 April 2023, and the switch to audit exemption came after that. The next accounts, to 30 April 2026, are due by 31 January 2027, so nothing is late. The board is three directors — appointed 1 September 2021, 27 April 2023 and 17 July 2026 — against 10 resigned officers, and the persons-with-significant-control tab returned an error on 20 November 2026, so the ownership chain could not be read from the register directly.

The network is the more practical gap. iD Mobile runs on Three, and none of the SIM-only pages names it, nor do the Post Pay terms, which refer to the Network and the iD Mobile Network throughout. No coverage percentage is published anywhere on those pages either — the claims are unquantified, and the only figure found was 5G coverage in more than 400 locations across the UK on the plans page. That matters because the independent evidence runs in iD’s favour, not against it. Which?, surveying 5,137 mobile customers in January 2026 and publishing on 28 July 2026, scored iD Mobile at 73% on a sample of 191 against Three at 65% on 452 — an eight-point gap over its own host — and named iD Mobile one of five Great Value providers. A brand that outscores the masts it rents has every reason to say whose masts they are.

Switching away, the eSIM, and the social tariff that does not exist here

Switching rules are not the same for a mobile plan as for broadband, and the difference is worth knowing because it is widely assumed otherwise. One Touch Switch — the gaining-provider-led process that went live on 12 September 2024 after an original industry deadline of 3 April 2023 — is set by conditions C7.18 to C7.27 of Ofcom’s General Conditions and applies, in Ofcom’s own words, to all fixed voice and broadband services, regardless of the technology or network the provider uses. A Fixed Communications Service is defined as one provided at a fixed location, and Ofcom explains the carve-out directly: mobile communications services are defined as services capable of being used while in motion. So leaving iD Mobile still runs on the older text-code route, not on one touch, and a mobile-network home broadband product would be the thing inside the newer regime.

eSIM is supported and the mechanics are plain. You choose eSIM at checkout, a QR code arrives by email once the order is confirmed, you need Wi-Fi, and the download might take approximately an hour. Compatibility is checked by dialling *#06# and looking for an EID number. Two rules are easy to miss and both are iD’s own words: the eSIMs are for mobile use only and are not currently compatible with tablets and wearables, and deleting your eSIM does not cancel your iD Mobile plan — which is exactly the mistake a customer switching handsets would make. You can move back to a physical SIM at any time, and replacements are requested through the app under Plan, then SIM and Phone Management. No one-eSIM-per-account rule and no limit on re-downloads is published.

On social tariffs the honest answer is that iD Mobile has none. It does not appear anywhere on Ofcom’s list of broadband and phone social tariffs, and neither does Three. The only two mobile-type entries on that list are SMARTY at £12 a month and VOXI For Now at £10 a month, both marked 5G where available and both UK-wide, against eligibility that starts with Universal Credit and extends at the major providers to Pension Credit, Employment and Support Allowance, Jobseeker’s Allowance and Income Support. Ofcom says only that it is encouraging companies to offer social tariffs rather than requiring it. The uncomfortable arithmetic for the two that do is that iD’s ordinary £6 plan undercuts both published mobile social tariffs by £4 and £6 a month, with no benefit check to pass — only a credit check. Ofcom’s page is stamped Published: 6 March 2024 and carries no update date, so treat the prices on it as the position when it was written.

Leaving early costs the whole remainder, and the ombudsman has two names here

The exit terms are where iD Mobile is least generous, and the clause is specific. Early Termination Fees are defined in clause 1.1 as your Tariff multiplied by the remaining Minimum Period. Clause 8.1.1 says that if you end the agreement without giving a reason on at least 30 days’ notice you must immediately pay the charges including any monthly line rental for any remaining Minimum Period, less any reduction iD makes for its avoided costs — and the size of that reduction is nowhere stated. On the 24-month unlimited plan at £16, walking away after six months is a bill for the remaining 18 months before any reduction, which is £288. For comparison, the nearest large rival on this part of the market caps the equivalent charge at 50% of the plan value per remaining month; iD starts from 100%. The one carve-out is clause 4.6.1: where the services are unavailable for 7 days or longer and the root cause is iD’s, no early termination fees apply.

The price-rise promise is better than most and the clause behind it is not. Four SIM-only pages carry the footnote that SIM only plans are not subject to annual CPI or RPI price increases, with no expiry date attached, and the header claim is no annual price rises on our award-winning SIM plans. The Post Pay terms, dated 6th Dec 2023 to Now, nonetheless say at clause 8.4 that iD may make changes to this Agreement and the Services you receive at any time. The pledge is specific and the variation clause is general, and a reader is entitled to notice that the promise lives on the shop page while the power lives in the contract. Handset plans are explicitly outside the pledge: the plans page states that the monthly price on phone plans will increase by £1.80 each April on your bill date.

Complaints are handled well on timings and sloppily on naming. iD aims to post a response with a resolution within 7 days; if that fails you can ask for a review, which ends in a written final position known as a deadlock letter; and you may go to alternative dispute resolution if the complaint is unresolved after six weeks, or at once on a deadlock letter, with 12 months to refer from the date of the letter. Six weeks is the current rule and iD states it correctly on both pages. The scheme itself, though, is called the Communications Ombudsman on the Complaints Code of Practice page and Ombudsman Services: Communications on the complaints procedure page — a name the scheme stopped using in July 2023. The contact details are the same body either way: 0330 440 1614, or PO Box 730, Warrington WA4 6WU. iD’s own complaints line is 0800 049 2402 and its postal address is PO Box 686, Salford M5 0PA, with online agents from 9am to 8pm on weekdays and 9am to 6pm at weekends and on bank holidays. No complaint volumes or resolution percentages are published on any of the three pages.

Where it wins

  • The commitment is priced rather than implied: the same £6, £7 and £8 appear on 30-day, 12-month and 24-month terms, so £7 buys 10GB rolling or 30GB over two years and the reader can judge the trade
  • Bill capping is free on every plan, adjustable between £0 and your credit limit in the app at any time, and the terms are explicit that the credit limit beside it is not itself a cap
  • Unused data rolls over once into the next month and is spent before the new allowance, on all pay-monthly plans
  • The no-annual-price-rise pledge on SIM only carries no expiry date, where several rivals limit theirs by footnote to contracts bought before a stated deadline
  • Roaming is included in 50 destinations with a 30GB cap that has been lifted by an open-ended promotion since 1 October 2024, and iD texts at 80% and 100% of both limits
  • Which? scored iD Mobile 73% against 65% for Three, the network it runs on, and named it one of five Great Value providers from a survey of 5,137 customers
  • The entry price of £6 undercuts both mobile social tariffs on Ofcom’s list, at £10 and £12, with no benefit test to satisfy
  • 5G is included at no extra cost on every SIM-only plan, and unlimited data is £2 a month cheaper over 24 months than on a rolling contract

  • No iD Mobile SIM-only page, and no clause of the Post Pay terms, names Three as the host network or publishes any coverage percentage; the terms refer only to the Network, and the only coverage figure found anywhere was 5G in more than 400 locations

Common questions

Which network does iD Mobile use?

iD Mobile runs on Three’s network, but it does not say so on any page where you can buy a SIM, and the Post Pay terms refer only to the Network and the iD Mobile Network. No coverage percentage is published on the SIM-only pages; the only figure found was 5G coverage in more than 400 locations across the UK on the plans page. Which? scored iD Mobile 73% against Three’s 65% in a survey of 5,137 customers published on 28 July 2026.

Does iD Mobile really not put prices up each year?

On SIM only, that is what it publishes: four of its SIM pages carry the footnote that SIM only plans are not subject to annual CPI or RPI price increases, and unlike several rivals it attaches no end date to the claim. Two qualifications. Clause 8.4 of the Post Pay terms still reserves the right to make changes to the agreement and the services at any time. And handset plans are outside the pledge — the plans page states that the monthly price on phone plans will increase by £1.80 each April on your bill date.

How does the bill cap work, and is it the same as my credit limit?

They are different, and iD’s own terms say so. The bill cap is free on all plans and you set it anywhere between £0 and your credit limit in the app or My Account; reach it and chargeable calls, texts and data stop. The credit limit is set by iD after a credit check, and clause 4.13 states that it does not act as a cap to prevent further charges arising. Note that your line rental, add-ons and paper billing sit outside the capped amount, so a £0 cap still leaves you paying your monthly price.

What happens to my data if I do not use it?

It rolls over once into the following month and is used before your new allowance, on all iD Mobile pay-monthly plans. Rollover data expires after one month, so the benefit does not build up — three quiet months in a row bank nothing by the third.

What does it cost to use my plan abroad?

Nothing extra in 50 destinations, subject to a 30GB monthly cap for customers who joined on or after 21 June 2023 — and that cap has been lifted by a promotion running from 1 October 2024 until further notice, which iD says it will announce the end of. Beyond 30GB when the cap applies, a surcharge of £3 per GB covers the rest of your allowance and then the rate is 10p per MB, which clause 4.2.1 itself converts to £102.40 per GB. Calls are 3.2p a minute and texts 1p. Ireland is outside the fair usage policy, and 63 days or more abroad in any 4-month period attracts further charges.

What will it cost me to leave early?

Your monthly tariff multiplied by every month left in the minimum period, under clause 1.1, less an unstated reduction for iD’s avoided costs, on at least 30 days’ notice under clause 8.1.1. On the £16 unlimited 24-month plan that is £288 if you go after six months. The exception is clause 4.6.1: if the service is unavailable for 7 days or longer and the cause is iD’s, no early termination fee applies.

Can I complain to an ombudsman, and which one?

Yes, after six weeks without a resolution or straight away if you have a deadlock letter, with 12 months to refer from the date of that letter. The scheme is the Communications Ombudsman — 0330 440 1614, or PO Box 730, Warrington WA4 6WU — though iD’s complaints procedure page still calls it Ombudsman Services: Communications, a name retired in July 2023. iD aims to respond within 7 days and its complaints line is 0800 049 2402.

Is switching to iD Mobile covered by One Touch Switch?

No. One Touch Switch went live on 12 September 2024 under conditions C7.18 to C7.27 of Ofcom’s General Conditions and applies, in Ofcom’s words, to all fixed voice and broadband services provided at a fixed location. Ofcom excludes mobile because mobile communications services are defined as services capable of being used while in motion. Moving a mobile number still uses the older text-code process.

Does iD Mobile offer a social tariff?

No. iD Mobile does not appear on Ofcom’s list of broadband and phone social tariffs, and neither does Three. The only mobile entries on that list are SMARTY at £12 a month and VOXI For Now at £10. In practice iD’s standard £6 plan is cheaper than both, though it needs a credit check rather than a benefit award, and Ofcom’s page is stamped 6 March 2024 with no update date.

Our verdict

iD Mobile is the cheap end of the market done carefully. £6 for 6GB rolling or 10GB over 24 months is genuinely cheap, the ladder grades the reward for committing instead of flattening it, and the two features it gives away — a bill cap you can set to £0 and data that rolls over once and is spent first — are both answers to the way a phone bill actually goes wrong. The price-rise pledge carries no expiry date, which is better than several rivals manage, and the independent survey evidence puts it eight points clear of the network it rents. Where it is weakest is the exit and the disclosure: the early termination charge is the whole remaining term rather than half of it, the fair-use number behind unlimited is not published at all, and neither the shop nor the contract will tell you whose masts you are buying. Worth it for a light or moderate user who will see the 24-month term out, and worth reading clause 1.1 twice if you might not.
