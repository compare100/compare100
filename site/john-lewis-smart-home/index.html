John Lewis Smart Home: a Free Two-Year Guarantee, and the Date It Does Not Cover

Rates and terms checked 16 November 2026 · Smart Things · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

John Lewis puts a free two-year guarantee on all 248 products in its Smart Home department, from a £19.99 Ring Indoor Camera to a £499.00 eufyCam S3 Pro two-pack, and its own guarantees page states the rule in one line: “Minimum 2 year guarantee now included on all electricals, 5 years on TVs”, “all at no extra cost”. Standard delivery is £4.50 and free above £50.

That is a real advantage and almost nobody else in this category matches it. The guarantee badge is printed on the listing grid rather than buried in a policy document, so you can see it before you click a product; “Free Guarantee” is one of the filters the shop offers on the department itself; and John Lewis says it “only use[s] repairers who are endorsed by us and manufacturers”. On a security camera or a video doorbell, two years of cover at no cost is worth more than the few pounds a competitor might shave off the price.

A smart device has a second clock, though, and it is not the guarantee. Every connected product sold in Britain carries a defined support period — the span over which the manufacturer promises security updates — and it is the number that decides how long the thing is safe to leave on your front door. John Lewis publishes the first clock on every listing and the second on none of them. This page reads both.

What £19.99 to £499 actually buys

The department runs to 248 lines, and the shape of it is four brands deep rather than broad. Ring supplies most of the doorbells and cameras: the Battery Video Doorbell 2K is £39.99 against a £79.99 was-price, the Battery Video Doorbell 2K Plus £69.99 from £149.99, the Wired Video Doorbell Pro (3rd Generation) £129.99 from £219.99, the Floodlight Cam Pro (2nd Generation) £169.99 from £249.99, the Spotlight Camera 4K Pro £139.99 from £219.99 and the Alarm 5-Piece Kit £139.99 from £219.00. The Indoor Camera at £19.99 is the cheapest thing in the department and the Indoor Camera Plus is £24.99 against £49.99.

Philips Hue is the lighting half. The Personal Wireless Lighting Bridge, which every bulb in the range needs, is £49.00; the Smart Dimmer Switch 2 is £19.99, the Motion Sensor £39.00, the Tap Dial Switch £44.99, the Smart Plug £20.09, and the outdoor range runs from a £94.49 Impress wall light to £319.99 for a set of three Lily stake lights. Google Nest covers the middle of the camera market — £62.99 for the indoor wired camera, £89.99 for the outdoor wired one, £125.00 for the battery camera, £179.99 for the battery video doorbell and £252.39 for the camera with floodlight. TP-Link's Tapo range is the budget floor: a C200 pan-and-tilt camera at £20.99, a C220 at £29.99, a TD20 battery doorbell mini at £34.00 and a D230S1 doorbell kit with chime and hub at £79.99.

Round the edges sit the things that make it a department rather than a shelf: a tado Smart Thermostat X at £129.00, Apple's wall-mounted HomeView at £299.00 with the two-year guarantee badge on it, Life360's Tile item finders from £49.50 for four, a TP-Link Deco BE22 Wi-Fi 7 mesh three-pack at £244.99, and the £499.00 eufyCam S3 Pro kit at the top. Amazon's Echo displays are stocked too, at £69.99 for the Show 5 and £399.99 for the Show 21. Promotions run on top of the shelf prices — “Save £119.99 when you buy a Ring Indoor Smart Security Camera and Ring Battery Video Doorbell 2K Plus together”, and 10% off through the shop's app — and the price promise badge, which John Lewis brands Never Knowingly Undersold, sits on the department page.

Delivery is the other half of the real cost. Standard delivery takes two to five days and costs £4.50, free above £50. Click & Collect is £2.95 and free above £40, through Waitrose, selected John Lewis shops and Booths. Next or named day is £7.95, morning delivery £10.95, and evening delivery inside the M25 £9.95 if you order before 10am. On the £19.99 camera, the cheapest way to get it is to collect it and pay £2.95, which is 15% of the price of the camera.

Two years of guarantee, three years of patches, and the sum nobody shows you

Here is the arithmetic the shop does not print. Buy the £49.00 Philips Hue Bridge today and John Lewis guarantees it until 16 November 2028. Signify, which makes it, promises security, quality and interoperability updates for “a minimum total of three (3) years from the date of your purchase from an authorised reseller”, and major platform updates for only “a minimum of one (1) year”. So on the Bridge the patches outlast the guarantee by about a year, and the feature updates stop a year into it. Buy a Hue bulb, lamp or sensor instead and Signify's figure is “a minimum total of five (5) years” on the same purchase-date clock, three years clear of the guarantee. Both of those are good outcomes, and both are invisible at the point of sale.

Anker, which makes the eufy cameras, states a different rule for a different reason. Its policy promises security updates “for a minimum period of three (3) years from the connected device's initial market release date” — not from the day you buy. On the £499.00 eufyCam S3 Pro kit, the dearest line in the department, that means the clock had already been running for some unknown time before the box reached the shelf, and John Lewis's listing does not print a release date, nor does Anker's policy page print one per product. A buyer cannot work out what they are getting. Anker does commit to at least six months' notice before support ends, and it says plainly that after end of life it “strongly advise[s] against the continued use of the applications” and will not guarantee compatibility with future iOS or Android releases. That is honest, and it is the only brand on this shelf whose published floor is shorter than the free guarantee John Lewis sells it with.

TP-Link's Tapo range, which is where the £20.99 and £29.99 cameras come from, measures the period from neither event: “The defined support period will end 3 years after the product's end-of-life date”, with no stated start. The company's statement of compliance, a PDF whose address was last updated in September 2026, is where the per-model dates live, and it is not linked from any John Lewis page. Google's figure is five years, and where Google starts counting has a section of its own below.

The point of the sum is not that any one of these promises is bad. It is that a guarantee and a support period answer different questions. The guarantee says John Lewis will repair or replace a camera that stops working. The support period says how much longer anyone will patch it. A device can sit inside its guarantee and outside its support, working perfectly and quietly unpatched, and nothing in the buying journey tells you when that crossover happens.

Buy a Ring here and the four-year promise is not the one you get

This is the sharpest thing on the page and it takes one fetch of Ring's own support article to establish. Ring's commitment reads: “We'll support your device with software security updates until at least four years from when you bought it new”. The next clause names where: “on amazon.com where Amazon is the seller or on ring.com”, “(or, in each case, until the date stated below if later).” And then the sentence that matters to anyone standing in Oxford Street: “If you got your device somewhere else, we'll support it with software security updates until at least the date stated below.”

John Lewis is somewhere else. Ring accounts for twenty of the first seventy-two lines the department shows, and every one of them is bought under the second rule, not the first. The four years from purchase that Ring's marketing leads with belongs to Amazon's own checkout and Ring's own; at John Lewis you get a fixed calendar date out of Ring's table, whatever day you happen to buy.

Much of the time that is better, not worse. The Battery Doorbell 2K at £39.99 and the Battery Doorbell 4K Pro at £219.99 both sit on 31 December 2031 in Ring's table, which is five years and 45 days from the day this page was written and comfortably beyond the four-year alternative. The Spotlight Cam Plus, Floodlight Cam Wired Plus, Pan-Tilt Indoor Cam and Alarm components are all on 31 December 2030. And the lines at the bottom of Ring's table are not in John Lewis's department at all: the first-generation Video Doorbell, the first-generation Chime and the first-generation Chime Pro all stopped being covered on 31 December 2024, the plain Floodlight Cam and the Video Doorbell 2 are on 31 December 2025, and the plain Spotlight Cam runs out on 31 December 2026, which is 45 days away. None of those is on sale here. That is a good buying team doing its job.

What a shopper cannot do is check any of it, because the names do not line up. John Lewis sells a “Ring Smart Video Doorbell Pro 2 (Hardwired)” at £79.99 and Ring's table contains no entry called Pro 2 — it lists a Video Doorbell Pro on 31 December 2029, a Wired Video Doorbell Pro (2021) on 31 December 2030 and a Wired Doorbell 4K Pro on 31 December 2030. John Lewis sells a “Ring Wired Video Doorbell 2” at £29.99 and Ring's table has both a Video Doorbell 2 ending 31 December 2025 and a Video Doorbell Wired ending 31 December 2030, five years apart. Neither match could be settled from the two companies' own pages, so neither date is quoted here as the answer. A product code on the listing, or a line in the specification table, would close the gap in a sentence.

Four Nest lines are on sale with the published date behind them

Google's support documentation says Nest devices “will receive automatic security updates for at least 5 years”, and the column it publishes them in is headed “Release date + 5 year minimum”. The clock starts “from the date we start selling them on the US Google Store”, and the dates in the table are written American-style with the month first, so 8/24/2021 is 24 August 2021.

Four of the Google lines in John Lewis's department come off that 2021 cohort. The Nest Cam battery-powered camera at £125.00, the two-pack of the same camera at £229.00, the Nest Cam with Floodlight at £252.39 and the Nest Video Doorbell battery model at £179.99 all trace back to a 24 August 2021 release, which puts their published minimum at 24 August 2026 — 84 days before this page was written. A buyer paying £252.39 today gets a John Lewis guarantee running to November 2028 and a manufacturer's published promise that expired in August.

The fair half of that is in the same table and it deserves saying plainly: a column headed “Currently receiving critical security updates” reads “Yes” against every one of those four, and against older Nest hardware going back to a 2015 camera. The promise has lapsed; the patches have not. Google is doing more than it undertook to do. The complaint is about disclosure, not about abandonment — and it is a complaint against the shop as much as the maker, because the date exists, it is published, it is free to read, and the listing that takes £252.39 does not mention it.

Two Google lines in the department are at the other end. The Nest Cam Indoor wired camera at £62.99 and the Nest Cam Outdoor wired one at £89.99 are, if they are the current third- and second-generation models, on a 1 October 2025 release and a 1 October 2030 minimum. That is the generational ambiguity again: Google's table also holds a Nest Cam (wired) from 5 October 2021 whose minimum ran out on 5 October 2026, 42 days ago, and nothing on the John Lewis listing says which one is in the box. The newer reading is the likelier one. It is not the certain one, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

Where the law puts the duty, and who is enforcing it

The defined support period is not a courtesy. Under the regime in force since 29 April 2024, a manufacturer of a connectable product has to publish it in plain language, and a distributor may not make the product available in the United Kingdom unless it is accompanied by a statement of compliance. The duty to put the support information next to the price, though, is written for the maker's own shop, not for a retailer's — which is why John Lewis carries no support date without being in breach of anything, and why the absence is a fair criticism rather than an accusation. The regulations also set no minimum length. They require only that the number be published.

So the honest position is that John Lewis is doing what the rules ask and less than a shopper needs. The statement of compliance can be a digital document, so a link on a product page would discharge it, and on the pages read for this review there was no such link: the Tapo plug listing's specification table runs from brand and country of origin to packaged dimensions and weight, with the guarantee line in the middle and no support period anywhere. The shop's own security page is about the website — “We maintain the highest levels of security on johnlewis.com” — and says nothing about the security of what it sells.

As for enforcement, the Office for Product Safety and Standards publishes a page setting out six powers — compliance notices, stop notices, recall notices, monetary penalties, forfeiture orders and court orders on information notices — and states that it “publishes details of any Compliance Notice that we serve, in line with our Enforcement Policy”. That page was last updated on 8 August 2025 and names no company, no product and no case. This is the fourth time a page on this site has read it and found the same thing. Treat it as a regime that is young rather than a regime that is ignored, but a buyer looking for someone to have been held to account will not find them yet.

What an independent survey says about the gap

Which? put the question to 119 product brands in November 2022 and reported in January 2023 that only 49% replied with clear information on minimum support periods. The finding that travels straight onto a John Lewis shelf is about where the clock starts: Which? recorded LG guaranteeing two years of support on smart TVs, dishwashers and washing machines counted “from when the product is launched, not when it was bought”, Sony two years after launch and Samsung three. Against those, Hisense was supporting smart televisions for ten years and Miele its dishwashers and washing machines for the same. The spread across one aisle of a department store was eight years.

The survey also priced the premium. A smart dishwasher, Which? found, cost on average nearly £300 more up front — £746 against £455 — while its estimated working life was about thirteen years, against six or seven years of support. That is a four-year-old survey and it is quoted here as the dated snapshot it is, but the arithmetic it exposes is unchanged: the hardware outlives the software, and the gap is widest exactly where the product costs most.

This is where John Lewis's guarantee earns its reputation and also meets its limit. Two years free, on everything, with endorsed repairers behind it, is better than the statutory floor and better than most of the high street. It is a promise about faults. It is not, and does not claim to be, a promise that the camera on your porch will still be receiving patches on the day the guarantee ends.

The Partnership's own numbers, and why they matter to a buyer

The John Lewis Partnership published unaudited interim results on 10 September 2026 for the 26 weeks to 1 August 2026, and they are worth a shopper's attention because a guarantee is only as good as the company behind it. Partnership sales were £6,292m, up 2%, split between Waitrose at £4,273m, up 4%, and John Lewis at £2,019m, down 2%. The loss before tax widened to £124m from £88m; stripping out the Partnership Bonus and exceptional items the loss was £89m against £34m. Exceptional costs were £35m, of which £24m was productivity restructuring and redundancy, £9m cloud technology modernisation and £2m the exit from Build to Rent.

The balance-sheet side is steadier than the profit line. Cash generated from operations was £131m, down £46m on the £177m of the year before, and total liquidity stood at £1.4bn. Cash spent on property, plant, equipment and intangibles came to £221m, and the Partnership expects to invest around £600m across the year, with the headline investment in its brands up 29% at £246m. Jason Tarry's line on it was that “we are managing the business with discipline and have chosen to keep investing in our customers”.

A £1.4bn liquidity position behind a two-year guarantee on a £19.99 camera is a reasonable thing to rely on. A department losing sales while the Partnership invests £600m is also the context in which free guarantees get reviewed, so the sensible reading is that the guarantee is worth having now and is a commercial decision rather than a constitutional one.

Common questions

How long is the John Lewis guarantee on a smart home device?

Two years as a minimum, free, on everything in the Smart Home department. The shop's guarantees page states it as “Minimum 2 year guarantee now included on all electricals, 5 years on TVs” and adds that it is “all at no extra cost”. The badge “2 year guarantee included” appears on individual listings, and “Free Guarantee” is one of the filters on the department itself. The full terms and exclusions sit on a separate page for Electricals, Technology and Lighting rather than in the summary.

Does John Lewis tell you how long a smart device will get security updates?

No. On 16 November 2026 no listing, product page, specification table or customer-service page in the Smart Home department carried a support period or linked to a statement of compliance. The shop's security page is about johnlewis.com rather than about the products. The law places the duty to publish the period on the manufacturer and the duty to pass on the statement of compliance on the distributor, so a link would satisfy it, and there was none. Read the maker's own support page for your exact model before ordering.

Is a Ring camera bought at John Lewis covered for four years?

Not under the four-year rule. Ring's commitment is “until at least four years from when you bought it new” on “amazon.com where Amazon is the seller or on ring.com”. Its own article then says: “If you got your device somewhere else, we'll support it with software security updates until at least the date stated below.” A Ring bought at John Lewis therefore gets the fixed date in Ring's table. For most of the range John Lewis stocks that is 31 December 2030 or 31 December 2031, which is longer than four years from today, so the outcome is usually better rather than worse.

Which smart devices at John Lewis are past their published support date?

Four Google lines, all tracing to an August 2021 release: the Nest Cam battery camera at £125.00, its two-pack at £229.00, the Nest Cam with Floodlight at £252.39 and the Nest Video Doorbell battery model at £179.99. Google's own table puts their five-year minimum at 24 August 2026. The same table marks all four as currently receiving critical security updates, so they are still being patched beyond the promise — but the promise itself has run out, and the listings do not say so.

How much is delivery on a smart home order?

Standard delivery is £4.50 and takes two to five days, free on orders above £50. Click & Collect is £2.95 and free above £40, available through Waitrose, Booths and selected John Lewis shops. Next or named day delivery is £7.95, morning delivery £10.95, and evening delivery inside the M25 is £9.95 if you order before 10am. On a single £19.99 camera the cheapest route is collection at £2.95.

Does the guarantee cover a device that stops getting updates?

No, and it does not claim to. A guarantee is a promise about faults: if the camera stops working within two years, John Lewis repairs or replaces it. A device that has passed its support period is not faulty — it works exactly as designed and simply is not being patched any more. The two promises run on different clocks and from different companies, which is why the support date is worth checking separately even when the guarantee is generous.

Our verdict

John Lewis is the best place on this list to buy a smart home device if what you care about is what happens when it breaks. A minimum two-year guarantee on all 248 products, at no cost, visible on the listing grid, with endorsed repairers and £1.4bn of liquidity behind it, is a better deal than the statutory floor and better than most of the competition, and the prices are real rather than notional — £19.99 for a camera, £39.99 for a doorbell that Ring's own table supports until 31 December 2031. Where it falls short is the other clock. The maker's support period is published, free to read and decisive, and it appears nowhere in the shop: four Nest lines are selling today with a minimum that ran out on 24 August 2026, a Ring bought here is covered to a fixed date rather than four years from purchase, and the eufy kit at £499.00 is on a three-year clock that started before it reached the shelf. None of that makes John Lewis a bad place to buy. It makes the two-year guarantee the only promise the shop will put in front of you, and the shorter of the two promises on several of the things it sells. Check the maker's own support page for the exact model before you order, and the department is a strong choice.
