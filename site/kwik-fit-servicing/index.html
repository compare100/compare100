Kwik Fit Servicing Review: Prices, Guarantees and the Paperwork

Rates and terms checked 8 September 2026 · Servicing · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Kwik Fit has been servicing cars in Britain since the first centre opened in Edinburgh in 1971, and the company behind the name is just as old: Kwik-Fit (GB) Limited was registered at Companies House on 27 April 1971, first as Euro Exhaust Centre Holdings Limited, then as Kwik-Fit-Euro Limited from 1981 until 1990. It now runs more than 600 centres across the UK including Northern Ireland, open seven days a week — 8.30am to 6pm Monday to Friday, 8.30am to 5pm on Saturday and 10am to 4pm on Sunday. Since June 2011 the group has been owned by the Japanese trading house ITOCHU.

What makes it worth a look for a service rather than just tyres is that the prices are published before you hand over the keys. A fixed grid on the site sets an interim service at £186.95 for an engine up to 1400cc rising to £251.95 above 3000cc, a full service from £246.95 to £311.95, an engine oil and filter change from £132.95, an electric vehicle service from £230.95, and a full service bundled with the MOT from £281.95. Most independent garages will not quote until they have seen the car. Service components and other mechanical parts fitted during the work carry a 12 month or 12,000 mile guarantee, and a service takes two to four hours depending on the vehicle.

One thing to settle by phone before you book. If you drive a hybrid, Kwik Fit's own pages disagree about whether it will take the car: the servicing FAQ says "We are not currently able to service hybrid vehicles of any type", while the service comparison page lists both the interim and the full service as suitable for hybrids, and the full service checklist includes a hybrid system check. Nobody has reconciled the two. The centre itself will know which answer is current.

The price grid, engine size by engine size

Kwik Fit publishes a full price table rather than a single headline. An engine oil and filter change runs £132.95 up to 1400cc, £152.95 to 2000cc, £172.95 to 3000cc and £192.95 above that. The interim service is £186.95, £211.95, £231.95 and £251.95 across the same four bands. The full service is £246.95, £271.95, £291.95 and £311.95. Add the MOT and the interim bundle starts at £221.95 and the full bundle at £281.95, rising to £286.95 and £346.95 for the largest engines. An electric vehicle service is £230.95, or £270.90 with the MOT.

Every one of those is a "from" price, and the site is straight about why: cost turns on engine size and on the oil the car needs, with synthetic and specialist grades costing more. The company says it will use "an oil equivalent to the manufacturer's specification" rather than naming a grade in advance, so the figure you see online is a floor rather than a quotation.

The intervals are conventional. Interim is every 6 months or 6,000 miles, full is every 12 months or 12,000 miles whichever comes first, and the EV service is every 24 months or 20,000 miles. The oil and filter change sits outside all of that: it includes safety checks but cannot reset the service light or stamp the book, which makes it a top-up between services rather than a cheap substitute for one.

What is on the checklist, and what is quietly left off it

The full service checklist runs to more than 60 inspection points, grouped into the cabin, the exterior, a pre-alignment check, under the bonnet, under the car, the replacement items and a road test. The cabin section covers instruments, warning lights, horn, wipers, the AdBlue or Eolys light, seat belts, clutch operation on a manual, climate control and an engine diagnostics read. The pre-alignment section takes in steering and suspension linkages, ball joints, shock absorbers, springs, a full tyre inspection covering tread, pressure and alignment, and the tyre pressure monitoring system. Under the bonnet it checks the battery, coolant, brake fluid, the brake, radiator and power steering hoses and the auxiliary drive belt. Under the car it covers a full brake inspection, fuel pipes, drive shaft joints and the exhaust.

The replacement items are shorter than the inspection list, and that is the part worth reading twice. A full service replaces the engine oil and filter and the air filter, and tops up the screen wash. An interim service replaces the oil and filter only. Kwik Fit states plainly that "Replacement spark plugs are not included in our service packages", and its interim service also excludes the fuel filter, the air filter, belts and power steering fluid, while the full service excludes spark plugs, the fuel filter and belts. Anything on those lists that your car is due comes as extra work on top of the published price.

The terms set out how that extra work is handled: "Once Services have started you will be informed immediately of any additional work identified. A new printed quotation will be produced on request and additional work will only be carried out with your agreement." Note the phrasing — the printed quotation appears if you ask for one.

MOT pricing, and the figure the government caps

The MOT is the one part of the bill nobody can inflate. The Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency sets a maximum fee of £54.85 for a class 4 car with up to eight passenger seats, £64.00 for a class 4 test that includes a seat belt installation check, £58.60 for a class 7 goods vehicle between 3,001kg and 3,500kg and £29.65 for a motorcycle. Those are ceilings, not prices, and a test station is free to charge less. Kwik Fit does not advertise a standalone MOT price on its servicing pages; it prices the test inside the bundles, where the gap between the £246.95 full service and the £281.95 full-service-with-MOT works out at £35.00, comfortably under the cap.

The test is worth taking seriously on the numbers. DVSA's own quarterly data for class 3 and 4 vehicles in April to June 2025 puts the initial failure rate at 27.24%, so better than one car in four fails first time. Lamps, reflectors and electrical equipment accounted for 10.36% of tests, suspension 8.65%, tyres 6.23%, brakes 6.10% and visibility 4.65%. Most of those categories overlap with items a full service inspects but does not replace, which is the practical argument for booking the two together rather than a fortnight apart.

Servicing off the dealer forecourt without losing the warranty

This is the question that sends most people to a chain like this one, and the answer is better than the folklore. UK competition law contains a specific block exemption for motor vehicle agreements: The Competition Act 1998 (Motor Vehicle Agreements Block Exemption) Order 2023, SI 2023 No. 501, made on 28 April 2023, in force from 1 June 2023 and running until 31 May 2029. Article 6 stops a supplier restricting an independent operator's access to the "information, tools or training" needed to repair a vehicle in a way that puts that operator at a disadvantage against an authorised repairer, and Article 5 bars restrictions on selling aftermarket parts to independent garages.

Kwik Fit sells a Manufacturer Service built to the maker's own schedule for the car's age, model and mileage, quoted individually rather than from the grid, using what it calls "competitively-sourced parts of 'Original Equipment' standard" and oils that "match or exceed the quality of the manufacturer's original oil". Its own wording on the warranty point is unusually firm: "We guarantee you that a stamp in your car's logbook from Kwik Fit will uphold any warranty you have with your car dealer, supporting your full dealer service history."

Two caveats belong with that. The first is that a manufacturer's warranty and a dealer's own extended warranty are different contracts with different conditions, and it is the second one that tends to carry the awkward clauses. The second is the paperwork, which is the subject of the next section.

The digital service record it cannot sign

Kwik Fit says so itself, on the manufacturer servicing page: "If your vehicle has a Digital Service Record (DSR) we will not be able to update or reset this." For a car with a paper logbook that costs nothing. For a car whose service history exists only on the manufacturer's own system — which covers most mainstream brands sold new in the last decade — it means the work is done to schedule, the invoice proves it, and the manufacturer's record still shows a gap. You are relying on the invoice and the stamp, not on the file a franchised dealer or a future buyer will look at.

It sits awkwardly beside the guarantee quoted above, which is written entirely around the logbook. If your car has no logbook to stamp, the sentence does not describe your situation, and Kwik Fit has not written a version that does. Worth asking the centre, before booking, exactly what documentation you will leave with.

The same page repeats a description of the law that has been out of date in Britain for years, telling readers that "In 2002, the European Parliament took the view that the practice of manufacturers forcing car owners to go to dealerships to get a service to uphold their warranty was too restrictive". The practical outcome has not changed — the 2023 Order preserves the position — but the authority for it is now a British statutory instrument rather than a European decision, and a page written this year could say so.

Two Kwik Fit pages, two different prices for the same service

The service pricing table gives an interim service from £186.95 and a full service from £246.95. The company's own guide to choosing a service, on the same site, gives an interim service "From £84 (based on engine size and oil type required)" and a full service "From £167". That is a gap of £102.95 on the interim and £79.95 on the full, for the same two products described in the same terms, and neither page is dated.

Neither figure is dishonest on its face; one of them is simply stale, and a reader has no way of telling which. If you arrive quoting £84 you will be quoted £186.95, and the fixed-price grid — the best thing about buying a service here — loses some of its point when the site carries two versions of it. The same absence of dates affects the hybrid answer in the introduction: two pages, two positions, no way to tell which was written last.

How the guarantee is worded for brakes, and how it is worded for a service

The guarantees document is versioned and dated, which is more than most chains manage — Version 1.9, 30 January 2026 — and several of the promises in it are generous. Brake pads and shoes carry a lifetime guarantee, with replacement sets "for as long as you own the vehicle". Bosch and NAPA batteries carry five years and unlimited mileage. Exhausts and catalytic converters carry three years and unlimited mileage. Shock absorbers carry a two-year manufacturer defect guarantee plus a 14-day satisfaction promise: if the ride has not improved, you get a refund and the original units refitted free, provided you kept them.

Now compare two sentences. On brakes: "we will guarantee all other new brake parts fitted, including labour for the first 12 months after fitment or the first 12,000 miles". On servicing: "Service components and any other mechanical parts, including starters and alternators are guaranteed for 12 months or 12,000 miles, whichever comes first." The brake clause names labour. The service clause does not. Elsewhere the document promises to "dismantle and repair or replace at its discretion any defective parts and reassemble them on your vehicle free of charge, subject to these guarantee conditions", which reads as though labour is covered — but the explicit mention in one clause and its absence in the other is exactly the kind of drafting that gets argued about at a counter. Ask, and get the answer on the invoice.

All the guarantees exclude wear, tear and corrosion, criminal or malicious damage, fire, theft, third-party repairs, competitive motorsport, and "any monetary or compensatory claim, in particular consequential or additional losses". The battery and brake guarantees also exclude company vehicles, hire cars, taxis and commercial use, and all of them require the original receipt.

Who decides it if the job goes wrong

Kwik Fit's terms are Version 13, dated 8 July 2025, and they are clear about the routine things. Services and MOTs booked online carry a 14-day cancellation right, or until 4pm on the day before the MOT appointment if that comes sooner; cancel before work starts and there is no charge; cancel after it has started and the company "reserve[s] the right to charge you for any Products or Services supplied". The contract is with Kwik-Fit (GB) Limited, company 01009184, VAT number GB 380 0948 50, under English law.

The dispute clause is where it thins out. If Customer Care cannot settle a complaint, the terms tell you to "ask Our Customer Care department for details of the Alternative Dispute Resolution Scheme that We participate in". The scheme is not named there, on the contact page, or in the FAQs. Under regulation 19 of the Alternative Dispute Resolution for Consumer Disputes (Competent Authorities and Information) Regulations 2015, a trader obliged to use an ADR body must give "the name and website address of the ADR entity" both on its website and in its general terms; where use is voluntary, regulation 19(2) requires the name and website in writing once the internal procedure is exhausted. Either way, a customer deciding whether it is worth pursuing a complaint cannot find out who would hear it without making a phone call.

For context on what the sector's own scheme offers: The Motor Ombudsman runs a Service and Repair Code approved by the Chartered Trading Standards Institute, first approved in 2008 and granted full Office of Fair Trading approval in November 2011, with around 7,500 businesses accredited across its four codes as at its December 2023 audit. Accredited garages must put estimates and quotations in writing as a breakdown of costs, contact the customer "for authorisation prior to commencement" of extra work, make replaced parts available to view until the car is collected, itemise parts, labour, environmental disposal charges and VAT separately on the invoice, acknowledge a complaint within ten working days and issue a final response within eight weeks, after which the customer has one year to refer the matter onward. Whether Kwik Fit is accredited to that code could not be confirmed from the Ombudsman's own published material, and Kwik Fit does not claim it — so treat the list above as the benchmark to ask about rather than as a description of what you are buying.

The group behind the 600 centres

Ownership has changed hands repeatedly. Ford bought the Kwik Fit group in June 1999 for more than $1bn, sold it to CVC Capital Partners in 2002, and PAI Partners took it on in 2005 before ITOCHU completed its purchase in June 2011, adding the 110-location STS Tyre Pros network at the same time. Today the immediate parent recorded at Companies House is European Tyre Enterprise Limited, which was notified as the person with significant control on 25 June 2025, taking over from Kwik-Fit Holdings Limited. ETEL describes its group — Kwik Fit, Stapletons Tyre Services, STS Tyre Pros and Murfitts Industries — as having "total annual sales in excess of £1.2bn", though that figure carries no date on the page. Kwik Fit's most recent accounts cover the year to 31 March 2025, with the next set due by 31 December 2026. In 2024 the group bought Fettle, a London bicycle servicing business.

On training, the company's own about page credits it with "Ofsted Outstanding accreditation" for its training academy. Ofsted's published record tells a more recent story: the Outstanding grade was awarded at an inspection on 20 to 24 April 2015, and at the most recent inspection, from 16 to 19 July 2024, Kwik-Fit (GB) Limited was graded Good in all five areas — quality of education, behaviour and attitudes, personal development, leadership and management, and apprenticeships. The provider had around 550 apprentices at that inspection, three-quarters of them aged 19 or under, almost all on the level 2 autocare technician standard. Good is a solid grade and the report is complimentary about the training plans; the marketing copy is simply eleven years out of date.

One product is currently unavailable rather than merely undersold. Kwik Fit Club, the monthly subscription covering tyres, MOT, servicing, air conditioning, batteries, brakes, exhausts, alignment and wipers, has been closed to new members: "Our Kwik Fit Club service is currently being upgraded and new memberships have been temporarily paused." Existing members keep their benefits. Its terms are still Version 2, dated 2 March 2023, and they note that the subscription is non-transferable, that an early termination fee applies after the 14-day cooling-off period, and that unused allowances are not refunded.

Where it wins

  • Prices published in a full grid by engine size before you book — £186.95 to £251.95 interim, £246.95 to £311.95 full — rather than quoted only after the car is on the ramp
  • Over 600 centres open seven days a week, including Sundays from 10am to 4pm
  • Service components and other mechanical parts guaranteed for 12 months or 12,000 miles, under a guarantees document that is versioned and dated (V1.9, 30 January 2026)
  • Bundling the MOT with a full service costs £35.00 on top, against the DVSA maximum test fee of £54.85
  • A Manufacturer Service to the maker's own schedule, with parts of Original Equipment standard, priced individually
  • Lifetime guarantee on brake pads and shoes, five years on Bosch and NAPA batteries, three years on exhausts and catalytic converters
  • Extra work only carried out with your agreement, with a printed quotation available on request

Where it falls short

  • Cannot update or reset a manufacturer Digital Service Record, so on most cars sold new in the last decade the maker's own service file will show a gap however good the work
  • Two pages on Kwik Fit's own site quote different starting prices for the same services — £84 and £167 on one, £186.95 and £246.95 on the other — and neither is dated
  • The servicing FAQ says hybrids cannot be serviced "of any type", while the service comparison page lists hybrids as suitable for both the interim and full service
  • The Alternative Dispute Resolution scheme is never named — the terms tell customers to telephone Customer Care to find out who would hear a complaint
  • The service guarantee covers parts for 12 months or 12,000 miles without mentioning labour, where the brake guarantee names labour explicitly
  • Spark plugs are excluded from every service package, and the interim service replaces nothing but the oil and filter
  • The about page still claims an Ofsted Outstanding rating awarded in April 2015; the July 2024 inspection graded the training provision Good in every category
  • Kwik Fit Club is closed to new members while the service is upgraded

Common questions

How much does a service at Kwik Fit cost?

An interim service starts at £186.95 for engines up to 1400cc, rising through £211.95 and £231.95 to £251.95 above 3000cc. A full service starts at £246.95 and rises to £311.95. An engine oil and filter change starts at £132.95 and an electric vehicle service at £230.95. All are "from" prices: the final figure depends on engine size and on the oil your car requires, with synthetic and specialist grades costing more.

Will a Kwik Fit service invalidate my manufacturer warranty?

Not by itself. The Competition Act 1998 (Motor Vehicle Agreements Block Exemption) Order 2023, in force from 1 June 2023 to 31 May 2029, protects an independent garage's access to the information and tools needed to service a car properly. Kwik Fit's Manufacturer Service follows the maker's own schedule using parts of Original Equipment standard, and the company states that its logbook stamp "will uphold any warranty you have with your car dealer". Two things to check: a dealer's own extended warranty is a separate contract with its own conditions, and Kwik Fit cannot update a manufacturer Digital Service Record.

What is the difference between the interim and the full service?

The interim runs every 6 months or 6,000 miles and replaces the engine oil and filter, with a visual brake inspection and a range of checks. The full service runs every 12 months or 12,000 miles and adds the air filter, a full strip-down brake check and a wheel alignment, bearing and balancing inspection. Neither includes spark plugs; the interim also excludes the fuel filter, air filter, belts and power steering fluid.

Does Kwik Fit do MOTs, and what does it charge?

Yes — the company is licensed by the DVSA to carry out MOT testing. It prices the test inside its bundles rather than advertising it alone: a full service with MOT starts at £281.95 against £246.95 for the service on its own, a difference of £35.00. The legal maximum any test station may charge for a class 4 car is £54.85.

Can Kwik Fit service a hybrid or an electric car?

Electric vehicles, yes: there is a dedicated EV service at £230.95, recommended every 24 months or 20,000 miles. Hybrids are unclear. The servicing FAQ states that "We are not currently able to service hybrid vehicles of any type", while the service comparison page marks hybrids as suitable for both the interim and the full service and the full service checklist includes a hybrid system check. Telephone the centre with your registration before booking.

How do I complain about work done at a Kwik Fit centre?

Start with Customer Care on 0800 75 76 77 or customercare@kwik-fit.com, keeping the original receipt, which the guarantees require before any remedial work can start. If that does not settle it, the terms say to ask Customer Care for details of the Alternative Dispute Resolution scheme the company participates in — the scheme is not named anywhere on the website. Trade-body benchmarks are useful here: The Motor Ombudsman's Service and Repair Code requires accredited garages to acknowledge a complaint within ten working days and issue a final response within eight weeks.

Our verdict

For a straightforward annual service on a car that is out of its dealer warranty, Kwik Fit is an easy thing to buy: you can read the price before you book, there are more than 600 places to book it, and the parts carry a 12 month or 12,000 mile guarantee. The full service at £246.95 with the MOT at £281.95 is a fair price for a 60-point inspection and a road test, and bundling the test makes sense given that better than one class 4 car in four fails first time. The reservations are about paperwork rather than spanners: it cannot sign a digital service record, its own site carries two sets of prices, it will not say who arbitrates a dispute, and its hybrid answer depends on which page you read. Ring the centre with the registration, ask what documentation you will leave with, and get the quotation in writing before the car goes up.
