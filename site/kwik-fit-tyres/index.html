Kwik Fit Tyres: Fitted Prices, the Label and What the Law Demands

Rates and terms checked 8 September 2026 · Tyres · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Kwik Fit is the largest tyre-fitting chain in Britain, with over 600 centres across the UK and Northern Ireland and many of them open seven days a week. It stocks more than twenty makes, from Michelin, Continental, Pirelli, Goodyear, Bridgestone and Dunlop at the premium end through Hankook, Yokohama, Vredestein and Uniroyal in the middle to Giti, Leao, Maxxis, Triangle and Westlake at the budget end. Every price the website shows is a fully fitted price, and the company is unusually clear about what that means: the tyre, a replacement valve, wheel balancing, disposal of the tyre coming off and VAT are all inside the figure you see before you book.

You search by registration or by the size on the sidewall, pick a centre and pick a slot. The prices online are the cheapest Kwik Fit does, and the company says so plainly — its web prices are exclusive to the site and most of its promotions never appear in a centre. Mobile fitting is a genuine alternative rather than a token: over 200 mobile fitters work seven days a week, evening slots included, and fitting at your home or workplace is free when you buy four tyres online. The free tyre check is done properly, with the car raised so each wheel can be turned, rather than as a glance across a forecourt.

Every new tyre carries a guarantee against manufacturing defects for the legal life of the tyre. A puncture, where the damage allows, is repaired from £29.95 including a new valve and rebalancing, to the British Standard the trade works to. Tyre insurance against accidental and malicious damage is offered separately at the counter, underwritten by a company regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, from £6.99 a tyre.

What the price on the screen actually buys

The number beside each tyre is the fitted number. Kwik Fit builds it from the tyre itself plus a fixed charge of £20.50 per tyre covering fitting, wheel balancing, the new valve and disposal of the old casing, with VAT on top of all of it. That fixed element is worth holding on to, because it is the part of the bill that never goes on sale. Kwik Fit states in the small print of its own promotions that all tyre discounts "apply to the cost of the tyre only and exclude charges for fitting, balancing, valve and disposal". On a set of four, that leaves £82.00 of the bill outside any code you enter.

The codes themselves are generous enough on the rubber. At the time of checking the site was running 10% off two Pirelli tyres or 15% off four under SAVEPI, 10% off two or more Goodyears under KFGOOD, 10% off two Continentals under COTYKF, 15% off four Giti tyres under GITISAVE, 10% off two Hankooks under KFHAN and 15% off four Bridgestone Turanza All Season 6 under KFBRAS. Students and key workers are offered up to 15%. Codes cannot be stacked with any other offer unless the offer says so, and only the Continental promotion carried a visible countdown to its expiry.

One thing the site does not do is publish a price list you can check against a counter. Kwik Fit says the prices on kwik-fit.com "are exclusive to the website and may vary from the prices set in your local Kwik Fit centre", and explains the gap as web promotions plus the efficiency of ordering stock on demand rather than holding it in every branch. The web price is the low one. There is no published in-centre equivalent to compare it with, so booking online is the only way to know what you are being charged before you arrive.

Fitting at a centre, or on your own driveway

Two speeds are on offer. Express fitting covers same-day and next-day appointments where the tyre is already in the right place; otherwise the site typically offers appointments three or five days from the date of purchase, because the tyre is ordered in for you. If you want the work done where the car already is, mobile fitting reaches most urban postcodes seven days a week, with an anytime slot cheaper than a half-day slot and the charge running up to £30 depending on which you choose. Kwik Fit gives a two-hour arrival window, the customer has to be there, and the fitter needs a level surface and room to work — steep drives, lowered cars and some heavier electric vehicles are the awkward cases. Wheels are balanced off the car and refitted with a new valve, the same as in a centre.

Two limits are worth knowing before you book. Tyres bought from Kwik Fit have to be fitted by Kwik Fit: the company states flatly that it is "not our company policy to offer a cash and carry service", so you cannot buy at the online price and carry the tyre to a cheaper fitter or your own garage. And mobile appointments are fitment-only, so a tyre cannot be dropped off and left.

The free tyre check is booked the same way, by registration. The technician inspects the whole tread rather than the outer shoulder, checks both inner and outer sidewalls, looks at wear pattern, size, load index and speed rating, and raises the car so each wheel can be rotated. Kwik Fit makes the point itself that a look at a tyre on a forecourt, with a quarter of its circumference on the ground, is not a check at all.

The letters on the sidewall label, and what one class is worth

Every new car tyre sold in Great Britain carries a standardised label, and the rules behind it are assimilated Regulation (EU) 2020/740, the revised scheme that took effect in May 2021. A distributor has to make sure the label is visible at the point of sale or, where the tyres are not on display, that the information reaches you during the buying process, and the same figures must appear in brochures and on websites.

For a car tyre the fuel efficiency classes are set against rolling resistance measured in newtons per kilonewton: class A is 6.5 or below, B runs 6.6 to 7.7, C 7.8 to 9.0, D 9.1 to 10.5 and E is 10.6 or above. Wet grip uses an index instead, where A is 1.55 or better, B 1.40 to 1.54, C 1.25 to 1.39, D 1.10 to 1.24 and E 1.09 or below. The European Commission's own guidance puts a single class step at three to six metres of extra stopping distance in the wet, and about 80 litres of fuel across the life of a set on a petrol or diesel car. Noise is shown as a measured figure in decibels alongside a three-bar symbol, against type-approval ceilings that run between 70 and 77 dB(A) depending on the tyre class; three decibels is a doubling of sound energy, so the gap between quiet and loud is larger than it looks. Two pictograms sit outside the letter scales: the three-peak mountain snowflake for tyres that pass a snow braking test, and a separate icon for tyres that pass an ice test.

The one thing to watch is that the scale changed. The 2009 scheme it replaced ran from A to G, and some official guidance pages still describe tyre labels that way. On a current car tyre the bottom of both scales is E.

What the law asks of a tyre, and what ignoring it costs

The standard everyone quotes comes from regulation 27 of the Road Vehicles (Construction and Use) Regulations 1986. For cars, light goods vehicles up to 3,500kg, zero-emission goods vehicles up to 4,250kg and light trailers, the grooves of the tread pattern must be at least 1.6mm deep in a continuous band across the central three-quarters of the breadth of the tread and round the entire outer circumference. Vehicles with more than eight passenger seats, and anything first used before 3 January 1933, work to 1.0mm. The same regulation bans a cut longer than 25mm or 10% of the section width that reaches the ply or cord, any lump, bulge or tear from a structural failure, exposed ply or cord, and a tyre not inflated fit for the use it is being put to.

The MOT tester applies the same list. Tread below the limit, a bulge, exposed cords and a valve likely to deflate are dangerous defects. A cut to the cords, two different sizes on one axle, radial and cross-ply mixed on one axle and a tyre pressure monitoring system that is not working are major defects. That last one catches more drivers than it should: TPMS is inspected on every M1 vehicle first used on or after 1 January 2012, with motor caravans, ambulances and hearses over 2,500kg excluded, so a warning light left on after a tyre change is a fail on a car that is otherwise perfect.

Get it wrong on the road and the offence sits under section 41A of the Road Traffic Act 1988. For a car the maximum is a level 4 fine of £2,500 with three penalty points; over 3.5 tonnes it rises to level 5, which has been unlimited for offences committed since 13 March 2015. The detail people miss is that the fine is charged per defective tyre while the points are normally endorsed once, so four bald tyres is a four-figure sum rather than a ticket.

Whether 1.6mm is where you should actually stop is a different question, and Kwik Fit answers it the way most of the trade does: many manufacturers advise replacing at 3mm, because stopping distances lengthen sharply below that. The 20p test works because the outer rim of the coin is just under 3mm — if the rim disappears into the groove you are somewhere above the legal minimum, not necessarily above the sensible one. For scale, TyreSafe's reading of the Department for Transport casualty figures put tyre-related deaths and serious injuries at 172 in 2024 against a five-year average of 163, with a 24% jump among riders of powered two-wheelers, the highest in a decade. The Department's own 2025 report, published on 30 July 2026, recorded 1,538 road deaths and 29,918 people killed or seriously injured.

When a puncture can be repaired, and when the answer is a new tyre

Kwik Fit repairs to British Standard BS AU 159, which fixes where on a tyre a repair may be made, how big it may be and how many are allowed. In practice that means the central third of the tread, known as the minor repair area. A nail through the middle of the tread is usually repairable; the same nail in the shoulder, close to the sidewall, is not, because the flexing there will not tolerate a plug and patch.

A repair is also refused if the tread is already below 1.6mm, if the object has done secondary damage inside the casing, if the rubber has deteriorated with age, if the bead or sidewall is damaged, if cords are showing, if a previous repair has failed, or if the tyre is bulging. Run-flat tyres are not repaired at all, because their reinforced sidewalls hide the structural damage caused by driving on them deflated — which is precisely what they are designed to let you do. Owners of cars delivered on run-flats should price a puncture at the cost of a tyre, not at the £29.95 a repair starts from.

Tyre age: the ten-year rule that is not about your car

The ten-year figure attached to tyres is real law, and it does not apply to the car on your drive. The Road Vehicles (Construction and Use) (Amendment) Regulations 2020, in force from 1 February 2021, prohibit a tyre whose week of manufacture — the code moulded into the sidewall under ECE Regulation 30 or 54 — falls more than ten years before the day the vehicle is used, on the front axle of a bus, on any single-wheel axle of a minibus, and on the front axle of a goods vehicle over 3,500kg. Retreads are dated from the retreading. The MOT manual mirrors it, failing tyres over ten years old on any front steered axle and on a minibus rear axle with single wheels.

For an ordinary car there is no legal age limit at all. What there is instead is advice, and Kwik Fit gives the same figure the manufacturers do: around ten years is the outer limit for a tyre regardless of how much tread is left, because rubber hardens and cracks whether or not the car is driven. A caravan, a classic or a second car doing a thousand miles a year is the case where tread depth tells you almost nothing and the date code tells you everything.

Second-hand tyres are held to a higher number than the one on your car

Kwik Fit's range is new tyres, and it is worth understanding what the alternative is legally required to be. Under the Motor Vehicle Tyres (Safety) Regulations 1994, a part-worn tyre sold in England, Scotland or Wales must have at least 2mm of original tread across the full breadth of the tread and round the entire outer circumference, and must be marked "PART-WORN" in capitals at least 4mm high, permanently and legibly, next to every approval mark — and not by hot-branding or cutting. It must also be free of the same defects the road regulations ban: no cut over 25mm or 10% of the section width, no bulges or tears, no exposed cords, no unrepaired penetration damage.

Read those two numbers together and the oddity stands out. A part-worn tyre must have 2mm across its full width before it may legally be sold, while a tyre already on your car is legal at 1.6mm across the middle three-quarters. The second-hand tyre is held to the higher standard, which is a useful thing to know when a price two-thirds below a new fitted price is being explained to you.

TyreCare, the insurance sold at the tyre counter

TyreCare is a real insurance contract, not a warranty extension, and it is priced off what the tyre cost: £6.99 on a tyre under £50, then £11.99, £16.99, £21.99, £27.99 and £32.99 as the price climbs in fifty-pound steps, and £42.99 on anything over £300. Insurance premium tax is included. It is underwritten by London General Insurance Company Limited, company number 1865673 of Staines-upon-Thames, authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority and the PRA under firm reference 202689. Kwik-Fit (GB) Ltd arranges and administers it, and states in the paperwork that it gives information rather than a personal recommendation.

What it covers is accidental damage — sudden, unforeseen deflation or other damage to the tyre or the valve — and malicious damage by a third party, which needs a police crime reference number. Repairs and replacements come with fitting, valve and balancing at no extra cost. What it does not cover is a long list: any tyre below the UK legal tread depth, wear and tear, manufacturing defects, fire, theft, damage outside the UK, Isle of Man and Channel Islands, damage to anything other than the tyre, consequential loss, vehicles used for hire, driving instruction, chauffeuring, racing, track days or rallying, commercial and agricultural use, modified vehicles, tyres without DOT markings, and buyers under seventeen.

The shape of the cover matters more than the price. It runs twelve months from purchase, but it ends earlier on the first replacement of the tyre, on the third repair, or when you sell the car — so a single unrepairable puncture consumes the policy on that tyre rather than leaving cover in place for the new one. You have fourteen days to cancel for a full refund if you have not claimed, and afterwards a pro-rata refund with a £5 minimum retained. Complaints go to Kwik Fit customer services on 0800 757677 and then, if you are not satisfied within six months, to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme covers 90% of a general insurance claim without an upper limit.

Two things about the documents themselves. The policy wording carries the version reference V12_0420 and the insurance product information document is FV1.6, dated March 2021, while the premium ladder on the live page runs up to £42.99 — the paperwork is several years older than the prices it sits beside. And neither document states Kwik-Fit (GB) Ltd's own regulatory status or firm reference number, only the insurer's; it could not be confirmed from the material Kwik Fit publishes, and a buyer deciding whether to take a regulated product from a tyre counter would reasonably want it on the page.

One group fits the tyre, another arm delivers it and a third grinds it up

Kwik Fit sits inside European Tyre Enterprise Limited, formed in 2011 when ITOCHU Corporation brought several long-established tyre and automotive businesses together, with group sales above £1.2 billion. The retail chains are Kwik Fit and STS Tyre Pros. The wholesale arm is Stapletons Tyre Services, which distributes tyres to the trade through eleven hubs. And since 20 December 2021 the group has also owned Murfitts Industries, the tyre recycler at Lakenheath in Suffolk, which handles something like 20 million car tyres a year and turns them into granulate for sports surfaces, playgrounds, pathways, carpet underlay and modified asphalt.

That makes the disposal element of the £20.50 unusual: the charge for taking your old tyre away is paid to a group that also owns the business which processes it and sells the material on. None of this is hidden — ETEL says so on its own site — and it is a reasonable explanation of why Kwik Fit can quote one all-in figure while independents itemise disposal separately. It is simply worth knowing that the fitting price covers three stages of the same group's business.

If the tyre fails, or the bill turns out to be wrong

The guarantee document is versioned and dated, which is more than most chains manage — V1.9, 30 January 2026. On tyres it says all new tyres are covered against manufacturing defects throughout the legal life of the tyre, and that where a defect is confirmed by the manufacturer's inspection you receive a refund for the remaining tread. That is not the same as a free replacement: it is money back in proportion to the tread you had not used, and it depends on producing the original paperwork, which the document tells you twice to keep safe. Reasonable wear, tear and corrosion are excluded, as is damage from criminal or malicious acts, fire and theft, and anything another garage has worked on since.

Wheel alignment is treated separately and honestly: Kwik Fit says alignment is affected by road conditions and driving and therefore cannot be covered by its guarantees, but it will recheck and readjust free of charge within 30 days of the work.

The complaints route is Customer Care on 0800 75 76 77. There is a real asymmetry here worth understanding before you buy the add-on rather than after. A dispute about TyreCare is a dispute about a regulated insurance contract, so it can be taken to the Financial Ombudsman Service, free, with a binding outcome. A dispute about the tyre, the fitting or the bill is an ordinary consumer contract; Kwik Fit's terms refer to an alternative dispute resolution scheme without naming it, so the only certain route is the county court or a claim under the Consumer Rights Act. The insurance you might not need has a stronger complaints backstop than the fitting you definitely bought.

Common questions

Does the Kwik Fit price include fitting?

Yes. The price shown online is fully fitted and covers the tyre, a replacement valve, wheel balancing, disposal of the old tyre and VAT. The fitting element is £20.50 per tyre, and it is excluded from every discount code, so a promotional percentage applies only to the cost of the rubber.

What is the legal tread depth, and when should I actually replace a tyre?

The law requires at least 1.6mm across the central three-quarters of the tread and round the whole circumference of the tyre. Driving on less risks a level 4 fine of up to £2,500 and three penalty points, charged per tyre. Many manufacturers advise replacing at 3mm, because wet stopping distances lengthen quickly below that, and the outer rim of a 20p coin is just under 3mm if you want a rough check at home.

Can Kwik Fit repair my puncture rather than sell me a tyre?

Often, but not always. Repairs follow British Standard BS AU 159, which allows them only in the central third of the tread. A puncture near the shoulder or sidewall, a tyre already below 1.6mm, exposed cords, a bulge, ageing rubber or a previous failed repair all rule it out, and run-flat tyres are never repaired. A repair starts at £29.95 including a new valve and rebalancing.

Is TyreCare worth buying?

It insures the tyre against accidental and malicious damage for twelve months at £6.99 to £42.99 depending on what the tyre cost, and it is underwritten by an FCA-regulated insurer with Ombudsman and FSCS backing. The catch is that cover on a tyre ends at the first replacement or the third repair, and nothing is paid on a tyre already below the legal tread depth. On an expensive tyre in a city where kerbing and vandalism are likely it can pay for itself in one claim; on a cheap tyre it rarely will.

Can I buy tyres from Kwik Fit and fit them myself?

No. Kwik Fit states that all tyres must be fitted by its own staff and that it does not operate a cash and carry service. Mobile appointments are fitment-only as well, so a tyre cannot be delivered and left with you.

Will a tyre pressure warning light fail the MOT?

Yes, on most modern cars. A tyre pressure monitoring system that is not working is a major defect, and it is inspected on every M1 vehicle first used on or after 1 January 2012, with motor caravans, ambulances and hearses over 2,500kg excluded. Have the sensor or the service kit sorted at the same time as the tyre rather than after the test.

What are the rules on part-worn tyres?

They may be sold, but under the Motor Vehicle Tyres (Safety) Regulations 1994 they must carry at least 2mm of original tread across the full breadth and round the whole circumference, be marked PART-WORN in capitals at least 4mm high next to every approval mark, and be free of cuts over 25mm or 10% of the section width, bulges, exposed cords and unrepaired damage. That 2mm is a higher figure than the 1.6mm your own tyres are legal at.

Our verdict

Kwik Fit is the most convenient way to buy tyres in Britain and it is honest about the number on the screen: the tyre, the valve, the balancing, the disposal and the VAT are all in it, at over 600 centres and through more than 200 mobile fitters seven days a week. Read the arithmetic before you enter a discount code, because £20.50 a tyre sits outside every promotion the site runs, and there is no published centre price to compare the web price with. The tyre guarantee is real but narrow — manufacturing defects only, settled as a refund for unused tread — and TyreCare is worth the £6.99 to £42.99 only if you understand that it stops at the first replacement. On price alone an online-only retailer with a local fitter will often beat it; on getting four tyres onto a car this weekend, at a published price, with a free check first and somebody to ring afterwards, very little else comes close.
