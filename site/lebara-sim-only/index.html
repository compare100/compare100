Lebara SIM Only: the price that has not moved since 2020

Rates and terms checked 15 November 2026 · SIM Only · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Lebara sells SIM-only plans from £5 a month on Vodafone's masts, and it says it has not raised a monthly price since 2020. Its own shop puts the UK customer count at more than 3 million and claims 98% coverage from the network underneath. Every figure here was read on 15 November 2026 from Lebara's own pages, Companies House, Ofcom, the CMA and the ASA.

The company started in 2001 selling international calling cards at 1p a minute, and the name is the first two letters of its three founders' names — LEon, BAskaran and RAtheesan. It now runs in five countries, sells SIMs through Tesco, Aldi and Sainsbury's as well as online, and in July 2026 Which? made it one of just five Recommended Providers in UK mobile, on a customer score of 78% against the 72% of Vodafone, the network it rents.

Two things are worth knowing before you click. Plans run either 30 days or 12 months, and there are no Lebara shops — everything is done online, by phone or by live chat. And Lebara's own plan pages would not open to an automated reader on 15 November 2026, so the data allowance each price buys is not quoted on this page. The prices are on the plan grid itself; what follows is everything around them that can be checked.

Vodafone adds £2.50 every April. Lebara says it has added nothing since 2020

Vodafone's consumer price policy is published in one sentence: “The monthly cost will increase each year on 1 April by £2.50 for Pay monthly plans with Airtime/Data.” Out-of-bundle charges rise separately, by the Consumer Price Index rate published in January of that year plus 3.9%. Customers on a social tariff or registered as financially vulnerable are left out of both.

Lebara rents that network and has taken the opposite position. Its own shop states that there have been no yearly price rises since 2020, and Which? describes the same thing from the customer's side: 30-day plans carry no mid-contract inflation increase, 12-month plans carry no annual rise, and if the renewal price is too high you can move network without a penalty.

The arithmetic is what makes it concrete. A £5 plan subject to Vodafone's own policy would cost £7.50 after one April and £10 after two — double in 24 months, on a price that started at the bottom of the market. On a £20 plan the same £2.50 is a 12.5% increase in the first year alone. Ofcom has said it will review its intervention on mid-contract price rises in 2027, so the gap between a capped plan and an uncapped one is being watched rather than settled.

The masts are Vodafone's, and 98% is not a 5G number

Lebara's 98% figure describes the network as a whole, which is the honest way to read any MVNO's coverage claim: the SIM reaches whatever Vodafone reaches. Ofcom's own measurement is less flattering on the newer generation. Mobile Matters, published 17 July 2025 and crowdsourced between October 2024 and March 2025, put 76% of Vodafone connections on 4G — the highest share of the four operators — and only 24% on 5G, the lowest. EE reached 32% on 5G, and Three had the fastest 5G downloads and the lowest 5G latency.

That is changing under Lebara's feet, because Vodafone and Three are now one business. A report by Frontier Economics published in July 2026 tracks the rebuild: multi-operator core network sharing live at more than 600 sites in August 2025, 8,000 by November 2025 and more than 10,000 mast sites by April 2026, with additional 1800 MHz spectrum deployed on nearly 15,000 legacy Three sites. More than 21 million customers were said to be benefiting by November 2025, and 7 million Three customers saw 4G speeds rise by an average of 20%.

Vodafone's 5G population coverage went from 59% to 64% between May 2025 and March 2026, and Three's from 65% to 72%. A Lebara customer rides all of it without being party to any of it, which is the position of every virtual operator and the reason the next section matters more than the marketing.

A regulator wrote the floor under this SIM's wholesale deal

On 5 December 2024 the Competition and Markets Authority cleared the Vodafone and Three merger, but only against legally binding commitments. Stuart McIntosh, who chaired the inquiry group, put it plainly: “It's crucial this merger doesn't harm competition,” and the merger “should be allowed to proceed — but only if Vodafone and Three agree to implement our proposed measures.”

Three of those measures reach a Lebara customer directly. The combined company must deliver an eight-year joint network plan, overseen by Ofcom and the CMA and reported on annually. Selected retail tariffs and data plans are capped for three years. And for three years the company must offer virtual operators “pre-agreed non-discriminatory wholesale terms (including prices)”, and roll over existing commercial terms with current wholesale customers when those come up for renewal inside the protection period.

Lebara is named in the CMA's own summary, alongside Sky Mobile, Tesco Mobile, Lyca Mobile and iD Mobile, as a value-focused virtual operator exerting some constraint on the market. The parties' own claim for the programme is £11 billion of UK investment. Virtual operators and network sub-brands together held 25.6% of UK mobile subscriptions in December 2025, up from 23.9% a year earlier, so the segment Lebara sits in is growing while the networks above it consolidate.

The limit is in the word “time-limited”. The retail caps and the wholesale terms run for three years; the network plan they were attached to runs for eight.

The international minutes are the product, and the country list has edges

This is a calling-card company that became a network reseller, and the inheritance shows in what the plans include rather than what they cost. Which? records international minutes on most packages reaching 41 countries, Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the USA among them, with free EU roaming on every deal and — unusually for a UK plan — roaming in India included as well. Switzerland, Monaco and Turkey are not covered.

Lebara also sells a separate travel eSIM through its own app, and the two numbers on that listing do not agree: the header offers data in 180 countries and the description underneath says 190+ destinations, through local network partners. Neither is printed here as the count, because the provider's own page carries both. The app itself is published by Lebara Media Services Limited under a Lebara Holding Limited copyright line, at version 1.23.1 in October.

The older pay-as-you-go business is still there and still works on top-ups of £5 to £50. The validity rule, as published by a top-up distributor rather than by Lebara, is that credit stays live while the SIM is used at least every 90 days; after 90 days of inactivity the credit becomes invalid, and after a further 365 days the SIM is deactivated and anything left on it is lost. That needs confirming against Lebara's own terms, which could not be read.

Who you are buying from, and who owns the name on the SIM

The contract is with Lebara Mobile Limited, company 06184980, incorporated 26 March 2007, active, registered at 5th Floor Broadwalk House, 5 Appold Street, London EC2A 2DA under SIC code 61900, other telecommunications activities. The 2001 founding story belongs to a different entity: Lebara Limited, company 04293563, was incorporated on 25 September 2001, which is the date the brand dates itself from.

Control moved inside the group in 2023. Lebara Holding Limited, company 14304790 and incorporated 18 August 2022, was notified as the person with significant control on 30 March 2023, and Lebara Limited ceased to be one on 25 May 2023. The register also shows a charge created on 4 February 2025 running to 58 pages, a board change on 1 July 2025 that replaced one director with two, and the registered office being amended twice in two days in October 2025 to correct Broadway House to Broadwalk House.

One filing is worth reading closely. Full accounts to 31 December 2024 were filed on 11 April 2025 and run to 30 pages. For 2025 the trading company filed audit exemption subsidiary accounts of 25 pages on 7 September 2026, together with a parent company statement of guarantee and 73 pages of consolidated parent accounts. The figures are still published somewhere; they are just no longer audited at the level of the company whose name is on the SIM.

The brand is also licensed outwards. Lebara's own shop says SIMs reach customers through brand licence partners as well as convenience shops and national retailers, and the phone shop at phones.lebara.co.uk is operated by AO Retail Ltd, company 03914998 of 5a The Parklands, Lostock, Bolton BL6 4SD, VAT number 945 6981 69.

There is no handset contract here at all, and that is deliberate

Lebara sells no phone on an airtime contract. Its shop lists handsets SIM-free only, from £229 for a Samsung Galaxy A26 5G to £1,299 for an iPhone 18 Pro Max, with an iPhone 17 at £899, a Galaxy S26 at £699 and premium refurbished stock from £229. If you want to spread the cost, the credit is NewDay Ltd's at 24.9% APR variable on an assumed £1,200 credit limit, and the monthly figures shown run from £6.62 to £40.48.

Separating the phone from the airtime is the better deal for most people and it removes the main reason anyone signs a two-year mobile contract. It also moves the consumer rights. The sale is AO's, not Lebara's: 14 days to change your mind under clause 13.3, 30 days to return a faulty handset under clauses 14.9 and 14.10, a refund within 14 days of the goods coming back under 14.11.2, prices that “may change at any time and without notice” under 7.3, and clause 16 making it your job to arrange airtime disconnection. Two contracts, two companies, two sets of terms.

The word unlimited, a policy with no numbers in it, and a ruling from 2022

On 10 August 2022 the Advertising Standards Authority upheld a complaint against Lebara Mobile Ltd over six 30-day SIM-only plans advertised on its own website as carrying “Unlimited UK minutes” and “Unlimited UK texts”. The ads had been seen on 2 February 2022. The caps Lebara actually applied were 3,000 minutes a month and 200 texts a day, and the ASA noted that some legitimate customers had exceeded them. It ruled the ads misleading under CAP Code rules 3.1, 3.3, 3.7 and 3.9, told Lebara the ads must not appear again, and told it not to describe minutes and texts as unlimited where a usage cap applies. It is the only Lebara ruling on the ASA's register.

Four years on, the obvious question is what the caps are now, and the answer is that Lebara's own documents do not say. The one policy document its site will release is the Fair Usage Policy, issued by Lebara Mobile Limited, which sets out eleven prohibited uses lettered a to k — spam, GSM gateways, automatic diallers, nuisance calls, viruses — and contains not a single number. No data cap. No minute or text cap. No tethering limit. No roaming day count. The only figure in it is a customer service telephone number.

The one cap figure in print belongs to somebody else. Which? reports that Lebara's unlimited data contracts are capped at 30GB fair usage. That is Which?'s number, not Lebara's, and it is quoted here as such. In Lebara's favour, Which? also records that out-of-bundle charges are set to zero by default, so running past an allowance stops the service rather than building a bill — which is the opposite of how a cap usually hurts.

A complaint here does not go to Vodafone's ombudsman

Ofcom approves two dispute schemes and the split runs straight through this market. Lebara belongs to the Communications Ombudsman, along with BT, EE, giffgaff, iD Mobile, Plusnet, Tesco Mobile and Utility Warehouse. Vodafone — the network Lebara runs on — belongs to CISAS, as do O2, Three, Sky, Smarty, VOXI, Lyca Mobile, TalkTalk, Virgin Media and NOW. You complain to Lebara first, and after six weeks, or on a deadlock letter, you can take it to its scheme without charge. Ombudsman Services was renamed Communications Ombudsman in July 2023, so older references to the old name point at the same body.

Ofcom's quarterly complaints report is the place to look next, and for Lebara it comes up empty rather than bad. The latest edition covers Q4 2025, October to December. It names O2 as the most complained-about pay-monthly mobile provider, mostly over contracts, and EE, Tesco Mobile and Three as the least complained about. Neither Lebara nor Vodafone appears in either group, and the per-100,000 figures sit in interactive charts and a downloadable dataset rather than in the page text, so no Lebara complaints rate is quoted here.

There are no Lebara shops. Support is by telephone and live chat, and the only number in any policy document its site releases is the customer service line on +44 207 0310791.

What could not be read, and what that costs a buyer

Nine attempts were made on 15 November 2026 to read Lebara's own pages: the SIM-only plan grid on two hostnames, the home page on three, the terms and conditions page twice, and the sitemap. Every one returned a client error. The same host served its robots.txt without complaint, and that file allows every crawler on every path, naming Anthropic's among fifteen it welcomes by name. So this is not a site asking not to be read; it is a site that an automated reader cannot reach.

Five things are therefore missing from this page, and each of them is a thing worth checking yourself before you buy. The plan grid and what each price buys. The early termination charge on the 12-month plan, which no readable Lebara document states. The EU roaming data cap and the out-of-bundle rates. The international country list in Lebara's own words rather than Which?'s. And the pre-contract contract summary that Ofcom requires every provider to give you before you commit, which could not be located for any Lebara plan.

What survives all of that is a verifiable shape: a cheap entry price on a big network, international minutes built in, a price-rise promise its host network does not match, an independent score six points above that host, and a wholesale deal a regulator has underwritten for three years. The two numbers you still have to read off the plan page are the price and the allowance — which happen to be the two the page itself shows you first.

  • One of only five Which? Recommended Providers in UK mobile on 28 July 2026, scoring 78% against Vodafone's 72% — and on a sample of 249, the largest of the five.
  • International minutes are built into most plans rather than sold as an add-on, reaching 41 countries including Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the USA.
  • Roaming in India is included alongside EU roaming on every deal, which almost no other UK plan offers.
  • 30-day plans carry no mid-contract inflation rise and 12-month plans no annual rise, so the price agreed is the price paid to the end of the term.
  • Out-of-bundle charges are set to zero by default, so an allowance runs out instead of running up a bill.

  • Phones are sold SIM-free from £229 rather than bundled into a two-year airtime contract, which is the cheaper way round for most people.

Common questions

Which network does Lebara use?

Vodafone. Lebara's own shop claims 98% coverage on Vodafone's network, which is a whole-network figure rather than a 5G one. Ofcom's Mobile Matters, published 17 July 2025, put 76% of Vodafone connections on 4G and 24% on 5G, the lowest 5G share of the four operators, against EE's 32%. Vodafone's 5G population coverage rose from 59% to 64% between May 2025 and March 2026 as the merged Vodafone and Three network was rebuilt.

Does Lebara put its prices up every year?

It says it has not since 2020. Which? records the same position from the customer side: no mid-contract inflation increase on 30-day plans, no annual rise on 12-month plans, and no penalty for leaving if the renewal price is too high. Vodafone, whose masts Lebara rents, raises its own pay-monthly plans by £2.50 every 1 April and its out-of-bundle charges by January's CPI plus 3.9%. Ofcom has said it will review its intervention on price rises in 2027.

Is Lebara's unlimited data really unlimited?

Which? reports a 30GB fair usage cap on unlimited data contracts. That figure is Which?'s: Lebara's own published Fair Usage Policy lists eleven prohibited uses and contains no numeric limit of any kind. The ASA upheld a ruling against the company on 10 August 2022 over unlimited minute and text claims that carried caps of 3,000 minutes a month and 200 texts a day. On the other side, out-of-bundle charges are set to zero by default, so an allowance running out does not create a bill.

Where do Lebara's international minutes reach?

Which? puts it at 41 countries on most packages, including Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the USA, with EU roaming on every deal and roaming in India included — which is rare on a UK plan. Switzerland, Monaco and Turkey are not covered. Lebara's separate travel eSIM app quotes 180 countries in its header and 190+ destinations in its description, so neither figure is given here as the count.

Who do I complain to if something goes wrong?

Lebara first, then the Communications Ombudsman, which is the Ofcom-approved scheme Lebara belongs to along with BT, EE, giffgaff, iD Mobile and Tesco Mobile. You can escalate after six weeks, or sooner on a deadlock letter, and it costs nothing. Note that Vodafone, O2, Three, Smarty and VOXI belong to the other approved scheme, CISAS, so Lebara and its own host network answer to different adjudicators.

Can I buy a phone from Lebara on a contract?

No. Lebara sells handsets SIM-free only, from £229 for a Samsung Galaxy A26 5G to £1,299 for an iPhone 18 Pro Max, through a shop operated by AO Retail Ltd, company 03914998. Spreading the cost means credit from NewDay Ltd at 24.9% APR variable on an assumed £1,200 credit limit, with monthly figures from £6.62. The handset sale and the airtime plan are two contracts with two different companies.

Our verdict

Lebara is the cheap end of a big network with the international calling of its calling-card past still built in, and the strongest thing on it is the price promise: £5 to start, nothing added since 2020, and no annual rise on either plan length, against a host network that adds £2.50 every April. Which? members rank it six points above Vodafone and the CMA has underwritten its wholesale terms for three years. The weakness is disclosure rather than product. The fair usage policy it publishes contains no numbers, the 30GB cap on unlimited data is Which?'s figure rather than Lebara's, and the ASA has already told the company once not to call a capped service unlimited. Read the price and the allowance off the plan page, and check the exit terms before a 12-month plan.
