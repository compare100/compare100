The membership is worth most on the cheaper bookings, which is the opposite of what you might assume.

One thing we could not establish: what the LuxPlus+ membership costs. The site invites you to “join our VIP Travel Club” and links to its terms, but no price appeared on any page we could read. Work out the sum yourself before joining — if the fee is more than the gap on the one trip you are booking, it only pays if you book again.

Prices are listed as “from” figures, and at least one offer carried an “ends in 3 days” countdown. Treat those as the usual deal-site urgency: the same hotel will have another offer.

The ATOL question, and what to ask before you pay

Here is the part we want you to read properly, because it is about your money rather than your room.

The site states: “ATOL protection provided for eligible flights and flight inclusive holidays”, and an “ATOL protected” badge appears near the top of the page. That is the right claim for a business selling flight-inclusive holidays to UK customers.

What we could not find, on the homepage, the booking policies page or the FAQ pages we read, is the ATOL number itself. An ATOL holder has a licence number, and the scheme works by you receiving an ATOL Certificate when you book, naming the licence holder and showing exactly what is protected. Without the number you cannot check the licence on the Civil Aviation Authority’s own register before you hand over money.

We are not suggesting the claim is untrue — we are telling you we could not verify it from the published pages, and that the verification is normally one click away. So:

  • Ask for the ATOL number before you pay for anything flight-inclusive. The 24-hour phone line is 0808 175 4987, and this is exactly what it is for.
  • Check that number on the CAA’s ATOL register yourself. It takes a minute and it is free.
  • Make sure the ATOL Certificate arrives with your confirmation, and read which parts of the trip it covers. A hotel-only booking is not ATOL protected, and never was — the protection attaches to the flight.
  • Pay by credit card. On a four-figure holiday that is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.

We also found no ABTA membership claimed, and no named dispute resolution scheme or ombudsman on the pages we read.

The second one is the one to be aware of: an active proposal to strike off means a company is heading towards being removed from the register. Its stated activities suggest it is not the travel seller, and the overseas Singapore registration looks the likelier contracting party — but “looks likelier” is not good enough when you are paying a deposit.

So ask. The company you are contracting with should be named on your booking confirmation and on the ATOL Certificate. Read it when it arrives, and keep it.

The underlying business is large and real — an Australian-founded group that entered the UK market and trades across several countries — so this is a disclosure problem rather than a credibility one. But a travel site that takes four-figure payments should name its company on its own pages, and this one does not.

What we could not read, and why we are telling you

We tried to report the cancellation and refund terms for you and could not. The booking policies page we fetched returned navigation and metadata rather than policy text, and the same was true of the FAQ and shop terms pages. So, plainly: we do not know what this site’s cancellation terms say, and we are not going to summarise terms we have not read.

That matters more here than on most sites, because of how this kind of offer usually works. Deal-based luxury travel is often sold as a purchase now, choose your dates later arrangement, with a validity period, blackout dates and a surcharge on peak nights. Whether that is how these offers work, how long you have to redeem, and what happens if you never do, are the three questions we would want answered before buying.

Before you pay, get these in writing:

  • Is this a dated booking or a voucher I redeem later, and if it is a voucher, when does it expire?
  • What is the cancellation scale, and is any part non-refundable?
  • Is there an amendment fee for changing dates, and are there blackout periods or peak supplements?
  • What is the ATOL number, and will I receive a certificate?

One more thing we noticed in passing. The pages carry “4.7/5” and “4.8/5” rating badges with no source named and no number of reviews. A rating with no provenance is decoration, not evidence, and we would not weigh it either way.

On the positive side of service, the contact arrangement is better than most: a freephone number on 0808 175 4987, staffed 24 hours a day, seven days a week. For a long-haul booking in a different time zone, that is genuinely valuable, and it is the number to use for every question in the list above.

Common questions

Is Luxury Escapes ATOL protected?

It says so: “ATOL protection provided for eligible flights and flight inclusive holidays”, with an ATOL badge on the site. But we could not find the ATOL number published on the homepage, the booking policies page or the FAQ pages we read, and without it you cannot check the licence on the Civil Aviation Authority register. Ring 0808 175 4987 and ask for the number before you pay for anything flight-inclusive, then check it yourself and make sure the ATOL Certificate arrives with your confirmation.

Is my hotel-only booking protected too?

No. ATOL protection attaches to flights and flight-inclusive holidays, so a hotel-only stay is outside the scheme however prominent the badge is. For a hotel-only booking your protection is your card provider and your travel insurance, which is a good reason to pay by credit card.

How much does membership save?

Between £20 and £70 on the nine offers we read. The largest gaps were £70 on three nights at the Harbour Grand Hong Kong, £349 against £419, and £70 on two nights in Paris, £529 against £599. The £849 long-haul stays saved only £40. We could not find the membership price published anywhere, so compare the fee against the gap on the trip you are actually booking.

What is the cancellation policy?

We do not know, and we will not summarise terms we have not read. The booking policies and FAQ pages we fetched returned navigation rather than policy text. Before paying, get four things in writing: whether you are buying fixed dates or a voucher and when that voucher expires, the full cancellation scale, any amendment fee and peak-date supplements, and the ATOL number.

Which company am I buying from?

The site does not say, which is the problem. The public registers show two British-registered entities under the name: Luxury Escapes (Singapore) Pte. Ltd., an overseas company numbered FC038864, incorporated 1 March 2021 with accounts filed to 30 June 2025; and Luxury Escapes Ltd, number 15394496, incorporated 7 January 2024 in London and currently shown as active with a proposal to strike off, whose registered activities are hotels and real estate rather than travel. We could not establish which one contracts with you, so check the name on your confirmation and the ATOL Certificate.

Are the ratings on the site reliable?

We would not rely on them. The pages carry 4.7/5 and 4.8/5 badges with no source named and no number of reviews behind them, so there is nothing to check. Judge the offer on the hotel, the dates and the total price including any supplement, not on a score with no provenance.

Our verdict

On price and presentation this is one of the better deal sites: real luxury hotels, short stays as well as long ones, and member against non-member prices printed side by side so you can see the saving rather than take it on faith. Two nights at The Hoxton Edinburgh for £219, or three at the Harbour Grand Hong Kong for £349 against £419, are offers worth looking at. Where we have to mark it down is the paperwork, and on a site taking four-figure payments for flight-inclusive holidays the paperwork is not a detail. It says it is ATOL protected and publishes no ATOL number; it names no selling company in its footer; the British company registered under the name is shown as active with a proposal to strike off and is classified as a hotel and property business rather than a travel seller; and we could not read its cancellation terms at all. None of that means anything is wrong — it means you should make one phone call before you pay. Ring 0808 175 4987, ask for the ATOL number and the name of the contracting company, check the number on the CAA register, confirm the cancellation scale and whether you are buying dates or a voucher, pay by credit card, and keep the ATOL Certificate.
