MADE Furniture Review: Prices, Delivery and the £60 Return

Rates and terms checked 20 November 2026 · Furniture · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

MADE sells sofa beds from £375, cotton duvet sets from £35 and flush ceiling lights at £79, and the cheapest item on its own front page on 20 November 2026 was a £65 Monroe table lamp. Delivery is £4.95 for anything one person can carry and £30.00 for the two-person items, which is most of the furniture. Every price here was read from made.com and from Next's MADE listings the same day.

The design side is still recognisably MADE. The brand dates itself to 2010 and describes its ethos as being “to make great design accessible and affordable”, with an in-house team working out of a London studio. You can also now sit on the furniture before buying it: the store locator shows a MADE store of “3327 Sq. Ft.of good stuff” at Next Home on Vulcan Road, Tinsley, Sheffield S9 2YL, and 47 further MADE concessions inside other Next Home stores.

What has changed is whose shop it is. The terms at made.com, stamped “Last updated: 30/09/2026”, open by saying the site “is operated by, and the goods are supplied by Next Retail Limited trading as MADE”, giving company number 04521150, VAT number 179 7658 90 and an address at Desford Road, Enderby, Leicester LE19 4AT. The company that built the name, MADE.COM DESIGN LTD (07101408), went into administration on 8 November 2022 and has been in creditors' voluntary liquidation since 14 June 2024. An order, a refund or a promise made by the old made.com sits with that liquidation, not with the shop on screen now, and a reader chasing one needs to know that before they pick up the phone.

What the delivery line adds to a £375 sofa

MADE prices delivery by how many people it takes to carry the thing. The terms say “Our standard courier delivery charge to home is £4.95 per order for one person delivery items”, while “Items requiring 2 person delivery are charged at £30.00 per delivery”. A small parcel sent to an Evri Parcel Shop instead is £3.50. On the cheapest sofa bed in the range, the £375 Haru single, the £30 adds 8% to the bill; on the £1,099 Jude two-seater it is 2.7%, so the charge bites hardest on exactly the orders a first-time buyer is most likely to place.

There is no order value at which delivery becomes free. The only free-delivery wording in the terms attaches to a second order placed within 30 minutes of the first, and it is “only offered where a delivery fee has been paid on the initial order”. Speed is better than the price suggests — the standard next-day service covers most orders placed before 11pm — but the same document also states flatly that “Delivery times cannot be guaranteed”, and warns that during a sale “Delivery of in-stock items can take up to 28 days”. Two sentences of the same page, and the gap between them is four weeks.

Thirty days, in two tiers, and an inspection in your front room

The ordinary promise is generous: “You may cancel or return your online order/purchase within 28 days from the day the goods were delivered”, with courier, Evri and postal returns “charged at £2.50 per return/collection” and small items going back free to most Next stores. None of that is the number a sofa buyer needs, because large furniture is carved out and run on its own clock.

MADE's own help article sets it out in two bands and notes that “All timings start on the day after delivery”. From day 1 to day 14: “If you wish to return or cancel your order, you must notify us within 14 days”, and “A charge of £30 will apply per collection”. After day 15 the position changes in kind, not just in price — “We will be unable to accept a return until we have carried out a full independent inspection of the goods in your home”, after which MADE will “collect the goods and charge you £60 to cover the cost of the inspection(s), collection(s) and re-stocking”, and “The sofa or furniture will then be refunded after an additional check at our warehouse”. The terms carry the same ladder from the other end, describing “A non-refundable collection charge of £30” on large homeware, furniture and upholstery “if delivery is refused, or collection is requested, in the first 14 days”, which “will increase to £60 if you return your item/s 15-30 days after the day of delivery”.

Put the two charges together and the arithmetic is worth doing before ordering. A £375 sofa bed that turns out to be wrong costs £30 to deliver and £60 to send back on day 20, so £90 of a £375 purchase is gone and none of it comes back — 24% of the price. Decide inside the fortnight and the same mistake costs £60 all in. The conditions attached are ordinary but strict: goods must go back “in their original packaging”, with “all parts and fittings” included, and “If the item has been assembled then it cannot be returned, unless faulty”. On flat-pack furniture that means the choice has to be made before the first screw.

Regulation 35 is the reason the £30 is lawful

MADE's help article ends by asserting that “The policy above complies with the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013”, and on the question of who pays for the lorry, it has the better of the argument. Regulation 29(1) of those Regulations lets a consumer “cancel a distance or off-premises contract at any time in the cancellation period without giving any reason”, and regulation 29(2) states that the period “begins when the contract is entered into and ends in accordance with regulation 30 or 31”. Regulation 35 then deals with the goods themselves: where the trader has not offered to collect, the consumer sends them back “without undue delay and in any event not later than 14 days after the day on which the consumer informs the trader as required by regulation 32(2)”.

The decisive paragraph is 35(5): “The consumer must bear the direct cost of returning goods under paragraph (2), unless” the trader agreed to bear them, or the trader failed to give the consumer the cost information required by paragraph (m) of Schedule 2. If that information is missing, paragraph (6) rewrites the contract so that the trader pays. MADE publishes £30 and £60 in two separate documents before anyone orders, so the charge is the consumer's to carry and the criticism is not that it is unlawful. Two narrower questions are left open, and nothing on the site answers either: paragraph (7) says “The consumer is not required to bear any other cost of returning goods under paragraph (2)”, which sits awkwardly beside a £60 figure that MADE itself describes as covering inspection and re-stocking as well as collection; and paragraph (8) protects a consumer from collection costs where the trader offered to collect, which is close to what happens when MADE sends its own inspector and its own van. Note that the text read on 20 November 2026 was the original “as made” version on legislation.gov.uk; the revised version was not opened, so a later amendment to these paragraphs has not been ruled out.

The register shows two companies, and one of them is still being wound up

Companies House makes the split plain. NEXT RETAIL LIMITED, company 04521150, was incorporated on 29 August 2002 as EVER 1889 LIMITED, became NEXT NEAR EAST LIMITED on 3 October 2002 and took its present name on 25 March 2003. It is active, registered at Desford Road, Enderby, Leicester LE19 4AT — the address printed in MADE's terms — and carries SIC codes 47710, 47721, 47789 and 47910. Its accounts to 31 January 2026 were filed on 9 October 2026 as 43 pages of audit-exemption subsidiary accounts, supported by a parent guarantee and by 271 pages of consolidated parent accounts filed on 22 July 2026, and its confirmation statement was made on 8 June 2026. Nothing there is late.

MADE.COM DESIGN LTD, company 07101408, is a different story and it is not a finished one. Incorporated on 10 December 2009 under SIC 47910, its registered office moved from 5 Singer Street, London EC2A 4BQ to Central Square, 29 Wellington Street, Leeds LS1 4DL on 17 December 2022. The insolvency record shows two cases: an administration that started on 8 November 2022 and ended on 14 June 2024, and a creditors' voluntary liquidation with the winding up commencing the same day. Zelf Hussain, Rachael Wilkinson and Peter David Dickens were appointed on 14 June 2024. The paper trail runs from an appointment of administrator filed on 16 November 2022, through a 58-page statement of administrator's proposal on 22 November 2022, an 18-page statement of affairs on 23 December 2022, an extension of the administration period on 9 November 2023 and the notice of the move to liquidation on 14 June 2024, to a 25-page liquidators' statement of receipts and payments to 13 June 2026, filed on 20 August 2026. The overview still shows accounts last made up to 31 December 2020 and the set due by 31 December 2022 as overdue, which is normal once a company is in the hands of liquidators rather than directors. MADE.COM GROUP PLC, the listed holding company, exists separately as company 13346124, incorporated on 20 April 2021 and also in liquidation, care of PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP at One Chamberlain Square, Birmingham B3 3AX.

None of this reflects on the shop trading today, and a reader should not read it as a warning about placing an order. It matters for one practical reason: four years after the collapse, the liquidators are still filing, so an unresolved claim against the old business has a different address, a different set of people and a different legal footing from a complaint about a delivery next week.

The two things the paperwork will not tell you

The first is how long anything is guaranteed for. The terms, the about page, the store locator and the returns article were all read on 20 November 2026 and not one of them gives a warranty period for a sofa, a bed, a wardrobe or a light. Compare that with what the rest of this category publishes as a matter of course and the silence is conspicuous — a sofa is the single most expensive thing most households buy from a website, and the number a buyer wants is the one number MADE does not print.

The second is who settles an argument. There is no complaints procedure, no response deadline, no named ombudsman and no alternative dispute resolution scheme anywhere that could be read. The nearest thing to a service standard is on the Contact Us page, where MADE says of email that “we aim to reply within 48 hours”, which is a target for an enquiry rather than a route for a dispute. The Furniture & Home Improvement Ombudsman, which is the sector's own scheme, lists six members under the letter N on 20 November 2026 — Nankivells, Natural Bed Company, Neville Johnson Ltd, Newcastle Beds Ltd, Nick Scali (Fabb) and Nolte Kitchens Retail Ltd — and no Next entity among them. The M listing on the same site returned no names at all on the day, so whether a MADE entity appears there could not be established and is not asserted either way here.

One smaller gap is a straight contradiction inside a single document. On Northern Ireland collections the terms say in one section that “Collections in Northern Ireland are £2.50.” and in another that “Returns collected by MADE from your home cost £3.50 per collection.” Two figures, one page, one subject: no Northern Ireland collection price is printed here, because MADE's own terms do not agree on what it is.

Send too much back and the account closes for good

Buried in the returns terms is a clause that very few furniture retailers publish at all. Under a heading on high returns, MADE reserves the right to “suspend or terminate your ability to place orders if we determine that the rate at which you return goods” is excessive, warns that “if your returns rate remains high, we may have to close your account”, and adds that “Should that happen, we will not be able to offer you an account in the future.” For credit customers the wind-down is spelled out: “your available credit will be restricted, and we will ask you to pay your balance down in accordance with your monthly statements before closing your account.”

Publishing the policy is more honest than hiding it, and a retailer facing a 30-day window on bulky goods has a real problem to manage. What is missing is the number. No return rate, no order count and no period is given, so a customer cannot tell whether three returns in a year is a problem or thirty, and the sanction is permanent rather than temporary. Read it next to the credit terms — the nextpay Credit Account carries a “Representative 24.9% APR variable” — and the two clauses point in opposite directions: one invites you to spread the cost of a sofa, the other can close the account you are spreading it on.

Where to sit on one before you buy

The original MADE was an online-only business with a showroom or two. The version Next runs is physically easier to shop: one MADE store at Next Home, Vulcan Road, Tinsley, Sheffield S9 2YL, described on the locator as “3327 Sq. Ft.of good stuff” and signposted as “Find us in Meadowhall”, plus 47 MADE concessions inside Next Home stores from Banbury and Basildon to Liverpool Aintree, Milton Keynes, Watford, Glasgow and York. That tally of 48 locations was counted from the locator page on 20 November 2026; the page itself prints no total. It is also a little rough around the edges — the Coventry entry is headed “Next Home - Banbury”, and the Bristol opening hours include “Satday” and “Wedsday”.

The range reads the same way wherever you look at it. On made.com the front page offered an Inara storage stool at £139, a Varri rug from £189, an Enya accent chair at £499 and an Elodie sideboard at £699. On Next's MADE pages the sofa listings ran from the £375 Haru single through £450 and £550 Haru two-seaters, a £475 Pax Boucle Haru, a £525 Maxton large two-seater sofa bed and a £599 faux fur Haru, up to a £749 Novara chaise lounge and a £1,099 Jude two-seater, with an Evander ottoman storage bed at £849 to £949, a pair of Aster dining chairs at £379 and Novara swivel accent chairs at £399 and £425. Lighting starts at £79 and bedding at £35. One oddity worth noting rather than trusting: Next's listing pages print “(4588)” beside the MADE Furniture heading and “(5605)” beside MADE Sofas, with no label saying what either number counts, so neither is quoted here as a range size.

Where it wins

  • Entry prices are low for the brand: sofa beds from £375, a pair of dining chairs at £379, ceiling lights at £79 and cotton duvet sets from £35.
  • Both furniture collection charges are published in advance, in figures, in two separate documents — which is exactly what regulation 35(5) requires before a trader can pass that cost on at all.
  • 28 days to change your mind on an ordinary order is a fortnight more than the law obliges MADE to give.
  • Small items can be returned free to most Next stores, which removes the £2.50 courier charge for anything that fits in a bag.
  • The seller is named in the first line of the terms, with a company number, a VAT number and a trading address, so there is no guessing who the contract is with.
  • You can see and sit on the furniture in 48 places, including one dedicated MADE store in Sheffield — something the original online-only business never offered at this scale.
  • MADE states which law its furniture returns policy is written to comply with, and says plainly that the clock starts the day after delivery rather than on the day itself.
  • Next Retail Limited's accounts to 31 January 2026 were filed on 9 October 2026 and its confirmation statement on 8 June 2026, with nothing overdue on the register.

Where it falls short

  • The real window on a sofa is 14 days, not the 28 advertised for ordinary orders: from day 15 MADE will not take it back until an inspector has been to your house.
  • £30 to deliver a two-person item and £60 to return it on day 20 is £90 of a £375 sofa bed, or 24% of the price, and none of it is refundable.
  • No guarantee or warranty length is published for any MADE furniture — not on the terms, the about page or the help article.
  • No complaints procedure, no response deadline, no ombudsman and no alternative dispute resolution scheme is named anywhere on the site.
  • No Next entity appears among the Furniture & Home Improvement Ombudsman's members under N, so the sector's own redress scheme does not sit behind the purchase.
  • The terms contradict themselves on Northern Ireland home collections, printing £2.50 in one section and £3.50 in another.
  • An assembled item cannot be returned unless it is faulty, so on flat-pack furniture the decision has to be final before assembly starts.
  • The high returns policy allows MADE to close an account permanently, and no return rate, order count or period is published at which that happens.
  • There is no order value at which delivery becomes free, and a sale order can take up to 28 days even for stock MADE says it is holding.
  • Anything bought from the old made.com before November 2022 belongs to a liquidation that was still filing receipts and payments in August 2026.

Common questions

Is made.com the same company I bought from before 2022?

No. The site is now “operated by, and the goods are supplied by Next Retail Limited trading as MADE”, company 04521150. MADE.COM DESIGN LTD (07101408), which ran the original business, entered administration on 8 November 2022 and has been in creditors' voluntary liquidation since 14 June 2024, with liquidators still filing in August 2026. A claim about an old order belongs to that liquidation, not to the current shop.

How much does it cost to send a MADE sofa back?

£30 per collection if you notify MADE within 14 days of the day after delivery. From day 15 to day 30 it is £60, and MADE will not accept the return until it has carried out “a full independent inspection of the goods in your home”, with a further check at its warehouse before the refund. Add the £30 two-person delivery charge and a returned sofa costs £60 inside the fortnight or £90 after it.

Does MADE give a guarantee on its furniture?

Nothing was found. The terms, the about page, the store locator and the returns help article were all read on 20 November 2026 and none of them states a warranty period for a sofa, bed or cabinet. That is not the same as there being no cover — your statutory rights apply regardless, and MADE will take back a faulty item — but no published guarantee length could be verified, so none is quoted here.

What does delivery cost?

£4.95 per order for one-person items, £30.00 per delivery for anything needing two people, and £3.50 to an Evri Parcel Shop. There is no free-delivery threshold at any order value. Most orders placed before 11pm go on the standard next-day service, though the terms say delivery times cannot be guaranteed and that sale orders for in-stock items can take up to 28 days.

Can I buy MADE furniture in a shop?

Yes. There is a MADE store at Next Home, Vulcan Road, Tinsley, Sheffield S9 2YL, and 47 MADE concessions inside other Next Home stores, counted from the locator on 20 November 2026. Small items bought online can also be returned free to most Next stores, with the airport stores excluded.

Who do I complain to if something goes wrong?

MADE itself, by email, phone or chat — the Contact Us page says of email that “we aim to reply within 48 hours”. No complaints procedure, escalation stage, response deadline or ombudsman is published, and no Next entity appears in the Furniture & Home Improvement Ombudsman's list of members under N. Disputes under the terms are left to the English courts.

Our verdict

MADE is now a Next brand in everything but the name over the door, and for a buyer that is mostly good news: the prices start lower than the old business charged, the seller is one identifiable company at one address, and you can go and sit on a £375 sofa bed in 48 shops instead of guessing from photographs. The catch is in the returns ladder rather than the price list. The 28-day promise on the front of the policy is not the one that governs furniture, where the cheap tier lasts 14 days at £30 a collection and the second tier costs £60 and an inspection in your living room. Add the £30 delivery and a wrong sofa is a £90 lesson. Against that, no published guarantee length and no named complaints or ombudsman route are real gaps for a purchase of this size, and they are the two things worth asking about before the order goes in rather than after the van has left.
