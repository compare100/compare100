MSC Cruises Review: What the Fare Covers and What the Contract Says

Rates and terms checked 21 November 2026 · Worldwide Cruises · Compare100 editorial team

MSC Cruises puts two charges inside the fare that most cruise lines add afterwards. Its own service charges page says “Service Charges / Gratuities are included in the cruise fare”, and its deal pages repeat “Port taxes and hotel service charges/gratuities included”. On a seven-night sailing that is roughly £100 a person a line like this would otherwise collect at the end, and on MSC it is £0.

The line runs 23 ships to more than 250 destinations in more than 100 countries, employs over 35,000 staff, and is the cruise arm of the Aponte family shipping business that its own history page traces back “more than 350 years”. MSC World Europa is “the line’s first LNG-powered ship and one of the world’s most energy-efficient cruise ships”, and MSC Euribia ran what MSC calls “the world’s first ever net zero Greenhouse Gas emissions voyage” on more than 400 tonnes of bio-LNG from Gasum, certified by ISCC.

One structural fact you need before anything else on this page makes sense. MSC does not sell one fare per cabin. It sells the same cabin at several prices under names — Bella, Fantastica, Aurea, and the MSC Yacht Club — and the difference between them is only partly the room. Clause 12.2 of MSC’s own booking conditions prices a change of date at “BELLA: £50 per person” and at “FANTASTICA, AUREA, YACHT CLUB: One Free of Charge”. The more you pay at the start, the more the contract lets you do later. That is not a criticism; it is the thing to understand before you choose.

What is already in the price, and what that is worth

Two charges decide whether a cruise fare means anything. One is the port tax, levied per passenger per port and often added at the end of a booking. The other is the daily service charge, which a cruise line bills to the onboard account for the whole voyage. MSC puts both inside the fare.

The service charges page at msccruises.co.uk is four sentences long and the first is the whole point: “Service Charges / Gratuities are included in the cruise fare”. The second is rarer still — “MSC Cruises does not recommend tipping individual members of staff”. The deal pages carry the same thing from the other direction: “Port taxes and hotel service charges/gratuities included”.

The absence of a figure there is the finding. A line that charges separately has to publish a rate, usually a per-person, per-night sum that differs by itinerary and by child age, and the sum is what a passenger argues about at the purser’s desk on the last morning. MSC has no rate to publish because it has no charge. For a couple on a seven-night cruise the saved amount runs to roughly £150 to £200 between them at the rates the separately-charging lines publish, and it is money that never appears on the bill rather than money refunded.

Fly-cruise packages go further. MSC’s own terms say “All Fly Cruises packages include direct flights to and from the embarkation port”, plus “Airport taxes and rearrangement of your travel in case of flight delay”. The rearrangement clause is the valuable half: a delayed flight on a self-assembled cruise holiday is the passenger’s problem, and on a package it is MSC’s.

What is not inside the fare is also stated. “Flights and any other add-ons including shore excursions will be available at the current selling rates”, and they are “payable by all guests under the same booking unless clearly stated in the offer”. Drinks are priced separately — MSC advertises “Premium drinks packages from just £196 pp for 7 nights”, which is £28 a night, with individual drinks capped at “€/£14 or $16” inside the package.

Bella, Fantastica, Aurea: the fare buys a contract, not a cabin

MSC sells the same physical cabin at several prices. The names are Experiences, and MSC’s own Experiences page sets out what each one carries. Read it beside clause 12 of the booking conditions and the pattern is clear: the premium is largely a premium on your rights.

Bella is sold “with guaranteed cabins”. You get “preference of cabin category (subject to availability)” and then, in MSC’s own words, “You cannot choose the cabin number.” The cabin is assigned within 4 days of departure. Changes are “Cruise changes for a fee”, and clause 12.2 names the fee: “BELLA: £50 per person”. Continental breakfast in the cabin is free, with a delivery cost on top.

Fantastica is offered on Balcony, Ocean View and Interior cabins and turns three of those around. You get the “Opportunity to choose the cabin and its location”, “1 Free cruise change”, and a “Chance to request preferred dinner sitting (subject to availability)”. Room service runs 24 hours with free delivery and the food priced individually, and in-cabin continental breakfast has no delivery charge. If MSC moves your cabin for operational reasons you receive “either a cabin category or a deck upgrade”.

Aurea covers Balcony and Suites, and Infinite Ocean View cabins on MSC World America, MSC World Asia, MSC Atlantic and MSC World Europa. It adds “Suites, Balcony and Ocean View cabins in the best locations”, “Priority boarding & luggage drop off”, and “Free time dining with My Choice dining in a dedicated restaurant or area” — which replaces the fixed sitting rather than asking for a preferred one. Around it sit free access to the Top Exclusive Solarium, a pillow menu, adults-only thermal area access on reservation, and 10% off spa treatments bought on board. An Aurea Suite adds priority tender and disembarkation, an in-suite espresso machine, ironing of 2 items per suite, a premium minibar with the first items free, and a welcome package of Prosecco and chocolate.

The MSC Yacht Club is a ship within the ship. Its page lists “Butler & Concierge 24/7”, an “Exclusive lounge, restaurant, sundeck & pool”, the “Premium Extra Drink package in all ship areas”, “Unlimited Browse Wi-Fi” valid for the whole cruise, and unlimited Thermal Suite access. Twelve suite grades are published with their areas, from the MSC YACHT CLUB INTERIOR SUITE at 15 sqm on decks 15 to 16 up to the MSC GRAND HAMPTONS VILLA on MSC World Atlantic at 230 sqm with 35 sqm of balcony on deck 15 and 30 sqm on deck 16, sleeping up to 8.

Set that beside clause 12.2 and the ladder reads as a legal one. Bella pays £50 a person to move a booking; Fantastica, Aurea and Yacht Club move one free. The conditions on that free change are identical for all of them: the request must come at least 30 calendar days before the original departure, and the new sailing must leave within 90 calendar days of it. Move outside that 90-day window and everyone pays £50 a person, Yacht Club included, and every later change after the first costs £50 a person again. Any difference in price and in insurance premium is the passenger’s.

So the question to ask before choosing an Experience is not which cabin you want. It is how certain you are of the date. A Bella booking you later move costs £50 a person more than the sticker; a Fantastica booking you never move has paid for a flexibility it did not use. Nobody at MSC frames it that way, and the arithmetic is entirely in MSC’s own two documents.

The company on the contract is in Geneva, and the one in Woking is a travel agent

The booking conditions define the counterparty as “MSC Cruises SA, whose registered address is Avenue Eugéne-Pittard 16 1206 Geneva” and state it plainly: “you will be entering into a Contract with MSC Cruises”. MSC’s UK contact page gives the same entity a Swiss registration — “MSC Cruises S.A. (CHE 11280 8357)”, at “Eugene Pittard 16, CH-1206 Geneva, Switzerland. Established in Switzerland, 2006.”

There is a UK company, and it is not the one on the contract. MSC CRUISES LIMITED, company 02974020, was incorporated on 6 October 1994 and has changed its name three times: THEMEPLAZA LIMITED until 17 November 1994, STARLAURO CRUISES LIMITED until 11 October 1995, MEDITERRANEAN SHIPPING CRUISES LIMITED until 21 February 2005. It is active, sits at the 15th Floor, Export House, Cawsey Way, Woking, Surrey GU21 6QX, and its registered activity is SIC 79110, travel agency activities. Accounts were last made up to 31 December 2025, the next are due by 30 September 2027, and nothing is overdue.

A second company handles the ships. MSC CRUISE MANAGEMENT (UK) LIMITED, 09361148, was incorporated on 18 December 2014 as MSC CTD (UK) LIMITED, renamed on 30 January 2015, and is registered to SIC 50100, sea and coastal passenger water transport, at 5 Roundwood Avenue, Stockley Park, Uxbridge UB11 1AF. Its accounts run to the same dates.

The register carries one more thing, dated and unexplained. On 20 February 2026 both of MSC Cruises Limited’s persons with significant control ceased: Mr Gianluigi Aponte and Mrs Rafaela Aponte-Diamant, each notified on 6 April 2016 with more than 25% but not more than 50% of shares and voting rights and the right to appoint or remove directors. In their place the company filed an active statement that it has no registrable person and no registrable relevant legal entity at all. The founders of the group are off the UK company’s PSC register and nothing has replaced them there.

None of this changes who answers the phone. It changes where a claim goes, and MSC has made that easier than the structure suggests: clause 26.1 reads “The present contract is governed by English law”, disputes go to the “exclusive jurisdiction of the English courts”, and only claims under EU 392/2009 are carved out. A British passenger contracting with a Swiss company for carriage on a ship still litigates in England.

Deposits, the cancellation ladder, and what the dates cost

The deposit is set by length and is non-refundable: “£75 per person for cruise only (up to 3-nights), £100 per person for cruise only (4-11 nights), £200 per person for cruise only (more than 12 nights)”, and £250 a person for any air-inclusive package — “including for infants up to 2 years”, which is worth noticing, because an infant who occupies no berth still costs £250 to book and £250 to abandon. A World Cruise takes 15% of the booking price within the first week.

The balance falls due 76 calendar days before departure on anything under 15 nights and 93 days on 15 nights or more. The cancellation ladder runs from those same dates. On a cruise under 15 nights: the deposit at 76 days or more, 25% from 75 to 56 days, 40% from 55 to 36, 60% from 35 to 15, 80% from 14 to 6, and 100% from 5 days to departure. On 15 nights or more: the deposit at 93 days or more, then 25% from 92 to 72, 40% from 71 to 59, 60% from 58 to 45, 80% from 44 to 15, and 100% from 14 days. Each percentage applies “or loss of deposit whichever is greater”. The 2026 to 2028 World Cruises take 15% at 90 days or more, 75% from 89 to 10 days, and 100% inside 9.

Two features of that ladder reward attention. The first is that the percentage bites before the money is owed: cancel 70 days out on a short cruise and 25% of the fare is payable even though the balance was only due at 76 days, so a passenger who paid on time has lost more than their deposit and one who cancelled a week earlier has lost exactly the deposit. The week between day 76 and day 75 is the most expensive week of the booking. The second is the “whichever is greater” footnote, which matters most on a cheap Bella cabin: on a fare where 25% comes to less than £100, the deposit is the floor.

Cancellation has to be in writing by email, with the tickets and confirmation invoice returned, and it must reach MSC “at least one working day before departure within 3pm GMT”. Anything later is a no-show, and a no-show is treated as a cancellation on the day of departure — 100%. Clause 13.6 waives the lot where unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances arise at the destination.

Price rises are capped at both ends. Clause 3.1 blocks any change “within the 20-day period before departure” or after full payment. Clause 3.2 allows movement for air fares, fuel, port and airport fees and exchange rates, in either direction, with fuel pegged at “0.33% of the price of the Cruise for every dollar of increase of the fuel per barrel (NYMEX Index)”. Clause 3.3 then absorbs the first slice: MSC “will absorb, and you will not be charged for, any increase equivalent to 2% of the price of your travel arrangements”, and above 8% the passenger may cancel for a full refund of amounts paid, with 7 calendar days to say so in writing. Insurance premiums are not refunded.

The limit on a claim is written in a currency nobody quotes

A cruise is carriage by sea, and carriage by sea is governed by the Athens Convention rather than by the ordinary law of holidays. MSC sets its limits out in clauses 16.9 and 16.12, in Special Drawing Rights — the IMF’s basket unit, not pounds.

For death, personal injury or illness the figure is 46.666 SDR under the Athens Convention 1974 and up to 400,000 SDR where EU Regulation 392/2009 or the Athens Convention 2002 applies, dropping to 250.000 SDR for claims arising from war or terrorism. For luggage or other property it is 833 SDR per passenger under the 1974 convention and 2,250 SDR under the later instruments, subject to per-passenger deductibles. For valuables deposited with the ship for safekeeping, clause 16.12 gives 1,200 SDR and 3,375SDR respectively.

Read those figures again and the punctuation is doing something strange. Inside a single clause MSC prints “46.666” and “250.000” with full stops and “400,000” with a comma, mixing the continental and the British convention in consecutive sentences about the same kind of money. The convention numbers are 46,666 and 250,000, so the meaning survives, but a reader working out whether the ceiling is forty-six thousand or forty-six point six has to know the Athens Convention already. Clause 16.12 then runs a figure into its unit — “3,375SDR” — and clause 16.17 prints an aggregate property limit of “£500,00 (in aggregate)”, which is not a number in either convention and is corrected nowhere in the document.

The sterling value of those SDR ceilings could not be established from a primary source for this review and is not given here. The IMF’s own current SDR valuation page, at imf.org, served a table headed “SDR rates as of: Friday, March 24, 2017” when it was fetched on 21 November 2026 — a rate nine years old, which cannot be used to convert a 2026 liability limit. The honest position is that the conventions are the authority and the figures are as printed.

The clause that will matter far more often is 16.2, and it is not in SDRs at all. Any claim that is not for death or personal injury is capped at “a maximum of three times the price which the affected Passenger paid for the Contract”, with insurance premiums and amendment charges excluded from that price. A missed port, a cancelled excursion, a cabin that floods, a week that is not the week sold — all of it is bounded by three times the fare. On a discounted Bella cabin, three times a low number is still a low number, which is the one place where buying the cheapest Experience quietly lowers a ceiling as well as a price.

Where to complain, by when, and to whom

MSC is an ABTA member and clause 20.4 gives the number: “We are a member of ABTA, membership number (Y1379).” Clause 21.2 adds that it “is bonded accordingly for all Holiday Packages sold in the UK”. The deadlines are short and they are stacked.

A written complaint about the cruise must reach customerservice@msccruises.co.uk “within 28 days of the termination of the Cruise”. Luggage is tighter: notice before or at disembarkation, or “within fifteen (15) calendar days” from disembarkation if the damage was not apparent. Anything brought under maritime passenger rights — accessibility, cancellation, delay — must be raised “within two (2) months from the date the service was performed”, with a reply inside one month and a final reply inside two. That clause cites its own instrument as “EU Regulation. 1177/2010”, stray full stop included.

If MSC’s answer does not settle it, clause 20.4 names the ABTA arbitration scheme, with the procedure at abta.com and limits of “£5,000 per person and £25,000 per booking form”. The EU ODR platform is also listed, and the document is candid that it “only registers the complaint and does not decide it”. No postal address for ABTA is given, and no telephone complaints line is published: the contact page’s after-cruise route is 0203 426 3010 option 6, Monday to Friday 9:30 am to 6:00 pm, closed at weekends and on bank holidays — which is 32.5 hours a week to reach a company whose ships sail every day of it.

Financial protection is where the paperwork thins out. Clause 21.1 says only that “When you buy an ATOL protected flight or flight inclusive package from us you will receive an ATOL Certificate”, and no ATOL number and no Civil Aviation Authority licence number appears anywhere in the current booking conditions. Clause 21 also never states the converse — that a cruise-only booking produces no certificate — which leaves the ABTA bond doing the work for the large share of MSC’s UK business that sails from Southampton with no flight attached. A reader who wants to know which scheme covers their money has to infer it.

What MSC keeps the right to change after you have paid

Clause 17.1 lets MSC and the master of the ship alter the itinerary, delay or advance a sailing, and omit or change ports, with the current wording adding that the discretion “must not be exercised unreasonably”. It also lets MSC “arrange for substantially equivalent carriage by another vessel” — a different ship, in other words, for the holiday you booked on a named one.

Clause 15.1 is the one worth knowing before you book an unpopular date. MSC may cancel with written notice and no compensation either for unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances or where the passengers enrolled are “smaller than the 50% of the passenger capacity of the relevant ship”. A sailing less than half sold can be cancelled outright, and the passenger then chooses between a substitute package, a rebooking or a full refund. The 2025 document framed the substitute as “a substitute package offered by the Company, of an equivalent or an higher quality”. Clause 14.5 separately allows a cabin to be reassigned to one with similar characteristics.

Minors are governed by clause 5.5 and the thresholds are specific. A minor is anyone under 21 on a voyage calling at a US port and under 18 otherwise. Minors may not occupy a cabin without an adult, with two exceptions: a cabin holding “1 minor aged no less than 12 years old”, or “2 minors where the oldest is at least 12 years old and the youngest is at least 8 years old”. More than two minors alone is not permitted. Pregnancy is capped at 23 weeks plus 6 calendar days, and clause 10.2 notes that “In compliance with Flag State requirements, there is a qualified doctor onboard” — without naming the flag state anywhere in the document.

Two versions of the same contract, both live, and a promotion with three end dates

MSC’s live terms page is headed “General MSC Cruises Booking Terms and Conditions 2026/2027” and “VALID FROM 1 JANUARY 2026”, and its “See the PDF version” link points at MSC-CRUISES-BOOKING-CONDITIONS-UK.pdf. A second file sits on the same site at a UK document path: msc-cruises-booking-conditions-2025-2026-uk.pdf, headed “GENERAL MSC CRUISES TERMS AND CONDITIONS” and “VALID FROM 1 JANUARY 2025”. Both open. Neither says it is superseded.

They do not agree. The older file charges 15% on a World Cruise cancelled 60 days or more before departure; the current one sets that band at 90 days or more. The older one lets a cabin hold minors “between 2 and 4”; the current one says “between 2 and 5”. The older one names the World Cruises of 2025 to 2027, the current one 2026 to 2028. The older one states English law and exclusive English jurisdiction with no carve-out; the current one adds the EU 392/2009 exception. Nothing here is a contradiction between two live claims, because the 2025 edition is identifiably the earlier document and is the one to disregard — but a buyer who reaches it first from a search engine reads a cancellation band 30 days out of date on the most expensive product MSC sells.

Both files share one feature that has nothing to do with dates. The line “MSC - Internal” appears at page break after page break through the current PDF — before clauses 19, 20, 21, 22.3, 24 and 25 among others — as well as throughout the 2025 one. It is a document-control marking from MSC’s own systems, and it is printed through the contract a UK customer is asked to accept. Nothing follows from it legally. It tells you the consumer contract was produced inside the same workflow as the internal paperwork and nobody took the stamp off.

The promotion running on 21 November 2026 has the same problem in miniature. Its terms are precise where it suits: “This offer is valid on new bookings only from 13/11/2026 to 08/12/2026”, applying to “selected Summer 26, Winter2627, Summer 2027 and Winter 2728 cruise only and fly cruise departures”, combinable with MSC Voyagers Club 5% and “not combinable with any other discounts, including Voyagers Exclusives 5+5% and Voyagers Selections”. Then the scope goes three ways. The Black Friday page itself says “Don’t miss your chance to save up to 30% on an extensive range of cruises sailing until 4 December 2027” and, further down the same page, “sailings right through to December 2028”. The Mediterranean deals page says “Save up to 30% on Cruises departing up until 31st May 2027”. Three end dates, one promotion, all on MSC’s own site, all live on the same afternoon.

And the discount is anchored to a sailing MSC will not price. The terms state that it “is based on MSC Virtuosa 18th May 2027 sailing for 7 nights on a cruise-only basis”, “based on an inside cabin on Bella Experience”, with rates “per person based on double occupancy (passenger 1 & 2)” and “3rd and 4th berth guests pay the usual applicable rate”. Ship, date, length, basis, cabin grade, Experience and occupancy — every variable a quote needs, published. The price itself is not, on that page or on any of the other five checked. A discount expressed as a percentage of a number the seller does not publish cannot be checked by the person being offered it, and that is the one criticism on this page that MSC could fix this afternoon by printing a figure.

Where it wins

  • Port taxes and service charges are inside the fare, so the headline price is close to the final one — on most lines a daily per-person service charge is added to the onboard account instead
  • MSC states plainly that it “does not recommend tipping individual members of staff”, which removes the awkwardness that the daily-charge model creates
  • Fantastica, Aurea and Yacht Club each carry one free change of cruise, which is a contractual right and not a goodwill gesture
  • The deposit is modest in absolute terms — £100 a person holds a 4 to 11 night cruise-only booking, with the balance not due for 76 days
  • English law and the exclusive jurisdiction of the English courts, despite the contract being with a Swiss company, so a UK passenger sues at home
  • Clause 3.3 absorbs the first 2% of any cost increase, and an increase above 8% gives a full refund of amounts paid
  • Clause 15.1 gives a choice of a substitute cruise, a rebooking or a full refund if MSC cancels, and clause 13.6 waives cancellation fees for unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances at the destination
  • LNG and bio-LNG are real, dated and ship-specific rather than a general environmental claim: MSC World Europa, and MSC Euribia’s certified bio-LNG voyage

  • MSC advertises no price for any cruise anywhere on its own UK website. Six of its own deal pages — the Black Friday page, special offers, cruises from the UK, 2027 cruises, summer from Southampton and Mediterranean cruises — were checked on 21 November 2026 and not one carried a per-person fare in its text. The sailing tables are built by script and arrive with headers only, down to an unfilled “{numberOfResults} Results found” placeholder

  • Three different end dates for one promotion, on MSC’s own pages. The Black Friday page says “sailing until 4 December 2027” and, lower down, “sailings right through to December 2028”; the Mediterranean deals page says “Save up to 30% on Cruises departing up until 31st May 2027”
  • Bella is the cheapest Experience and the one the 30% is calculated on, and on Bella “You cannot choose the cabin number” — MSC assigns it within 4 days of departure. A reader comparing MSC’s lead-in against another line’s is comparing a specified cabin with an unspecified one
  • Every page break in the live consumer booking-conditions PDF carries the line “MSC - Internal”. It is a document-control marking from MSC’s own systems, printed through the contract a UK buyer is asked to accept
  • A superseded contract is still served from MSC’s own UK document path. msc-cruises-booking-conditions-2025-2026-uk.pdf is headed “VALID FROM 1 JANUARY 2025”, while the live terms page is “General MSC Cruises Booking Terms and Conditions 2026/2027”, “VALID FROM 1 JANUARY 2026”, and links to a different file.   • Clause 16.2 caps everything that is not death or personal injury at three times the fare. A cancelled flight, a missed port, a ruined week — all of it is bounded by what the passenger paid
  • Clause 16.17 prints its aggregate property limit as “£500,00 (in aggregate)”, which is neither £500 nor £500,000, and nothing in the document corrects it

Common questions

Do I have to pay gratuities on an MSC cruise booked in the UK?

No. MSC’s own service charges page says “Service Charges / Gratuities are included in the cruise fare”, and it adds that MSC “does not recommend tipping individual members of staff”. Its deal pages confirm “Port taxes and hotel service charges/gratuities included”. On a seven-night cruise that is roughly £100 per person that a separately-charging line would bill to your onboard account instead.

What is the difference between Bella, Fantastica and Aurea?

Partly the cabin and largely the contract. On Bella “You cannot choose the cabin number” and MSC assigns it within 4 days of departure, and a change of date costs “BELLA: £50 per person”. Fantastica gives the “Opportunity to choose the cabin and its location”, one free cruise change, and a chance to request a dinner sitting. Aurea adds the best locations, priority boarding and luggage drop-off, and free-time My Choice dining. Fantastica, Aurea and Yacht Club all get “One Free of Charge” change, subject to asking at least 30 calendar days out and rebooking within 90 calendar days.

How much is the deposit and when is the balance due?

£75 per person on cruises up to 3 nights, £100 for 4 to 11 nights, £200 for 12 nights or more, and £250 per person for any air-inclusive package including infants up to 2 years. It is non-refundable. The balance falls due 76 calendar days before departure on cruises under 15 nights and 93 days on cruises of 15 nights or more. A World Cruise takes 15% of the booking price in the first week.

What does it cost to cancel?

On a cruise under 15 nights: the deposit at 76 days or more, 25% from 75 to 56 days, 40% from 55 to 36, 60% from 35 to 15, 80% from 14 to 6, and 100% inside 5 days. On 15 nights or more: the deposit at 93 days or more, 25% from 92 to 72, 40% from 71 to 59, 60% from 58 to 45, 80% from 44 to 15, and 100% inside 14 days. Each band applies “or loss of deposit whichever is greater”. Notice must be in writing and reach MSC at least one working day before departure by 3pm GMT, or it is treated as a no-show at 100%.

Is an MSC cruise ATOL protected?

MSC’s booking conditions say that “When you buy an ATOL protected flight or flight inclusive package from us you will receive an ATOL Certificate”, but they publish no ATOL number and no CAA licence number, and they never state which bookings fall outside the scheme. What they do give is ABTA membership number Y1379 and a bond covering “all Holiday Packages sold in the UK”. A cruise-only sailing from Southampton has no flight in it, so the ABTA bond rather than ATOL is the protection to ask about before you pay.

Who am I actually buying from?

MSC Cruises S.A. of Avenue Eugène-Pittard 16, 1206 Geneva, Switzerland, Swiss register CHE 11280 8357. The conditions state that “you will be entering into a Contract with MSC Cruises”, meaning the Swiss company. The UK company, MSC CRUISES LIMITED (02974020, Woking), is registered to SIC 79110 travel agency activities, and a second company, MSC CRUISE MANAGEMENT (UK) LIMITED (09361148, Uxbridge), to SIC 50100 sea and coastal passenger water transport. Clause 26.1 still makes the contract subject to English law and the exclusive jurisdiction of the English courts.

Can MSC cancel my cruise or change the ship?

Yes, in defined circumstances. Clause 17.1 allows itinerary changes, port omissions and “substantially equivalent carriage by another vessel”. Clause 15.1 allows outright cancellation with no compensation where enrolled passengers are “smaller than the 50% of the passenger capacity of the relevant ship”, or for unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances — and you then choose between a substitute cruise, a rebooking or a full refund.

What is the most MSC will pay if something goes wrong?

For anything other than death or personal injury, clause 16.2 caps it at “a maximum of three times the price which the affected Passenger paid for the Contract”, excluding insurance premiums and amendment charges. Death, injury and luggage run on the Athens Convention instead: 46.666 SDR under the 1974 convention and up to 400,000 SDR under EU Regulation 392/2009 or the 2002 convention for injury, 250.000 SDR for war or terrorism, 833 or 2,250 SDR for luggage, and 1,200 or 3,375 SDR for valuables deposited for safekeeping. Those are Special Drawing Rights, not pounds, and the IMF’s own current-rate page served a 2017 valuation when checked on 21 November 2026, so no sterling equivalent is given here.

Why can I not find a price on MSC’s website?

Because MSC does not publish one. Six of its own UK deal pages were checked on 21 November 2026 — Black Friday, special offers, cruises from the UK, 2027 cruises, summer from Southampton and Mediterranean cruises — and none carried a per-person fare in its text; the sailing tables arrive with column headers and an unfilled “{numberOfResults} Results found” placeholder, the listings being loaded afterwards by script. The only prices in the static pages are add-ons: “Premium drinks packages from just £196 pp for 7 nights”, drinks “from £28 per night”, and “up to £250pp onboard credit” on selected Yacht Club sailings.

Our verdict

MSC is the rare cruise line whose advertised price is close to its real one. Port taxes and the daily service charge are inside the fare, a seven-night couple keeps roughly £150 to £200 that another line would take at the purser’s desk, and MSC goes as far as saying it “does not recommend tipping individual members of staff”. The deposit is £100 a person on a week-long cruise, the balance is not due for 76 days, the first 2% of any cost increase is absorbed, and a British passenger contracting with a Swiss company still sues in the English courts. The catch is that the fare itself is nowhere to be found. MSC publishes the discount and not the price: its own terms name the MSC Virtuosa of 18 May 2027, 7 nights, inside cabin, Bella, as the basis for “up to 30%” off, and no page on the site says what that sailing costs — while the same promotion carries three different end dates across three of MSC’s own pages. Choose the Experience on how certain you are of your dates rather than on the room: Bella is £50 a person to move and gives you no say in which cabin you get until 4 days before you sail, while Fantastica upward buys one free change and the right to pick the room. Read the version of the booking conditions dated 2026/2027, not the 2025 PDF that is still live, and take clause 16.2 seriously before you decline travel insurance — outside death and injury, three times the fare is the ceiling on everything.
