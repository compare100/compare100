MyTyres Review: 133 Prices in One Tyre Size, and a Contract from 2017

Rates and terms checked 21 November 2026 · Tyres · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

A 205/55 R16 on MyTyres starts at £42.82 for a Nankang Econex NA-1 94V XL, and that price already has VAT and carriage inside it. Figures checked on 21 November 2026 against the shop's own size pages. The same size runs up to £96.95, and a Continental ContiEcoContact 5 91H sits at £71.58 against Continental's own recommended price of £140.00.

Four brand pages in that one size return 133 offers between them — 15 Nankang, 30 Goodyear, 35 Michelin and 53 Continental — and every listing carries the three tyre-label grades before you click anything. The shop's own FAQ says prices "include VAT and transport costs within mainland Britain", with a £5.00 surcharge if you order a single tyre and nothing extra on motorbike tyres. Returns run to 30 days rather than the statutory 14, and the terms say the shop collects the goods from your door.

MyTyres has been selling to British drivers for twenty-five years. Companies House records the UK company's incorporation on 19 January 2001; Delticom AG's own history page dates the launch of mytyres.co.uk to August 2001 and calls it the first online tyre shop the group opened outside German-speaking Europe. Delticom still lists it as the group's flagship shop, and the parent has been listed in the Prime Standard of the Deutsche Börse since October 2006.

From £42.82 to £96.95 in a single tyre size

A 205/55 R16 is the size used across this site to compare one tyre seller with another, because a price only means anything at a fixed size — a 205/55 R16 and a 225/45 R17 are different products. MyTyres serves no all-brand page for that size a text fetch can open, but the brand-filtered pages do, and between four of them it returns 133 offers: 15 Nankang, 30 Goodyear, 35 Michelin and 53 Continental.

The budget floor is genuinely low. Nankang's Econex NA-1 in 94V XL is £42.82 in stock on a two-to-five working day lead time, with the Cross Seasons AW-6 at £50.49, the Winter Activa 4 at £50.63 on limited stock and the Sportnex AS-2+ at £52.37. Four of the cheapest tyre come to £171.28 delivered to your door.

Where the shop looks strongest is the middle of the range, because it prints the maker's recommended price next to its own. A Continental ContiEcoContact 5 91H is £71.58 against a recommended £140.00 — a shade under 49% off — and a ContiPremiumContact SSR 91V run-flat is £84.31 against £184.00, or 54% off. The PremiumContact 7 91H is £76.44, the EcoContact 6 91V £78.15 and the UltraContact 91W £78.68. Goodyear's EfficientGrip Performance 2 91H is £72.99 and the Vector 4 Seasons Gen-3 94V XL £79.48, with a 5% code (2026GYRDGB) advertised on the Goodyear page. Michelin is the dearest of the four: CrossClimate 3 91H at £89.16, CrossClimate 3 91V at £89.38 and Primacy 4+ 91H with a rim-protection ridge at £89.46, which is £356.64 for a set.

The spread inside one size is the useful number: the dearest tyre verified, a Continental WinterContact TS 870 91T at £96.95, is 2.26 times the cheapest. That is a real choice rather than a price gradient, and it is wider than any fitting chain's shelf.

The company that takes the money has had five names

The shop names its seller in two different places and the two do not match. The current contact page gives "Delticom Ltd., Eden House, Two Rivers Business Park, Station Lane, Witney, Oxon, OX28 4BL" with "Directors: Kai Dopmann" and VAT registration number 768 4385 79. An older contact page on the group's `ssl.delti.com` host, footer-dated 17 August 2006, gives "Delticom Tyres Ltd., 9 Thames Street, Charlbury, Oxford OX7 3QL" with "Directors: Rainer Binder" and the same VAT number.

Companies House settles it, and the answer is that these are not two companies. DELTICOM LIMITED, number 04144833, is active, was incorporated on 19 January 2001 as DELTI-COM LIMITED, and its registered office is the Witney address. It has carried five names: DELTI-COM LIMITED to 1 June 2004, DELTICOM TYRES LIMITED to 20 January 2017, DELTITRADE LIMITED to 4 October 2017, DELTILOG LIMITED to 23 November 2023, and DELTICOM LIMITED since. Its SIC codes are 45320, retail trade of motor vehicle parts and accessories, and 47110, retail sale in non-specialised stores with food, beverages or tobacco predominating — the second of which has nothing to do with tyres.

So the current page is right and the old one is nine years out of date on the name and almost thirteen years out of date on the director: the register shows Kai Dopmann appointed on 16 July 2008 and still in post, and Rainer Binder resigned as both director and secretary on 1 January 2014. Eleven officers have passed through the company and ten have resigned. Accounts are made up to 31 December 2025 and are not overdue; the confirmation statement, due by 31 October 2026, is.

Above it sits Delticom AG of Sehnde, near Hanover, registered at HRB 58026 with VAT number DE 812759861, chaired on the supervisory board by Karl-Otto Lang and run by an executive board of Philip von Grolman, Nathalie Kronenberg and Andreas Prüfer. MyTyres is one of a dozen Delticom shop fronts aimed at British buyers — Tirendo, GigaTyres, MotoTyres, TyresOutlet, TyresGuru, Wheels24, 123Tyres and others, plus two trade sites — which is worth knowing if you are comparing two of them and think you are comparing two businesses.

A contract dated February 2017 against a law that changed in 2014

The terms page is titled "General Terms and Conditions as per 01.02.2017", and reading it against the current regulations is the single most useful thing a buyer can do before ordering.

Section 3 says: "You may revoke your contractual declaration within two weeks without providing reasons in written form (e.g. email)", the period commencing "with the receipt of the goods at the earliest". Two weeks is the right length. Regulation 30(3) of the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 ends the cancellation period "at the end of 14 days after the day on which the goods come into the physical possession of" the consumer. Section 1 of the terms uses 14 days for the same thing, so the document is internally inconsistent about its own headline figure.

The older copy of the terms on the group's own `ssl.delti.com` host is worse. It says the 30-day policy "extends over and beyond the legal requirement of 7 days for unwanted goods". Seven days was the figure in the Consumer Protection (Distance Selling) Regulations 2000, and regulation 2 of the 2013 Regulations — headed "Regulations superseded" — provides that those 2000 Regulations "do not apply in relation to contracts entered into on or after 13th June 2014". That sentence has been twelve years out of date since the day it stopped being true.

Section 4 is the one to read twice. It makes the buyer liable for return carriage "if the object delivered corresponds to the ordered item" and "if the price of the object to be returned does not exceed the amount of 40.- Euros". There is no 40-euro threshold anywhere in the 2013 Regulations. Regulation 35(5) says simply that "The consumer must bear the direct cost of returning goods" unless the trader agreed to bear them or failed to tell the consumer they would bear them, and regulation 35(7) adds that "The consumer is not required to bear any other cost of returning goods". There is no value limit of any kind, and a sterling contract that turns on a euro figure is not quoting UK law. Section 5 also charges "a flat rate for postage and packaging" to anyone cancelling after the statutory window closes and "all-inclusive handling costs of 100 GBP" on complete wheels, neither of them quantified beyond that.

Nothing in the terms names a governing law, a jurisdiction or any alternative dispute resolution body. The older contact page offers only that "Complaints will be acknowledged within 5 working days" at an address in Charlbury that is not the company's registered office. The homepage states "We offer a 2 year warranty" and no warranty clause appears in the terms at all, so what that two years covers could not be established and should be asked before ordering rather than assumed.

Three different delivery areas on one shop

Delivery included in the headline price is MyTyres' best feature and the site cannot decide where it applies. The production-year page says "Our tyre prices include VAT and packaging as well as shipping costs within mainland UK." The FAQ says "The prices include VAT and transport costs within mainland Britain." And every one of the four brand-filtered size pages read for this review — Nankang, Goodyear, Michelin and Continental — carries the sentence "Prices include VAT and delivery within Ireland", on a `.co.uk` domain selling in sterling.

Mainland UK, mainland Britain and Ireland are three different places, and the pages a buyer actually shops on are the ones naming the third. No figure is printed on this page for the delivery area as a result: the shop's own pages disagree, and the honest answer is to say which two say what and let a buyer check their own postcode in the basket.

What is consistent is the surcharge ladder, and it sits only in the FAQ rather than beside any price: £9.50 extra per tyre to the Scottish islands, £9.75 to Northern Ireland and £14.75 to the Isle of Man and Isle of Wight, with the Channel Islands not served at all. Orders of a single tyre carry £5.00 on top, so one Econex NA-1 is £47.82 rather than £42.82. Motorbike tyres go free. Since 6 April 2025, section 230 of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 has required a trader inviting a purchase to give the total price — "any fees, taxes, charges or other payments" the consumer "will necessarily incur" — and to state separately any "freight, delivery or postal charges... not included in the total price". A ladder of island surcharges living two clicks from the product is exactly the shape that provision addresses.

What B, C and 71 dB actually mean

Every MyTyres listing shows three grades, and that is a legal duty rather than a courtesy. The Government's own tyre-labelling guidance tells a distributor in Great Britain to "give the information on the fuel efficiency, wet grip (where applicable) and external noise during the buying process" where "the tyres offered for sale are not visible to the customer", which is every online order. MyTyres does it on every row.

The thresholds come from assimilated Regulation (EU) 2020/740, and its Annex I grades a C1 tyre — the class covering passenger cars, vans and small trailers — "according to the 'A' to 'E' scale". For rolling resistance in N/kN the bands are A at 6.5 or below, B 6.6 to 7.7, C 7.8 to 9.0, D 9.1 to 10.5 and E 10.6 or above. For wet grip, by index G, they are A at 1.55 and above, B 1.40 to 1.54, C 1.25 to 1.39, D 1.10 to 1.24 and E 1.09 or below. Five classes, not seven. Worth knowing when reading the guidance: the gov.uk page, last updated on 5 October 2023, says Great Britain runs an A-to-G scale and Northern Ireland an A-to-E one, and points the reader at the superseded Regulation No 1222/2009 for detail. The annex itself carries no A-to-G scale for a car tyre, and the letters MyTyres prints all fall inside A to E.

Read the grades against the prices and the ladder stops being about money alone. A Continental ContiEcoContact 5 at £71.58 grades B for rolling resistance, B for wet grip and 71 dB. A Michelin CrossClimate 3 at £89.16 grades C, B and 72 dB, and a Michelin Primacy 4+ at £89.46 grades C, A and 69 dB — the best wet grip and the quietest of the three, and the worst of the three on fuel. The European Commission puts one class step at three to six metres of extra wet stopping distance, so the gap between the Primacy's A and the Continental's B is a car length that £18 buys.

Getting them on the car is somebody else's bill

A tyre bought this way arrives at the house. Fitting is a separate transaction with a separate business, and MyTyres runs its own network for it: the contact page claims "More than 2,100 partner garages" and "2,100+ Local Fitting Partners", against the parent's figure of around 24,000 across Europe.

How it works is set out on the page where the shop recruits them. A garage can "register your company as a partner garage free of charge", customers "receive a link where they are asked to set a fitting appointment", and the garage's price is described as "Price including VAT (customer pays directly at the partner garage)". The production-year page promises that "we'll take care of finding a fitter in your area to fit the tyres for you" at "quality craftsmanship at a reduced price".

What no page on the site gives is a number. There is no fitting price list, no per-tyre figure, and no indication of the charge before the appointment is set — so the fitted cost of four tyres cannot be worked out from MyTyres' own pages at all, and no figure for it is printed here rather than guessed at. On a set of Econex NA-1s at £171.28 delivered, a typical high-street fitting, balancing, valve and disposal charge is a large fraction of the tyre bill; on a set of Michelins at £356.64 it is a much smaller one. That asymmetry is the whole reason the cheapest tyre on a results page is a smaller saving than it looks, and it is worth ringing the nominated garage for its price before committing.

The production-year filter nobody explains

Buried in the service menu is the most interesting thing on the shop, and it has no explanatory text whatsoever. A page at `/dot-2026` is titled "Search for tyres — Production year 2026 guaranteed" and offers a filter row reading "Production year 2026 guaranteed: Yes". That is it: a filter and a label, with not one sentence anywhere on the page saying what is guaranteed, by whom, or what happens if a 2025 tyre turns up.

It matters because of what the group's own FAQ admits. "We only sell new tyres. We do not sell old stock", it says, and then: "The tyre industry sells tyres up to 2 years old as new." It also tells the buyer to "Check the DOT number to find out how old a tyre is" — useful advice, except that the DOT week-and-year code is moulded into the sidewall and nothing in the ordering process shows it. None of the 133 listings read for this review displayed a production year or DOT date on the product row.

There is no legal age limit on a car tyre in any case. The Road Vehicles (Construction and Use) (Amendment) Regulations 2020, in force on 1 February 2021, ban a tyre more than ten years past its week of manufacture on the front axle of a bus, any single-wheel axle of a minibus and the front axle of a goods vehicle over 3,500kg. Cars are not in it. So a shop that lets you insist on this year's rubber is offering something the law does not and a tyre bay cannot — and then declines to explain it, which is the reverse of the usual complaint about online retail.

Nine months of 2026, read from the parent's own figures

Delticom publishes its numbers because it has to, and they say a shop under pressure rather than a shop in trouble. The nine-month statement of 12 November 2026 gives revenues of €318 million against €343 million for the same period of 2025, gross merchandise volume of €388 million against €422 million, and third-quarter revenue of €104 million against €107 million. Turnover is down about 7% and volume about 8%.

Earnings moved the other way. Operating EBITDA rose to €9.2 million from €6.6 million, EBITDA to €8.9 million from €6.3 million, and EBIT to €1.6 million from minus €2.0 million, leaving consolidated net income at minus €0.5 million against minus €3.2 million. The equity ratio improved to 20.0% from 17.4% and liquidity stood at €5.3 million. Full-year guidance is €480 million to €520 million of revenue and €19 million to €24 million of operating EBITDA, against about €484 million of revenue in 2025. The 2026 annual general meeting approved a dividend of €0.12 a share, and market capitalisation was €29.8 million at 31 December 2025.

The headcount is the figure that explains the website. Ninety people were employed at the end of the last quarter, down from 94 at the end of the first, and they run 364 shops in 95 countries carrying more than 600 brands and over 90,000 tyre models for nearly 21 million customers. That is roughly €3.5 million of nine-month revenue per employee. A company at that ratio keeps its prices, stock and label data current because software does that; it does not get round to rewriting a contract from 2017 or a contact page from 2006, because a person has to.

Common questions

Who actually sells me the tyres on mytyres.co.uk?

DELTICOM LIMITED, company number 04144833, registered at Eden House, Two Rivers Business Park, Witney, Oxfordshire OX28 4BL, with VAT registration number 768 4385 79. It is active, was incorporated on 19 January 2001 and is a subsidiary of Delticom AG of Sehnde, near Hanover. An older contact page on the group's own server still names "Delticom Tyres Ltd." at 9 Thames Street, Charlbury — that is the same company under a name it stopped using on 20 January 2017, not a second business.

Is delivery really free?

For most of Britain, yes: the FAQ says prices "include VAT and transport costs within mainland Britain". Three exceptions are worth knowing before you order. A single-tyre order carries £5.00 on top. Delivery to the Scottish islands adds £9.50 a tyre, Northern Ireland £9.75 and the Isle of Man or Isle of Wight £14.75. The Channel Islands are not served. Note also that the brand-filtered size pages themselves say "Prices include VAT and delivery within Ireland", which does not match the FAQ, so check the basket total rather than the product row.

How long do I have to send tyres back?

The shop offers 30 days from purchase under § 5 of its terms, which is longer than the 14 days regulation 30 of the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 gives you from the day the goods reach you. Who pays the return carriage is less clear: § 3 says the shop collects the goods, § 4 makes you liable where the item's price "does not exceed the amount of 40.- Euros", and the FAQ says "The return transport costs will be debited to your account". UK law has no such threshold — regulation 35(5) puts the direct cost of return on the consumer unless the trader agreed to bear it or failed to say so — so get the position in writing before you send anything back.

Can I make sure I am not sent old stock?

Partly. The service menu carries a page titled "Production year 2026 guaranteed" with a filter that restricts results to tyres made this year, although nothing on the page explains the guarantee. The group's own FAQ is candid about why it exists: "The tyre industry sells tyres up to 2 years old as new." No product row displays a DOT date, so the filter is the only lever you have before delivery; the sidewall code is the check afterwards. There is no legal age limit on a car tyre — the ten-year rule introduced on 1 February 2021 applies to buses, minibuses and goods vehicles over 3,500kg.

What do the three letters on each listing mean?

They are the tyre label: rolling resistance, wet grip and external noise in decibels. Assimilated Regulation (EU) 2020/740 grades a car tyre on an A-to-E scale for the first two — rolling resistance A at 6.5 N/kN or below through to E at 10.6 or above, wet grip A at an index of 1.55 or above through to E at 1.09 or below. A Continental ContiEcoContact 5 at £71.58 shows B, B and 71 dB; a Michelin Primacy 4+ at £89.46 shows C, A and 69 dB. One class step on wet grip is three to six metres of extra stopping distance on the European Commission's own figure, which is the sort of gap worth paying £18 to close.

Does MyTyres fit the tyres?

No, but it will arrange it. The shop claims more than 2,100 UK partner garages and says it will find a fitter in your area; the garage then sends a link to book an appointment and is paid directly, at a "Price including VAT (customer pays directly at the partner garage)". No fitting price appears anywhere on the site, so the fitted total for a set cannot be calculated before you book. On a £171.28 set of budget tyres that charge is a significant share of the bill and on a £356.64 set of Michelins a much smaller one, which is worth a phone call to the garage first.

Our verdict

Buy the tyre, read the contract with one eye open. On price, range and transparency about what the price covers, MyTyres does the job better than most: 133 offers in a 205/55 R16 across four brands, £42.82 at the floor, the maker's recommended price printed beside its own, all three label grades on every row, 30 days to change your mind and a filter that will restrict results to tyres made this year. The paperwork is the weak half. Terms dated 1 February 2017 disagree with themselves about the cancellation window and hang return carriage on a 40-euro threshold that is not in UK law; an older copy still quotes a seven-day right that stopped existing on 13 June 2014; four product pages tell a British buyer that delivery is included "within Ireland"; and the fitting charge, which is the rest of the real cost, is published nowhere. None of that touches the tyre you receive, and all of it is worth two minutes before you order — particularly the basket total if you live on an island, and a call to the nominated garage for its fitting price.
