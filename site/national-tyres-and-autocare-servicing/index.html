National Tyres and Autocare Servicing: Prices, the MOT Sum and the Halfords Stamp

Rates and terms checked 8 September 2026 · Servicing · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

National Tyres and Autocare has been fitting tyres in Britain since August 1970, when Marsham, Briggs and Gorrills were folded together into what the company called the country's first national network of tyre fitting centres. Servicing came later and now sits alongside the tyres, exhausts, brakes, batteries and MOT work at more than 200 National-branded garages. The prices are published before you book: an interim service starts at £219.99, a full service at £279.99 and a major service at £329.99, each stepping up with engine size, and every part fitted carries a guarantee of two years or 24,000 miles — twice the length the fast-fit trade usually offers.

National has been part of Halfords since December 2021, when the group paid £62m for it. That matters at the counter rather than in the boardroom. Halfords now runs around 600 consumer garages, of which National is roughly a third, and the branch finder on National's own site marks a long list of former National sites as "(Now a Halfords centre)" or "(Halfords Autocentres)". Book a service under the National name and the stamp that goes in your book reads "Halfords Autocentre Menu Service". Nothing about that is hidden, but it decides which brand your paperwork carries and which website you should be booking on, so it is worth knowing before you ring.

The most useful thing on the price list is the MOT. A test at National is £49.99 against a statutory maximum of £54.85, and buying it with a service brings it down again — to £29.85 alongside a major service, which is £25 below the most any garage in the country is allowed to charge.

What a service costs before anybody looks at the car

National publishes a full grid rather than a single headline price, and the only variable is engine size. An interim service is £219.99 up to 1499cc, £249.99 from 1500cc to 2499cc and £289.99 above that. A full service runs £279.99, £309.99 and £349.99 across the same three bands. A major service is £329.99, £359.99 and £399.99. The steps between the bands are £30 and £40 every time, which makes the grid easy to read and easy to check against a quotation.

The gaps between the service levels are worth doing in your head before you book. Moving from an interim to a full service costs £60 on a small engine; moving from a full to a major costs £50. The major is sold as a two-yearly job rather than an annual one, so on a car that does average mileage the real comparison is one major every other year against two fulls — £329.99 against £559.98 on the smallest band, with the major throwing in the brake fluid and cabin filter that the full service leaves alone.

Orders over £200 can go through Payment Assist and be spread across four months. Every service on the grid clears that threshold, the £219.99 interim included, so the option is there on any of them.

The MOT is priced off the government's cap, not off National's own MOT price

This is the part of the price list nobody seems to have taken apart, and it is the best value on the page. The Department for Transport sets a maximum fee of £54.85 for a class 4 MOT and a garage may charge anything below it. National charges £49.99 for a test on its own. Bundle it with a service and the grid shows the following: an interim service and MOT is £259.84 against £219.99 for the service alone, a full service and MOT is £314.84 against £279.99, and a major service and MOT is £359.84 against £329.99.

Subtract, and the MOT inside those bundles costs £39.85, £34.85 and £29.85. Those three numbers are exactly £15, £20 and £25 below the £54.85 statutory maximum, and they hold across all three engine bands — the arithmetic is identical on the £289.99 interim and the £399.99 major. The discount is built off the legal ceiling, not off National's own shelf price, and it rises with the size of the service you buy.

The marketing describes the same offer as "30% off your MOT (worth £49.99)" with a service, rising to 40% with a full service and 50% with a major. Those percentages applied to £49.99 would give £34.99, £29.99 and £24.99, and every actual bundle is £4.86 dearer than that — £4.86 being precisely the gap between National's £49.99 MOT and the £54.85 cap. Read against the cap the offer is honest and generous; read against the price on National's own MOT page it falls a fiver short of what it says. Either way, £29.85 for a test is a good deal, and it is the number to quote at the counter.

Fifty checks, sixty checks, seventy checks, and what the extra ten buy

The three service levels are sold on inspection counts: 50 or more points on the interim, 60 or more on the full, 70 or more on the major. The interim covers the oil and filter change, brake operation and pad condition, tyres, wipers, battery security, clutch, steering and suspension components and the exhaust. The full service adds an air filter that National values at up to £70, plus mirrors, seatbelts, door locks and an assessment of any chip or crack in the windscreen.

The major adds two items that genuinely cannot wait indefinitely: a cabin filter valued at up to £60 and a brake fluid change with the system bled, valued at up to £49.99, along with an alternator charging-rate test. Brake fluid absorbs water over time and most manufacturers ask for it every two years regardless of mileage, so the two-yearly major is priced around the interval that actually matters rather than an arbitrary one.

Add the quoted part values together and the major service includes up to £179.99 of consumables against a £329.99 price on the smallest engine. That is the company's own valuation rather than a shop price you could verify, so treat it as an indication of what is in the box, not as a saving.

Two years and 24,000 miles, which is longer than the trade norm

The servicing pages carry one line that does more for a buyer than any of the pricing: "All parts fitted at Halfords Autocentres and National Autocentres are guaranteed for a minimum of 2 years or 24,000 miles (whichever is sooner)." Fast-fit chains more commonly guarantee parts for twelve months or 12,000 miles, so National is offering double on both halves of the test, and the guarantee is honoured across the whole group estate rather than only at the branch that did the work.

What the sentence covers is parts. The labour position is set out separately under the fitting warranty on the rights page, alongside manufacturer guarantees on the goods themselves and an accidental damage guarantee that is sold as an extra rather than included. If the point of the visit is a repair rather than a service — a clutch, a set of discs, an exhaust — ask which of the three is doing the work before the car goes up, because they have different lengths and different claim routes.

Servicing away from the dealer, and the stamp that says Halfords

Having a car serviced outside the franchised network does not void a manufacturer warranty provided the schedule is followed and the parts meet the maker's specification, which is why a chain like National can sell against a main dealer at all. Halfords puts the saving at "on average, 20% less than a main dealer would charge", a group-wide claim without a worked example behind it, so it is a reason to get a dealer quotation for comparison rather than a figure to rely on.

Two details of the paperwork are worth knowing in advance. The stamp National puts in a paper service book records a "Halfords Autocentre Menu Service" — accurate, since that is the schedule being followed, but not the National name a buyer might expect to see later. And electronic service records cannot be stamped at all: the pages state plainly that "Electronic service books cannot be stamped with this service." Most manufacturers moved to digital records years ago, and a car whose history lives in the maker's system will come away from a National service with an invoice and nothing in the online record. Keep the invoice; on resale it is the only evidence you have.

£54 a year that includes an MOT, if you book on the right website

Halfords Motoring Club has around six million members, of whom about 420,000 pay for the Premium tier and generate roughly £22m of subscription revenue a year. Premium costs £54 and includes one MOT worth up to £49.99, a £10 welcome voucher, two ten-point car checks valued at £30, one free fitting, unlimited next-day delivery and 5% off member pricing. On the MOT alone the membership is close to paying for itself, and the 5% then runs against servicing.

The catch is procedural and easy to fall over. Halfords' own help page confirms that "You can use your benefits at any National Tyres garage", but to apply them to an online booking you have to book through Halfords.com and pick the National branch there, rather than booking on national.co.uk. A member who books the obvious way, on the National site, arrives without the discount attached. If you hold Premium, start on the Halfords side and choose the National garage from that end.

Where a complaint goes, and how far it can get

National's terms and conditions name an alternative dispute resolution body, which is more than many garages do: complaints can be taken to The National Conciliation Service at no charge. The NCS is accredited by the Chartered Trading Standards Institute under delegated powers from the Secretary of State for Business and Trade, under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, and it handles vehicle servicing, maintenance and repair disputes as well as vehicle sales. Conciliation is free to the consumer, you have twelve months from the business's final response to bring a case, the average process runs 72 days and an outcome is issued in writing within 90. Claims below £50 can be refused.

Two limits are worth understanding before you rely on it. The scheme's own rules state that "Any outcome of the ADR procedure is not legally binding" on either side, and if conciliation fails, arbitration carries a consumer fee running from £48 plus VAT on claims between £50 and £1,000 up to £345 plus VAT above £15,000, refundable if the claim succeeds. That is a materially weaker position than the Motor Ombudsman offers at Halfords-branded garages under the same ownership, where the final decision binds the accredited business once the consumer accepts it and the service is free throughout.

The gap between National's own two pages does not help. The rights page sets out a complaints procedure — email or write, or ring 0808 100 0015, examined within two working days and an aim to resolve within five — and then sends anyone still unhappy to "your local Trading Standards office or your local Citizens' Advice Bureau", without mentioning the conciliation scheme the terms and conditions point to. A customer who reads the rights page and not the terms will never learn the ADR route exists.

The estate is shrinking, so check the branch is still there

Halfords' preliminary results for the 53 weeks to 3 April 2026 report group revenue of £1,801.7m, with Autocentres at £739.7m and an underlying operating profit of £21.0m from that division. The estate finished the year at 588 locations — 496 consumer garages and 92 commercial fleet sites — down from 632, after 43 underperforming garages were closed during the year. The group also runs about 250 mobile vans trading as Halfords Mobile Expert and National.

Alongside the closures, 103 sites now trade under the Fusion format, which combines a retail store and a garage on one site, with roughly 35 more planned in the current financial year. Both programmes work through the same estate National sits in, and the company has flagged "ongoing weakness in the tyres market" with only some stabilisation late in the year. None of that is a reason to avoid a service, but it is a reason to ring the branch rather than trust a map pin, particularly if the one you use is the sort of small standalone site the closure programme has been aimed at.

Two price lists on one website

National's servicing pricing page and its MOT and service packages page do not agree, and the gap is wide enough to matter. The pricing grid puts an interim service and MOT at £259.84, a full service and MOT at £314.84 and a major service and MOT at £359.84 on the smallest engine band. The packages page advertises the same three bundles "from £189.99", "from £244.99" and "from £289.99" — £70, £70 and £70 apart respectively. A search listing for the pricing page itself shows a full service "price from £209.99", a third figure again.

A "from" price usually signals a promotional rate, a smaller vehicle class or a branch-specific offer, and none of those explanations is set out on either page. The practical answer is to put your registration and postcode into the quote tool, which is what both pages ask you to do, and to get the figure in writing before the car is booked in. Do not assume the higher grid is the one you will be charged, and do not assume the lower one is available at your branch either — this needs confirming with the garage, not with the website.

The company behind the name

The trading entity is National Tyre Service Limited, company number 00986754, incorporated on 12 August 1970 and VAT registered as 785 4130 19. Its registered office at Companies House is Icknield Street Drive, Washford West, Redditch — Halfords' own home town — while the consumer terms and conditions still give Regent House, Heaton Lane, Stockport, the address the business used under its previous owners. The last full accounts cover the period to 28 March 2025 and were filed on 19 November 2025. Henry Birch was appointed a director on 16 April 2025, the day after Graham Stapleton's termination, mirroring the change of chief executive at the parent.

The ownership history is unusually busy for a garage chain: BTR plc bought it in 1985, the German tyre maker Continental AG in May 1991, and a management buyout took it to Axle Group Holdings for £24m in December 2001. By 2018 it was running 235 locations with 1,192 employees. Halfords paid £62m in December 2021, creating a combined network of 604 garages and 235 mobile vans at the time. Roughly 7,500 people now work across the group's garages and its 400-plus retail stores.

Where it wins

  • Parts guaranteed for a minimum of two years or 24,000 miles, double the usual fast-fit term, and honoured across around 600 group garages
  • The bundled MOT costs £39.85, £34.85 or £29.85 — £15 to £25 below the £54.85 statutory maximum
  • A major service includes brake fluid and a cabin filter on a two-yearly cycle that matches most manufacturers' own interval
  • An ADR body is named in the terms and conditions, and it is CTSI-accredited
  • Payment Assist spreads anything over £200 across four months
  • Halfords Motoring Club Premium at £54 includes an MOT worth up to £49.99 and is usable at National garages

  • Two of National's own pages price the same bundles £70 apart — £259.84 against "from £189.99" for an interim service and MOT — with no explanation of the difference on either page

Common questions

How much is a service at National Tyres and Autocare?

As checked on 8 September 2026, an interim service is £219.99 up to 1499cc, £249.99 from 1500cc to 2499cc and £289.99 above 2499cc. A full service is £279.99, £309.99 and £349.99 across the same bands, and a major service is £329.99, £359.99 and £399.99. A separate packages page advertises lower "from" prices, so confirm the figure for your registration before booking.

Is the MOT cheaper if I book it with a service?

Yes, and by more than the headline suggests. On its own an MOT is £49.99. Added to an interim service it costs £39.85, to a full service £34.85 and to a major service £29.85 — £15, £20 and £25 below the £54.85 statutory maximum for a class 4 car. The discounts are the same in cash terms whatever your engine size.

How long are parts guaranteed for?

A minimum of two years or 24,000 miles, whichever comes first, on parts fitted at National and Halfords Autocentres. That is twice the twelve months or 12,000 miles common elsewhere in the fast-fit trade. Fitting is covered separately under the fitting warranty, so ask which applies if the visit is a repair rather than a scheduled service.

Will a National service affect my manufacturer warranty?

Not if the manufacturer's schedule is followed and the parts meet its specification. The practical risk is documentary rather than mechanical: National cannot stamp an electronic service record, so on a car with a digital history you should keep the invoice as your proof that the work was done on time.

Who do I complain to if the work is wrong?

Start with National — email customersupport@national.co.uk or ring 0808 100 0015; the company says complaints are examined within two working days and it aims to resolve them within five. If that fails, the terms and conditions allow a free referral to The National Conciliation Service, which is CTSI-accredited and covers servicing and repair disputes. You have twelve months from the final response, the average case takes 72 days, and the outcome is not legally binding on either party.

Is National Tyres the same company as Halfords now?

It is a Halfords subsidiary. National Tyre Service Limited (company 00986754) has traded since August 1970 and was bought by Halfords for £62m in December 2021. Around 200 garages still carry the National name within a group estate of roughly 600, the branch finder marks a number of former National sites as Halfords centres, and the service book stamp records a Halfords Autocentre menu service.

Our verdict

National is a straightforward thing to buy a service from: the grid is published, the only variable is engine size, and the parts guarantee of two years or 24,000 miles is longer than most of the chains it competes with. The bundled MOT is the standout — £29.85 alongside a major service is £25 under the legal maximum, and it is worth asking for by that number. The reservations are about which page you happen to read. Two parts of the site price the same bundle £70 apart, the MOT discount is advertised as a percentage of a figure it is not actually calculated from, and the dispute route named in the terms is missing from the page about your rights — and is weaker than the ombudsman covering the Halfords-branded garages next door in the same group. Get the quotation in writing with your registration against it, ask whether the branch is National or now a Halfords centre, and if you hold a Motoring Club membership start the booking on the Halfords site.
