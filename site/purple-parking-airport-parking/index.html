Purple Parking Review: Prices, Car Parks and the Small Print

Rates and terms checked 7 September 2026 · Airport Parking · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Purple Parking is one of the older names in British airport parking, and it still does the straightforward thing well: you put in your dates, it shows you what the car parks near your terminal charge, and you pay a fraction of what the barrier would take on the day. The site covers 31 UK airports and ports, from Heathrow and Gatwick down to Humberside, Exeter and Teesside, with Dover and Southampton docks alongside them. It claims “35+ years experience” and more than nine million cars parked, and at Gatwick it will currently sell you eight days of park and ride from £4.21 a day, or £33.75 for the week.

One thing is worth knowing before you start comparing, because it decides who you are actually dealing with. Since 2 November 2017 Purple Parking has been a brand owned by Holiday Extras, the Kent travel-extras company founded in 1983 by Gerry Pack, and the site's own privacy policy puts it plainly: “Purple Parking is a part of Holiday Extras Limited.” So this is a booking platform rather than a car park operator. At Gatwick it lists 29 car parks and only one of them carries the Purple Parking name; the cheaper four on the list that day were Cophall, Airparks, APH and Holiday Extras' own park and ride. That is a good thing for a shopper — you are seeing a real market, not one company's price list — but it means the company that takes your money is not the company that holds your keys.

The security side is better documented than most of this trade manages. The Purple Parking site at Heathrow Terminals 2 and 3, on Bath Road at TW6 2AL, holds the Park Mark award and runs entry and exit barriers, CCTV and perimeter fencing, with a free shuttle taking 10 to 12 minutes to the terminal. At Terminal 4 the bus runs every 10 to 15 minutes from 03:30 until midnight. Across Heathrow the site lists more than 20 options with transfer times of 3 to 20 minutes, and meet and greet from £10.63 a day.

The company you contract with, and the one part of it a regulator watches

Holiday Extras Limited was incorporated on 21 January 1983 under the name Apple Booking Company Limited, became ABC Holiday Extras Limited in 2001 and took its present name in 2003. It is active, registered at Ashford Road, Newingreen, Hythe, Kent CT21 4JF, filed accounts to 31 March 2025 with the next set due by 31 December 2026, and lodged its most recent confirmation statement on 26 January 2026. The group says more than eight million travellers a year book through it directly or through partners, and that 99.8% of customers say they would book again.

Airport parking itself has no financial regulator. Nobody authorises a car park, nobody sets a capital requirement for a booking platform, and there is no ombudsman with jurisdiction over a lost set of keys. The one strand of the group that is regulated is the travel insurance sold alongside the parking: Holiday Extras Cover Limited is an insurance intermediary authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority under firm reference number 828848, and the policies are underwritten by Great Lakes Insurance UK Limited, FRN 955859. That authorisation covers the insurance and nothing else. If the dispute is about your car, the FCA and the Financial Ombudsman Service are not in the picture at all.

What does apply is ordinary consumer law. The Consumer Rights Act 2015 requires the service to be carried out with reasonable care and skill, and the common law of bailment applies the moment a member of staff takes your keys — the car park is holding your property and has to take reasonable care of it. Those are real protections, but they are enforced in the county court rather than by a free complaints scheme, which is a different proposition from an insurance or banking complaint.

A week at Gatwick costs less than four visits to the drop-off zone

The prices are the reason the site exists, and they are worth setting against what the airports themselves charge. On an eight-day Gatwick search the five cheapest park and ride options came out at Cophall £4.21 a day (£33.75), Airparks £4.24 (£33.92), APH £4.36 (£34.89), Holiday Extras £5.12 (£40.98) and Purple Parking's own site at £5.37 (£42.99), with transfers of 10 to 15 minutes. Meet and greet at Gatwick ran from £5.87 a day, or £47.00 a week, up to £17.12 a day, £137 a week for the premium valet products.

Heathrow was dearer, as it always is. Maple Parking's express park and ride showed at £5.12 a day, £40.95 a week, NCP Flightpath at £5.85 (£46.79), Purple Parking's own Terminal 2 and 3 site at £6.87 (£54.95) and Good to Go at £7.37 (£58.99). Meet and greet started at £10.63 a day, £85.00 a week with the official airport product a few pence behind at £10.70 a day and £85.61 a week.

The comparison that makes those numbers land is the airport's own charge for doing nothing at all. As of January 2026, dropping someone at the Gatwick terminal costs £10, a rise of 43% from £7 and double the £5 the airport introduced in April 2021. Heathrow charges £7 for a maximum of ten minutes, up from £6. Bristol is £8.50, London City and Southend £8, Manchester £6.40, Birmingham, Edinburgh and Glasgow £6, Cardiff £3. Being dropped at Gatwick and collected again costs £20 in kerbside charges alone, so four such visits come to £40 — more than the £33.75 that parks the car for eight days behind a barrier.

The market behind all this is large and growing. UK airports handled 299,351,416 terminal passengers in 2025 on Civil Aviation Authority data completed on 23 March 2026 — Heathrow 84,462,273, Gatwick 42,769,164, Manchester 32,061,307. A single percentage point of those passengers driving and parking is three million cars.

The 2017 pre-pack, and the fingerprints it left on the paperwork

The brand and the business behind it parted company nine years ago. Purple Parking Holdings Limited, company number 08956186, incorporated on 24 March 2014 as Paris 128 Limited and renamed in October 2015, entered administration on 2 November 2017 with Paul David Williams and Benjamin John Wiles of Duff & Phelps appointed. Trade press at the time reported debts of around £2 million and a business carrying roughly 300,000 holidaymakers a year, with the brand and website sold in a pre-packaged administration sale after no buyer could be found for the whole company. Holiday Extras bought the brand, the customer base and the website that same day, and says car parks stayed open and bookings were honoured throughout.

The corporate tidying up took another seven years. The administration ended on 29 October 2020, the same day compulsory liquidation began on a petition filed on 12 October 2020; the liquidation concluded on 22 October 2024 and the company was dissolved on 19 March 2025. Two sister companies incorporated on 1 August 2017 at 55 Baker Street, London W1U 7EU — Purple Parking Services Limited (10894350) and Purple Parking Meet and Greet Limited (10894363) — were both dissolved on 8 January 2019.

That history still shows in the terms. The Heathrow and Gatwick conditions on the current site name the contracting party as “Purple Parking Limited whose registered office is 55 Baker Street, London W1U 7EU” — the old address, not Holiday Extras' registered office in Hythe. A search of the register returns the Holdings, Services and Meet and Greet companies, all dissolved, and no active company of that exact name that this review could locate. The privacy policy on the same site names Holiday Extras Limited as the data controller. It is almost certainly a page that has never been rewritten rather than anything sinister, but a customer trying to work out who to write to is entitled to a straight answer, and the two documents give different ones.

Park Mark became Safer Parking in April 2026

The nearest thing to an independent rating in this trade is the police-backed security accreditation, and it changed hands this year. From 1 April 2026 Police Crime Prevention Initiatives took over direct management of the scheme from the British Parking Association, which had run it on their behalf, and rebranded the awards: Park Mark became Safer Parking, Park Mark Plus became Safer Parking Plus, Park Mark Freight became Safer Parking Freight, and — the one that matters most to anyone handing over a key — the Approved Meet and Greet Operator scheme became Safer Parking Meet and Greet. Park Access and Park Access EV were renamed Safer Parking Accessible and Safer Parking Accessible EV after consultation with Disabled Motoring UK.

Accreditation is awarded on assessment of the physical site: surveillance, lighting, access control, signage and management of the space, with police involvement in setting the standard. Purple Parking's Heathrow Terminal 2 and 3 and Terminal 4 sites both carry the award, with barriers, CCTV, fencing and staff patrols listed. It is a genuine, externally assessed standard and it is the single most useful filter a driver has, because it applies to the car park rather than to the website selling it.

The awkward part is that the group's own explainer has not caught up. The Holiday Extras page about Park Mark still describes the scheme as “managed by the British Parking Association” and “awarded by the Association of Police Officers” — a body that ceased to exist in 2015 and a management arrangement that ended in April 2026. Whether every meet and greet operator sold through the site holds the specific meet and greet accreditation is not stated on the listings, and could not be confirmed for this review. If you are booking valet parking, that is the question worth asking before you pay.

Free cancellation, unless you booked the cheap one

“FREE Cancellation” sits on the search results and on the airport landing pages, and for most bookings it is accurate — you can amend or cancel through the customer login. The Gatwick conditions go further and grant a 14-day cancellation right with a full refund, reducing to a refund “minus any reasonable costs” where the booking starts inside those 14 days.

That is worth more than it looks. Regulation 28(1)(h) of the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 removes the statutory 14-day right from contracts for “the supply of accommodation, transport of goods, vehicle rental services, catering or services related to leisure activities, if the contract provides for a specific date or period of performance”. A car park booked for named dates sits close enough to that wording that many operators simply refuse refunds. Purple Parking gives the right anyway on that page, which is a better term than the law demands.

The exceptions are where the money goes. Bookings made at the SAVER price “cannot be amended, and no refund will be given if the booking is cancelled” — and the SAVER price is very often the one at the top of the results, because it is the cheapest. The credit and debit card booking fee is non-refundable whatever you do. Meet and greet carries a £25 no-show fee for non-arrivals or cancellations inside one hour. Come home early and you still “pay the whole fee for the service booked”; come home late and the car park charges the extra locally at its own published rate, which is not the rate you booked. And the price promise is quoted two different ways on the same site: the terms and conditions page says a cheaper identical booking found “within 24 hours” will be matched, while the Heathrow conditions say the company “will endeavour to match the price” if you find it within 7 days.

What the car park will not pay for

The liability wording repays five minutes of reading, because it is narrower than most drivers assume. The company accepts liability for loss or damage “only where the same is proved and to the extent that it is proved to be caused by our negligence, wilful act or default or breach of statutory duty”. Excluded from that are minor scratches, dents or chips to paintwork; damage attributed to poor light, weather or a dirty car; windscreens and glass; mechanical and electrical failure, flat batteries, key fobs and alarms; wheels, tyres and punctures; and the interior and anything left in it. Any claim must be reported before you and the vehicle leave the site.

There are conditions on your side too. The keys have to be left with staff — the wording is that the company “will not be liable for insurance where the keys have not been left with our staff”. The car has to be roadworthy, taxed, MOT'd, with legal tyres and enough fuel to reach the car park and come back. Black cabs, left-hand drive cars and vehicles fitted with disabled controls are not accepted and carry no liability if they are parked anyway, and the same applies to cars with roof boxes or trailers outside the size limits. The Terminal 2 and 3 listing states a 2.15 metre vehicle limit, described on the page as a length; 2.15 metres is a standard height clearance rather than any car's length, so that one is worth confirming with the operator if you drive something tall.

Two further clauses are easy to miss. On meet and greet, the vehicle may sit at the terminal for up to 90 minutes after you hand over the key, and the company “will not accept liability for any incident involving your vehicle until such time that it is moved by us”. And if the car is parked for more than 16 days at the Terminal 2 and 3 site it may be moved to a different secured location entirely.

Airport parking is on the CMA's list now

The Competition and Markets Authority began using its new direct enforcement powers against online pricing on 18 November 2025, opening investigations into 8 businesses and sending advisory letters to 100 more across 14 sectors. The published list of sectors names “parking and airport parking” alongside package holidays, rail travel, cinemas and gyms. The practices it identified are failing to include mandatory charges upfront, adding unavoidable fees at checkout and presenting misleading headline prices. Final price transparency guidance followed on 10 December 2025, and under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 the CMA can now fine a firm up to 10% of global turnover without going to court first.

The regulator has already shown it will use the machinery on this industry: in a December 2025 decision announced the following February it fined Euro Car Parks £473,000 — not for a pricing breach, but for failing to respond to a statutory information notice. For a driver, the practical effect of all this is simple. The headline day rate on any parking site should now include the compulsory fees, so when you reach the payment page it is worth checking whether the total has grown, and the non-refundable card booking fee in the Purple Parking terms is exactly the sort of charge the guidance is aimed at.

Where a complaint goes when there is no ombudsman

The contact page offers live chat, an online amendment and cancellation portal and a customer complaint form, and publishes a service figure most companies keep to themselves: “We resolve 80% of issues within 21 days (most are resolved within 7 days).” Putting a number on that at all is more than the great majority of parking firms do.

What it does not offer is a telephone number, a postal address for complaints or the name of any alternative dispute resolution or ombudsman scheme, and the site's own FAQ section sends readers to the Holiday Extras help centre rather than answering in its own right. There is no equivalent of the Financial Ombudsman Service here: if the form does not settle it, the route is a county court claim against whichever company the terms say you contracted with, which brings the 55 Baker Street problem back into focus. Photograph the car at handover and at collection, keep the booking confirmation, and raise any damage before you drive off the site, because the terms require exactly that.

Where it wins

  • Real choice rather than one company's price list: 29 car parks at Gatwick and more than 20 options at Heathrow, including rivals cheaper than Purple Parking's own site
  • Prices that stand up against the airports: £33.75 for eight days at Gatwick, against £10 to drop someone at the terminal for a few minutes
  • 31 UK airports and ports covered, including Dover and Southampton docks and small regional airports such as Exeter, Humberside and Teesside
  • Park Mark, now Safer Parking, accreditation on the Heathrow T2/T3 and T4 sites, with barriers, CCTV, fencing and patrols listed
  • A 14-day cancellation right granted in the Gatwick conditions, which regulation 28(1)(h) of the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 does not require
  • Backed by an established parent: Holiday Extras Limited has traded since 1983 and books over eight million travellers a year
  • Short, published transfer times — 3 to 20 minutes at Heathrow, 10 to 15 at Gatwick, with a T4 bus every 10 to 15 minutes from 03:30
  • A published complaint-handling figure: 80% of issues resolved within 21 days, most within 7

Where it falls short

  • The cheapest fares are the least flexible: SAVER bookings cannot be amended and are not refunded at all, so the headline price and the free-cancellation promise rarely belong to the same booking
  • The terms name a contracting party, Purple Parking Limited at 55 Baker Street, that no active company on the register appears to match; the three companies that carried the name are all dissolved, while the privacy policy names Holiday Extras Limited
  • The price promise is stated two ways on the same website — 24 hours in the terms and conditions, 7 days in the Heathrow conditions — and excludes voucher and third-party discounted prices either way
  • The liability carve-outs are wide: minor scratches, dents and chips, glass, wheels and tyres, batteries and electrics, and everything inside the car, with claims required before you leave the site
  • On meet and greet the car can sit at the terminal for up to 90 minutes with no liability accepted until staff move it, and a £25 no-show fee applies inside the last hour
  • No telephone number, no postal complaints address and no ombudsman or ADR scheme named, so an unresolved dispute means a county court claim
  • The group's own Park Mark explainer is out of date, still crediting the British Parking Association and the Association of Police Officers after Police CPI took the scheme over in April 2026

Common questions

Who owns Purple Parking?

Holiday Extras Limited, which bought the brand, customer base and website on 2 November 2017 when the previous operator entered administration. Holiday Extras Limited was incorporated on 21 January 1983 and is registered at Ashford Road, Newingreen, Hythe, Kent CT21 4JF under company number 01693250. The Purple Parking privacy policy states that “Purple Parking is a part of Holiday Extras Limited”.

Did Purple Parking go into administration?

Purple Parking Holdings Limited (08956186) entered administration on 2 November 2017 with administrators from Duff & Phelps appointed, and the brand and website were sold that day in a pre-packaged sale. The administration ended on 29 October 2020, compulsory liquidation concluded on 22 October 2024 and the company was dissolved on 19 March 2025. The website and the bookings continued throughout under Holiday Extras.

Can I cancel a Purple Parking booking and get my money back?

It depends on the fare. Standard bookings can be amended or cancelled through the customer login, and the Gatwick conditions grant a 14-day cancellation right with a full refund, or a refund less reasonable costs if the booking starts inside those 14 days. Bookings made at the SAVER price cannot be amended and are not refunded. The card booking fee is non-refundable in every case, and meet and greet carries a £25 fee for a no-show or a cancellation inside one hour.

Is the car park liable if my car is damaged?

Only where negligence, a wilful act, default or breach of statutory duty is proved. The terms specifically exclude minor scratches, dents and chips to paintwork, windscreens and glass, mechanical and electrical faults including flat batteries, wheels, tyres and punctures, and the interior and its contents. Damage must be reported before you and the car leave the site, so inspect it there rather than at home. On meet and greet, no liability is accepted for the first 90 minutes while the car is still at the terminal.

What does Park Mark mean on an airport car park?

It is a police-backed security accreditation covering surveillance, lighting, access control and site management. From 1 April 2026 Police Crime Prevention Initiatives took over direct management from the British Parking Association and renamed the awards, so Park Mark is now Safer Parking and the Approved Meet and Greet Operator award is now Safer Parking Meet and Greet. Purple Parking's Heathrow Terminal 2/3 and Terminal 4 sites carry the award.

Is Purple Parking cheaper than parking at the airport itself?

On the searches run for this review it was substantially cheaper than the on-the-day gate price, with Gatwick park and ride from £4.21 a day and Heathrow from £5.12. The site advertises savings of up to 70% against turning up without a booking. Official terminal-side products are also sold through the same search — at Heathrow the official meet and greet showed at £10.70 a day against £10.63 for the cheapest independent equivalent — so it is worth comparing the airport's own car parks on the same results page rather than assuming off-airport always wins.

Who do I complain to if something goes wrong?

Purple Parking takes complaints through an online form and live chat, and says it resolves 80% of issues within 21 days and most within 7. There is no ombudsman for airport parking and no alternative dispute resolution scheme is named on the site, so an unresolved claim would go to the county court. The Financial Ombudsman Service has no jurisdiction here — the group's FCA authorisation, firm reference 828848, covers Holiday Extras Cover Limited's travel insurance only.

Our verdict

As a way of finding cheap parking near a British airport, Purple Parking does the job: 31 airports and ports, a genuine spread of operators rather than one brand's own car parks, and eight days at Gatwick from £33.75 when the airport charges £10 just to stop at the kerb. The security accreditation on its Heathrow sites is a real, externally assessed standard. Read the fare type before you pay, though, because the SAVER price that wins the comparison is the one that cannot be amended or refunded, and read the liability list if you are handing over keys. The paperwork behind the brand has not been updated since the 2017 pre-pack, and with no ombudsman standing behind a parking booking, knowing exactly who you contracted with matters more here than it would on an insurance policy.
