Bike insurance is a stubbornly manual market. Plenty of the firms that price a 125cc commuter, a restored Bonneville and a 200bhp adventure bike sensibly are small brokers with schemes rather than household-name insurers, and finding them one at a time is an evening's work. One form, three cover levels and a lot of bike shapes

The questionnaire runs the three statutory tiers. Third party only is the legal floor and pays for injury and damage you cause to other people. Third party, fire and theft adds the two losses that account for most bikes that never come back. Comprehensive brings in damage to your own machine, and on a bike it is often no dearer than the middle tier, because insurers read it as a signal about the rider rather than the risk.

What matters more on two wheels is the extras list, and the panel quotes on most of it: pillion cover, helmet and leathers, personal effects, legal expenses, European use, protected no-claims discount and breakdown. Helmet and leathers is the one riders underestimate — a lid and a set of textiles can run past £1,000, and a comprehensive policy will not automatically pay for either.

The machine list stretches well past road bikes to mopeds, scooters, quads, 50cc and 125cc learner bikes and classics. One entry on that list needs care: Quotezone also names electric scooters, and privately owned e-scooters remain illegal to use on public land anywhere in Great Britain. Only rental scooters in designated trial areas are permitted, they are limited to 15.5mph, and the operator supplies the third-party cover, so there is nothing for a rider to buy.

Who Seopa answers to, and who pays it

Seopa holds FCA permission as a non-investment insurance intermediary and a credit broker, which is the correct shape of authorisation for a firm that introduces you to insurers and to the finance arrangement behind monthly payments. Its insurance mediation falls within the Financial Services Compensation Scheme: 90% of a claim with no upper limit for ordinary insurance mediation, and 100% with no upper limit for compulsory classes, which is the bracket bike insurance sits in.

The company is candid about payment. Its initial disclosure document states that providers pay "a flat fee, a commission or a combination of the two", and the how-we-make-money page ties that payment to a completed sale rather than a click. The same document is equally blunt about the limits of the service: "You will not receive advice or a recommendation from us", and the panel "do not claim to be whole of market".

If something goes wrong with the introduction itself, complaints go to Seopa Customer Relations in Belfast, and to the Financial Ombudsman Service at Exchange Tower, Harbour Exchange Square, London E14 9SR, or 0800 023 4567, once eight weeks have passed. That route is worth knowing about. In 2024/25 the ombudsman took 14,082 complaints about car and motorcycle insurance and upheld 42% of them, against 38% across insurance generally and 34% across every financial product it handles.

What a bike quote is really reacting to

Two forces are pulling in opposite directions on price. Riding is getting more popular: L-category registrations in May 2026 were up 14.9% on the same month a year earlier, with the year to date up 15.8%, adventure bikes up 29.1% in the month and road sport up 22.8%. More new machines on the road usually means more theft-attractive stock and more claims.

Against that, the underlying motor market has cooled. The Association of British Insurers put the average motor premium at £560 in the first quarter of 2026, down 3.6% on a year earlier, though repair costs have not followed — insurers paid out £2.9bn in the quarter, £1.9bn of it on repairs, with the average accidental damage claim up 8% to £3,699.

Then there is the risk the underwriter is actually pricing. Department for Transport figures record 315 motorcyclists killed and 5,481 seriously injured in a single year, a rate of 111 deaths per billion vehicle miles across 2.85bn miles ridden. That rate, not your postcode, is why a bike premium behaves so differently from a car one — and it is also why the trend is better than riders expect, with fatalities down 46% since 2004 while traffic fell only a tenth.

The rules that turn a cheap quote into an expensive week

Insurance on a bike used on the road is compulsory, and the penalties are not proportionate to the saving. A police stop without cover brings a £300 fixed penalty and 6 points, the court can add an unlimited fine and a disqualification, and the machine can be seized and in some cases destroyed.

The licence rules catch people out just as often, because the quote form asks about entitlement and the answer changes the price. Category AM starts at 16 and covers mopeds restricted to 45km/h. A1 starts at 17 and allows up to 125cc and 11kW. A2 starts at 19 and caps power at 35kW. Unrestricted category A is 24 by direct access, or 21 by progressive access from A2.

Compulsory basic training lasts 2 years, and letting it lapse without passing the full test carries a fine of up to £1,000 and up to 6 penalty points. Buy a year's policy on a CBT with four months left on it and you are paying for cover you cannot legally use for two thirds of the term.

Where the service stops short

The panel is the first limit. Up to 25 providers is a decent spread for a bike, but Seopa says plainly that it is not whole of market, and the specialist end of motorcycling — classic valuations, multi-bike policies, track day extensions, heavily modified machines — is exactly where a single missing broker costs you real money. Anyone with an unusual bike should ring two direct-only names as well.

The second is contact. Quotezone's privacy policy confirms that the three cheapest online providers may get in touch, and that the telephone service allows up to 5 providers to call, with email, SMS and WhatsApp on top where consent has been given. Providers may also run a soft credit search, which shows on your file without affecting your rating. None of that is hidden, but it is more contact than a rider comparing prices out of curiosity expects.

The third is the gap between a quote and a price. Seopa's own terms say the display of a quote "is not a representation from us that insurance will be available to you at the price shown", and the £256.34 figure is the level half of May's quotes fell below rather than a typical amount paid. Results are ordered on price, so excess levels, modification rules and claims handling do not surface until you click through — and Defaqto rates the policies on that panel, not the comparison service carrying them. The satisfaction score is thin evidence too: 4.83 out of 5 is a strong number, but it rests on 141 reviews from a service running millions of quotes a year. One loose end worth flagging: the initial disclosure PDF still shows an Adelaide Street address while Companies House and the site's legal page both give Amelia Street, so the document appears to be overdue an update.

Common questions

Does Quotezone insure my motorbike itself?

No. Seopa Limited is an intermediary, not an insurer. It collects your details once, passes them to a panel of up to 25 providers and shows you what they quote. The policy, the excess, the claims line and the certificate all come from whichever provider you buy from.

Is the £256.34 figure what I should expect to pay?

Not exactly. It is the level that 51% of motorbike quotes issued through the service in May 2026 came in below — a quote threshold, not an average premium, and not a promise. Seopa's terms state that a quote on screen is not a representation that cover will be available at that price once the insurer has your full details.

Will insurers start phoning me?

Some will. The privacy policy allows the three cheapest online providers to make contact, and the telephone comparison route allows up to 5 providers to call, with email, SMS and WhatsApp used where you have given consent. Preferences can be changed through the site's opt-out centre, though quote confirmations still come through.

What happens if the comparison service itself lets me down?

Complain to Seopa Customer Relations in Belfast first. If eight weeks pass without resolution, or you are unhappy with the answer, the Financial Ombudsman Service will look at it free of charge on 0800 023 4567. It upheld 42% of the 14,082 car and motorcycle insurance complaints it took in 2024/25.

Does comparing on price alone cost me anything on a bike?

It can. Bike policies differ sharply on helmet and leathers limits, pillion cover, modification declarations and whether a European trip is included. Two quotes twenty pounds apart can carry excesses hundreds of pounds apart, so the cheapest line on the results page is a starting point for reading the wording rather than a decision.

Our verdict

For an ordinary bike on an ordinary licence, Quotezone does the job it claims to: one form, up to 25 prices, nothing to pay, and a regulated firm with more than twenty years of trading behind it. Treat £256.34 as a sanity check rather than a target, expect three providers to contact you, and read the excess before the premium. Riders with a classic, a modified machine or a garage full of bikes should use it as a first sweep and then phone the specialists it does not carry.
