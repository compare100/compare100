Saga Travel Insurance Review

Rates and terms checked 16 August 2026 · Travel Insurance · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Saga writes travel insurance for people aged 50 and over, and nobody else. That restriction is the whole point of the product: rather than treating older travellers as an awkward edge case to be surcharged, the entire policy is built around them.

It shows in the limits. The Plus tier carries £20 million of emergency medical cover and £20,000 for cancellation — four times Aviva's top-tier cancellation limit. There is no upper age limit at all, and cruise cover is included as standard rather than sold as an add-on.

The trade is that you cannot buy it if you are 49, and premiums for a healthy 52-year-old on a short European break will often look expensive against a mainstream insurer.

The limits are in a different league

Set the Plus tier against a mainstream insurer's top tier and the gap is stark:

  • Cancellation: £20,000 against a typical £5,000
  • Baggage: £10,000 against a typical £2,500
  • Medical: £20 million against a typical £15 million

The cancellation figure is the one that earns its keep. A couple booking a three-week escorted tour or an ocean cruise can easily commit £12,000 or more in non-refundable payments. That sits comfortably inside Saga's limit and well outside most competitors'.

The £10,000 baggage limit is similarly aimed at a specific customer — someone travelling with camera equipment, jewellery or medical devices, where £2,500 does not cover the contents of the case.

No upper age limit, and why that is rarer than it sounds

Most mainstream travel insurers taper away as you age. Annual policies often close to new customers at 75 or 79, and single-trip premiums climb steeply from 80 onwards. Some decline outright past 85.

Saga has no upper age limit on any policy. An 88-year-old can buy the same Plus cover as a 52-year-old, subject to medical screening.

For anyone in their eighties who still travels, that is often the difference between insured and uninsured. It is worth knowing that the specialist market for very elderly travellers is thin, and prices there are frequently worse than Saga's.

Medical screening: what 'diagnosed only' actually excludes

Saga covers most pre-existing conditions after screening, but with a specific and important restriction: only diagnosed conditions are covered. Anything undiagnosed or still under investigation is excluded.

That distinction catches people out. If you are waiting on test results, or have symptoms your GP is still investigating, that condition is not covered — and neither, potentially, is anything that turns out to be connected to it. The policy will cover a diagnosed heart condition you have declared; it will not cover chest pain you are being investigated for.

The practical advice is to complete screening after your diagnosis rather than during investigation, and to re-screen if your health changes between buying the policy and travelling. Declaring more is always safer than declaring less.

Cruise cover included, and the 180-day allowance

Most insurers treat cruising as an add-on, charging extra for cabin confinement, missed port departures and itinerary changes. Saga includes it as standard, which reflects who the product is for.

Trip length is generous too: 180 days on a single trip, against Aviva's 120. The annual policy allows 180 days of travel a year with a 45-day per-trip maximum, extendable to 60 or 90 days for some regions.

That combination — long trips, cruise cover as standard, no age cap — is aimed squarely at retired travellers taking extended winter breaks, and it is a coherent product for exactly that person.

Who should not buy this

Anyone under 50 cannot, and that is a hard line.

Beyond that, a healthy 52-year-old taking a £600 long weekend in Spain is paying for limits they will never approach. Twenty thousand pounds of cancellation cover on a £600 trip is dead weight, and a mainstream insurer's mid tier will almost certainly cost less for equivalent practical protection.

The value appears when the trip is expensive, long, or when your age or medical history makes mainstream insurers expensive or unwilling. Below that threshold, you are paying for headroom you cannot use.

Where it wins

  • £20m emergency medical and £20,000 cancellation on the Plus tier — far above mainstream limits
  • No upper age limit on any policy
  • Cruise cover included as standard rather than charged as an add-on
  • Single trips up to 180 days, and 180 days a year on annual cover
  • £10,000 baggage limit suits travellers carrying valuable equipment
  • 5-star Defaqto rating
  • Most pre-existing conditions covered after screening

Where it falls short

  • Unavailable to anyone under 50
  • Expensive relative to mainstream insurers for short, cheap trips
  • Only diagnosed conditions are covered — anything under investigation is excluded
  • Annual policies cap individual trips at 45 days despite the 180-day annual allowance
  • High limits are wasted on travellers who will never approach them

Our verdict

The right choice for expensive trips, long trips, cruises, and anyone whose age or medical history makes mainstream insurers awkward. The £20,000 cancellation limit and absent age cap are genuinely difficult to match elsewhere, and cruise cover as standard removes a common add-on cost. It is the wrong choice for a healthy person in their early fifties taking a cheap short break, where you are paying for headroom you will never use. If you are waiting on a diagnosis, complete medical screening once you have it — conditions under investigation are not covered.

Common questions

Can I buy Saga travel insurance if I am under 50?

No. Fifty is the minimum age and there are no exceptions. Every Saga product is built around over-50s travellers.

Is there an upper age limit?

No. Saga has no upper age limit on any of its travel policies, which is unusual — many mainstream insurers close annual policies to new customers at 75 or 79.

Does it cover cruises?

Yes, as standard rather than as a paid add-on. Most insurers charge extra for cruise cover.

What does 'diagnosed conditions only' mean?

Saga covers pre-existing conditions that have been formally diagnosed and declared at screening. Conditions that are undiagnosed or still under investigation are excluded, along with anything that turns out to be related to them.

How long can I travel for?

Up to 180 days on a single trip. Annual policies allow 180 days of travel a year with a maximum of 45 days per trip, extendable to 60 or 90 days for some regions.
