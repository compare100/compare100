SamBoat Review: 50,000 Boats, a French Contract and Who Carries the Risk

Rates and terms checked 19 September 2026 · UK Boating Holidays · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

SamBoat lists more than 50,000 boats for hire and its own pricing guide puts a motorboat at about €300 a day before fuel, a week aboard a monohull sailboat at roughly €2,400 and a catamaran at €4,300, with a skipper adding around €200 a day. Search the United Kingdom and 161 boats come back, from two-berth narrowboats in Staffordshire to a skippered sailing day out on Windermere. Figures checked on 19 September 2026 against SamBoat's own pages.

The business was built in Bordeaux in 2014 by Laurent Calando and Nicolas Cargou, and it was already carrying 25,000 boats across 120 countries when Dream Yacht Charter bought it on 6 November 2018, backed by a €5 million fundraising to which the Next Stage fund contributed €2.9 million. The two founders and their twenty staff stayed on. Since then the platform has absorbed LateSail, Vents de Mer, Argos and CYC, and it now advertises more than 1,400 destinations and around 300,000 people sailing with it each year. Plenty of the English canal boats on it are marked "No licence", and on most listings a skipper is optional rather than compulsory.

One thing is worth knowing before you book rather than after: the hire contract is with the boat's owner, not with the platform. SamBoat's terms, last updated on 4 April 2026, say it "acts as a technical and commercial intermediary in putting Users in touch" and "is not a party to the Rental Agreements concluded between Users". The company behind the site is LACANI SAS of 223 Avenue Émile Counord, Bordeaux, registered at Bordeaux under RCS 799 758 412 with share capital of €2,030,000, and the whole arrangement runs under French law. That decides who you deal with when a boat is not where it should be, so it is better read now than at a pontoon.

Where the money goes when you press book

SamBoat takes its living from both sides of the transaction, and the owner's half is published plainly: a service fee of "20% (twenty percent)" of the rental, dropping to "10% (ten percent) for extras such as stand-up paddleboarding, masks and snorkels, fishing equipment". That is the number an owner weighs against listing the boat privately, and it is in the same range as the big accommodation platforms.

Your side is less tidy. The terms describe a separate Renter Protection Fee covering "payment security, assistance, customer support and Booking management services", and say both charges are "due upon confirmation of the Booking, regardless of the actual completion of the rental" — but nowhere in the document is that fee expressed as a percentage or a sum. You see it as a line on the checkout total rather than as a published rate, so compare the all-in price rather than the headline day rate when you are weighing two boats.

Payment is handled by Adyen N.V. in Amsterdam for ordinary bookings and by Stripe Payments Europe in Dublin where the boat comes from a broker or partner fleet. The owner is paid "within 24 (twenty-four) hours after the first day of rental", which is quick by charter standards and tells you the platform is holding your money only briefly.

The paperwork a canal boat needs before anyone may hire it out

This is the part a marketplace listing cannot show you, and on English and Welsh canals it is specific. The Canal & River Trust requires a hire operator to hold a business licence, to carry "third party and public liability insurance cover... which covers liabilities of at least two million pounds for each claim" from an insurer authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, and to hold a hire boat safety scheme certificate — a requirement that has applied to every hire boat regardless of age since 1 April 2018.

There is more. The operator must "prepare a detailed handover procedure which you must use each time you hire out the boat", covering navigation rules, locks and bridges, boat handling and equipment, and must run "a 24 hour emergency call out service", unless it holds British Marine's Quality Accredited Boatyard certificate instead. New operators pay a non-refundable £105 including VAT to have an operating proposal assessed, and the Trust aims to give an in-principle decision within six weeks.

SamBoat's own requirement of an owner is a single sentence: the boat must be "insured for the rental activity". The platform sets out no check on licences, safety certificates or handover training, so on an inland listing those are questions for the owner before you pay, not afterwards.

Skippered, bareboat, and the coding question at sea

Away from the canals the rules change again. The Royal Yachting Association's guidance is that a vessel needs to be commercially coded "to operate a boat for skippered or bareboat use usually for commercial gain" and "to take people afloat who pay more than a reasonable contribution towards the trip". Coding means a survey against the Maritime and Coastguard Agency's small commercial vessel code, published as MGN 280, and for a skippered boat it also means a commercially endorsed skipper.

Most of the UK listings on SamBoat are either professional operators, who will have been through that, or inland boats where the code does not apply. The grey area is a privately owned sea boat being let out by its owner for cash. Ask two questions before you book one: is the boat coded for the area you are going to, and does the owner's policy cover hire and reward? The platform's answer to both is that these are the owner's responsibility, so the checking falls to you.

On the water itself the terms put the practical duties on the renter: hold the required pleasure boat licence, see that "people on board wear a life jacket or buoyancy aid", return the boat clean, with the same fuel level, and never sublet it or charge the people aboard.

Four cancellation policies, and the one you get by default

Owners choose from four policies and the choice is theirs, not yours, so it is worth reading on the listing before the dates are set. FLEXIBLE refunds everything up to the day before departure. MODERATE puts half the cost on the owner if you give more than two weeks' notice. STRICT charges you the full rental "regardless of the notice period". And where the owner has picked nothing at all, ZEN applies: "70% (seventy percent)" back if you cancel five days or more ahead, and nothing at all inside five days.

Weather is the usual reason a boat holiday falls apart, and the terms are strict about it. Force majeure needs an "unforeseeable, irresistible event external to the parties", evidenced by a special weather statement from a competent authority or an administrative ban on navigation, and the document says in terms that "normal or foreseeable weather conditions... do not in themselves constitute force majeure". A grey, blowy week on the south coast is not a refund.

An owner who cancels on you owes SamBoat a penalty of "50% (fifty percent) of the rental amount, with a minimum of €200.00", which is a real deterrent. Note, though, that the penalty is payable to the platform rather than to you.

Deposits, damage and the tariff for bringing a boat back dirty

The security deposit is set by the owner and taken from your card "48 hours (forty-eight hours) before the start of the rental", so it lands before you have seen the boat. It covers "damage caused during the rental, namely the amount of repairs, fuel level defects, rental delays, expert fees, administrative fees", and if SamBoat has to activate it, "5% (five percent) of the amount of the Security Deposit will be debited with a minimum of 100 € (hundred) Euros" as a handling charge on top of whatever the damage costs.

Several charges are fixed rather than argued over, which cuts both ways: €175 for abnormal exterior dirt, €155 for abnormal interior dirt, "40 Euros + 2.50 Euros / liter" for returning with the tank down, a minimum of €200 for tears, burns, stains or breakages, and the hourly rate increased by 50% for bringing the boat back late. Knowing the tariff in advance is better than discovering it, and a photographic record at handover and return is the cheapest insurance there is.

Complaints have short fuses. A renter has "24 (twenty-four) hours from the date of taking possession" to raise a problem and an owner has 48 hours from the end of the rental, so a fault noticed on day three of a week is already outside the window.

The cover you can buy, and the cover nobody is selling you

SamBoat does not insure the boat. The terms are explicit that owners must arrange their own cover for the rental activity and that the platform excludes liability if something is damaged. What it does sell, as a registered intermediary under ORIAS number 16001576, are three add-ons arranged with insurance partners including MAIF, Gritchen, Mutuaide Assistance and NEAT.

Deposit insurance covers up to €5,000 and starts at €30, refunding the money taken from your card after an accident. Cancellation insurance covers up to €20,000 from €20, for named reasons including illness, accident, redundancy, theft of documents, natural disaster, terrorism and riot, and it has to be bought at the time of booking rather than later. Repatriation cover pays the cost of getting you home or to hospital with 24-hour assistance. On the owner's own policy arranged through the platform, the excess is "10% of the amount of damages with a minimum of €500 and a maximum of €1,000".

Set the deposit cover against the deposit being asked. On a boat wanting €2,000 held on your card, €30 is a sensible trade; on a small inland boat with no deposit at all — and several UK listings ask for none — it buys you nothing.

Two pages of the same group, two fleet sizes

SamBoat's own site says "+ 50 000 to rent worldwide". Dream Yacht's page about SamBoat, published by its parent, says the platform has "over 30,000 boats to search". Both were live on 19 September 2026 and they cannot both be current. The gap is large enough to matter if you are choosing a platform on breadth, and the honest reading is that one of the two figures has not been revised since the fleet moved — it was 25,000 boats in 120 countries at the 2018 acquisition, so the direction of travel is clear, the arithmetic is not.

A second mismatch sits in the small print. The guarantees page names Stripe and MangoPay as the payment processors; the terms of 4 April 2026 name Adyen N.V. and Stripe Payments Europe. Neither is alarming on its own, and the terms are the document that binds, but a marketing page that has drifted from the contract is a reminder to take figures from the contract wherever both exist.

The satisfaction score on the home page — 4.88 out of 5 from 215,700 users — is the platform's own count of its own customers, not an independent assessment. There is no Defaqto, Which? or Fairer Finance rating for boat rental marketplaces, so no external rating was found for this page.

Where a British customer stands if the boat is not there

There is no United Kingdom company behind any of this. Dream Yacht Charter UK Limited, company number 12143353, was incorporated on 7 August 2019 at 27 New Dover Road, Canterbury, and was dissolved on 26 December 2023; an earlier Dream Yacht Charter UK Ltd, 07501705, was dissolved on 11 November 2014. The contract, the complaint and the court are all French: the terms are "governed by and subject to French law", disputes may go to a consumer mediator free of charge under Article R616-1 of the French Consumer Code, and proceedings may be brought where the defendant is based, where the boat was hired, or where the claimant is.

Nor is a boat hire an ATOL-protected flight or an ABTA-bonded package, so those familiar acronyms do nothing here. The protection a British customer does have runs through the card. Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 makes a credit card provider "jointly and severally liable" with the supplier for misrepresentation or breach of contract, on any single item with a cash price above £100 and not more than £30,000 — which covers most weeks afloat and few day hires. Below that, or on a debit card, chargeback is the route, and it is a scheme rule rather than a legal right.

Paying by credit card for anything over £100 therefore does more for you here than any of the optional add-ons, and it costs nothing.

What 161 UK listings actually look like

The UK is not where this platform is strongest, and the search results say so. England's bareboat canal boats come almost entirely from one Staffordshire fleet — boats of 47 to 70 feet sleeping two to eight, every one marked "No licence" — plus a 60-foot narrowboat at Adlington. Ullswater returns a single boat. Windermere has a 1985 Jeanneau Tonic 23 that is skippered only, with the skipper included free, fuel included and no deposit required, offered by the day or in two- and four-hour blocks.

That is a thin national choice next to the hundreds of boats the same platform lists around the Mediterranean, and it means the UK boater is often comparing one boat against no boat rather than one boat against three. Where a listing is a professional fleet — as the Staffordshire narrowboats appear to be — you are effectively booking a traditional hire firm through SamBoat's checkout, with SamBoat's cancellation terms and fee layered on top of the firm's own.

For a Mediterranean week, or for a catamaran in the Caribbean, the breadth is real and the comparison works in the platform's favour. For a week on the Llangollen Canal, check what the yard charges direct before you assume the marketplace is cheaper.

Where it wins

  • Very large choice abroad — more than 50,000 boats and 1,400 destinations, with or without a skipper
  • Owner commission is published at 20%, and 10% on extras, so the economics are not hidden
  • Clear damage tariff: €175 exterior, €155 interior, €40 plus €2.50 a litre for fuel, so there is little to argue about
  • Deposit and cancellation cover are cheap for what they hold — €5,000 from €30 and €20,000 from €20
  • Owners are paid within 24 hours of the first day, and an owner who cancels pays a penalty of 50% with a €200 minimum
  • Plenty of English canal listings need no licence at all, and several ask for no security deposit

Where it falls short

  • The fee you pay as a renter is never expressed as a percentage or an amount anywhere in the terms — only as a line on the checkout total
  • The default ZEN policy keeps 30% of the rental even when you cancel five days ahead, and SamBoat's own fees may come back as a credit note that is "personal, non-transferable, non-refundable" rather than as money
  • You have 24 hours from taking possession to complain; a fault found midweek is already out of time
  • Safety compliance rests entirely with the owner — the platform publishes no check on hire boat safety certificates, Canal & River Trust business licences or MCA coding
  • No UK company stands behind the booking: Dream Yacht Charter UK Limited was dissolved on 26 December 2023, and the contract, mediator and courts are French
  • The fleet is quoted at over 50,000 boats by SamBoat and over 30,000 by its own parent, and the UK choice is thin at 161 boats — one of them on Ullswater

Common questions

Who am I actually hiring the boat from?

The boat's owner. SamBoat's terms say it "acts as a technical and commercial intermediary in putting Users in touch" and "is not a party to the Rental Agreements concluded between Users". The platform takes the payment, holds the deposit arrangement and offers to mediate, but the contract for the boat is between you and the owner, under French law.

What does SamBoat charge?

The owner pays 20% of the rental, reduced to 10% on extras such as paddleboards and snorkelling equipment. You pay a Renter Protection Fee covering payment security, assistance and booking management, which the terms do not express as a percentage or a sum — it appears in your checkout total. Both are due on confirmation, "regardless of the actual completion of the rental".

When is the security deposit taken, and what if there is damage?

It is debited 48 hours before the rental starts. If SamBoat has to use it, a handling charge of 5% of the deposit with a minimum of €100 is taken on top of the repair cost. Fixed charges apply for some things: €175 for abnormal exterior dirt, €155 inside, €40 plus €2.50 a litre for missing fuel, a minimum of €200 for breakages, and the hourly rate plus 50% for a late return. Deposit insurance covering up to €5,000 starts at €30.

Do I need a licence to hire a narrowboat through SamBoat?

Most of the English canal listings are marked "No licence", which reflects the ordinary position on inland waterways: the hire operator holds the Canal & River Trust business licence and the hire boat safety scheme certificate, and gives you a handover instead. At sea it is different — many listings require a pleasure boat licence, and where you take a skipper the Royal Yachting Association's guidance is that a boat operated for commercial gain should be commercially coded and the skipper commercially endorsed.

What protection do I have as a UK customer if something goes wrong?

Not ATOL or ABTA, neither of which applies to a boat hire. Your practical protection is the card: Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 makes a credit card provider jointly and severally liable with the supplier for misrepresentation or breach of contract where the item costs more than £100 and no more than £30,000. Beyond that, SamBoat offers to mediate, and French consumer mediation under Article R616-1 is free to use.

Our verdict

For a week in the Mediterranean or a catamaran somewhere warm, SamBoat is a genuinely broad market and the published 20% commission, the fixed damage tariff and the cheap deposit cover make it easy to work out what you are paying. In the UK it is a different proposition: 161 boats, a single Staffordshire fleet carrying most of the canal listings, and a contract that sits under French law with no British company behind it. Read the cancellation policy on the individual listing rather than assuming the default, ask an owner of a private sea boat about coding and hire-and-reward insurance, pay by credit card for the Section 75 cover, and compare the all-in checkout total against the yard's own price before you book at home.
