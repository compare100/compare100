Start Rescue Breakdown Cover Review

Rates and terms checked 23 August 2026 · Breakdown Insurance · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Start Rescue is the retail brand of Call Assist Limited, a Colchester company incorporated on 16 November 1998 and still run from Axis Court on North Station Road. It is not a patrol force in the mould of the AA or the RAC. Call Assist buys recovery capacity from a network of more than 400 approved recovery operators across the UK and a further network reaching over 45 countries in Europe, and sells the cover under its own name. Group-wide it says it looks after over 2 million breakdown customers.

The pricing is the reason most people arrive. One Star vehicle cover starts at £31.58 a year or £3.16 a month, and the most popular Three Star policy — roadside, nationwide recovery and home assistance — is £55.12 a year. Start Rescue's own renewal page sets that against rival three-star equivalents priced between £70.85 and £172.06. Add a second vehicle and a further 10% comes off.

Cheap breakdown cover has a reputation, and this is the part that separates Start Rescue from it: the company has been a Which? Recommended Provider every year since 2019, taking a seventh consecutive title in 2025 on an 86% customer score, and its Three Star policy carries a Defaqto 5 Star expert rating. Its own service data for November 2025 to April 2026 puts over 78% of vehicles repaired at the roadside, against a target of a recovery operator with you inside 60 minutes.

The five-rung ladder, and where the money goes

Cover is sold in stars rather than named tiers, and each rung buys one clearly defined thing.

One Star, £31.58 a year, is roadside assistance plus local recovery within 10 miles. That radius is the whole product: break down 12 miles from home and the recovery operator has no obligation to take you the rest of the way. Two Star, £46.76, replaces the 10-mile limit with nationwide recovery, which for most drivers is the single most useful upgrade on the page. Three Star, £55.12, adds home assistance. Four Star, £90.37, adds European cover and a theft and break-in benefit. Five Star, £106.66, adds accident, theft and vandalism cover plus assistance where the driver is taken ill or injured, worth up to £1,000.

Monthly payment is offered at £3.16, £4.68 and £5.51 for the bottom three rungs. Four and Five Star are annual-payment only. Personal cover, which follows the driver into any eligible vehicle rather than sitting on one registration, is also restricted to the first three tiers.

The benefit limits are identical across the ladder and are worth knowing before you need them: up to £250 towards alternative transport or a hire vehicle up to 1600cc, up to £150 for one person to travel back and collect the repaired car, and emergency accommodation at £150 for a lone traveller or £75 each, capped at £500 per incident. Misfuelling assistance is also capped at £250. Abroad, the accommodation cap rises to £1,000 per incident and alternative travel to £500, with £200 for two people to return for the vehicle and a £200 reimbursement ceiling on private towing off a European motorway.

The product the regulator does not score

Anyone comparing car or home insurance has two public yardsticks. Neither exists here, and that is worth understanding before you weigh any breakdown provider against another.

The FCA published its general insurance value measures data for 2025 on 21 July 2026. It reports claims acceptance rates, claims complaints and claims costs as a share of premium across dozens of retail products — motor sits at 99% acceptance, home combined at 62 to 71%, travel at 83 to 86%. Breakdown is absent, and deliberately so: the FCA states that it is not requiring firms to report claims cost information for legal expenses insurance or vehicle breakdown insurance. There is no official acceptance rate for this product line, from Start Rescue or anyone else, and any page quoting one has made it up.

The second gap is pricing. ICOBS 6B, the rulebook chapter that bans charging existing customers more than an equivalent new customer at renewal, applies only "in relation to a home insurance or motor insurance policy or any related additional product sold to a consumer". A standalone breakdown policy bought direct is neither. Start Rescue's pledge that customers "never pay more than our standard website retail prices" is therefore a commercial promise it has chosen to make, not a rule the FCA can enforce against it. That is a point in its favour — most of the market makes no such promise — but it is a promise, and the company also notes that a renewal price can still rise as the vehicle ages.

With no regulatory scorecard, the policy document is the evidence. The rest of this page works from it.

Who actually carries the risk

Call Assist Limited arranges and administers the policy under FCA firm reference 304838, which covers insurance distribution. It does not carry the risk. For policies starting on or after 1 November 2024 the insurer is ARAG Legal Expenses Insurance Company Limited, firm reference 202106, a Caerphilly-registered company originally incorporated on 28 May 1909 and authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority. Policies that began on or before 31 October 2024 remain with Ageas Insurance Limited, firm reference 202039. If you are renewing something bought in 2023, the name on the claim is not the name on this year's certificate.

ARAG is best known in Britain as a legal expenses underwriter, which surprises people, though the arrangement is ordinary enough in the assistance market. What matters practically is that the claims decision belongs to the insurer, and the recovery itself belongs to a third contracted party — the local operator dispatched from that 400-strong network. Three organisations stand between the phone call and the tow.

Start Rescue's own agreement is explicit that the sale is non-advised: "you will not receive personalised advice or recommendations". It also states the firm "may act on behalf of the insurer" in handling your premium, so money paid to Start Rescue counts as received by the insurer, and that it "may also receive a commission and/or other economic benefits from the insurer". Choosing your own tier is your job, and the five-rung ladder is designed so that the £31.58 headline is the one you see first.

Limits that decide whether a claim pays

Read the Insurance Product Information Document and a handful of conditions do most of the work.

The first 24 hours. Any claim within 24 hours of buying is excluded. Buying cover from the hard shoulder does not work, which is the point of the rule.

The next 48. Whatever tier you paid for, "for the first 48 hours from the start date of your policy, cover is limited to One Star cover only". Pay £106.66 for Five Star on a Friday and you have 10-mile local recovery until Sunday. Mid-policy upgrades need the same 48 hours' notice.

One mile from your door. On tiers without home assistance, help "within 1 mile radius/straight line of your registered home address" is excluded. Most breakdowns happen on a cold morning on the drive, which is what makes the £8.36 step from Two Star to Three Star the one worth thinking hardest about.

Six claims, £15,000. The IPID caps cover at "maximum six (6) claims per period of insurance" and at claims "totalling more than £15,000 in any one period". A repeat of the same fault inside 28 days is excluded unless you can show the vehicle was permanently repaired and roadworthy.

Vehicle rules. Cars and motorcycles of any age qualify and vans up to 3.5 tonnes, but vans over 8 years old at inception are excluded, and for European travel the policy excludes "vehicles over 10 years old at the date this policy incepted". Towed caravans and trailers must not exceed 7 metres, or 23 feet, and must use a standard 50mm tow ball. Minibuses, limousines, motorhomes, horseboxes, campervans and anything with more than ten seats are out, as are racing, rental, hire and courier use. Cover runs across Great Britain, Northern Ireland, the Isle of Man, and Jersey and Guernsey for non-residents.

Where the sales page and the policy wording part company

The breakdown cover page lists "unlimited callouts" among the standard benefits of every tier, and the FAQ repeats it: "We allow unlimited call-outs for each vehicle that is insured". The Insurance Product Information Document for the same product restricts cover to six claims per period of insurance and £15,000 in total. Both statements come from Start Rescue. Only one of them is the contract, and it is the shorter number. Six callouts is more than most drivers will ever use and the limit is far from the meanest in the market, but a page advertising "unlimited" while its own key facts document says six is the kind of gap the Financial Ombudsman looks at closely, and it should be reconciled.

Refunds are the second place to slow down. The 14-day cooling-off period returns the premium and the arrangement and administration fee in full, provided no claim has been made. After that the wording is flat: "you will not be eligible for any refund of premium or the Fee". Pay £106.66 in January, sell the car in March, and there is nothing to reclaim. The one exception runs the other way — if Start Rescue cancels, giving seven days' written notice, and no claim has been made in the first six months, the premium and the fee are refunded in full.

The fee itself is the third. The arrangement and administration agreement confirms a fee is charged and that it "varies" with the risk, but never states an amount. The advertised £31.58 is described as including insurance premium tax and fees, so the total is what it says — you simply cannot see the split, or what the fee would be on a different vehicle, until you have run a quote.

Complaints, and what the Ombudsman sees

Complaints go to Start Rescue first. The policy terms commit to acknowledging one within five working days and responding within 20 working days. If the answer is unsatisfactory, the Financial Ombudsman Service at Exchange Tower, London E14 9SR takes it free of charge on 0800 023 4567 or 0300 123 9123, and you have six months from the final response to refer it.

The Ombudsman publishes what breakdown complaints are actually about, and the list is a fair checklist for any provider. Renewal features twice — policies renewed automatically when the customer no longer wanted them, and policies not renewed when the customer assumed they would be. Then premiums rising sharply at renewal, being charged more for a policy at the roadside, ending up with dual cover through two overlapping policies, mechanics taking too long to arrive, incorrect advice or a fault caused during a repair, and vehicles damaged during recovery. On timing, the Ombudsman says it weighs "what you advertise as your usual timeframes" — which makes Start Rescue's published 60-minute target a commitment it can be measured against, not marketing.

Start Rescue auto-renews both annual and continuous monthly policies by default, and given how often the Ombudsman sees renewal disputes, the setting is worth finding in the online account at the point of purchase rather than eleven months later. Note also that a monthly policy can only be stopped up to two days before the next period begins.

Common questions

How soon after buying am I covered?

Not immediately. Claims within 24 hours of purchase are excluded, and for the first 48 hours from the start date cover is limited to One Star — roadside assistance and local recovery within 10 miles — whatever tier you paid for. Upgrading an existing policy also needs 48 hours' notice before the higher cover applies.

Will they come out to my driveway?

Only on Three Star and above. One and Two Star exclude "home assistance or assistance within 1 mile radius/straight line of your registered home address". Three Star, at £55.12 a year, is £8.36 more than Two Star and is what buys that first mile.

Is it really unlimited callouts?

The website and FAQ say unlimited. The Insurance Product Information Document for the same policy says "maximum six (6) claims per period of insurance" and excludes claims totalling more than £15,000 in any one period. A repeat of the same fault within 28 days is also excluded unless the vehicle was permanently repaired. Treat six as the working figure and ask Start Rescue to confirm which document governs.

Who pays the claim?

ARAG Legal Expenses Insurance Company Limited, firm reference 202106, for policies starting on or after 1 November 2024. Policies that began on or before 31 October 2024 are still underwritten by Ageas Insurance Limited, firm reference 202039. Call Assist Limited, firm reference 304838, arranges and administers the cover but does not carry the risk. All three references are checkable on the FCA Register.

Can I get my money back if I cancel?

Within 14 days of the start date or of receiving your documents, whichever is later, you get the premium and the arrangement and administration fee back in full provided no claim has been made. After 14 days there is no refund on either an annual or a continuous monthly policy, though a monthly policy can be stopped up to two days before the next period begins.

What does European cover include?

It sits on Four Star at £90.37 and Five Star at £106.66, and the network reaches over 45 countries. Abroad the limits are up to £500 for alternative travel, £150 per person for emergency accommodation to a maximum of £1,000 per incident, £200 for two people to return and collect the vehicle, and up to £200 reimbursed for private towing off a motorway. Vehicles over 10 years old at the policy's inception date are not covered for European travel.

How do I complain?

Start Rescue acknowledges a complaint within five working days and aims to respond within 20. If you are unhappy with the answer, the Financial Ombudsman Service at Exchange Tower, London E14 9SR will look at it free of charge on 0800 023 4567 or 0300 123 9123, and you have six months from the final response to refer it.

Our verdict

Start Rescue does the cheap end of breakdown cover properly, which is rarer than the price suggests. Seven consecutive Which? Recommended Provider titles, a Defaqto 5 Star rating on the £55.12 Three Star policy and a 78% roadside fix rate are the numbers that matter, and the benefit limits do not shrink as the price falls. The catches are structural rather than hidden: 10-mile recovery at One Star, a one-mile exclusion around your own home below Three Star, One Star cover for the first 48 hours, and no refund at all once 14 days have passed. Two Star at £46.76 buys nationwide recovery and Three Star at £55.12 buys the driveway, and for most households that £8.36 step is the decision. The gap between an advertised "unlimited call-outs" and a policy document that says six is the one thing Start Rescue should tidy up.
