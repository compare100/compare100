Tesco Mobile Phone Contracts Reviewed

Rates and terms checked 10 September 2026 · New Phone Contract · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Tesco Mobile has been selling airtime since 2003 and is now the largest mobile virtual network operator in the UK, carrying more than 6 million customers on Virgin Media O2's network. It is a 50:50 joint venture between Tesco plc and Virgin Media O2, and the two owners renewed that arrangement for a further ten years on 31 January 2024, when the figure they quoted between them was 5.5 million customers. The company on the paperwork is Tesco Mobile Limited, incorporated on 29 May 2003 under the name Reywas Newco 3 Limited and registered at Tesco House, Shire Park, Kestrel Way, Welwyn Garden City. The masts belong to O2: 99% of the UK population is covered for 4G, with 5G in most large towns and cities. Pay monthly plans open at £7.50 a month and SIM only at £9.

A Tesco Mobile phone deal is two agreements rather than one, and that is worth understanding before you start configuring anything. The handset sits on a device plan of 12, 18, 24, 30 or 36 months — "You pay the same for your phone whatever length you choose", in the company's own words, so a longer plan spreads one fixed total over more months instead of costing more. The airtime sits on a separate rolling one-month usage contract you can move up or down once a month. Both leave your account on a single Direct Debit. The handset half is a credit agreement, which is why Tesco Mobile Limited holds Financial Conduct Authority permission for consumer credit and why Tesco Stores Limited appears beside it as a credit broker introducing customers to it.

The promise the company leads on — a monthly price that does not move mid-contract — is a Clubcard product. The Tariff Promise in the January 2026 pay monthly terms is worded precisely: "If you are a Clubcard Customer and have joined or upgraded to our Clubcard Price deal(s) since December 2024, we will not increase the monthly subscription price of your Pay Monthly Service tariff during your Minimum Contract Period". A Clubcard is free, and a number can be linked by texting CLUBCARD to 28578, so this is a condition rather than a wall — but it is a condition, and a buyer without one is on a different deal at a different price.

Two contracts, one Direct Debit

The device plan is the part with the tie-in. Pick a term and the handset price is divided across it: the iPhone 17e in 256GB was listed at £17.99 a month with nothing to pay up front, which across the 36-month plan the page defaults to comes to £647.64 for the phone. Choose 24 months instead and the monthly figure rises while the total stays where it is, which is the opposite of how a bundled contract behaves — there, a longer term usually means a bigger bill overall.

The airtime half is genuinely monthly. Tesco Mobile lets you move a tariff "down as well as up once per month only", effective from the next bill date, with one limit worth reading twice: "You can't go below the lowest price point for the original tariff you joined on". So the floor is set on the day you sign, not by whatever the cheapest plan happens to be in a year's time.

Upgrading early is a matter of clearing the handset balance. Anytime Upgrade is the credit-sale agreement behind the device plan; Anytime Upgrade Flex adds a trade-in option in the final six months, where the company buys the handset back and, in its wording, "will credit the proceeds from buying back the Device directly to your Tesco Mobile credit account to pay the remaining Repayments". The phone has to be in full working order, unrepaired by anyone unapproved, with no software locks or trackers left switched on, and you have to be upgrading to another Anytime device at the same time. There is no charge for using it: "We will not charge you any fees for exercising your Flex Promise option."

Month 24 is the hinge in the paperwork. Cancel the usage contract before the end of month 24 and, in Tesco Mobile's words, "you will have to pay off the remaining balance of your mobile handset credit agreement in full". Cancel after it, on a credit agreement longer than 24 months, and you may carry on paying the handset off monthly. A 30 or 36-month device plan therefore behaves like a 24-month commitment for the first two years and a genuinely separable loan after that.

What a Clubcard price is worth in pounds

Tesco Mobile prints both numbers, which is more than most sellers do, and totals the difference for you rather than leaving it as a percentage. On SIM only at the time of writing: 12GB with unlimited minutes and texts at £9 a month with a Clubcard against £10 without, a £24 gap across 24 months; 100GB at £14 against £17.50, a gap of £84; and unlimited data at £20 against £25, a gap of £120, with a £50 Tesco gift card attached to that one. Those are discounts of roughly 10%, 20% and 20%.

Handset deals carry the same split, advertised as savings rather than rates — £198 off an iPhone 17 sold with an iPad A16, up to £396 off a Samsung Galaxy S26 FE with a £100 gift card, up to £504 off a Google Pixel 11 Pro XL. Pay as you go handsets carry a £10 discount bought with a top-up, again Clubcard only, with the Doro Leva L20 at £49, the Moto G06 Power at £79 and the TCL K70 4G at £104.

Points are the smaller half of the loyalty maths and it is easy to overrate them. Pay monthly customers collect "a point for every £1 you spend on your mobile bill", and every 250 points converts to £2.50 in vouchers — a penny a point. A £20 SIM plan therefore generates 240 points a year, worth £2.40 in vouchers, against a Clubcard price saving of £60 over the same twelve months on that plan. The discount is the benefit; the points are a rounding error beside it. Clubcard Plus at £7.99 a month doubles both the data and the points from Tesco Mobile and adds 10% off two Tesco shops a month, which only pays if the grocery side is used.

It is fair to ask whether loyalty prices are real discounts or inflated list prices. The Competition and Markets Authority looked at exactly that and published its review on 27 November 2024, covering around 50,000 grocery products on loyalty promotions and surveying 2,439 shoppers. It found that for 92% of them the non-member price during the promotion was the same as or cheaper than before it, with loyalty discounts running 17% to 25% across the five supermarkets studied, and 77% of shoppers holding a Clubcard. It also asked supermarkets to do more for shoppers without a smartphone and for under-18s. That review covered groceries, not phone contracts, and nothing equivalent has been published on loyalty pricing in mobile — so it is context rather than a clean bill of health for this particular price list.

Households with more than one line can add Family Perks, which needs at least two eligible active pay monthly subscriptions on the same billing account and allows up to five. Tariffs taken out before 2 July 2012, special offer tariffs and other excluded tariffs cannot choose a Perk.

How long the freeze lasts, and who sits outside it

The Tariff Promise runs for the minimum contract period and stops there. Tesco Mobile's own pricing page is straightforward about what happens next: "An annual increase to this price each April will only apply once your minimum contract period ends." The freeze is a fixed-term guarantee, not a permanent policy, and a customer who lets a contract run on will meet an April rise like anyone else.

The dates matter more than usual here. Tesco Mobile moved to showing increases in pounds and pence at the point of sale from 17 December 2024, roughly a month before Ofcom's prohibition on inflation-linked mid-contract rises began to bite on contracts taken from 17 January 2025. Customers who joined on non-Clubcard deals between 27 March 2023 and 16 December 2024 were not swept up by either change: their contracts still rise by "the Consumer Price Index (CPI) rate of inflation published each January + 3.9%". Anyone in that band who has not upgraded since is on the old percentage formula, and the pounds-and-pence promise on today's sales pages says nothing about them.

What the company does not publish is a single figure for the rise itself. The pricing page says the increases "have been shown in pounds (£) and pence (p) at the point of sale", which is Ofcom-compliant and useless for comparison shopping: you cannot see the number until you have chosen a deal and reached the checkout. At least 30 days' notice is given before a price change takes effect, the older service terms promise 30 days' written notice of any change made to a customer's significant disadvantage, and both sets of terms reserve the right to pass on "price changes outside of our control, such as national changes in VAT".

Roaming, spend caps and the numbers buried in the service terms

Home from Home covers 48 destinations — the EU states plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Gibraltar, Guernsey, Jersey, the Isle of Man, Monaco, San Marino, Vatican City and the French and Portuguese overseas territories including the Azores, Madeira, the Canaries, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Mayotte, Réunion and French Guiana — with the UK allowance usable at no extra cost and, in Tesco Mobile's words, "no cap/usage limit". A fair usage policy sits behind that: spend more time or use more of your allowance abroad than at home and a surcharge follows, with a warning text first.

Outside that list the rate to know is 1p per MB in the USA, Canada, South Africa, Australia and New Zealand. That sounds trivial and is not: 1p a megabyte is £10.24 a gigabyte, so a single evening of video on holiday can cost more than a month of the plan itself.

The service terms carry a default spend cap for anyone who does not choose one. Tariffs at £15.00 and below are capped at £50.00, tariffs above £15.00 and up to £25.00 at £100.00, and higher tariffs at £150.00. A customer can set the cap as low as £0, after which incoming calls in the UK and texts anywhere still work. The cooling-off period is 14 working days from receipt of the SIM and, where there is one, the handset, which must go back in its original packaging with all its components within 14 days. Cancel later and the early termination charge is the monthly subscription multiplied by the months remaining, less VAT, less a discount of "approximately 3% for any benefit we might receive". Tesco Mobile's liability is capped: "our liability will not exceed £5,000 in any 12 month period". Orders are limited to five SIM cards per transaction, and any change to the amount or date of a payment carries 10 days' notice.

One bill, two ombudsmen, and a complaints table the company publishes itself

Because the handset and the airtime are separate agreements, a complaint about them goes to different places. A dispute about service, billing or coverage goes to the Communications Ombudsman at PO Box 730, Warrington WA4 6WU or 0330 440 1614, and Tesco Mobile's own complaints page sets the trigger at six weeks: "If you remain unhappy after 6 weeks and would like take this matter further you are statutorily entitled to refer your complaint to an alternative dispute resolution scheme." Six is the current figure because Ofcom shortened the wait from eight earlier in 2026. A dispute about the credit agreement behind the phone goes instead to the Financial Ombudsman Service on 0800 023 4567. Customer care is on 0345 301 4455, business customers on 0345 601 2448, and letters go to PO Box 710, Darlington DL1 9JU.

Add Tesco Mobile Protect and there is a third document set. The cover is administered by Asurion Europe Limited under FCA reference 502545 and underwritten by WDP Insurance Limited, regulated by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission and subject to limited FCA regulation under reference 786246. The product information document is dated August 2026. Damage Cover takes accidental, liquid, malicious and pet damage plus out-of-warranty breakdown; Full Cover adds loss and theft. Cosmetic damage is excluded unless the claim is for a cracked screen, as is anything wrong with the device before the policy started. Claims are settled by repair or replacement rather than cash, the policy runs for a maximum of five years, and there are 28 days to cancel with a refund if no claim has been made. Premiums and the excess are set by the value of the handset and confirmed in the welcome pack rather than published in a table, so neither can be compared before buying.

The more useful independent measure is the firm's own return. For July to December 2025, Tesco Mobile reported 199 consumer credit complaints opened and 208 closed, 196 of them (94%) within eight weeks, with 33 (16%) upheld; and 222 insurance complaints opened and 230 closed, 99% within eight weeks, with 8% upheld. Consumer credit complaints have fallen a long way from the 356 opened in January to June 2024. Ofcom's own quarterly table for January to March 2026, published on 30 July 2026, puts Tesco Mobile at 2 complaints per 100,000 pay monthly customers against an industry average of 2 — and Ofcom's caveat should travel with the number, since it treats providers separated by less than one complaint per 100,000 as comparable.

Where the paperwork lags behind the sales pages

The flexibility claim is the one that needs qualifying. The usage contract really is monthly, but the month-24 rule means that leaving a 36-month device plan in year one obliges you to settle the entire handset balance at once. A customer who reads "rolling monthly contract" as "I can walk away in month 10" has read only half of it, and the half that binds them is in the credit agreement rather than on the deal page.

The absence of a published increase figure is a real gap. Ofcom's rule requires the pounds-and-pence amount to be shown prominently at the point of sale, and Tesco Mobile says it is — but nothing on the public pricing page tells a shopper comparing networks what the April rise will be on a plan they have not yet configured. The one group who can work theirs out are the customers still on CPI plus 3.9%, who are on the formula the current marketing implies has been retired.

Several of the documents behind the offer are years old. The Anytime Upgrade terms carry a date of 5 September 2018 for a product being sold in 2026, and the Family Perks terms were last updated on 1 June 2018. The code of practice still points customers to "Ombudsman Services: Communications" at ombudsman-services.org, while the help pages name the Communications Ombudsman at commsombudsman.org — the same scheme under a name it stopped using, which is the kind of mismatch that sends a frustrated customer to the wrong website. The January 2026 service terms also print their default spend caps as £15.00 and below, above £15.00 to £25.00, and above £27.50, leaving tariffs priced between £25.00 and £27.50 unaccounted for; that reads like a drafting slip rather than a policy, and it needs confirming with the company before anyone relies on it.

Two smaller things. Tesco Mobile Limited's Financial Conduct Authority firm reference number does not appear on the consumer pages examined for this review, even where the credit permission is described — it is worth checking on the Financial Services Register before signing a device plan. And the awards on display, from Trusted Reviews, Expert Reviews and Uswitch, are publisher awards rather than ratings from an assessment body. There is no Defaqto or comparable independent rating for a mobile network, so Ofcom's complaints table and the company's own complaints return are the nearest things to an outside verdict that exist.

Where it wins

  • Handset and airtime are separate agreements, so the airtime price does not keep being charged for a phone already paid off
  • Clubcard Price deals freeze the monthly airtime price for the whole minimum term, in writing, in the service terms
  • The same handset total whichever device plan length you pick, from 12 to 36 months
  • Clubcard and non-Clubcard prices are printed side by side with the 24-month difference totalled
  • 48 roaming destinations included with no allowance cap, plus 1p per MB in five long-haul countries
  • The company publishes its own six-monthly complaints figures, and Ofcom's Q1 2026 table puts it level with the industry average
  • Anytime Upgrade Flex trade-in in the final six months carries no fee

Where it falls short

  • The price freeze is conditional on holding a Clubcard: without one you pay up to £120 more over 24 months on the same SIM plan and still face an annual April increase
  • Cancelling the "rolling monthly" usage contract before the end of month 24 makes the entire remaining handset balance payable in full
  • No annual increase figure is published anywhere public — you only see it once you have configured a deal and reached the point of sale
  • Customers who joined non-Clubcard deals between 27 March 2023 and 16 December 2024 are still on CPI plus 3.9% percentage rises
  • Key documents are badly out of date: Anytime Upgrade terms dated 5 September 2018, Family Perks 1 June 2018, and a code of practice still naming an ombudsman brand that has been retired
  • A tariff can never be moved below the entry price of the plan you originally joined on, however cheap later plans become
  • Insurance premiums and excesses are set by handset value and only confirmed in the welcome pack, so they cannot be compared before buying
  • The default spend cap bands in the January 2026 terms leave tariffs between £25.00 and £27.50 unspecified

Common questions

Does Tesco Mobile put prices up mid-contract?

Not on a Clubcard Price deal taken since December 2024. The January 2026 pay monthly terms promise not to increase the monthly subscription price during the minimum contract period for those customers, and an annual April increase only starts once that period ends. Customers on non-Clubcard deals taken between 27 March 2023 and 16 December 2024 are still on CPI plus 3.9% each year, and deals from 17 December 2024 onwards show the increase in pounds and pence at the point of sale.

Which network does Tesco Mobile use?

O2's. Tesco Mobile is a 50:50 joint venture between Tesco plc and Virgin Media O2, renewed for ten years on 31 January 2024, and it is the largest mobile virtual network operator in the UK with more than 6 million customers. Coverage is quoted at 99% of the UK population for 4G, with 5G in most large towns and cities.

Can I leave a Tesco Mobile contract early?

The airtime contract is monthly and can be cancelled, with an early termination charge on any minimum term still running — the monthly subscription multiplied by the months left, less VAT, less roughly 3%. The handset is separate. Cancel the usage contract before the end of month 24 and the remaining balance of the handset credit agreement becomes payable in full; after month 24, on a longer credit agreement, you can keep paying it monthly.

How much does a Clubcard actually save on a phone plan?

On the SIM only plans listed at the time of writing, £24 over 24 months on the 12GB plan (£9 against £10), £84 on 100GB (£14 against £17.50) and £120 on unlimited data (£20 against £25). Handset deals are advertised as lump-sum savings instead, from £198 to £504 on the offers running at the time of writing. Clubcard points are a much smaller benefit: a point per £1 of bill, with 250 points converting to £2.50 in vouchers.

Who do I complain to if something goes wrong?

It depends which half of the deal is at fault. Service, billing and coverage complaints go to Tesco Mobile first on 0345 301 4455 and then, after six weeks, to the Communications Ombudsman on 0330 440 1614. Complaints about the credit agreement behind the handset go to the Financial Ombudsman Service on 0800 023 4567, as do complaints about Tesco Mobile Protect, which is underwritten by WDP Insurance Limited in Gibraltar and administered by Asurion Europe Limited.

Our verdict

Tesco Mobile is doing something structurally better than most sellers of phone contracts: the phone and the airtime are two agreements, the handset costs the same total whichever term you choose, and the airtime price is frozen in writing for the minimum period rather than stepped every April. The catch is a loyalty card, and it is worth being blunt about the size of it — £120 over 24 months on the unlimited SIM plan, plus an annual rise the Clubcard customer avoids. A Clubcard is free, so most readers should simply get one before quoting. Read the credit agreement rather than the deal page before choosing a 30 or 36-month device plan, because the flexibility the sales pages advertise does not fully arrive until month 25.
