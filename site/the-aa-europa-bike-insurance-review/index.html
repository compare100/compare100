AA Motorbike Insurance (Europa Bike) Review

Rates and terms checked 29 August 2026 · Motorbike Insurance · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

The AA badge on a motorbike policy has always belonged to somebody else: Europa Bike, a Bristol broker that has done nothing but two-wheeled insurance since 1996. Companies House has the firm as Europa Group Limited, number 03279177, incorporated on 15 November 1996 under its original name of Motor Cycle Direct Insurance Services Limited and renamed on 3 December 2003. Its terms of business set out a panel of five insurers for annual motorbike policies — Highway, Aviva, Tradex, Ageas and AXA — and since 1 August 2023 every policy has carried basic legal expenses cover at no extra charge.

That specialism is the reason four household names put their own badge on Europa's paperwork. The group has arranged bike cover behind AA Motorcycle Insurance and RAC Bike Insurance, and motor cover behind Ford Insure and Vauxhall Insurance, alongside its own brands Motorcycle Direct, Onequote Direct, Ridersure and Wiser Choice. One thing has changed and it matters if you came here from a search for the AA: the AA's own motorbike page now reads “we’re sorry, the AA is no longer offering insurance for motorbikes”, followed by “if you’re an existing policyholder don’t worry – you’re still covered” and “AA Motorbike Insurance is provided by Europa Bike.” The book did not lapse; it simply lost the badge, and Europa Bike runs it directly.

What sits underneath is a straightforward specialist motor policy. Third-party property damage is covered to £20,000,000 against a statutory minimum of £1,200,000, the claims line on 0344 335 2934 is staffed 24 hours a day, and no claims bonus builds to nine years. Comprehensive, third party fire and theft, third party only and a laid-up option for a bike off the road are all sold, and the current policy booklet carries the version stamp FEB26(V), so the wording is being maintained rather than left to age.

One Bristol broker, four familiar badges

Europa Group trades from Unit 2, West Point Row, Great Park Road, Bradley Stoke, Bristol BS32 4QG, and is authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority under firm reference 309794 — a number you can check yourself on the FCA Register. The group describes six brands of its own: Motorcycle Direct for bikes and scooters sold direct, Onequote Direct for car, van and home, Ridersure for brokers, Europa Underwriting, Wiser Choice and Wiser Choice Bike. On top of those it has run white-label programmes for the AA, the RAC, Ford and Vauxhall, and says Ford Insure alone delivered more than 500,000 policies over five years.

The AA-branded paperwork is still hosted on Europa's own servers, which is how the arrangement is visible from outside. The legal expenses wording issued with AA bike policies carries the reference LEI/AA/06/19 and names two further firms: 4th Dimension Innovation Limited of Thorpe Business Park, Egham (firm reference 516498) as administrator, and Financial and Legal Insurance Company Limited of Cheadle (firm reference 202915) as insurer. That wording covers uninsured loss recovery to £100,000 with solicitors' costs capped at £100 an hour, motor prosecution defence to £2,500, and requires a claim to be reported within 120 days of the incident. The AA page itself refers to policies purchased or renewed from 10 September 2020.

A small inconsistency is worth knowing about before you go looking for the company. The terms of business dated August 2024 still give the address as Europa House, Midland Way, Thornbury, Bristol BS35 2JX. Companies House and the current website footer both give the Bradley Stoke address. The registered number and firm reference are identical in both, so this is a move that one document has yet to catch up with rather than two different companies.

What the policy pays on, and where it stops

Liability for injury to other people is unlimited, as the Road Traffic Act requires; damage to their property is covered to £20,000,000, which is a little over sixteen times the £1,200,000 the law actually demands. Comprehensive adds accidental damage, spare parts and accessories, and recovery of the bike to the nearest approved repairer. Third party fire and theft strips that back to fire and theft losses. The laid-up option covers fire, theft and accidental damage only while the bike sits at the garaging address you declared, and drops the requirement for tax and an MOT.

New motorcycle replacement is one of the better clauses and one of the most conditional. It applies where the bike is stolen, or where repair costs run past 70% of its list price including VAT, within six months of purchase — and only if you were the first registered owner and own the bike outright or on hire purchase. Leasing and contract hire are outside it. Otherwise the settlement basis is market value, the agreed figure on your schedule or the cost of repair, whichever is lowest.

Riding abroad is allowed for 30 days per trip up to 90 days in any one period of insurance, across the EU and designated countries. No claims bonus runs to nine years with protection available, and the booklet publishes the full step-back table for up to three fault claims. The exclusions are the ordinary ones for the class: helmets, leathers and personal belongings are not covered by the policy itself, theft is excluded where the keys were left in the ignition, and unauthorised use by someone known to you is excluded unless you report it to the police. Defence costs after a death on the road are capped at £1,000 for any one occurrence, which is modest next to the £2,500 in the separate legal expenses wording.

Six add-ons, and what each one is worth

Because the core policy leaves out protective kit and most of the legal cost of an accident, the optional list is where a lot of the value sits:

  • Legal expenses: up to £100,000 for non-fault claims, including personal injury and motoring defence. The basic version has been included free on policies since 1 August 2023.
  • Helmet and leathers: up to £1,000 for protective gear damaged in an accident — the gap the main policy leaves.
  • Personal accident: up to £25,000 for injury, disability or death.
  • Replacement vehicle: a hire bike of up to 650cc for up to 14 days after an accident, theft or vandalism.
  • Excess Protect: refunds the excess up to £600 a year on accidental damage, fire, theft, vandalism and flood claims.
  • Key Protect: up to £1,500 towards locksmiths and immobiliser reprogramming for lost, stolen or broken keys.

Discounts are offered for no claims history, secure parking and riding experience. Europa is paid a flat fee by the insurer for arranging the optional extras, which is disclosed in the terms of business rather than buried.

The fee schedule, in full

This is a non-advised sale: the terms of business say plainly “you will not receive advice or a recommendation from us”, and the firm acts as your agent in arranging the insurance while acting as the insurer's agent when collecting premiums and paying refunds. Premiums are treated as held by the insurer under risk transfer, and any interest earned on them is kept by Europa Group. Commission is taken from the insurer as a percentage that varies with the bike and the rider, plus a percentage of the loan where the premium is paid monthly through the group's credit provider.

The charges published in the August 2024 document are:

  • Renewal: up to £75
  • Mid-term change: £40 through the contact centre, £20 through the online portal
  • Cancelling inside the 14-day cooling-off period: £40
  • Cancelling after it, where the policy came from a price comparison site: £75
  • Cancelling after it, where it did not: £40
  • Policy voided for misrepresentation: £75
  • Duplicate documents: £10
  • Correcting a new business error: free

Paying monthly costs more than paying in one go, which the document states rather than leaving you to work out. The cooling-off period runs 14 days from the start of the policy or the day you receive the documents, whichever is later. Europa Group is covered by both the Financial Ombudsman Service and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme, and takes complaints at crt@europa-group.co.uk.

Its own complaints numbers are published, and they are not flattering

Europa Group publishes its complaints return on its own website, which most brokers do not, and the figures for 1 January to 30 June 2026 are worth reading before you buy. It opened 215 complaints and closed 209. Of those, 102 were closed inside three days and 107 took longer than three days but under eight weeks. None took more than eight weeks, which is a genuinely good result. But 44% were upheld — the firm agreed the customer was right in nearly half the cases it decided — and the total redress paid on all upheld complaints was £3,562, against £1,221 paid out on complaints it rejected. Spread across roughly 92 upheld cases, that is an average of about £39 each.

For scale, the Financial Ombudsman Service's annual data published on 21 May 2026 recorded 13,420 new complaints about car or motorcycle insurance in 2025/26, upheld at 35%, within 214,600 complaints across all financial products at an average uphold rate of 30%. A 44% uphold rate at the firm's own first stage sits above that, though the two are not measuring quite the same thing: a broker's internal figure counts service and administration disputes that never reach the ombudsman.

The wider value picture comes from the FCA's general insurance value measures data for calendar year 2025, published on 21 July 2026. Motor claims costs rose to 59% of premium from 54% the year before while premiums fell 7% and policies in force grew 4%, and the motor claims acceptance rate stands at 99%. Motorcycle is not itemised separately in that release, so there is no bike-specific acceptance rate to hold any insurer against.

The risk the premium is actually pricing

Bike premiums look punishing next to car premiums until you look at the casualty rates. The Department for Transport's motorcyclist factsheet, published on 30 July 2026, records 386 motorcyclists killed on Britain's roads in 2025 and 5,710 seriously injured, 6,096 killed or seriously injured in total. The fatality rate works out at 128 per billion vehicle miles and the serious injury rate at 1,891. Across 2021 to 2025, 92% of motorcycle KSI casualties were male and 62% of fatalities happened away from junctions on rural roads. The rate has improved — down 37% across all severities since 2004 — but it remains an order of magnitude above car travel.

Demand is going the other way. Motorcycle Industry Association registrations to May 2026 were running 15.8% ahead of the same point in 2025, with adventure bikes up 24.8% year to date and road sport up 16.9%. Meanwhile the Association of British Insurers reported on 29 July 2026 that insurers paid a record £3.2 billion in motor claims in the second quarter, with the average claim up 4% to £4,900 and the average motor premium paid rising £6 to £566. More riders, dearer repairs and a casualty rate like that is the arithmetic behind every bike quote you will be given this year.

Where it wins

  • Motorcycle-only specialist since 1996, and the firm actually behind AA, RAC, Ford and Vauxhall-badged cover
  • Third-party property damage to £20,000,000, against a legal minimum of £1,200,000
  • Basic legal expenses cover included free on policies since 1 August 2023
  • Claims line staffed 24 hours a day, seven days a week
  • Not one complaint took longer than eight weeks to close in the first half of 2026
  • Laid-up cover available for a bike off the road, without needing tax or an MOT
  • New motorcycle replacement within six months where repairs pass 70% of list price

Where it falls short

  • The AA has stopped selling motorbike insurance altogether, and the Europa Bike site now leads to a customer portal for existing policyholders rather than a quote form — new riders are pushed towards the group's Motorcycle Direct brand instead
  • 44% of the 209 complaints closed between January and June 2026 were upheld, and the total redress paid on them came to £3,562, an average of roughly £39
  • No excess figures appear in the policy booklet or the summary document — the wording says only that the amount is shown on your schedule, so you cannot compare it before you quote
  • The fee schedule is steep for a bike policy: up to £75 to renew, £75 to cancel if you bought through a price comparison site, £40 for a phone amendment and £10 for a duplicate document
  • Helmets, leathers and personal belongings are excluded from the policy itself and only covered by buying the £1,000 add-on
  • No sales or service telephone number is published — contact runs through live chat, Monday to Friday 9am to 6pm and Saturday 9am to 1pm
  • The published documents are out of step with each other: a summary dated August 2023, terms of business from August 2024 carrying a superseded Thornbury address, and a booklet stamped FEB26(V)

Common questions

Can I still buy AA motorbike insurance?

No. The AA's own motorbike page states that it is no longer offering insurance for motorbikes. Existing policies remain in force and Europa Bike, which arranged them, now looks after the book directly. If you want a new quote from the same firm, it sells under its Motorcycle Direct brand.

Who underwrites the cover?

Europa Group Limited is a broker, not an insurer. Its terms of business name a panel of five insurers for annual motorbike policies: Highway, Aviva, Tradex, Ageas and AXA. The policy booklet does not name the insurer at all, referring only to “the insurer described in your schedule”, so you will not know which of the five you have until the documents arrive.

What is the excess?

It is not published. Both the policy booklet and the insurance product information document confirm that an excess applies but state the amount is shown on your individual schedule. Ask for it in writing before you accept a quote, and ask separately about any additional excess for young or newly qualified riders.

How long can I ride abroad on the policy?

Thirty days per trip, up to a maximum of 90 days in any one period of insurance, across Great Britain, Northern Ireland, the Channel Islands, the Isle of Man and EU or designated countries.

Is Europa Bike properly regulated?

Europa Group Limited is authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority under firm reference 309794 and registered in England and Wales as company 03279177. It is covered by both the Financial Ombudsman Service and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme. The firm reference is verifiable on the FCA Register.

What does it cost to change or cancel a policy?

The August 2024 terms of business set a mid-term change at £40 through the contact centre or £20 online, cancellation at £40 inside the 14-day cooling-off period, £40 afterwards, or £75 afterwards if the policy came from a price comparison site. A voided policy costs £75 and duplicate documents £10. Insurer charges may apply on top.

Our verdict

Europa Bike is a competent motorcycle specialist that has spent three decades doing one thing, and the policy behind the AA badge is a reasonable one: a £20,000,000 third-party limit, a five-insurer panel, free basic legal cover and a 24-hour claims line. The catch is that the badge has gone. The AA no longer sells bike insurance, Europa Bike's own site now serves existing policyholders rather than new ones, and a rider looking for a fresh quote from this firm needs its Motorcycle Direct brand instead. Existing AA policyholders are still covered and should read the fee list before their renewal lands: at up to £75 to renew and £75 to cancel, the administration can move the price as much as the risk does.
