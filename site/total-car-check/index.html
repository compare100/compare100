Total Car Check Review: A £9.99 Report and a Free One That Is Genuinely Free

Rates and terms checked 11 September 2026 · HPI Checks · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Total Car Check has been selling vehicle history reports since 15 October 2009, when Total Car Check Ltd was incorporated in England under company number 07043189. The consumer app followed on 8 April 2015 and is still being updated — version 7.6.0 landed on 7 October 2025 — and it holds 4.9 out of 5 from 37,000 App Store ratings, which is a larger and higher-scoring sample than most of the paid-for check brands manage. By the time it was acquired the business was running over 42 million UK vehicle checks a year and growing revenue 30% year on year. Its own site now claims 33 million vehicles checked in the last twelve months.

Since 17 July 2023 the company has been owned outright by Motorway Online Ltd, the used-car marketplace, which holds 75% or more of both the shares and the voting rights. Founder David James stayed on as Motorway's director of vehicle data. That is worth knowing before you start, because it is the plainest explanation of how a full provenance report can be sold for £9.99 when the established names charge double: the report is not the whole business behind it.

The free check is real rather than a teaser, and Total Car Check is unusually clear about where it stops. Enter a registration and you get 20+ data fields without paying — make, model, fuel type, BHP, road tax status, MPG and running-cost estimates, and the complete MOT history. What the free tier does not include, in the company's own words, is "if a vehicle has an outstanding finance agreement attached to it, or if it has ever been involved in a incident that resulted in an insurance firm classifying the vehicle as a write off". Those two answers are the reason anybody pays, and they sit behind the £3.99 Silver and £9.99 Gold tiers.

£3.99 buys the write-off answer, £9.99 buys the finance answer

Three paid tiers sit above the free one, and the split between them is cleaner than most providers manage. A Basic Check covers police stolen markers, a VIN-to-registration match and mileage data. The Silver Check at £3.99 adds insurance-sourced stolen data on top of the police feed, write-off status, and scrap, unscrapped and salvage history. The Gold Check at £9.99 is the one with the outstanding finance search, a valuation, and what the company describes as "over 80 data points".

The finance result is more detailed than the yes-or-no that some cheap checks return. Where an agreement is live, Total Car Check says the report "will provide details of the agreement type, start and end date and number, as well as the finance provider and finance provider's contact details". That is enough to ring the lender yourself and ask for a settlement figure, which is the only thing that actually resolves the situation. A report that says "finance found" and nothing else leaves you no further forward.

Buying in bulk changes the arithmetic sharply. Gold falls to £4.99 each in a three-pack at £14.99, and to £3.99 each in a five-pack at £19.99. Silver falls to £1.99 each in a five-pack at £9.99. If you are working through a shortlist of four or five cars — which is what most people buying privately actually do — the five-pack is the sensible purchase and the per-report cost lands well under half the single price.

The finance search is Experian's, not the register everyone else licenses

Most of the British vehicle check market runs on one register, licensed out to brands that put their own badge on the front. Total Car Check does not sit in that arrangement. Its finance guarantee is issued by Experian Ltd, named in both the guarantee wording and the privacy policy, and the terms of service describe the underlying feeds as coming from "the Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency, the Police, the Motor Insurers' Bureau and their respective data suppliers".

For a buyer this is mostly a point about coverage rather than quality. Finance agreements are registered by lenders with the credit reference agencies, and different agencies do not hold identical files at identical moments. A lender that files with one register and not another produces a clear report on one site and a hit on another, and neither site is lying. It is the strongest argument for treating any single check as one piece of evidence rather than a verdict — and, at these prices, for running a second check from a different data source on a car you are serious about.

The privacy policy also names the other suppliers behind the service, which is more disclosure than the category usually offers: Braintree and PayPal for payments, Cloudflare for fraud prevention, Microsoft Azure for hosting, SendGrid for email, and MyCarNeedsA.com for the garage and servicing information. The company's data protection registration is ZA008598.

What Motorway gets out of a £1.99 report

Motorway is not a small buyer. Its own site claims 8,000+ verified dealers, over 1 million people who have used it to sell a car, an average of £1,600 more than a part-exchange offer, and 84% of sellers getting more than market price. At the time of the acquisition its sales had "more than doubling to a value of £1.7 billion" in 2022, with the platform listing over 1,000 cars a day. It charges the seller a success fee calculated on the final sale price, deducted from the payment, with nothing to pay if the car does not sell.

Put the two businesses side by side and the logic of a £1.99 vehicle check is not hard to follow. Somebody typing a registration into a check site is either buying a car or thinking about selling one, and the second group is exactly who Motorway wants. The check does not have to make money on its own.

There is nothing improper in that, and it is a large part of why the price is what it is. It is worth holding in mind for one reason only: if the same site that valued your car also invites you to sell it, that valuation is being produced by a company with a commercial interest in the answer. Take the number as a starting point and test it somewhere unconnected before accepting an offer.

The £30,000 headline comes with a doorstep checklist

The Gold tier's £30,000 guarantee is a finance guarantee, underwritten by Experian, and it pays "up to the market value of the vehicle at the time you make the claim" subject to that ceiling. It runs for 2 years from the date of the finance check. So far so good.

The conditions are where it gets demanding. Before handing over any money you must confirm that the VIN on the report matches the VIN on the car; confirm the seller's full name "and verify this by checking their passport or driving licence"; confirm the seller's address "by checking a utility bill"; and obtain a signed receipt showing the purchase amount and date. All of it has to happen before the purchase, and the vehicle has to be bought inside the United Kingdom.

Asking a stranger on their driveway for a passport and a utility bill is a reasonable thing to do and almost nobody does it. That is the practical gap in this cover: the guarantee is written for a careful buyer, and the buyer most exposed to undisclosed finance is the one who was in a hurry. There are further exclusions worth reading before you rely on it — a false registration mark or VIN voids it, so does paying "an unreasonable purchase price", defined as below 70% of market value, and cash payments above £1,000 or 10% of the price are not protected.

The other guarantee is smaller than its own number suggests

Stolen markers and write-off categories are covered by a separate document, the Total Car Check Guarantee, and its numbers are considerably tighter than the £30,000 on the front of the Gold tier. On a stolen vehicle it pays the lesser of one third of market value, one third of the purchase price, the cost of acquiring good title, or £10,000. On a Category A or B write-off the ceiling drops to £3,000, and on Category S, C, N or D it drops again to £1,500 — still capped at a third of whichever figure is lower.

A third of the purchase price is the operative limit for most people. On a £6,000 car that is £2,000, not £10,000, and on a Category N result it would be £1,500 at best. The wording also states that the guarantee is limited to VS&TD and police stolen data and does not extend to condition, MOT history or finance, so the two guarantees do genuinely different jobs and neither backs up the other.

The timing conditions are stricter too. The check must have been run "no more than seven (7) days prior to purchasing", and cover lasts 12 calendar months from the order date or until you sell the car, whichever comes first — half the life of the Experian finance guarantee. Free checks are excluded entirely. So are non-UK registered vehicles, salvage auction purchases, cars bought 30% or more below market value, and cash payments above £1,000 or 20% of the price. You must be at least 17, buying privately, at the seller's home address, with a complete V5 whose VIN matches.

Small print that has drifted out of date

Two documents on the site disagree about where the company is. The terms and conditions of service give the registered office as "International House, 24 Holborn Viaduct, London, England, EC1A 2BN". Companies House and the site's own privacy policy both give 12-13 Wells Mews, London W1T 3HE, an address the company moved to on 7 April 2025 from a PO Box in Cardiff. Nothing turns on it for a buyer, but the terms are the document you would quote in a dispute and they are the one that has not been updated.

The cancellation position is stated properly, at least. Total Car Check cites the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 and explains that a single report "is delivered to you, and therefore completed, immediately after you have submitted your request", so the usual 14-day right does not survive delivery. Multi-check packages do keep a 14-day right from the conclusion of the contract, which is a meaningful distinction and one many sites leave vague.

Liability is capped hard. The terms limit "TCC's total aggregate liability" to 125% of the fees paid — on a £9.99 report that is £12.49, outside the guarantees. The accuracy wording is candid about what that means: "Whilst we use reasonable care to ensure data contained within a report is accurate, correct and up to date, TCC cannot guarantee this to be the case."

Support is email only, at enquiries@totalcarcheck.co.uk, "Monday to Friday from 9am to 5pm", with a target of replying "within 24hrs". There is no published telephone number and no complaints procedure on the customer support page. For a £9.99 purchase that is defensible; for a £30,000 guarantee claim it means the first step is an email into a queue.

Two marketing claims that do not quite line up

The home page carries "Over 15 years of experience in vehicle history and data" and, a little further down, "Over 300 million checks completed since 2018". The first is right — the company was incorporated in 2009, which is closer to 16 years. The second counts from 2018, and neither figure is dated on the page, so a reader cannot tell whether the 300 million and the "33 million vehicles in the last year" are being measured the same way or which one is current.

The headline price has the same shape. The site's own page title advertises "the lowest cost car check in the UK - £1.99". That price exists, but it is the unit cost of a five-pack of Silver checks at £9.99, and Silver does not include the outstanding finance search. A single check at the advertised tier is £3.99, and a single report with the finance answer in it is £9.99. The £1.99 is real; it is just five checks away, and it is not the product most people arriving from a search for a car check think they are buying.

Neither point is a reason to avoid the service. Both are a reason to read the price table rather than the banner.

Nobody rates vehicle data checks, and nobody arbitrates them

A provenance check is a data service, not a financial one, so the machinery consumers are used to leaning on does not reach it. There is no Financial Conduct Authority permission to look up, no Financial Ombudsman Service to escalate to, and no Defaqto, Which? or Fairer Finance star rating for the category — searching for one returns nothing because none exists. If Total Car Check declines a guarantee claim, the options are its own complaints handling, a chargeback through your card issuer, or the small claims track. That is true of every provider in this market, which is why the wording of the two guarantees above is worth more attention than the headline number on either.

For scale, the Office for National Statistics recorded 312,250 police-recorded vehicle offences in England and Wales in the year ending March 2026, published on 23 July 2026 — down 11% on the previous year, within 1.6 million recorded thefts overall, themselves down 8%. Vehicle crime is falling, and the stolen marker is the least likely of the paid answers to change a purchase. The finance search is the one that earns the £9.99, and it is the one the free tier deliberately withholds.

Common questions

What does the free Total Car Check actually show?

More than 20 data fields at no cost: make, model, fuel type, BHP, road tax status and estimated cost, MPG and running-cost estimates, and the full MOT history. It does not show outstanding finance or insurance write-off status — the company says so explicitly. Those two answers are in the £3.99 Silver check (write-off) and the £9.99 Gold check (finance and write-off).

How much is a full check and is it cheaper in bulk?

A single Gold Check is £9.99. Three cost £14.99, which is £4.99 each, and five cost £19.99, which is £3.99 each. Silver is £3.99 singly and £9.99 for five, or £1.99 each. If you are looking at more than two cars, the multipacks are the obvious buy — and multipacks keep a 14-day cancellation right that a single report loses on delivery.

Is the £30,000 guarantee worth anything in practice?

It is real and it is underwritten by Experian Ltd for 2 years, but it only covers outstanding finance and it attaches conditions most buyers do not meet: verifying the seller's identity against a passport or driving licence, their address against a utility bill, matching the VIN, and taking a signed receipt — all before you pay. Cash above £1,000 or 10% of the price is unprotected, and paying below 70% of market value voids it.

Who owns Total Car Check?

Motorway Online Ltd, which acquired the business on 17 July 2023 and holds 75% or more of the shares and voting rights. Total Car Check Ltd remains a separate company, number 07043189, incorporated on 15 October 2009 and registered at 12-13 Wells Mews, London W1T 3HE. Founder David James joined Motorway as director of vehicle data.

Should I use Total Car Check instead of a check from a different provider?

Use it as well, rather than instead, on a car you are serious about. Finance agreements are registered by lenders with credit reference agencies, and no single register holds every agreement at every moment. Total Car Check's finance data comes from Experian while much of the market licenses one other register, so at £9.99 a second opinion from a different source is cheap cover on a purchase worth thousands.

Our verdict

Total Car Check is the cheapest credible way to answer the two questions that matter about a used car, and the free tier is worth running on any registration before you even arrange a viewing. At £9.99 — or £3.99 each in a five-pack — the Gold report costs little enough that checking three or four cars is a rounding error against the price of any of them, and the Experian-sourced finance data makes it a useful second opinion alongside the register most of the market licenses. Read both guarantee documents before you rely on either: the £30,000 is a finance guarantee with a doorstep checklist attached, and the stolen and write-off cover behind it is measured in thousands rather than tens of thousands. Treat the report as evidence, not as insurance, and it is very good value.
