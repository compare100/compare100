Towergate Boat Insurance

Rates and terms checked 28 August 2026 · Boat Insurance · Compare100 editorial team

Affiliate disclosure: we may earn a commission if you take out a product through links on this page. It costs you nothing extra and does not influence how providers are listed.

Ask for Towergate boat insurance today and you will be dealing with Navigators & General, a Brighton marine specialist that has been writing pleasure craft since 1921. That is not a rebadged call centre: the desk has been quoting yachts, narrowboats, sports boats and dinghies for a hundred and five years, spent forty-one of them inside Zurich after a 1983 acquisition, and moved to The Ardonagh Group in 2024. Most of the staff sail, row or cruise something themselves, which shows in the range — twelve separate products covering everything from a canoe to a commercial workboat.

The standard cover is strong for the money. Third party liability runs to £5 million on yachts, motorboats, sailing craft and small craft, and £3 million of third party and passenger liability on inland waterways. Rescue and evacuation is covered to £50,000, and there is another £50,000 for family and guests hit by an uninsured vessel. Machinery damage is included from new as standard where you can show routine maintenance, tenders stay covered even when used on their own, and an RYA qualification can take up to 10% off the price. Park in a marina and the excess disappears; prove the other party was at fault and it disappears again.

Two things changed underneath the brand and neither was announced loudly. The risk stopped sitting with Zurich on 1 December 2024, and from 1 November 2025 every Towergate marine policy moved to Geo Underwriting Services Limited, trading as Navigators & General. The Towergate name itself was retired across the group on 17 November 2025 in favour of Everywhen. The same team answers the phone on 01242 531172, the cover is largely the same cover, but the names on your certificate are not the names you bought from — and that matters more than it sounds when you come to claim.

Three names between you and the money

Follow the chain and it runs four deep. You buy from Navigators & General, which is a trading name of Geo Underwriting Services Limited, authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority under firm reference 308400 and registered at 2 Minster Court, Mincing Lane, London EC3R 7PD. Geo is the managing general agent — it prices the risk, issues the documents and handles the claim, but the money behind the policy belongs to HCC International Insurance Company plc, trading as Tokio Marine HCC, which holds firm reference 202655 and is regulated by both the FCA and the Prudential Regulation Authority. If you take the optional legal expenses cover, that section is arranged by Arc Legal Assistance and underwritten by AmTrust Specialty Limited under firm reference 202189, so a legal claim goes somewhere different again.

The Towergate side has moved too. Towergate branded policies sat with Advisory Insurance Brokers Limited, firm reference 313250, company number 4043759, which now trades as Everywhen after the group retired the Towergate name on 17 November 2025. The rebrand covered more than thirty-five broking businesses carrying roughly £2 billion of gross written premium and over 500,000 customers across more than 100 UK offices. Type the old Towergate boat address into a browser and you land on an Everywhen page whose only real function is a button reading "Go to Navigators & General". Existing Towergate marine policies keep their old branding until renewal and then come out the other side as Navigators & General documents.

None of that changes your protection. Both firms are FCA regulated, the policy carries Financial Services Compensation Scheme cover, and eligible complaints reach the Financial Ombudsman Service after eight weeks or a final response, whichever comes first. It does change who you ring, and it means a policy schedule from 2023 and a policy schedule from 2026 will name different companies for the same boat.

Agreed value is less agreed than it sounds

Marine insurance is usually sold on the promise that you fix the boat's worth in advance rather than argue about depreciation after a sinking. Navigators & General offers agreed value across its pleasure craft range, and it is worth having. Read the Freedom Sailing wording, though, and the default basis is market value — the cost of replacing the vessel with allowance for type, age and wear — with agreed value sitting as an option in Part B of the policy. Whether you actually bought it will be printed on your schedule and nowhere else, so that line is worth finding before you need it.

Even with agreed value selected, the deduction does not go away entirely. The wording states that deductions for age are made on any life rafts, tenders, outboards and trailers at 10% a year up to a maximum of 50%. A five-year-old outboard on an agreed-value policy is therefore a half-price outboard. The same logic runs through the general settlement clause: old materials are replaced with new without a betterment cut, except for sails, protective covers, running rigging, outboard motors, batteries and personal belongings, where the insurer may reduce the claim for age and condition. Those are precisely the items most likely to be damaged.

Personal belongings carry their own ceilings — £300 for any single item, rising to £750 if the boat is your home — and cover for contents stops if the vessel is left unoccupied for more than 45 days in a row. Theft of a tender doubles your excess unless the tender carries a permanent unique identifier. There are useful small benefits pulling the other way: key cover pays up to £500 a claim and £2,000 a year with no excess, and a lockout pays £60 an incident.

The line on your schedule that decides a claim

Cruising limits do more work in marine insurance than any other clause, and this policy runs five of them. Non-tidal UK waters covers inland systems only, with tidal stretches permitted purely to get in and out. Coastal UK waters reaches 12 nautical miles offshore. The continental Brest to Elbe range adds the near continent but forbids anything north of 61 degrees. Elbe to La Rochelle moves its southern boundary with the calendar — 48 degrees north between October and March, 46 degrees north from April to September. The European inland and coastal option holds you to 5 miles offshore. Weather, danger or an order from a government or legal authority are the only permitted reasons to cross the line.

Inland waterways policies work differently again. They are British inland waters only, with no continental extension, but they include up to 30 days of UK coastal use in a policy year at no extra charge, and free personal accident cover to £60,000 for one event covering the owner and non-paying guests. Liveaboard cover extends household belongings, alternative accommodation and £3 million of liability to members of the household.

Some restrictions sit outside the geography. The third party product declines vessels built before 1960, anything capable of more than 50 knots, ferro cement hulls, static houseboats and amateur-built or converted craft. Surveys are generally required on boats over 23 feet and more than 20 years old, and once one is done satisfactorily no further survey is asked for at least five years. Machinery breakdown cover attaches to newer hulls, and here the marketing and the wording do not quite line up: the product pages say cover runs for up to three years from new as standard, while the Freedom Sailing wording conditions that section on the vessel being under five years old from completion of build.

Where the small print will catch you out

Sail alone and read this twice. You are not covered single handed for more than 18 hours in any 24 hour period, and not covered single handed at all if the vessel is over 10 metres, unless the schedule is endorsed to say otherwise. Racing is excluded for masts, spars, fittings, sails and rigging unless Racing Risks has been bought in Part B, though the underwriter will look at events, racing weeks and RORC or JOG entries on referral.

The exclusions read like a surveyor's report. Osmosis, marine growth and gradual water ingress are out. So is gradual deterioration, unless it could not have been spotted by routine inspection or prevented by servicing and maintenance in line with an engineer's, surveyor's or manufacturer's advice — a clause that puts your maintenance record on trial. Machinery failure caused solely by the breakdown of a component part is excluded. Personal accident cover stops entirely at age 75, and medical expenses under the sailing wording pay £500 for any one incident, where the motorboat product page advertises up to £1,000 following an accident on board.

Excess figures are the real gap. Nothing published names a number: the wording refers you to the schedule throughout, so the excess only becomes visible once you have a quote in hand. There is no retail fee schedule on the site either, so instalment charges, mid-term adjustment fees and cancellation costs are not knowable before you engage. A total loss carries no excess, marina and ashore claims carry none, and a proven non-fault claim carries none, which is genuinely good — but the number that applies the rest of the time is yours to extract on the phone.

How to check them, and what nobody publishes

The Financial Ombudsman Service published its 2025/26 annual data on 21 May 2026, recording 214,600 new complaints across all financial products and an average uphold rate of 30%. Pleasure craft is not one of the product lines it breaks out, so there is no boat insurance uphold rate to hold this insurer against — a limitation worth knowing rather than a criticism of the firm. The 14 day cooling-off period applies from the day the documents arrive, and gives a full premium refund provided you have not claimed and do not intend to. Watch the automatic renewal clause if you pay by credit agreement, because consenting to the credit consents to the roll-over.

Two claims made about this insurer could not be verified from outside a quote. No current Defaqto star rating for the pleasure craft range could be found on the provider's own material, so treat any starred badge you see elsewhere as needing confirmation. A multi-vessel or fleet discount is widely assumed to exist for owners with more than one boat, but nothing on the provider's public pages or in the broker product brochure sets out the terms, so ask for it in writing rather than expecting it. Twelve month laid-up policies at reduced rates are confirmed for boats out of use, and small craft moorings allow up to 28 days between April and September.

Before buying, get three things in writing: the excess that applies to a hull damage claim, whether agreed value or market value is showing on your schedule, and the exact cruising range against where you actually intend to go. Then quote it against Craftinsure, GJW Direct and Haven Knox-Johnston, because the pleasure craft market is small enough that the same hull can attract very different numbers.

Common questions

Is Towergate boat insurance still available?

Not under that name. The Towergate marine book transferred to Geo Underwriting Services Limited, trading as Navigators & General, on 1 November 2025, and the Towergate brand was retired across the group on 17 November 2025 in favour of Everywhen. Existing policies keep their Towergate branding until renewal, then convert. The team is reachable on 01242 531172.

Who actually underwrites the policy now?

HCC International Insurance Company plc, trading as Tokio Marine HCC, under firm reference 202655. Navigators & General is a trading name of Geo Underwriting Services Limited, firm reference 308400, which acts as the managing general agent. Policies written before 1 December 2024 sat with Zurich instead.

How much third party liability do I get?

£5 million as standard on yacht, motorboat, sailing and small craft policies, and £3 million of third party and passenger liability on inland waterways cover, which extends to household members on a liveaboard extension. Marinas, harbours and recognised mooring areas generally require evidence of third party cover before they will take your lines.

Does agreed value mean I get the full sum insured?

Not on everything. Agreed value fixes the vessel's figure, but the wording still deducts 10% a year, up to a maximum of 50%, from life rafts, tenders, outboards and trailers. Sails, protective covers, running rigging, batteries and personal belongings can also be reduced for age and condition. Check whether your schedule shows agreed value or market value, because market value is the default.

Will I need a survey?

Generally yes if the boat is over 23 feet and more than 20 years old. Once a survey has been completed satisfactorily, no further survey is normally requested for at least five years. Machinery cover is aimed at newer hulls, so an older boat should expect hull and liability cover rather than breakdown protection.

How far can I sail on a standard policy?

It depends which of the five cruising ranges is on your schedule. UK coastal reaches 12 nautical miles offshore. Brest to Elbe adds the near continent but bars anything above 61 degrees north. Elbe to La Rochelle runs to 48 degrees north from October to March and 46 degrees north from April to September. European inland and coastal holds you to 5 miles offshore. Inland waterways policies are British inland waters plus 30 days of UK coastal use.

What if I have a complaint?

The internal route aims to resolve within three business days, escalating to the Customer Relations Unit with a response inside eight weeks. After that, or after a final response, eligible customers can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service at Exchange Tower, London E14 9SR, on 0800 023 4567. The policy also carries Financial Services Compensation Scheme protection.

Our verdict

A century-old marine specialist wearing a new set of names. The cover itself is competitive — £5 million of liability as standard, £50,000 for rescue and evacuation, no excess in a marina or on a total loss, tenders covered independently and up to 10% off for an RYA ticket — and the 1921 pedigree is real rather than marketing. The changes are administrative: Zurich stepped back from the risk on 1 December 2024, Tokio Marine HCC carries it now under firm reference 202655, and the Towergate marine book moved to Geo Underwriting on 1 November 2025 before the Towergate brand itself was retired that same month. What deserves your attention is not the branding but the wording. Agreed value still depreciates outboards, tenders and life rafts at 10% a year to a 50% floor, no excess figure is published anywhere on the public site, single handed cover stops at 18 hours and at 10 metres, and the cruising range on your certificate is the clause that will decide a claim. Get the excess, the valuation basis and the cruising limits confirmed in writing, then quote it against Craftinsure, GJW Direct and Haven Knox-Johnston before you commit.
